## AI Use

Load when anyone travels into the Greyfens — the great fog-drowned peat wetland at the heart of the Sundering Reach. This file covers the fen as a **physical place**: its terrain, its named natural features, its hazards with DCs, and what lives there. Usable for any story; nothing here depends on a particular plot.

## One-Sentence Identity

A vast, fog-drowned peat wetland where the ground lies about how solid it is, sound comes from the wrong direction, and travelers who leave the marked paths are not always found.

## What A Traveler Notices First

Fog that never fully lifts. Black water between hummocks of red-brown peat, drowned trees standing bare, and the white gleam of old bone in the shallows. Sound carries strangely here — a voice from one direction, footsteps from another. Pale lights drift at night over the deep water. And, more than anywhere else in the Reach, the lingering dead are simply *present*: standing in the water, quiet, sorrowful, and in no hurry.

## Terrain

Peat bog and open fen, waterlogged year-round and impassable in stretches. Firm ground exists but is discontinuous — a lace of hummocks, old causeway fragments, and gravel spits threaded through standing water. The peat is deep, and in places it floats: ground that holds a sheep will not hold a laden traveler.

Visibility is the defining problem. Fog sits in the hollows even on clear days and can close in within the hour, and the flat, featureless terrain offers almost nothing to navigate by once it does.

**Getting through it:** local guides maintain marked firm-paths — lines of cut withy stakes, notched posts, or piled stones — and renew them each season. These are the only reliably safe routes. Hiring a fen-guide from a nearby village is the single most useful thing a stranger can do, and every local will say so.

## Named Features

- **The Bone Weir** — a natural snag where the current piles drowned timber against a choke of dead trees. It collects everything the water carries: driftwood, lost gear, animal carcasses, and, often enough, the drowned. Locals work it for salvage and dread it in equal measure.
- **The Lantern Maze** — a wide stretch of deep pools and false footing where drifting lights are thickest at night. Travelers have been led into open water here for as long as anyone has kept count. Pure environmental hazard.
- **The Still Pool** — a perfectly round, dark, motionless pool that local custom treats as sacred, or at least as somewhere you do not swim, fish, or raise your voice. Offerings are sometimes found at its edge.
- **The Drowned Blind** — a sunk fowler's hide, half-collapsed into the water, on a spit of firm ground deep in the fen. Long abandoned by whoever built it, and periodically re-occupied by people who would rather not be found.
- **The Standing Dead** — a broad shallows where the lingering dead gather in unusual numbers, standing in the water among the drowned trees. Not hostile; not going anywhere. Locals detour around it out of respect rather than fear.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Sinking ground** (off the firm path) | DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) each stretch to read safe footing | Failure: mired — DC 13 Strength to pull free, half speed until then. Hard failure: begins to sink; DC 15 Athletics or descend 1d6 stages toward drowning. A rope or a second pair of hands ends it immediately. Always telegraphed by the ground's "give" underfoot. |
| **Killing fog** | DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) to hold direction without a guide or marked path | Failure wastes hours and may loop the traveler back to a hazard already passed. Disadvantage on Perception beyond 30 ft. while it holds. |
| **False lights** (Lantern Maze) | DC 13 Wisdom save to disbelieve | Failure draws the traveler toward deep water. Telegraphed — the lights behave as though they *want* to be followed. |
| **Cold exposure** | DC 12 Constitution save after a night in the open fen without shelter | Failure: 1 level of exhaustion. |
| **Bad water** | DC 12 Constitution save if drinking unboiled fen water | Failure: poisoned for 4d6 hours; peat water is drinkable but foul, standing water is not. |

## Fauna And Encounters

**Mundane wildlife (the common case).** The fen is rich, and most of what a traveler meets simply lives here.

- **Waterfowl and wading birds** in enormous numbers — the fen's main food resource and the reason fowlers work it at all. Harmless; a flock going up all at once is the fen's best early-warning system.
- **Fen constrictors** — large water-snakes in the deep channels. Use a **Giant Constrictor Snake-like** profile, or a lesser **Constrictor Snake-like** one for smaller specimens. Ambush from cover, drag toward deep water, disengage if they cannot get a grip in a round or two. Telegraphed by disturbed water and drag-marks on the peat.
- **Giant frogs and toads** in the shallows — **Giant Frog-like**, CR 1/4. Nuisance predators; dangerous only in numbers or to the very small.
- **Insect swarms** — **Swarm of Insects-like**, worst in warm still weather. More a misery and an exhaustion risk than a real threat, but they will drive an unprepared camp out into the dark.
- **Fen cats and otters** — harmless to adults, ruinous to unattended supplies.
- **Boar** on the drier hummocks — **Boar-like**, CR 1/4, genuinely dangerous when cornered or protecting young.

**The lingering dead.** The Greyfens hold more of them than anywhere else in the region, and any traveler who spends a night here will likely see one. Run them per [`../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md) — **not hostile by default**. They stand, they drift, they mourn, and they ignore anyone who does not trouble them. A traveler who attacks one, robs a grave, or panics can turn a sad encounter into a lethal one. A properly performed folk rite (DC 13 Religion) settles one peacefully, and locals will thank you for it.

**People.** The fen is a good place to not be found, and is used accordingly: smugglers moving goods off the roads, poachers working other people's fowling grounds, and the occasional hermit or outlaw. Most prefer to avoid a stranger entirely. Use **Bandit-like** or **Scout-like** profiles; see [`../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md).

## Level Range And Danger

**Level 2–5. Moderate-to-high danger — and almost all of it is terrain, not teeth.** The fen kills by drowning, exposure, and getting lost, not by monsters. A well-supplied party that hires a guide and stays on the marked paths faces **low** danger; the same party off-path in fog faces the most dangerous ground in the region.

**Telegraph it.** Every local will warn a stranger, at length, and offer to sell them a guide. That warning is the mechanic.

**Retreat:** follow the marked stakes back toward firm ground; failing that, follow running water downstream, which eventually reaches the river or a settlement.

**Resting:** no safe long rest in the open fen — exposure, damp, and insects. Villages at the fen edge, a guide's hut, or a dry hummock with a proper fire are the recovery points.

## Resources

- **Salvage** at the Bone Weir: lost gear, tools, timber, and whatever the water has taken lately. Genuinely lucrative and genuinely grim.
- **Fen herbs** — DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to gather; useful for healer's kits and folk remedies. A real reason for herbalists to risk the fen.
- **Peat** — the region's fuel, cut in banks at the fen's drier margins.
- **Fowl, fish, and eels** — the fen feeds the villages around it.
- **Reeds and withy** — thatch, baskets, and the stakes that mark the paths.
