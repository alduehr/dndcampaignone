## AI Use

Load for the contested ruin-country astride the continent's central mountains. This is the largest ruin complex in Orrun and the reason a great many people travel very far to dig.

## One-Sentence Identity

The shattered surface heart of the fallen Custodian Concord — an enormous contested ruin astride the Greatspine, where every power on the continent scrambles for records and relics.

## Geography

An immense complex of collapsed halls, broken avenues, buried courtyards, and toppled colonnades, spread across high ground where the **Greatspine** cordillera crests. The stump of the Concord's great spire is visible for miles and serves as the region's landmark, waypoint, and grave-marker.

Defining features:

- **The Ruin'd Crown** — the ruin complex itself: miles of it, only partly explored, and mapped chiefly by rumor.
- **The defended margin** — the outer fringe where a fortified scavenger-town holds a perimeter and enforces gate-law.
- **The Pilgrim wastelands** — dry scrub south of the ruin, sprawling with camps.
- **The deep interior** — where halls still stand roofed and streets still run whole; entirely unsafe and universally acknowledged as such.
- **Melt-streams and dry cisterns** — water is scarce and tactical inside the ruin; the Concord's cisterns are dry, and knowing which streams still run is worth paying for.

**Borders:** the Glassmere League's country (west, down the Crown Road), the Greatspine passes (north), the Saltmere basin (south), barely-mapped eastern track-country toward the great forest (east).

## Climate

High, cold, and thin-aired, with hard winters and short seasons. Weather comes over the ridge fast. Snow closes the higher approaches for months; the ruin holds cold in its stone long after the surrounding country has warmed.

## Culture And Livelihood

There is no native population. Everyone here came for the ruin.

The scavenger-town is a frontier boomtown of the harshest kind: gate-law, posted rules, war-captains, and a market in salvage, maps, guides, and rumor. The Pilgrim Camps below are poorer and more desperate — relic-seekers, the ruined, the bereaved, and preachers offering explanations. Expedition parties come and go, each with charters, patrons, and rivals.

Common trades: salvage, guiding, portering, water-selling, arms and repair, forgery of provenance, and the buying and selling of information about what lies where.

**Who holds it:** nobody holds the ruin. A **scavenger-town warden** holds the only defended ground and enforces her own law within it. Scholarly expeditions, merchant-bank relic agents, and independent delvers all operate under a rough, constantly renegotiated understanding, and all of them spy on each other. Outside the gate, possession is the entire body of law.

## Settlements

- **Crownmouth** — the fortified scavenger-town on the ruin's northwestern margin; the only functioning settlement in the region and the last reliably safe ground.
- **The Pilgrim Camps** — sprawling temporary shanty-camps on the southern fringe; large, poor, volatile, and growing.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The Crown's deep interior** — the true dungeon-country of the region and the reason the rest of it exists. Collapse, disorientation, no water, and old things that have not left.
- **The Greatspine ridge** — altitude, cold, and the contested passes.
- **The Pilgrim wastelands** — dry scrub, thin water, and thousands of people camped in it.
- **The waypoint** — a guide-kept camp inside the outer ruin; the only base of any kind beyond the gate.

## Travel Routes

- **The Crown Road** west to the great river-cities — two to three weeks; well-traveled as far as the scavenger-town, then effectively abandoned.
- **The Greatspine Passes** north — faster, higher, and contested by everyone who can post men on them.
- **The Pilgrim Road** south toward the salt sea.
- **The Eastern Track** toward the great forest — barely mapped and rarely used.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Echo Realm**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md).

## General Danger And Tone

High danger, heavily telegraphed. The margin is survivable; the interior is not, and everybody says so — the guides refuse routes, the war-captains post hard rules, and the number of camps thins to nothing a mile past the gate.

Standing hazards: collapse and unstable stone, altitude and cold, no potable water, getting lost in miles of identical halls, rival expeditions, and the ruin's dead — which are numerous, old, and the single most consistent thing every returning delver reports.
