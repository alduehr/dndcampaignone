## One-Sentence Identity

A storm-battered, fiercely independent western coast of grey cliffs, shingle coves, fishing villages, and the salt-pans that supply the whole frontier.

## Geography

West of the fens the salt marshes give onto the cold **Pale Sea**. The coastline is grey cliff and shingle cove, broken by headlands, sea-caves, and tidal salt-pans, with offshore stacks and reefs beyond the surf. Half-ruined Concord lighthouses and small stone sea-shrines stand on the headlands, weathered nearly featureless.

Defining features:

- **The Drowned Lamp** — the great ruined Concord lighthouse at the foot of its headland; the Coast's chief landmark, and long since claimed by the tide.
- **Wrackmouth harbor** — a deep-water cove that makes the Coast's only proper port possible.
- **The salt-pans** — tidal evaporation flats and their working camps, mostly toward the marsh edge.
- **The Skerries** — offshore sea-stacks and reefs west of the coast; the region's wreck-ground and its most dangerous water.
- **The Verdance mouth** — south along the coast, where the great river of the northwest quarter reaches the sea. The gateway to the inland water-route.

**Borders:** Sundering Reach (east, via the salt-marsh and the town of Saltmargin), the Pale Sea (west and north), the Verdance mouth and Caradril's coastal hinterland (south and southeast), open wild coast (north).

## Climate

Cold, wet, and wind-scoured, with frequent storms and thick sea-fog. Winter is genuinely dangerous. Autumn is gale season, and the season of wrecks — every coastal village expects to lose boats, and plans for it.

## Culture And Livelihood

Coast folk are their own people: independent, weather-hardened, blunt, and deeply superstitious about the sea. The sea gives them everything and takes a tithe in drowned men every year, and their culture is built around that bargain. They live close to the dead because the sea keeps giving them back.

Common trades: fishing, salt-making, boat-building, coastal shipping, kelp and shellfish, wrecking and salvage on the wild coast, and smuggling — which every harbor-town denies and every harbor-town does.

Death-rites here have a distinct **salt-and-tide** flavor found nowhere else on the frontier: bodies (and empty coffins, when the sea keeps the body) are seen off at the tide-line, with salt, at a particular state of the water. The Coast's **Mourners' Circle** chapters — their elders titled salt-mothers — are respected far above any magistrate.

**Who holds it:** loosely claimed by the Reachward Compact, which fields a thin harbor-watch. In economic terms the region is dominated by the **Cinder Ledger**, the chartered merchant-bank out of Caradril that controls the salt trade and much of the shipping. Politically, each harbor-town governs itself through its own harbor-moot, and outsiders' writs stop at the tide-line.

## Settlements

- **Wrackmouth** — the Coast's hub: a cliff-and-harbor town of ~1,100 around a deep cove; the region's main port and salt-market.
- **Cobble Strand** — a poor, devout shingle-cove fishing village under the Drowned Lamp's headland.
- **Saltmargin** — the salt-marsh gateway town on the Reach's side of the border; most travelers enter the Coast through it.
- **Salt-pan camps** — semi-permanent worker settlements out on the flats.
- **The wreckers' strand** — an informal, unlovely settlement on the wild coast where salvage is landed and few questions are asked.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The cliff-road and headland sea-shrines** — the coastal travel layer; exposed, narrow in places, and fog-prone.
- **The sea-caves and smuggler coves** — tidal caves along the cliff-foot; several flood completely at high water.
- **The Drowned Lamp headland** — the ruined lighthouse and the rocks below it; storm-cut and tide-locked.
- **The Skerries** — offshore stacks and reefs. Wrecks, seals, seabirds, and a reputation no fisherman disputes.
- **The salt-pans and marsh edge** — flat, exposed, and confusing in fog.

## Travel Routes

The **Pale Road** runs the coast from Saltmargin through Cobble Strand to Wrackmouth — about three days — with Saltmargin about five days from the frontier's center.

From Wrackmouth, the **water-route** runs down the coast and up the Verdance to the city-state of Caradril, roughly six or seven days by ship, weather permitting. This is often faster and safer than any overland road, and is the Coast's great advantage. Overland south along the coast takes eight or nine days and is rougher going.

Coastal travel means tides, storms, and fog. Local skippers who know the water are worth whatever they ask.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Pall**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Windswept, hard, and honest. The sea is the great antagonist and it does not need help. Sailors and locals telegraph danger constantly — an experienced traveler who listens to them is usually fine.

Standing hazards: storms and high seas, tide-traps in caves and on causeways, cliff-falls, sea-fog, cold-water immersion, and false lights on the rocks. Wreckers and smugglers are a real criminal current on the wild coast. The Skerries are the region's one place that everybody, including the criminals, agrees is a bad idea.
