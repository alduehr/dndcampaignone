## AI Use

Load for the cold northeastern mining frontier. This is the harshest inhabited region on the continent and the one with the least law; a good dark mirror of the northwestern frontier.

## One-Sentence Identity

A second, harsher frontier of cold stone, deep mines, and petty-warlord holds, where might is the whole of the law and the ruins on the fringe are called "bad ground" and left alone.

## Geography

Cold stone country beneath the **Karran Teeth**, the jagged northeastern range that walls the continent off from the northern ocean. Bare rock, thin soil, scree, mountain melt-streams, and mine-workings everywhere the ore runs.

Defining features:

- **The Karran Teeth** — the near-impassable barrier range; extreme cold and altitude, and the Sunder Ocean somewhere beyond it.
- **The mining basin** — the central bowl where the region's market town and muster-ground sit.
- **The Deep Cuts** — the richest ore-complex in the Marches; deep, wet, and lately unlucky.
- **The Old Iron forts** — scattered crumbling Concord road-forts on the northeastern fringe, which even the warlords' men will not camp in.
- **The Iron Road** — a partly Concord-paved road running north-south through the region; tolled by whoever is strongest that year.

**Borders:** the Wender Steppe (west), the great eastern forest (south and southwest), the Karran Teeth and the far north (north and east).

## Climate

Cold, dry, and high. Long hard winters with heavy snow on the upper roads; brief summers. The passes are shut for months. Mountain weather arrives with no warning and the region has very little natural shelter.

## Culture And Livelihood

Might makes law. Holds are fortress-towns under warlords whose authority extends exactly as far as their men do, and the region's stability is precisely as good as the strongest warlord's health. Mercenary companies hire out at the market town; ore-factors keep the ledgers that constitute the second law.

Common trades: iron, copper, silver and lesser ores, smelting and smithwork, mercenary soldiering, toll-taking, and provisioning the mines.

Culture is blunt, transactional, and surprisingly punctilious about specific things: contracts of hire are honored, ore-weights are honest, and cheating a mine-crew is the one crime the region reliably punishes. Hospitality is bought, not given.

**Who holds it:** the warlords, in rough order of strength — a paramount warlord at the largest fortress-town, a pass-toll warlord on the mountain approaches, and lesser holds between. The mining-and-mercenary town is the closest thing to neutral ground, run in practice by an ore-factor's ledgers and a bribable toll-post. Scholarly expeditions from the south come looking for the fringe ruins and are regarded as eccentric but well-funded.

## Settlements

- **Brask's Hold** — the paramount warlord's fortress-town; toll, inspection, and iron discipline. Notable outsiders are looked over personally.
- **Karran-Gate** — the mining-and-mercenary town: market, muster-ground, and the region's only real neutral ground.
- **The Deep Cuts** — a work-camp complex at the mines, not a town.
- **The Old Iron forts** — ruins, not a settlement, and treated as dead ground.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The Karran Teeth** — an environmental wall rather than a place: extreme cold, altitude, and no help. The continent's northeastern limit.
- **The Old Forts track** — the northeastern approach to the ruins. Locals report that the country gets worse the closer you get, and stop going well before the walls.
- **The mine-country between holds** — spoil-heaps, adits, sumps, and abandoned workings; treacherous and unmarked.

## Travel Routes

- **The Iron Road** — the region's north-south spine; weeks end to end, tolled at the paramount hold, partly Concord-paved and partly rubble.
- **The Deep Cuts access road** — permit-gated by whoever owns the mines this season.
- **The Old Forts track** — northeast, weeks, and dangerous enough that guides refuse it outright.
- **The Teeth Pass** — north, near-impassable, and closed most of the year.

West lies the steppe; south and southwest, the great forest.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Ashlands**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Hard, cold, and mercenary. In the holds a traveler is safe as long as they pay, bow, or are useful; outside them the rule is whatever the nearest armed party decides.

Standing hazards: cold and altitude, mine collapse and bad air, warlord muscle and hired companies, mountain predators, and the fringe ruins — which the warlords themselves prohibit approaching, on grounds they describe only as "bad ground." Miners have lately been refusing certain deep workings, which in this region is a significant statement.
