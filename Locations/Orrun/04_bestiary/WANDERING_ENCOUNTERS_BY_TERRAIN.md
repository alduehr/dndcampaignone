## AI Use

Roll or pick from these tables when a traveler is crossing ordinary country in Orrun and no location-specific material applies. These are the **everyday dangers of the world** — weather, animals, desperate people, bad ground, and the occasional dead who has not gone. Nothing here is tied to any particular plot, faction, or ruin; it is safe to use in any adventure set in this world.

Stat references point to the sibling files in this folder. Everything is written for **one traveler or a small group**, with warning signs, morale, and a way out of every row.

## How To Use

1. Pick the **terrain** the traveler is crossing.
2. Roll **d10** (or pick to fit the scene).
3. Read the **type tag** — most rows are not fights.
4. Apply any **travel variant** (night, storm, fog, cold, heat) from the table at the bottom.
5. For levels 6–10, swap in a row from that terrain's **Harder Country** list instead.

**Do not roll** when: the road is patrolled and it is broad daylight; the travelers just finished a hard fight and are limping to shelter; an encounter would only tax movement without creating a decision. Roll when the result creates a **choice, a risk, or a complication**.

## Type Legend

- **[Beast]** — an animal or predator. Telegraphed; has a morale break; usually wants food or distance, not a fight. → `COMMON_WILDLIFE.md`
- **[Folk]** — people. Mostly social; the dangerous ones want goods, not corpses. → `HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`
- **[Hazard]** — ground, water, or weather. A check and a decision, not initiative. → `ENVIRONMENTAL_HAZARDS.md`
- **[Dead]** — someone who has not finished leaving. Rarely hostile; usually resolvable. → `FOLK_UNDEAD.md`
- **[Sign]** — evidence of something *else*, offering the chance to avoid, prepare, or investigate.
- **[Find]** — a thing worth having: shelter, forage, salvage, a shortcut.

## 1. Forest And Woodland (d10)

| d10 | Encounter | Type | Solo Danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wolf-sign: scat, a stripped carcass, prints crossing the path in numbers | [Sign] | none (warning) |
| 2 | A boar in a rooting-ground, unwilling to yield the trail | [Beast] | low–mod |
| 3 | Charcoal-burners or woodcutters at a smoking clamp; wary, then talkative | [Folk] | none |
| 4 | Poachers checking snare-lines who would rather not be seen | [Folk] | low |
| 5 | Deadfall and blowdown across the way — go around (slow) or over (risky) | [Hazard] | low |
| 6 | A web-thick hollow where the birds have gone quiet | [Beast][Sign] | mod |
| 7 | Someone's offering left on a stump or a stone, recently renewed | [Sign] | none |
| 8 | A lost child, forager, or dog, three hours from anywhere | [Folk] | none |
| 9 | A dead man standing among the trees, facing the way he was walking | [Dead] | low |
| 10 | A dry hollow with old firewood and a stone ring — a good camp, if it's free | [Find] | none |

**Harder country (levels 6–10):** a bear on a kill and unwilling to leave it · a deserter camp holding the only bridge · a great boar the locals have named · a deep-wood stretch where the trail simply stops · a wrathful dead at a robbed grave.

## Night Camp (d8)

Roll once for an unguarded or badly-sited camp, or whenever the fiction invites it.

| d8 | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing. Cold, quiet, and a real long rest. |
| 2 | Something comes to the edge of the firelight, looks, and leaves. |
| 3 | Rain, wind, or frost — the fire needs work or the rest is broken. |
| 4 | Small thieves: rats, a fox, a bold bird. Rations or a small item goes missing. |
| 5 | Other travelers, drawn by the fire. Wary, tired, negotiable. |
| 6 | Predators test the camp: circling, one probe, gone if answered. |
| 7 | A light or a voice out in the dark that does not come closer. |
| 8 | Someone is watching the camp and does not want to be seen (scout, poacher, or worse). |

**Solo rule:** a bad camp roll should cost *rest, resources, or sleep* before it costs hit points. Never open an ambush on a sleeping lone traveler without a chance to notice — a passive Perception, a noise, or an animal's reaction.

## Travel Variants (apply to any table)

| Variant | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Night** | Perception and Survival DCs +2; [Beast] rows more likely; [Folk] rows more dangerous; always offer the choice to camp or push on. |
| **Storm / heavy rain** | Ranged and vision penalties; add a flood, lightning, or cold-soak hazard; navigation DC +2 to +4. |
| **Fog** | Vision under 30 ft; getting lost and being ambushed both easier — but so is *evading* anything on the road. |
| **Cold / snow** | Exhaustion via Constitution saves; shelter becomes the objective; tracks are easy to read and travel is slow. |
| **Heat / drought** | Exhaustion via Constitution saves; water becomes a resource; animals concentrate at whatever water there is. |
| **Season** | Spring: floods and mud. Summer: heat, flies, fire. Autumn: storms, and beasts in rut. Winter: cold, hunger, bold predators. Shift the table toward its seasonal hazard. |

A variant should **change the decision** (camp, reroute, pay, push on at a cost), not simply add damage.

## Solo And Small-Group Rules

- **Default to the non-combat reading.** Most rows are a decision or a conversation.
- **One real threat at a time.** If more danger is wanted, raise the single creature's tier — do not add bodies. Two foes against one traveler already plays like a deadly fight.
- **Telegraph, then allow a choice.** The [Sign] rows exist so that the dangerous rows are never a pure surprise.
- **Morale is standard.** Animals break when hurt or outmatched; people break when the job stops being worth the risk.
- **Retreat exists in open country.** An overland encounter should almost never trap anyone; a fleeing traveler who abandons something (a mule, a load, a route) usually gets away.

## Scaling Notes

| Level band | How to run these tables |
|---|---|
| **1–2** | Prefer [Folk], [Hazard], [Sign], and [Find] rows. Soften DCs by 1–2. A single wolf is a warning, not a fight. |
| **3–5** | As written. |
| **6–10** | Use the Harder Country lists. Raise hazard DCs by 2. Field one larger predator, one better-led human band, or one dead that has turned — not more bodies. |
| **11+** | These tables become **color, not challenge**. Narrate them as texture; reserve actual rolls for weather, terrain, and social rows, and take real threats from the region's own material. |
