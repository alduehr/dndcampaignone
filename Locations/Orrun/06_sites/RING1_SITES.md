## AI Use

Load when anyone in the three regions ringing the Sundering Reach — the **Ashgarden Vale** (south), the **Tollwood** (east), the **Pale Coast** (west) — enters a ruin, a grove, a sea-cave, or a tomb. Nine authored sites, described as physical places: footing, water, tide, dark, air, what lives there, and what can be carried out. Three of them are among the largest Concord works outside the Reach; three are pre-Concord and stranger; three are held by living people, where the fight is the failure state.

**Relic-exposure ("thin-touch")** applies here as in the Reach: **DC 14 Con** on prolonged handling of raw relics; **disadvantage on death saves** until a folk rite (**DC 13 Religion**) or a long rest at a working shrine. See [`SUNDERING_REACH_SITES.md`](SUNDERING_REACH_SITES.md).

## The Ashgarden Vale

### The Buried Cloister

**Type** collapsed Concord cloister · **Region** an orchard hillside an hour south of Orchardmere · **Level** 2–4 · **Danger** low–moderate (unstable ruin)

**What It Is.** A Concord wayside cloister — a small residential house of the old order, attached to the road system, which kept written records and performed rites for travelers who died on the road. It came down in the Quietfall and the orchard grew over the top of it. Concord Remnant scholar-pilgrims have opened a dig-shaft into it and are working through it methodically.

**Approach and Entrance.** A dig-shaft and a collapsed cloister-arch on a wooded slope, entirely public knowledge in Orchardmere. The scholars' survey camp sits at the mouth. They will let a useful stranger in, bargain, or compete — the entrance is a negotiation, not a lock.

**Layout.**
1. **The Dig-Mouth** — the camp, the shaft, the shored arch. The social gate.
2. **The Cloister Walk** — a half-collapsed colonnade. Bad footing, loose vault-stones overhead, the first inscription.
3. **The Scriptorium** — the record-room, partly intact. Shelves of Concord text, most of it legible, all of it in a language nobody local reads.
4. **The Settling Cells** — a row of niche-cells. Minor relics still in some of them. Cold.
5. **The Sunk Chapel** — the lowest room, ankle-deep in ground water. The cloister's altar.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable vault (Cloister Walk) | DC 13 Perception to spot | DC 13 Dex save or 1d6 falling stone, briefly pinned (DC 12 Athletics) |
| Flooded chapel | DC 11 Acrobatics on slick stone; DC 12 Con at the back | Falls; foul air, minor |
| Relic-exposure (Settling Cells) | DC 14 Con | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** A scavenging animal in the Cloister Walk (**Giant Badger-like** or **Swarm of Rats-like**, CR 1/8–1/4), telegraphed. In the cells and chapel, one lingering dead: a brother of the house who stayed at his post — **Specter/Ghost-like**, AC 12, ~26 HP, sorrowful, **not hostile unless the cells are stripped disrespectfully in front of him**. He asks for his vigil to be ended kindly. Parley (**DC 12 Insight/Persuasion**) or folk rite (**DC 13 Religion**).

The Remnant scholars are the real complication — competing for the scriptorium is a social scene, not a fight.

**Salvage.** Concord metal and fittings, ~30 gp. One or two minor Remembrance relics from the cells. A near-complete page of the Concord script (the Remnant pays ~50 gp; a Mourners' Circle elder would rather it were buried). The brother's keepsake, which has a grave it belongs in.

**Getting Out.** Open shaft, short walk back to Orchardmere. Safe short rest at the dig-mouth; safe long rest at the scholars' camp if you are on terms with them.

### The Pellow Grange

**Type** working farmstead over an old field-shrine · **Region** a few miles from Tilbrook · **Level** 2–4 · **Danger** low–moderate; social and reputational

**What It Is.** A walled farmstead the **Cinder Ledger** took for debt, now standing empty except for a caretaker by day and a dig-crew by night. The Ledger's men are quarrying an old Concord field-shrine in the back field for its relics and crating them for shipment south. Tilbrook knows this. Tilbrook is not allowed near.

**Approach and Entrance.** Barred gate, a house pennant, a caretaker's lodge, a dog. By night there is lamplight in the back field and the sound of digging. Scout it, talk past the caretaker, go over the wall, or wait for the night shift to change. **Storming it is the failure path**, not the intended one.

**Layout.**
1. **The Farmyard** — gate, lodge, barn, dog. The stealth/social gate.
2. **The Foreman's Office** — the old farmhouse's front room. The dig-ledger and a strongbox.
3. **The Cellar** — the relic-store. Crates, labelled, addressed south.
4. **The Back Field** — the open quarry over the shrine, worked by lamplight.
5. **The Shrine-Pit** — the exposed Concord field-shrine at the bottom of the dig. Minor relics, inscription, and graves the digging has gone through.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Wall and dog | DC 13 Stealth; DC 12 Animal Handling to quiet the dog | Detection |
| Dig-pit collapse | DC 13 Dex save on loose spoil | Slide into the pit, 1d6, DC 11 Athletics to climb |
| Cellar air / unsafe shoring | DC 12 Con; DC 13 Perception to spot a bad shore | Fumes; collapse |
| Relic-exposure (Shrine-Pit) | DC 14 Con | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** All living, all human, none of them keen. Caretaker (**Commoner-like**) and dog by day. By night, 2–3 dig-crew (**Thug-like**, AC 11, ~32 HP) and a foreman (**Spy/Thug-like**, AC 13, ~40 HP) who fights only cornered and surrenders or runs at half HP — he is a wage-man.

In the Shrine-Pit, one grieving lingering dead the digging woke: **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~22 HP, **not hostile**, standing over a disturbed grave. Folk rite **DC 13**. This is the site's moral centre and it is not a combat encounter.

**Salvage.** The dig-ledger — relics logged by which grave they came out of. It is leverage, not loot, and it is the most valuable thing in the building. Crated relics. The foreman's strongbox, ~80 gp in wage-coin and paperwork. Loose shrine salvage.

**Getting Out.** Open fields on every side; the crew will not chase far from the walls. If caught, the default is capture and ransom, or being marched off the property — not killing.

### Saint Veddow's Tomb

**Type** capped Concord shrine under a pilgrimage church · **Region** beneath Saint Veddow's Rest · **Level** 4–6 · **Danger** moderate–high (cold, dread, a guardian)

**What It Is.** The largest Concord installation known outside the Sundering Reach. A century ago somebody capped it, sanctified it, and built a pilgrim hall on top; the saint's legend grew over the lid. The upper tomb is beautiful and much-visited. Below it the building stops being a church and starts being something considerably older, colder, and larger than the church that covers it. The resident **Ashen Wardens** keep the inner door and would rather nobody went through it.

**Approach and Entrance.** A bronze-bound door at the back of the pilgrim hall, beneath the flower-altar. **Getting through that door is the real lock of this site**, and it is a social one: persuade the resident Warden (hard — she guards on instinct, not orders), obtain the shrine-keeper's sacred permission, find the old service stair the Remnant have been quietly excavating, or force it, which is sacrilege in a town full of pilgrims. The air off the door is wrong-cold and pilgrims will not stand near it.

**Layout.**
1. **The Saint's Vestibule** — a lovely upper chamber, flower-draped sarcophagus. It is empty, and always was.
2. **The Keeping Cells** — a ring of niche-chambers. The inscription calls them keeping-cells; what was kept in them is argued over. Minor relics remain in several.
3. **The Warden's Watch** — a guard-cell where resident Wardens have kept vigil in shifts for a hundred years. Their own records are here.
4. **The Cold Stair** — a long descent. The cold and the dread build with every flight; this is the site telling you to stop.
5. **The Antechamber** — the deepest reachable room: a great inscribed hall built around a capped shaft. Something under the cap hums, very faintly, if you put a hand on it.
6. **The Sealed Shaft** — capped, and staying that way. It goes down past anything a person survives. **A gate, not a puzzle.**

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Forcing the upper door | — | Cracks the seal: DC 14 Con or thin-touch, and the guardian rouses hostile instead of wary |
| Cold and dread (Cold Stair) | DC 13 Wis save, cumulative | Frightened until a rest |
| Collapsing floor (Antechamber) | DC 14 Perception; DC 13 Dex save | 1d6 into rubble |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con in the cells and antechamber | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** Handling a keeping-cell relic stirs a faint, sorrowful lingering dead (**Specter-like**, AC 12, ~17 HP, not hostile). One drifting dead may be met on the Cold Stair; settle or pass it.

In the Antechamber: **the tomb-warden**, a guardian dead bound to the capped shaft. **Wraith/Ghost-like**, AC 13, ~52 HP at level 5; chill touch, frightful presence (**DC 13 Wis**), a withering wail (**DC 14 Con**, 3d8 necrotic, once per short rest). It is not mindless. It demands to know why you are disturbing what it keeps, and it will accept an answer.

- **Rite (best).** A true rite acknowledging the dead kept here (**DC 15 Religion**, or **DC 13** with a Warden assisting) settles it; it fades.
- **Parley.** **DC 15 Insight or Persuasion** to convince it you mean the dead no harm. It lets you pass and answers one question.
- **Fight.** Tough but beatable. It **cannot leave the antechamber** — retreat up the Cold Stair always ends it.

Scaling: level 4, ~40 HP and a 2d8 wail; level 6, ~64 HP and it calls one drifting dead to help.

**Salvage.** Two to four minor Remembrance relics from the keeping-cells, and a genuinely hard choice about what to do with them. A Warden's seal-token from the Watch — worth little, but it marks the bearer to Ashen Wardens elsewhere as someone who has been down there. Concord salvage and one inscribed plate. **No shaft treasure**; the shaft is sealed.

**Getting Out.** Open retreat up the Cold Stair at any point; nothing here follows past the antechamber. The Warden House above is the recovery point, which is one more reason to befriend the Wardens rather than force their door. **No safe rest below the Vestibule.**

## The Tollwood

### The Greenward Toll-Station

**Type** flooded Concord road-vault · **Region** under the East Road's broken arch at Tollstone Cross · **Level** 2–4 · **Danger** low–moderate (water, cold)

**What It Is.** The cellar-works of a Concord toll-station on the great east causeway, drowned when the causeway broke. The station above is a ruin the **Tollmen** have made into a roost; the stair down is the one part of their own hideout they refuse to use. Locally it is "the cold stair."

**Approach and Entrance.** A rubble-choked stair down from the Broken Arch, behind the Tollmen's roost — which means a toll or a conversation before you ever reach the ruin. Alternatively a flooded culvert in from the Green Mile, which avoids the road entirely and is wetter.

**Layout.**
1. **The Choked Stair** — the descent. Rubble, rising damp, air that comes up cold.
2. **The Toll-Hall** — the main vault, knee-to-waist deep. Old toll machinery, a drowned cart, and its salvage.
3. **The Channel Room** — a smaller room built around a stone-lined channel, inscribed along its length, faintly and unmistakably warm.
4. **The Sunk Strongroom** — an optional flooded side vault: the station's strongbox, and a broken relic.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Deep water | DC 13 Athletics in the deep stretches | Swept toward a sump; DC 13 Athletics or a rope to recover |
| Cold damp | DC 12 Con on a long stay | Minor fatigue |
| Collapsing stair/vault | DC 13 Perception; DC 13 Dex save | 1d6 falling stone |
| Relic-exposure (Channel Room) | DC 14 Con | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** One drowned lingering dead in the Toll-Hall — a toll-keeper or a traveler lost when the road went. **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~22 HP, sorrowful, **not hostile**; parley **DC 12**, folk rite **DC 13**. A flooded-cellar scavenger in the strongroom if you go there (**Giant Frog/Constrictor/Swarm-like**, CR 1/4–1), telegraphed.

**Salvage.** The station strongbox: ~70 gp in old toll-coin plus a Concord road-token. One minor relic and a broken second. Road-salvage worth ~30 gp to either the Ledger or the Remnant. And the Tollmen will pay, or at least stop charging you, if you clear the stair they are frightened of.

**Getting Out.** Open stair up; the Green Mile culvert as a second exit. Nothing follows out of the vault. Short rest possible on the dry upper stair; no long rest below.

### The Hanging Oaks

**Type** pre-Concord sacred grove · **Region** deep in the Coldhearth wood · **Level** 4–6 · **Danger** moderate (dread, old dead, giving offense)

**What It Is.** A clearing of enormous, gnarled oaks, hung with offering-cords, bone-charms, and rags — some of them fresh, most of them not. The woodfolk have been leaving offerings here since long before the Concord, and will tell you the practice predates anything with a date on it. The dead in this grove are old and hard to move, and they will speak to people who come correctly. That is why the **Gravecallers** meet here.

**Approach and Entrance.** A deer-path off the Coldhearth wood. The approach telegraphs itself thoroughly: offering-marks thicken, the birds stop, and the dread builds (**DC 13 Wis**). A local guide will bring you to the edge and stop there.

**Layout.**
1. **The Offering-Edge** — the threshold, hung with woodfolk offerings. The choice to enter respectfully or not is made here and the grove notices.
2. **The Old Oaks** — the grove proper. Vast trees, deep leaf-litter, lingering old dead standing among them.
3. **The Cell's Hollow** — a sheltered dip where the Gravecallers camp and hold their meetings.
4. **The Hung Tree** — a single ancient oak at the grove's heart, where the oldest dead answers.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Grove-dread | DC 13 Wis on entry, DC 14 at the Hung Tree | Frightened until a rest, or until you make a proper offering |
| Taboo-breaking | — | Defiling offerings or attacking the lingering dead turns the **entire grove** hostile. Telegraphed and entirely avoidable |
| Snaring roots and deadfall | DC 13 Athletics/Acrobatics in the deep grove | 1d6 on a fall |
| The answering dead's price | — | Being spoken to may cost a memory, an offering, or a promised service. A roleplay cost, never a death |

**Inhabitants.** Old forest lingering dead among the oaks — **Specter/Ghost-like** but older and stranger, AC 13, ~30 HP. Mostly indifferent to the respectful. They **resist easy laying**: folk rite **DC 15**, and some of them cannot be settled at all, only honored and left.

The Gravecaller cell in the Hollow: a speaker (**Cult Fanatic/Warlock-like**, AC 12, ~45 HP, capable of *speak with dead* at will), 2–4 recruits (**Cultist/Commoner-like**), and often a local guide who has come too far in (**Scout-like**). **They want a witness, not a fight.** They turn dangerous only if attacked or betrayed.

At the Hung Tree: the oldest answering dead. **Ghost-like**, AC 13, ~52 HP; frightful presence and a withering touch (3d6 necrotic, recharge) *if* provoked. It is not a slayer — it tests visitors and speaks to those who pass. Reverence, an offering, and careful parley (**DC 15**) work far better than steel, and rousing it wakes the whole grove.

**Salvage.** Old offering-charms and pre-Concord tokens — minor value, and selling them is a serious taboo in every village within a day's walk. Rare deep-wood herbs at the grove's edge (**DC 13 Survival**). The Hung Tree may give a gift to someone who earns it — a rite-blessing, or the guidance of an old dead — but it is never taken.

**Getting Out.** Back along the deer-path; the grove's dead do not pass the Offering-Edge. Making an offering on the way out atones for minor offense. **No safe rest in the grove** unless you have made an offering and the cell vouches for you.

### The Old Mast

**Type** deep-wood primeval stand · **Region** the Tollwood's forbidden heart · **Level** 6–10 · **Danger** high, gated — the wood itself

**What It Is.** The oldest stand in the Tollwood, and the reason the Concord's east road bends where it bends. There is no ruin here and no built thing at all: only trees that get older, darker, and more attentive the further in you go, and a presence beneath them that has been asleep a long time and is not entirely asleep now. The woodfolk keep rules about this place. The Gravecallers will not come here.

**Approach and Entrance.** There is no door. You walk in past the last offering-marks and keep walking. **This site telegraphs harder than anything else in the region:** the marks stop, the guide stops, the dread mounts in stages, and the wood actively turns people around. A low-level intrusion is signposted as suicidal, and should be described that way at the table.

**Layout.**
1. **The Failing Path** — past the edge. The last marks. The wood begins misleading you (**DC 16 Survival** to hold direction).
2. **The Watcher-Stands** — stands of very old trees and the things that keep them, which test intruders and turn them back rather than kill them.
3. **The Still Heart** — the deepest grove: silent, timeless, and aware of you. Where the encounter happens.
4. **The Root-Dark** — a sense of enormous depth under the Still Heart. **The wood does not allow it and no route exists.** A player who insists is not killed; they are lost, marked, and eventually turned out of the wood a long way from where they went in.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| The misleading wood | DC 16 Survival per leg, DC 18 near the Still Heart | Strands you deeper; cumulative. **A guide cannot help past the edge** |
| Cumulative dread | DC 14 Wis, rising to DC 16 | Frightened; on repeated failure, compelled to leave — which is the wood pushing you out alive |
| The watchers' tests | — | Entangle, fear, displacement. Non-lethal by default; they mean to turn you back |
| The price of an answer | — | A memory, a true offering, or a binding promise. Never a cheap death |

**Inhabitants.** The **watchers** at the stands: use an **Awakened Tree-like** or **Dryad-like** profile scaled up — AC 14, ~60 HP, entangle, frightful presence, slam. They warn, test, and turn people back, and fight only the violent and the stupid. Retreating ends the encounter every time.

At the Still Heart there is **nothing to kill**. The climax is an ancient awareness taking your measure and deciding whether to grant you passage, an answer, or neither. Reverence, woodcraft, and honest parley resolve it (**DC 16**). Force fails outright and gets you lost.

**Salvage.** **Nothing you can safely take.** The Old Mast does not give loot and taking from it is the gravest taboo the woodfolk have. What it can give, to someone who earns it, is favor: safe passage through the deep wood afterward, a rite-blessing, or the guidance of one of its old dead.

**Getting Out.** Following the dread outward leads out. Accepting the watchers' turn-back leads out. An offering on the way out atones. The whole design of this place is a survivable warning — but pressing past every warning has real consequences, and one of them is the wood waking up properly, which is a regional disaster.

## The Pale Coast

### The Wreckers' Caves

**Type** tidal sea-cave system · **Region** under the headland at Cobble Strand · **Level** 3–5 · **Danger** moderate; the tide is the killer

**What It Is.** A maze of sea-caves under the Drowned Lamp headland, dry at low water and drowned at high. Wreckers stash plunder here. Smugglers move relics through. And the deepest cave is a place where the drowned dead answer readily, so the grieving find their way down to it — which is why the **Gravecallers** run a current out of it.

**Approach and Entrance.** A shingle cove and a low cave-mouth, passable only at low-to-mid tide. The village warns strangers plainly. There is a lookout posted.

**Layout.**
1. **The Cove Mouth** — the lookout and the tide-gate.
2. **The Plunder Cave** — the wreckers' stash: ship-goods, and crated sea-relics labelled for a buyer up the coast.
3. **The Wreckers' Hall** — the gang's camp. Their leader and their salvage-ledger.
4. **The Tide-Maze** — flooding side-caves. The navigation problem, and a haunting.
5. **The Listening Pool** — the deepest chamber, where the Gravecaller current meets.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **The tide (core)** | DC 14 Survival to time entry and exit | A missed tide traps you in a flooding cave: DC 13 Athletics to reach high ground, DC 13 Con for cold immersion, then wait it out or risk drowning. Telegraphed by the tide-table and the wet walls |
| Slick rock and deep pools | DC 13 Acrobatics/Athletics | Falls into cold water |
| Wreckers' alarm | DC 13 Perception to spot / DC 13 Stealth to pass | Brings the gang |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con near the crated relics | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** Living, mostly. A lookout and 2–4 wreckers (**Bandit/Thug-like**, AC 12–13, ~16–32 HP) who prefer threats and ransom and break on morale. Their leader in the Hall (**Veteran/Spy-like**, AC 15, ~45 HP) fights only cornered, surrenders or flees on morale, and can be turned — the wrecking pays badly and the trade is turning against them.

In the Tide-Maze, one drowned lingering dead (**Specter-like**, AC 12, ~26 HP), sorrowful, settled by the coast's salt-and-tide rite (**DC 13 Religion**). At the Listening Pool, the Gravecaller current: a speaker (**Acolyte/Cult Fanatic-like**), one or two recruits, and usually a grieving local who should not be there. Parley first; they are not an ambush.

**Salvage.** The salvage-ledger — sea-relics logged by which wreck they came off, and where they were sent. Leverage, and the best thing in the caves. Crated relics. The wreckers' plunder, ~120 gp of ship-goods and coin, some of which belongs to the families of people who drowned. A drowned one's keepsake, which has an owner.

**Getting Out.** At the right tide, out the cove and up the cliff to Cobble Strand. The tide limits pursuit as much as it limits you. Caught by wreckers: capture and ransom. Caught by the tide: high ground and patience. **No rest in the caves.**

### The Drowned Lamp

**Type** ruined Concord sea-shrine · **Region** the headland above Cobble Strand · **Level** 4–6 · **Danger** moderate–high; the tide is the clock

**What It Is.** Everyone calls it the lighthouse. It was never a lighthouse — there is no lamp-housing, no fuel store, no stair built for a keeper carrying oil, and the room at the top is a shrine. It is a Concord sea-shrine, and the coastal songs about its light "calling the drowned home" are older than anyone's grandmother. The cliff-foot gave way at some point and took the lower levels under the tide-line.

**Approach and Entrance.** The Lamp Stair, a cliff-path up from Cobble Strand. The upper tower is open ruin and safe. The lower levels are reached by a tide-cut stair that floods at high water. **The tide-table is the key to this site** and the villagers keep one.

**Layout.**
1. **The Broken Tower** — the open upper ruin and the lamp-room, which contains an inscribed shrine-focus and no lamp.
2. **The Keeper's Round** — the keeper's quarters, and the keeper's own written records, which are legible.
3. **The Tide-Cut Stair** — the descent. Floods at high water. This is the clock.
4. **The Settling Hall** — the main lower chamber. Inscribed, drowned at flood, faintly warm to the touch, and where the drowned gather.
5. **The Sea-Shaft** — below the waterline, capped, tide-locked. **A drowning death and a gate. It stays shut.**

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **The tide (core)** | DC 14 Survival to time the descent | Misjudging it floods the Settling Hall: DC 13 Athletics to reach the stair, DC 13 Con for cold immersion. A stranded diver waits out the tide or drowns — and the rising water is visible the whole time |
| Cliff and wet stone | DC 13 Athletics on the Lamp Stair and the wet stair | Falls are serious here |
| Cold-damp | DC 12 Con | Exhaustion |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con near the raw relics | Thin-touch |
| Collapsing ruin (tower) | DC 13 Perception/Dex | 1d6 falling stone |

**Inhabitants.** Handling a relic in the upper rooms stirs one faint drowned dead (**Specter-like**, AC 12, ~22 HP, not hostile). On the stair and in the Settling Hall, drowned lingering dead rise with the tide — **Specter/Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~30 HP each; settled by the coast's salt-and-tide rite (**DC 14 Religion**) or by parley.

In the Settling Hall: **the lamp-keeper**, a guardian dead bound to the lower works. **Wraith/Ghost-like**, AC 13, ~52 HP at level 5; chill touch, a drowning grip, frightful presence, and a keening wail (**DC 14 Con**, 3d8 necrotic/cold, once per short rest). It challenges rather than ambushes.

- **Rite (best).** The full salt-and-tide rite (**DC 15 Religion**, or **DC 13** with a coastal **Mourners' Circle** elder's help) settles it and it goes.
- **Parley.** **DC 15** to convince it you mean the drowned no harm; it lets you pass and answers a question.
- **Fight.** It breaks off if you retreat up the stair ahead of the tide. It cannot leave the water.

Scaling: level 4, ~40 HP and a 2d8 wail; level 6, ~64 HP and more drowned rise with the water.

**Salvage.** Two to four minor sea-relics. A keeper's tide-token from the Keeper's Round, which marks the bearer to coastal Mourners and Wardens as someone who has been below. Concord salvage and an inscribed plate. **No shaft treasure.**

**Getting Out.** Up the Lamp Stair; nothing here leaves the water, and **the tide itself drives you out** if you linger — a safety valve built into the geology. Cobble Strand below is the recovery point.

### The Skerry Shrine

**Type** half-drowned Concord sea-shrine on an offshore stack · **Region** the Skerries, off Wrackmouth · **Level** 6–9 · **Danger** high, gated by the sea

**What It Is.** The largest of the coastal Concord shrines, standing half-awash on a sea-stack in a reef where ships have been dying for a century. The Skerries are wreck-tangled, tide-swept, and thick with the drowned; the wreckers use the reefs and keep their worst secrets on the stack; and the shrine itself is old, big, and still standing.

**Approach and Entrance.** A boat crossing from Wrackmouth or the wreckers' strand, **only in fair weather and the right tide**. Every skipper on the coast will refuse at least once. False lights on the reefs pull boats onto the rocks. This site is gated by seamanship and weather, and it should be described that way before anyone commits.

**Layout.**
1. **The Reef Approach** — the false-light reefs and the wreck-field. The crossing, and the gate.
2. **The Skerry Strand** — the stack's shingle. Wrecks, the wreckers' stash, and the gathered drowned.
3. **The Drowned Porch** — the shrine's awash entrance. Inscription above the tide-line.
4. **The Keeping-Deep** — the main chamber, awash at high tide, inscribed throughout, faintly warm. The oldest relics, and the climax.
5. **The Sea-Throat** — the shrine's shaft, descending into deep water. **Drowned, lethal, and a gate.**

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **The crossing** | DC 16 seamanship/Survival, DC 18 in worsening weather | Swamps or wrecks the boat. Cold-sea drowning; recoverable only with a skipper and the right tide. The chief danger |
| False lights on the reef | DC 14 Wis | Drives the boat onto the rocks |
| Tide-lock | DC 15 Survival to time the Keeping-Deep | A missed tide strands you on an awash stack. Survivable by waiting, if you are alive |
| Cold-water immersion | DC 14 Con | Exhaustion or worse |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** The worst of the coast's drowned gather on the strand — **Wraith/drowned-spirit-like**, AC 13–14, ~45–60 HP. Telegraphed, and **they resist easy laying**: the right rite, the right offering, and salt-lore matter, and some can only be honored or escaped. Wreckers may be guarding the stash (**Bandit/Veteran-like**).

In the Keeping-Deep: **the skerry-keeper**, the eldest of the coastal guardian dead. **Wraith/Ghost-like**, AC 14, ~70 HP at level 7; keening wail (**DC 15 Con**, 4d8 cold/necrotic, recharge), drowning grip, frightful presence, and it can call a drowned one to it once per short rest. It fades or breaks off if you retreat to the boat ahead of the tide; it cannot leave the shrine or the water. **The rite and reverent parley (DC 16) are the intended resolutions**; force is the worst path and the sea agrees.

Scaling: level 6, ~56 HP and fewer gathered drowned; levels 8–9, ~84 HP and the reef fully roused.

**Salvage.** The oldest and best sea-relics on the coast, and the hardest choice about them. The wreckers' worst stash — significant plunder plus whatever they most needed to hide. A keeper's deep-token. **No shaft treasure.**

**Getting Out.** The boat and the tide are both the gate and the retreat, and a good skipper is the lifeline for this entire site. Nothing here leaves the water. **No rest on the Skerries**; Wrackmouth or Cobble Strand, a crossing away, are the havens.
