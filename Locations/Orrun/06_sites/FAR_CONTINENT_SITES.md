## AI Use

Load for play anywhere on Orrun beyond the northwestern frontier and Caradril. Fifteen high-tier sites (levels 8–16), one or two per far region, each written as a physical place: how you get there, what the ground and the air and the water do to you, what lives in it, what you can carry out, and how you leave.

**These are optional destinations, not a chain.** They are scattered across a continent that takes months to cross (see [`../01_geography/TRAVEL_AND_ROUTES.md`](../01_geography/TRAVEL_AND_ROUTES.md)). Each stands alone.

**Two design constants across all of them.** First, **every one of them is an environmental problem before it is a combat problem** — cold, ash, brine, tide, current, gas, collapse, or simply distance from any friendly roof. Preparation, a local guide, and knowing when to turn around are the real mechanics. Second, **all guardians are bound to their sites and none of them pursue.** Leaving always works.

**Concord guardian-constructs.** Several sites hold them: figures of fitted stone and cold light, still at their posts. Use a **Stone Golem-like** profile scaled to the tier (AC 17–18, 95–180 HP), resistant to nonmagical damage, one slam, one ranged force/necrotic burst on a recharge, and a relic-exposure aura. **They are posted sentries, not monsters.** Each issues a spoken challenge and waits. Reading the challenge and speaking the correct response (**Concord Script, DC 15–16**) makes it stand aside and let you work. This is the intended solution everywhere it appears.

**Relic-exposure ("thin-touch").** **DC 14–15 Con** on prolonged handling of raw relics or long exposure in relic-dense chambers; **disadvantage on death saves** until a folk rite (**DC 13 Religion**) or a long rest at a working shrine. See [`SUNDERING_REACH_SITES.md`](SUNDERING_REACH_SITES.md).

## The Verdance Reaches (Ring 2 — the first step outward)

### The Nine Locks Sunken Stair

**Type** flooded Concord lock-works · **Region** the Verdance, at the Nine Locks by Marrowfen Stair · **Level** 8–12 · **Danger** moderate–high (water, currents)

**What It Is.** The Verdance climbs into the interior by a staircase of nine locks, and the locks are Concord-built — still working, a century after the people who cut them stopped existing. Beneath them lies a set of sub-chambers that are plainly not lock machinery: dressed stone, inscription, a sealed inner room. Bargefolk have known about them forever and have no theory worth repeating.

**Approach and Entrance.** Down a maintenance shaft between the fourth and fifth locks, or through a drowned sluice at low water. The **Cinder Ledger's** inland arm runs the locks and keeps lock-keepers on them, so access is a charter, a bribe, or a quiet night.

**Layout.**
1. **The Lock-Stair** — the great works above: nine chambers, barge traffic, keepers, and slick lock walls (**DC 13 Dex**).
2. **The Flooded Sub-Locks** — half-drowned chambers below, threaded with old stone channels. Zero visibility in the silt.
3. **The Lock-Vault** — the sealed inner chamber, warded and inscribed, faintly warm.
4. **The Drowned Channel** — a stone watercourse running out of the vault and downstream, silted solid a hundred feet in. **Impassable. A gate.**
5. **The Lock-Keepers' Cache** — the old Concord lock-wardens' stores.

**Hazards.** Flooding on the sluice cycle (**DC 15 Survival** to time it, **DC 14 Athletics** if caught — a bargeman negates this entirely); currents and deep water (**DC 14–15 Athletics**); the vault ward (**Concord Script or DC 15 Investigation**; forcing it rouses the keeper); a flooded lock on the cache (**DC 15 Thieves' Tools**).

**Inhabitants.** Drifting lingering dead in the sub-locks — **Specter/Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~30–48 HP, bound to the water, parley- or rite-able (**DC 15**). One stronger keeper-dead in the Lock-Vault (**Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~52–64 HP), which stands down if the closing words are read aloud from the wall (**Concord Script or DC 15 Religion**). Living: Ledger lock-keepers and corridor guards (**Guard/Spy-like**), who eject rather than kill.

**Salvage.** Concord relics and old glass; corridor salvage; a possible water-warded minor item. A rubbing of the vault inscription. Goodwill from the bargefolk, which is worth more on this river than coin.

**Getting Out.** Marrowfen Stair is a short haul upstream. The flooding cycle physically drives you out on a schedule, which is the built-in safety valve. Everything down here is bound to the water.

## The Glassmere League

### The Reliquary Vaults

**Type** great scholarly archive and relic strongroom · **Region** beneath the Reliquary, Glassmere · **Level** 9–13 · **Danger** moderate–high; social and legal

**What It Is.** The **Concord Remnant's** grand chapter-house in the League's greatest river-city, and the vaults under it. The public college above is a genuine institution — reading rooms, displays, lecture halls. The lower stacks are ward-locked, and the deepest strongroom holds the largest collection of Concord relics assembled anywhere. It is not a dungeon. It is a very well-defended library with a bank vault at the bottom.

**Approach and Entrance.** As a guest scholar (earn a lector's leave — slow, safe, limited). By a rival banker's key (Glassmere's banking houses feud, and one of them will fund a nuisance). By buying the layout and the ward-schedule from the city's information trade. Or by stealth, which is the riskiest and richest.

**Layout.**
1. **The Reliquary** — the public chapter-house. Scholar-guards, cover problems, and the sheer scale of the collection as your first real impression.
2. **The Reading-Vaults** — restricted lower stacks; ward-locked Concord records, and a scholar who notices things.
3. **The Relic-Vault** — the deep strongroom. Massed relics, and a guardian standing in the middle of them.
4. **The Lector's Study** — the chapter head's private room: working papers, correspondence, and the chapter's own theories about what they are collecting.
5. **The Lost Stacks** — the uncatalogued deepest shelves. Ward-trapped and rich.

**Hazards.** Ward-glyph locks (**Concord Script or DC 15 Investigation**; failure is an alarm, not a blast in the face); the relic-vault's massed relic-exposure (**DC 14 Con**); cover failure (Deception vs. scholarly Insight — these are perceptive people); the city's own intrigue, which cross-pressures anyone poking at the chapter.

**Inhabitants.** Scholar-guards and chapter agents — **Spy/Mage-like**, AC 13–14, ~40–60 HP, with hold person, counterspell, and a relic one-off at high tier. **They warn, control, and eject**; nobody kills in their own chapter-house. One Concord guardian-construct in the relic-vault (see above — the watchword stands it down).

**Salvage.** Concord relics and rare salvaged records (tier-3 value); a scholarly minor item; the chapter's correspondence, which is leverage; and standing with either the chapter or one of its rivals, depending on how you played it.

**Getting Out.** The city is the retreat — step out into Glassmere at any point. The guardian is bound to the strongroom. Failure costs you an alarm, an ejection, and a suspicious institution — not a life.

### The Three Bridges Counting-Deep

**Type** banking vault-complex · **Region** the Three Bridges banking quarter, Glassmere · **Level** 9–12 · **Danger** moderate; a heist

**What It Is.** The **Cinder Ledger's** continental counting-house: a grand public banking floor, a hall of underfactors, a deep strongroom-archive, a refining cellar, and the provost's own strongbox. This is where the house's paperwork actually lives, which makes it the single most informative building on the continent for anyone who wants to know how the relic trade works.

**Approach and Entrance.** Open an account. Pose as a relic-seller. Turn an underfactor. Accept funding from a rival banker who wants an audit forced. Or go in over the roof. **Combat is the failure path and the building is designed to make that obvious.**

**Layout.**
1. **The Counting-Floor** — the public bank. Tellers, clients, provost-guards, and a cover problem.
2. **The Clerks' Hall** — underfactors and day-ledgers. The turnable inside.
3. **The Counting-Deep** — the deep strongroom-archive: the master purchase-books, logging relics by the region they were bought in and where they were sent on.
4. **The Refiners' Cellar** — where raw relics are cut, reset, and graded for the market. Hot, chemical, gloved.
5. **The Provost's Strongbox** — coin, deeds, and files on people who would rather those files did not exist.

**Hazards.** The strongroom lock (**DC 16 Thieves' Tools**, **DC 13** with a clerk's key); a fine lock on the strongbox (**DC 17**); counting-house wards (**DC 14 Investigation**); refining fumes and relic-exposure (**DC 14 Con**); and the city's banking politics, which will find you whether you want them to or not.

**Inhabitants.** Provost-guards and strong-arms (**Thug/Spy-like**, AC 13–14, ~16–45 HP) whose job is to catch and eject. A vault strong-arm and a refiner's guard (**Veteran-like**, AC 14, ~45 HP). Clerks and underfactors, several of them turnable (**DC 14–15 Persuasion or Insight**). **No undead. No guardians. This one is entirely people.**

**Salvage.** Substantial coin and deeds; the provost's leverage files; and the master purchase-book, which is a map of an entire continental trade and worth more to the right person than the strongbox.

**Getting Out.** Keep your cover and walk out the front. Lose it and you are ejected, indebted, and remembered. The city absorbs you either way.

## The Concord Heartlands

### The Ruin'd Crown

**Type** vast surface ruin-field · **Region** astride the Greatspine, reached from Crownmouth · **Level** 10–14 · **Danger** high; far from any friendly roof

**What It Is.** The broken heart-country of the old Custodian Concord, ruined to the horizon: toppled colonnades, drowned plazas, a shrine-dome cracked open like an egg, all built on a scale the frontier has no vocabulary for. It is picked over daily by scavengers out of Crownmouth, dug by scholarly expeditions, and camped in by relic-pilgrims who have nowhere better. Standing in it is the point — nothing else on the continent conveys what the Concord actually *was*.

**Approach and Entrance.** From **Crownmouth**, the fortified scavenger-town on the ruin's edge: hire a guide, pay the war-captain's toll, walk in. From the **Pilgrim Camps** in the fallen forum. With a scholarly expedition, which buys access and a rivalry at once. Or alone over the Greatspine, which is how people vanish.

**Layout.**
1. **The Ruin-Edge** — the scavenged outer city. Tents, fires, tolls, rival diggers, and unstable masonry everywhere.
2. **The Fallen Forum** — a drowned plaza turned camp. Desperation, fever, and relic-hawkers selling fakes.
3. **The Crown Archives** — the great surviving archive-hall, and the reason scholars come. Shelf on shelf of legible Concord record.
4. **The Sundered Shrine-Dome** — the cracked dome. The oldest dead of the city are in here, and something still keeps it.
5. **The Deep Vaults** — the deepest surviving strongrooms: guardian-thick, treasure-dense, and **bottoming out in rubble and collapse.** They do not go anywhere.

**Hazards.** Structural collapse (**DC 14–15 Perception/Dex**, 3d6–5d6 and a blocked path); archive script-wards (**DC 15–16**; failure is a force-blast or a roused guardian); the eldest dead's regard (**DC 15–16 Wis**, psychic strain); far-frontier exposure and navigation (**DC 14 Survival** — a Crownmouth guide negates it).

**Inhabitants.** Concord guardian-constructs, apex-scaled (AC 18, ~150–200 HP; watchword stands them down). The Crown's eldest dead — **Ancient Remembrance**, use a **Ghost/Wraith-like** profile scaled up, AC 15, ~90–120 HP, reverence and rite before violence. Living: Crownmouth's war-captain and her toll-takers, scholarly expeditions, a heretic preacher with a following in the camps, and rival diggers of every stripe. The *human* rivalry is often the real climax here.

**Salvage.** Concord artifacts of genuine power (the Crown's great prizes); significant coin and salvage from the vaults; and the archive's records, which are the reason anyone with a brain came.

**Getting Out.** Crownmouth and the Pilgrim Camps are the havens and always reachable; guardians are ruin-bound. The deep vaults dead-end downward, and should be described that way rather than left ambiguous.

## The Marrowdowns

### The Barrow-Fields

**Type** pre-Concord barrow complex · **Region** the chalk downs, reached from Marrowmoot · **Level** 8–12 · **Danger** moderate–high

**What It Is.** Mound after mound on the open chalk, far older than the Concord, holding the down-country's ancient dead. They have lain quiet for centuries, which is precisely why the shire is unsettled: over the last year or two they have grown restless. Barrow-thieves have cracked several of the nearer mounds, the shire-reeve wants that stopped, and the shire's **Mourners' Circle** elder wants the dead settled properly, in that order of urgency depending on whom you ask.

**Approach and Entrance.** The barrows are not hidden — they are open downland, walkable in an afternoon from Marrowmoot. They are forbidden by custom, not by wall. Come by the reeve's leave (the honored path), by the Mourners' Circle elder's counsel (the reverent one), with the robbers (fast and costly), or just walk out there.

**Layout.**
1. **The Open Mounds** — the field's edge, where the thieves have already dug. Loose spoil and collapsed shafts.
2. **The Door-Glyph Mound** — a great sealed barrow whose door carries two layers of carving, one much older than the other.
3. **The Gathered Barrow** — a central chamber where the down-folk's dead have collected, restless and aware of you.
4. **The Barrow-Lord's Chamber** — the complex's heart. The richest grave-goods, and their owner.
5. **The Deep Mounds** — the oldest and unrobbed. Best treasure, worst inhabitants, **bottoming out in chalk.**

**Hazards.** Dug-mound collapse (**DC 13–14 Dex/Perception**, 2d6–4d6); the glyph-door wards (**DC 14–15**; forcing rouses a guardian); the barrow-lord's regard (**DC 14–15 Wis**); and defiling a mound, which rouses the whole complex *and* brings the shire down on you.

**The Glyph-Door (puzzle) — three valid paths.** Read the layered carvings (**DC 14–15 Script/History**); the Mourners' Circle elder's star-and-grave lore opens it by song (**DC 14 Religion**), and she will teach it to a respectful visitor; or a barrow-robber's stolen key, which costs standing. Accept any clever fourth answer.

**Inhabitants.** Restless barrow-dead — **Specter/Wraith-like** scaled dry, AC 12–13, ~36–52 HP, bound to the mounds, settled by rite, song, or parley. **The barrow-lord**: an ancient chieftain-dead, **Ghost-like**, AC 14, ~80–100 HP, **parley and reverence first** — it is not malicious, it is disturbed, and it will talk to someone who is not carrying its grave-goods. Living: barrow-robbers (**Bandit/Thug-like**, AC 13, ~22–30 HP), who scatter.

**Salvage.** Pre-Concord grave-goods (tier-2/3 valuables, and a possible barrow-warded minor item). The reeve's gratitude, which in the Marrowdowns is a lawful base and a route south. The Mourners' Circle elder's old songs, which are worth learning.

**Getting Out.** Open country in every direction; Marrowmoot is the haven; the dead are bound to the mounds. Walking away from this site is the easiest of any in this file.

## The Saltmere Reaches

### The Drowned Towns

**Type** submerged ruin-dive · **Region** the receding Saltmere, out from Brackhold · **Level** 11–15 · **Danger** high (cold brine, salt-collapse, blackout)

**What It Is.** The inland salt sea is shrinking, and as it goes down it is uncovering towns. Whole streets, salt-crusted and brine-preserved, with the people still in them — cured hard by the salt in the postures they drowned in. The salt-clans hold the drowned towns sacred and forbid the dive. Scavengers do it anyway. Lately the drowned dead have been stirring, which everyone has an opinion about and nobody can explain.

**Approach and Entrance.** With a diver who knows the safe streets (fast, and it makes clan enemies). By the clans' leave, earned by recovering a lost kinsman or settling the stirring dead (slow, socially safe). At the worst-receded season, which bares the most ruin and rouses the most dead. Or across the salt flats alone, which the mirage-country punishes.

**Layout.**
1. **The Lake-Edge and Bared Streets** — the receded shore where the streets come out of the crust. Scavenger camps, clan-watchers.
2. **The Brine-Drowned Concourse** — the main street, half-flooded, salt-stiffened doors and stalls. Household dead posed where they stopped.
3. **The Salt-Choir** — a drowned temple-square where the town's dead have gathered, facing inward. The eerie heart of the dive.
4. **The Clan-Vaults** — the old salt-merchants' sunken strongrooms.
5. **The Deep Drowned Quarter** — the never-receded streets, in pitch brine. Best salvage, worst dead, **bottoming out in silt and salt-rock.**

**Hazards.** Salt-collapse footing (**DC 14 Dex/Athletics**, 3d6 and a fall into brine); cold-brine immersion (**DC 14–15 Con**, exhaustion or 2d6 cold); silt blackout (Perception at disadvantage — run a guide-line); flooded vault locks (**DC 16 Thieves' Tools**); mirage-country on the flats (**DC 15 Survival**).

**Inhabitants.** Salt-cured drowned dead — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~40–60 HP, bound to the drowned streets, rite- or parley-able, and at the Salt-Choir best passed by reverence (**DC 15 Religion or Insight**) rather than fought. One vault-bound guardian dead (AC 13, ~64–72 HP). Living: rival scavengers, clan-watchers, and relic buyers on the shore.

**Salvage.** Salt-cured old glass and brine-preserved valuables; lost clan heirlooms, which are the socially valuable haul; a possible salt-warded minor item.

**Getting Out.** Brackhold and the lake-edge camps are havens. **The dive self-limits** — cold and air force you up, which is the built-in valve. Surfacing escapes everything down there.

## The Sallowmarch Protectorate

### The Drowned Steps

**Type** submerged pre-Concord causeway · **Region** off the Mardenflow delta, out from Reedmouth · **Level** 10–14 · **Danger** high (tide, fever, patrols)

**What It Is.** An ancient causeway of enormous dressed blocks that strides out of the delta and under the sea, heading for open water. It is older than the Concord and older than the delta's memory, and it runs out into nothing anyone will name. The fever-folk shun it. The smugglers use its channels. The occupying garrison denies it exists, and arrests people for saying otherwise.

**Approach and Entrance.** With the delta's smugglers, who know the channel-routes. By the marsh-healer's counsel, if the stirring dead have frightened her enough to send someone. At the lowest ebb, which bares the most steps and strands the slow. Or straight past the garrison, which adds a pursuit layer to everything else.

**Layout.**
1. **The Fever Channels** — the deep backwaters threading out toward the causeway. Reed, heat, smuggler-marks, patrols.
2. **The Tidal Causeway** — the great stepped roadway itself: awash to the knee at ebb, gone at flood.
3. **The Drowned Gate-House** — a half-submerged gate structure on the causeway, where the older dead have gathered.
4. **The Sea-Vault** — a flooded chamber beneath the gate-house: the builders' drowned hoard.
5. **The Far Steps** — the seaward run, descending under deep water. **They end. In open ocean, in ruin, and in nothing. A gate, and an honest one.**

**Hazards.** The rising tide on the steps (**DC 14 Survival** to time it; being caught means stranded or swept); fever exposure in the channels (**DC 14 Con**, disease or exhaustion); getting lost in the backwaters (**DC 15 Survival** — a smuggler or marsh guide negates it); slick steps (**DC 13 Dex**); the flooded vault lock (**DC 16**); garrison patrols, best evaded or bribed.

**Inhabitants.** Older-fall drowned dead on the causeway — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~40–56 HP, bound to the stone, rite- and parley-able. A vault-bound drowned guardian (AC 13, ~64 HP), which a closing reverence settles. Living: smugglers (allies for a price) and imperial patrols (**Guard/Veteran-like**, and much better avoided than fought — arresting you is the least of what they can do).

**Salvage.** Pre-Concord valuables and old glass from the sea-vault; a possible tide-warded minor item; the gratitude of the delta-folk or their smugglers, either of which buys you a base in a region that has none for outsiders.

**Getting Out.** Reedmouth and the delta camps are havens; **the tide self-limits the visit**; the dead are bound to the causeway. Leave the steps and you have left them.

## The Hollow Gulf Ports

### The Wreck-Reef

**Type** shipwreck graveyard and drowned harbour-quarter · **Region** off Calderport, the Hollow Gulf · **Level** 10–15 · **Danger** high (the sea)

**What It Is.** The warmest, most cheerful dangerous place on the continent. A reef off the greatest southern port where a century of ships have died, tangled hull against hull, and beneath it a whole harbour-district the rising sea took generations ago — streets and warehouses, now dived professionally by crews out of Calderport who treat the whole thing as a trade rather than a haunting.

There is nothing supernatural here at all. It is a salvage adventure, and it belongs in this file precisely because most of the continent is like this: busy, sunlit, and completely unbothered.

**Approach and Entrance.** Hire a salvage crew. Take passage with a licensed privateer. Buy a sanctioned charter from the port-mistress (slower, taxed, safer, and it settles claim disputes before they start). Or hire a boat and go, which the currents punish.

**Layout.**
1. **The Wreck-Reef** — the graveyard proper. Tangled hulls, salvage-buoys, rival crews, and claim politics.
2. **The Tangled Hulls** — the diveable wrecks: cargo holds, drowned cabins, foreign goods.
3. **The Drowned Harbour-Quarter** — the sunken older district. Streets and warehouses, silt-choked, and something big living in one of them.
4. **The Deep Wrecks** — the richest and most dangerous, in the worst current.
5. **The Foreign Hold** — an overseas ship's hold: goods, curios, and charts of the world beyond Orrun.

**Hazards.** Currents and deep water (**DC 14–15 Athletics**); wreck-tangle (**DC 14 Athletics/Acrobatics** — being trapped underwater is the real killer here); air and cold on long dives (**DC 13 Con**); and salvage-claim disputes, which are a social hazard with real teeth in a port town.

**Inhabitants.** A reef predator — a great eel or a large shark, **Giant Shark-like**, AC 13–15, ~70–120 HP, sea-bound and thoroughly avoidable with a lookout. Rival salvage crews and privateers (**Bandit/Veteran-like**), who fight over claims and flee at half HP. **No undead. No guardians. Nothing old.**

**Salvage.** Rich foreign cargo and overseas curios; a saltwater-warded minor item; and charts and lore of the lands across the water, which is the real prize for anyone whose horizon has been the continent.

**Getting Out.** Surface and sail. The dive self-limits on air and cold. The ports are all around and none of them care what you did out there as long as your charter was in order.

## The Sunmark

### The Great Grove At Sunhollow

**Type** living sacred grove · **Region** the warm Sunmark Wilds, at Sunhollow · **Level** 8–13 · **Danger** moderate–high; almost none of it combat

**What It Is.** Not a ruin. A vast, lush, living holy grove, tended by grove-tribes who have kept the same faith for longer than the Concord existed and have never had much to do with it. Their burial rites are famously effective — their dead rest, reliably, and travelers who have come from the troubled north find that fact genuinely startling. The grove is a green sanctuary, and the tension in it is not undead but **people who want to take something from it**.

**Approach and Entrance.** By the grove-keeper's leave (the proper way — earn it with humility and a service). With a grove-singer as guide (the deepest access). As a pilgrim, along the Green Roads. Or by cutting your way in, which turns the wardens, the wilds, and the tribes against you at once.

**Layout.**
1. **The Green Roads** — pilgrim-and-trade paths through warm wilds toward the camps. Easy to get lost in, and cutters' camps at the edges.
2. **The Grove-Camps** — the tribes' settlements at the sacred forest's edge. Warm, peaceable, watchful of takers.
3. **The Sacred Grove** — the living heart: enormous trees, grove-shrines, and the tribes' dead lying quiet among them.
4. **The Heart-Tree** — the oldest tree, and the honored dead around it. Nothing here is hostile. That is the strange part.

**Hazards.** Getting lost in the lush forest (**DC 14 Survival** — a grove-guide negates it); wild beasts on the approach (avoidable); and **giving offense**, which is the actual danger. Cutting, taking, or trespassing turns the wardens out and gets you barred, and the tribes have long memories.

**Inhabitants.** Grove-wardens — **Druid/Scout-like**, AC 14–15, ~45–70 HP, with nature magic. **They warn, then ward, then escort you out.** They do not pursue and they do not kill if they can help it. The grove's dead are *at peace* and are not an encounter. The optional fight here is a moral one: logging crews, relic-hunters, or a zealot band who have come to take the grove's secret, and whom the tribes cannot fight alone.

**Salvage.** The grove-keepers' friendship and a grove-blessing or charm (a possible minor item). A safe southern base, if you defend the place. The living rite itself, shared only with the deeply trusted (**DC 16 Religion or Insight** and a great deal of earned goodwill), which is a genuine piece of practical knowledge worth carrying north.

**Getting Out.** The Grove-Camps are havens. There is nothing at the heart to flee from. This is the one site in the file where the correct play is to sit down and stay a while.

## The Hethewald Free Holds

### The Old Holds

**Type** forbidden deep-wood Concord ruins · **Region** deep in the Hethewood, out from Hethemoot · **Level** 9–13 · **Danger** high, gated by the forest

**What It Is.** Concord ruins swallowed by the eastern great-wood — toppled halls, root-cracked inscription, whole buildings under a century of leaf-fall. The free-holds forbid them, and have maintained, for generations, a set of bargains and offering-marks whose stated purpose is to leave what is in the deep wood asleep. The bargains have been slipping for a couple of years. The holds are frightened about it in a way that they will not discuss with outsiders until they trust you.

**Approach and Entrance.** With the Speaker's leave, granted to someone investigating why the bargains are failing (slow, far safer). By learning the grove-mother's old songs, which is the reverent key. With a scholarly expedition. Or against the holds' law, which is fast and turns both the wood and the holds against you.

**Layout.**
1. **The Marked Wood** — the forbidden forest's edge, hung with the bargain-tokens the holds maintain. Past here the wood begins misleading you.
2. **The Drowned Holds** — the ruins proper: toppled halls, root-split stone, collapse everywhere.
3. **The Grey-Hands Hall** — the central ruin-hall, which the old songs name and do not explain. The best-preserved inscription, and the gathered dead.
4. **The Sleeping Grove** — the oldest heart, where a forest-bound presence rests under the failing bargains. **A closed older heart. There is no way down and no way past it.**
5. **The Root-Vaults** — root-swallowed strongrooms. The salvage.

**Hazards.** The misleading wood (**DC 16–18 Survival**, cumulative — this is the site's signature danger, and a forest-guide or the old songs are the only reliable answer); structural collapse (**DC 15**, 3d6–5d6); the presence's regard (**DC 15–16 Wis**, psychic strain and a compulsion to leave, which is the wood pushing you out alive); root-locked vaults (**DC 16 Athletics/Thieves' Tools**); and breaking a bargain-mark, which rouses both the wood and the holds.

**Inhabitants.** Deep-wood lingering dead — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~40–56 HP, bound to the ruins, settled by rite, song, or parley. The **forest-bound presence** in the Sleeping Grove: use a scaled **Ancient Remembrance** or high-tier guardian profile, AC 14–15, ~80–110 HP, but understand what it is for — **reverence or withdrawal are the resolutions. Force fails and strands you.** Living: scholarly expeditions, logging crews at the wood's edge, and hold-wardens who take the bargains seriously.

**Salvage.** Deep-wood Concord relics and root-vault salvage (tier-3). The old songs, which are a lore prize and a lasting friendship. The free-holds' alliance, if you guarded the ruins rather than stripping them.

**Getting Out.** Hethemoot and the grove-village are the havens; the presence and the dead are bound to the ruins and grove; leaving the marked wood escapes everything. A guide makes withdrawal reliable, which is why you hire one.

## The Karran Marches

### The Old Iron Forts

**Type** peripheral Concord forts and deep ore-mines · **Region** the Karran Teeth, out from Karran-Gate · **Level** 10–14 · **Danger** high (mines, gas, cold, no law)

**What It Is.** Concord forts scattered high through the northeastern peaks, and beneath them ore-mines that have been driven straight into Concord workings. The mining crews call it **humming iron** — old metal in the rock that carries a note you feel in the jaw rather than hear — and crews that work near it too long come back *thin*. The frontier here is ruled by whoever is strongest that year, and both the local warlord and the ore-factor would pay well to have the bad cuts made workable again.

**Approach and Entrance.** On the warlord's commission (the frontier-honored path — clear a haunted cut, or a humming fort). On the ore-factor's coin. Down with a mining crew who know the workings (negates most mine risk, at the cost of putting them in it with you). Or alone into the peaks, which the cold and the height handle.

**Layout.**
1. **The Fort-Mouth** — a Concord fort's broken gate, now a mercenary camp and ore-stage. High exposure and rough politics.
2. **The Deep Cut** — the great ore-mine, driven into old workings. Collapse, choke-gas, and the dark.
3. **The Humming Workings** — the inner Concord chambers where the iron hums. Where crews change.
4. **The Inner Chamber** — the fort's sealed heart, and its sentry.
5. **The Lost Cuts** — abandoned deep workings: ore, salvage, the worst air, and the worst of what is down there.

**Hazards.** Mine collapse (**DC 15 Perception/Dex**, 3d6–5d6 and a blocked path); choke-gas (**DC 14–15 Con** — carry a flame or a bird, and listen to crew-lore); relic-exposure in the humming workings (**DC 15 Con**); high-peak exposure (**DC 14 Survival**).

**Inhabitants.** Lingering dead in the workings — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~40–56 HP, bound, rite- and parley-able. Crews who have "changed" are people, not monsters, and are usually the site's saddest scene rather than its fight. One Concord guardian-construct in the Inner Chamber (AC 17, ~95–120 HP; **the watchword stands it down**, and there is a reader in Karran-Gate who can supply it for a price). Living: mercenaries and rival crews (**Thug/Veteran-like**), who respond to strength and coin.

**Salvage.** Ore-wealth, old glass, and Concord salvage (tier-3); a possible warded minor item; the warlord's respect or the factor's trade-favor, either of which gives you a base on a frontier that grants very few.

**Getting Out.** Karran-Gate and the warlord's hold are havens; the guardian and the dead are bound; a crew-guide makes withdrawal reliable. The cuts dead-end in rock.

## The Wender Steppe

### The Sky-Stones

**Type** living standing-stone shrine · **Region** the open steppe, near the Cold Springs winter-camp · **Level** 8–13 · **Danger** moderate–high; almost none of it combat

**What It Is.** A wind-worn ring of enormous standing stones on a rise in the grass-sea, raised by the horse-clans' forebears long before the Concord and **entirely unrelated to it** — different stone, different working, no star-marks, no inscription anybody has ever been able to compare to anything. It is a living holy place, not a ruin: the clans' wind-singers come here to read omens, and have done so continuously for as long as the clans have existed.

It is also the best place on the continent to be reminded that the Concord was one empire among many things, and that most of the world never noticed it fall.

**Approach and Entrance.** **Hospitality is the key.** Earn a clan's guest-right and you will be escorted to the stones as a matter of course. Travel with a wind-singer for the deepest access. Come during the winter-camp or a summer gathering. Riding out alone across the grass both endangers you and insults the people whose grass it is.

**Layout.**
1. **The Open Grass** — trackless steppe, migration-paths, clan camps, sky. Getting there is the challenge.
2. **The Sky-Stones Ring** — the standing circle on its rise. Older than any ruin the traveler has seen, and in use.
3. **The Singing-Place** — the ring's heart, where the singers read. A cold spot, and an uneasy one lately, which the singers acknowledge and do not explain to outsiders.
4. **The Old Cairns** — the clan forebears' graves nearby. Honored, tended, and **at rest** — the steppe's own rites hold, and always have.

**Hazards.** Getting lost on the trackless grass (**DC 15 Survival** — a clan rider negates it); cold and exposure (**DC 13 Con**); **violating guest-custom**, which is the real hazard and bars you from everything; a chill unease at the Singing-Place (**DC 14 Wis**, no damage); and raiders on the approach.

**Inhabitants.** **No undead. No guardians. Nothing hostile at the shrine at all.** The only combat is on the ride out: a raid-lord's mounted reavers (**Scout/Veteran-like**, AC 14–15, ~40–55 HP), skirmishers who are far better outridden than fought, and who break at half HP.

**Salvage.** No loot. What this site gives is a clan-token and guest-friendship, which is passage and aid across an enormous region; a wind-singer's reading, which is a scene rather than an item; and a steppe-craft gift — a good horse, a warded charm, a possible minor item.

**Getting Out.** The clan camps are mobile havens and always somewhere within a day's ride if you have friends. There is nothing at the stones to flee.

## The Highmark Passes

### The Frozen Works

**Type** ice-locked Concord station · **Region** the high passes of the Highmark Spine · **Level** 12–16 · **Danger** very high — **the cold is the dungeon**

**What It Is.** A Concord station in the high cold passes beyond the northern wall, among the oldest works anyone has found. The dry cold has preserved it in a way no southern ruin has ever been preserved: machinery unrusted, inscription unblurred, doors that still close. Scholars who know it exists talk about it the way sailors talk about a strait they have not survived yet.

The most isolated survivor-holds on the continent live within reach of it, and know things about it that nobody has written down.

**Approach and Entrance.** Over the high passes, gear-gated and prep-gated — **this is not a "you can try it and see" site, and the DM should say so before anyone commits.** With a survivor-hold guide (the only reliable route and the only reliable haven). With an **Ashen Warden** or a **Concord Remnant** high-expedition, both of which have their own stakes in the works.

**Layout.**
1. **The High Pass** — the ice-locked approach. Survivor-holds, frozen markers, whiteout.
2. **The Frozen Station-Front** — the station's intact face: machinery and inscription, all legible through clear ice.
3. **The Ice-Vault Archive** — the station's records, preserved whole. The reason to come.
4. **The Cold-Locked Heart** — the sealed inner chamber, and an intact sentry standing in it.
5. **The Deep Ice** — the deepest vaults. Best-preserved relics, worst cold, **bottoming out in ice and bedrock.**

**Hazards.** Lethal cold and exposure (**DC 15 Con**, exhaustion *plus* cold damage, stacking toward death — gear, fire, and time limits are mandatory); ice-collapse and frozen falls (**DC 15 Dex/Perception**, 4d6–6d6 and buried); whiteout navigation (**DC 16 Survival** — a survivor-guide negates it); relic-exposure at the heart (**DC 15 Con**).

**Inhabitants.** Ice-preserved lingering dead — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~48–64 HP, intact, aware, bound to the station, and rite- or parley-able. Concord guardian-constructs, apex-scaled and in unusually good repair (AC 17–18, ~120–180 HP; **the watchword stands them down**, and an Ashen Warden can also do it by standing).

**Salvage.** The best-preserved Concord artifacts anywhere (tier-3/4, and the far frontier's great prize). Rubbings and copies of the ice-vault archive, which scholars across the continent will bid ruinously for. The loyalty of a survivor-hold, which is a base in the least habitable country on Orrun.

**Getting Out.** **The sole haven is a survivor-hold**, and reaching it is the whole retreat plan — which is why prep is not optional here. Everything in the station is bound; the cold is what kills you, and it kills you on the way home as readily as on the way in.

## The Emberfell Theocracy

### The Ashfast Deep (the caldera descent)

**Type** volcanic ruin descent · **Region** beneath Cinderhold, the Emberfells · **Level** 12–16 · **Danger** very high — **the heat is the dungeon**

**What It Is.** A descent from the volcanic mining town into the fire-mountain below it. The Emberfell fire-priests read the volcanoes as divine and return their dead to the fire, rendering them into a hard glassy substance the black market buys under the same name the northern frontier uses: **old glass**. Below the rendering-deeps lie older works the Concord left, half-consumed by the mountain, which the theocracy forbids and the desperate dig anyway.

**Approach and Entrance.** With masked mining guides out of Cinderhold. Along the pilgrim ways, which buys access and scrutiny in equal measure. With the black-market diggers who follow the old glass down, which is illegal under the Pyre's law. Or over the caldera rim, which is the fastest way to die of this site.

**Layout.**
1. **The Cinder Mines** — the worked volcanic mines. Heat, masks, gas pockets, ore, and old glass in the rock.
2. **The Forbidden Stair** — the theocracy-sealed descent. A guard post, a seal, and rising heat.
3. **The Rendering-Deeps** — where the fire-cult returns its dead. Lava channels, ash, and rites in progress.
4. **The Concord-Touched Vault** — an old Concord work the mountain has half-eaten: inscription under ash, fire-warded.
5. **The Magma Throat** — the living fire below. **Instant death, explicitly telegraphed. A gate, not a challenge.**

**Hazards.** Staged lethal heat by depth (**DC 14–15 Con**, exhaustion escalating to fire damage — heat protection is the actual key to this site); toxic gas pockets (**DC 14–15 Con**, lethal in the deep pools); lava-rock and channels (**DC 15 Dex**, high lava damage); molten collapse (**DC 15**); fire-wards on the vault (**DC 16**, a fire-blast or a roused guardian).

**Inhabitants.** Fire-touched lingering dead in the rendering-deeps — use a **Wraith-like** profile with fire flavor, AC 15, ~80–100 HP, bound, and reverence works on them as it does anywhere. A fire-elemental or fire-construct guardian in the vault: AC 16–17, ~110–140 HP, heat aura, magma-grip, fire-blast, **bound to the chamber**. Living: the theocracy's Pyre-wardens (zealous, and they enforce), miners, a heretic-scholar who studies the fire-glass in secret and is the best ally available, and black-market diggers.

**Salvage.** High-tier fire-glass and Concord-volcanic relics and artifacts (tier-3/4). The heretic-scholar's alliance, or a zealot enemy. Substantial old-glass value, and a decision about what to do with it.

**Getting Out.** Cinderhold and Ashfast are havens; the guardian is vault-bound; the heat itself forces you up on a clock. **Gear up before you descend, not after** — telegraph that at the table.

### The Cindern Waste Buried Works

**Type** ash-drowned Concord ruin · **Region** the forbidden Cindern Waste, out from Cinderhold · **Level** 13–16 · **Danger** very high — **the ash is the dungeon**

**What It Is.** Concord works buried under volcanic ash in the badland the fire-priests forbid, over which the theocracy has built its own certainties without ever going down to look. Ash preserves almost as well as ice. The orthodox ban digging; the heretic scholars want it desperately; the miners keep breaking into voids they were not looking for.

**Approach and Entrance.** With the heretic-scholar's circle, who know the safe waste-routes and want in badly (the key access, and a defiance of the Pyre). Through a mining tunnel that has already broken into the works. Straight across the forbidden waste past the wardens' patrols. Or alone into the ash, which is the same mistake as the caldera by a different route.

**Layout.**
1. **The Forbidden Waste-Edge** — ash dunes, warden patrols, and the ban's markers.
2. **The Ash-Drowned Entry** — a Concord structure half-swallowed; a miner's break or a heretic's dig-shaft.
3. **The Buried Hall** — the works' central hall, ash-preserved. Inscription, intact, extensive.
4. **The Ember-Heart** — the sealed inner chamber and its ash-and-fire-touched sentry.
5. **The Deep Ash-Vaults** — the deepest chambers: intact relics, worst air, **bottoming out in ash and bedrock.**

**Hazards.** Ash-storms (**DC 15 Con** — choking, blinding, exhausting; shelter and time them); ash-collapse and unstable ground over buried voids (**DC 14–15**, 4d6–6d6 and buried); choke-ash underground (**DC 14 Con** — ventilate, and use miner-craft); heat (**DC 14 Con**); and the Pyre-wardens, who are a faction problem, not a monster.

**Inhabitants.** Ash-locked lingering dead — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~52–64 HP, bound and rite-able. Ash-and-fire-touched Concord guardian-constructs (AC 17–18, ~120–160 HP, immune to fire; **the watchword stands them down**, and the heretic-scholar can read it). Living: Pyre-wardens (**Veteran/Priest-like**, AC 16, ~60–90 HP), avoidable and negotiable; heretic diggers; miners.

**Salvage.** Intact ash-preserved Concord artifacts (tier-3/4). Copies of the buried hall's inscription. A foothold in a fractured theocracy — the heretics' alliance or the orthodoxy's enmity, and it is difficult to have neither.

**Getting Out.** Cinderhold is the haven and the waste itself is hostile the whole way back. Guardians and dead are bound. Ash-storms force you out on their own schedule, which is a valve — but **preparation against the ash is mandatory and should be telegraphed before anyone sets out.**
