## AI Use

Load when a character asks what happens after death, encounters the restless dead, or wants to know how the afterlife works in this world. This is common belief and folk understanding — what nearly anyone in Orrun could tell you, or would argue about over a drink. It contains no hidden or setting-specific truth; use it as-is for any story in this world.

## Summary

Vael's cosmology is built around three life-passages rather than a crowded pantheon, and around one unsettling, openly-discussed problem: since a historical catastrophe called the Quietfall, the dead do not always leave cleanly.

## The World and the Thresholds

- The world of **Vael** is the living world. Beyond it lie the **Three Thresholds**: Birth, Living, and the **Last Door** (death).
- Standard planar cosmology, elemental and outer realms, and conjured creatures all exist as expected — most people simply don't think much about them day to day. Ordinary folk care far more about the Last Door than about the outer planes.

## Death and the Quiet Country

- The dead pass through the **Last Door** into the **Quiet Country**, a still, gray peace. Good funeral rites are believed to help the dead pass smoothly; the old Custodian Concord was famous for tending this duty for everyone, everywhere.
- Since the Quietfall, the Last Door "sticks." Some of the dead do not pass on cleanly — they linger as pale, sorrowful figures called **Remembrances**, seen near old ruins, shrines, and graves.
- **Nobody agrees on why.** It's a live, ordinary argument, not a settled fact:
  - Some blame divine anger — that the Thresholds are punishing the world for the Concord's fall.
  - Some call it a curse laid on the land itself.
  - Some say it's simply the land's grief, given shape.
  - Some scholars and skeptics insist it's just decay — old magic wearing thin with no one left to tend it.
  - A superstitious few whisper that the dead are being *called* somehow, though by what or whom is anyone's guess.
- All of these are valid things an NPC might believe and argue for. None of them is confirmed. A given story or campaign can decide the truth for itself — or leave it a mystery.

## Living With Remembrances

- A Remembrance is usually harmless if left alone, though deeply unsettling — grief given a shape that lingers near where it died or was buried.
- Common folk practice: **name the dead once, and release them.** Calling a dead name back is considered dangerous and taboo (see the folk beliefs in `GODS_AND_FAITHS.md`).
- Speaking with the dead — magically or otherwise — is treated as a serious, frightening act, associated with outlawed practitioners in some regions. It is not, however, impossible; see `MAGIC_AND_REMEMBRANCE.md`.

## Quick Reference

| Question | Common Belief |
|---|---|
| Where do the dead go? | Through the Last Door, into the Quiet Country's still gray peace. |
| Why do some linger as Remembrances? | Disputed — divine anger, a curse, the land's grief, or simple decay are all commonly heard answers. |
| Is resurrection safe? | No — rare, costly, and has grown harder and more unreliable since the Quietfall. |
| Can the dead be spoken to? | Widely considered forbidden and frightening; associated with outlawed practitioners. |
