# Sites Of The Sundering Reach

## AI Use

Load when anyone in the Sundering Reach goes into a ruin. Eight authored sites — flooded Concord works, a mine delving, two barrows and an archive-cairn, a fen, and a merchant's strongroom — described as **physical places a traveler walks into**: entrances, footing, water, air, what lives there, what can be carried out, and how to leave. Nothing here depends on a particular plot; drop any of them into any game set in the Reach.

## The Reach's Ruins In General

The Reach is built on the Custodian Concord's bones (see [`../03_culture/WORLD_HISTORY_PUBLIC.md`](../culture/world-history-public.md)). Concord stonework is instantly recognizable: vast fitted blocks with no visible mortar, star-marks cut at doorways and lintels, and long runs of the old script that nobody in the Reach can read. Salvage from it is the region's second economy, and everyone does it while claiming not to.

Three things are true of almost every Concord ruin here:

- **It is lower than it was.** The peat is still swallowing them. Assume water, and assume the lowest level is flooded.
- **It has dead in it.** Not always, not always hostile — run them per [`../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../bestiary/folk-undead.md), which is deliberately silent about *why*.
- **It has raw relics in it.** The grey glass, stone, and bone that relic-hunters call **old glass**. Valuable, portable, and unpleasant to carry for long (see the relic-exposure hazard below).

**Relic-exposure ("thin-touch").** A recurring hazard at every site with raw relics in quantity. Prolonged handling or prying loose in-situ relics: **DC 14 Constitution save** or take *thin-touch* — **disadvantage on death saving throws** until a proper folk rite is performed over the sufferer (**DC 13 Religion**, any competent grave-keeper) or the sufferer takes a long rest at a working shrine. Reversible, cumulative in fiction but not in mechanics, and always telegraphed: locals will point out somebody who has it and say "he went thin."
