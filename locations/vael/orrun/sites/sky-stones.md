# The Sky-Stones

**Type** living standing-stone shrine · **Region** the open steppe, near the Cold Springs winter-camp · **Level** 8–13 · **Danger** moderate–high; almost none of it combat

**What It Is.** A wind-worn ring of enormous standing stones on a rise in the grass-sea, raised by the horse-clans' forebears long before the Concord and **entirely unrelated to it** — different stone, different working, no star-marks, no inscription anybody has ever been able to compare to anything. It is a living holy place, not a ruin: the clans' wind-singers come here to read omens, and have done so continuously for as long as the clans have existed.

It is also the best place on the continent to be reminded that the Concord was one empire among many things, and that most of the world never noticed it fall.

**Approach and Entrance.** **Hospitality is the key.** Earn a clan's guest-right and you will be escorted to the stones as a matter of course. Travel with a wind-singer for the deepest access. Come during the winter-camp or a summer gathering. Riding out alone across the grass both endangers you and insults the people whose grass it is.

**Layout.**
1. **The Open Grass** — trackless steppe, migration-paths, clan camps, sky. Getting there is the challenge.
2. **The Sky-Stones Ring** — the standing circle on its rise. Older than any ruin the traveler has seen, and in use.
3. **The Singing-Place** — the ring's heart, where the singers read. A cold spot, and an uneasy one lately, which the singers acknowledge and do not explain to outsiders.
4. **The Old Cairns** — the clan forebears' graves nearby. Honored, tended, and **at rest** — the steppe's own rites hold, and always have.

**Hazards.** Getting lost on the trackless grass (**DC 15 Survival** — a clan rider negates it); cold and exposure (**DC 13 Con**); **violating guest-custom**, which is the real hazard and bars you from everything; a chill unease at the Singing-Place (**DC 14 Wis**, no damage); and raiders on the approach.

**Inhabitants.** **No undead. No guardians. Nothing hostile at the shrine at all.** The only combat is on the ride out: a raid-lord's mounted reavers (**Scout/Veteran-like**, AC 14–15, ~40–55 HP), skirmishers who are far better outridden than fought, and who break at half HP.

**Salvage.** No loot. What this site gives is a clan-token and guest-friendship, which is passage and aid across an enormous region; a wind-singer's reading, which is a scene rather than an item; and a steppe-craft gift — a good horse, a warded charm, a possible minor item.

**Getting Out.** The clan camps are mobile havens and always somewhere within a day's ride if you have friends. There is nothing at the stones to flee.
