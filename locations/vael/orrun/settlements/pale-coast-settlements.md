## Regional Character

The Pale Coast is grey water, high cliffs, shingle coves, and a great deal of weather. Its economy is fish, salt, coastal shipping, and salvage — the last of these being both an honourable trade and, in the wrong coves, a criminal one. The **Pale Road** runs along the clifftops linking the settlements; sea passage down the coast and up the river toward the city is faster and, in good weather, safer.

Coast folk are weathered, blunt, and superstitious in a wholly practical way. Houses carry a knot of salt-grass and a piece of sea-glass at the door and a tide-table chalked by the lintel. The **salt-and-tide rite** — the coastal form of the Mourners' funeral custom, for settling the sea's drowned — is the region's central observance, and coastal Mourners ("salt-mothers") hold real authority. Custom demands a cairn and fresh salt for every drowned body recovered, and an empty place kept at the hearth "for the one the sea sends back."

Coastal Concord ruins are common: drowned lighthouse-stumps, sunken stonework, causeways that run out into the water. Salvaging them is legal, profitable, and generally regarded as slightly disreputable.
