# Hollowmere

**Town · ~900 people · the Reach's largest settlement**

### First Impression
Hollowmere rings a wide hollow of dark, still water. Stilt-houses and peat-smoke crowd the firm ground; planked walkways cross the boggy edges; and out in the flooded basin, when the fog thins, you can see broken grey shapes just under the surface — the tops of something old and enormous. The town smells of peat, brine, and cold water. People are kind but watchful, and every doorway has salt at the sill and a small star above it.

### Public Overview
Founded roughly a century ago on the basin's rim, Hollowmere grew because the edge offered firm ground, peat to cut, fish to net, and ruins to scavenge. It is the seat of the Reachward Compact and the natural hub for the region's roads: east to the bridge-town, west toward the salt trade, south toward the orchard country, north toward the mining heights. Its people are peat-cutters, fishers, coopers, netmakers, and salvagers, and most of them are related to each other.

### Districts and Notable Areas
- **The Rim** — the firm-ground core: market, council hall, the better houses.
- **The Drowned Lantern** — the town inn, on the water's edge; lodging, hot food, and the region's busiest rumor-bench.
- **The Boards** — the plank-walkway stilt district over the bog; poorer, louder, closer to the water.
- **Mourner's Green** — the cemetery and the Mourners' cottage; the town's oldest continuous institution.
- **The Counting-House** — the Cinder Ledger's local seat; loans, salvage-buying, hired work.
- **The Warden Rest** — a modest safe-house kept for the Ashen Wardens.
- **The Basin Shore** — the flooded ruin's edge. Not forbidden, exactly, but nobody goes there for pleasure.

### Leadership
A **reeve** elected by, and answerable to, a small council of householders and trade-heads. The reeve keeps order, hears disputes, and quarrels with the council over money. A handful of watchmen enforce compact law.

### Shops and Services
Lodging and food at the inn; a chandler, a cooper, a fishmonger, a peat-merchant, a hedge-healer with a good herb-cupboard; boats and fen-guides for hire at the shore; the counting-house for loans, appraisal, and paid work. No arcane services and no reliable magical healing — for that, travel south or to the city.

### Laws and Customs
No grave-robbing (taken very seriously). Weapons are fine; drawing them is not. Salt at the sill, a star at the door, mirrors covered in a house with a death in it, the dead named once and released. Outsiders are tolerated and put to work quickly; you earn trust here by how you treat other people's dead.

### Local Colour
Peat-boys with barrows, netmenders arguing over a torn seine, a fen-guide who will take your coin and then spend an hour telling you why you should not go. A funeral is a whole-town event and no one works through one.
