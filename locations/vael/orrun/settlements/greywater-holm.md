# Greywater Holm

**Village · ~100 people and falling · the last firm ground before the deep fen**

### First Impression
The road gives out into a corduroy of half-sunk logs, and the village rises on a low holm barely above the black water — turf-roofed cottages, a single crooked longhall, and a burial-ground that has crept up onto the only dry rise because the lower graves will not stay dry. Mist hangs even at midday. It is quiet in a way that feels like held breath.

### Public Overview
Greywater Holm was a peat-cutting and fowling village of a couple of hundred souls and is now perhaps half that. Families leave for the towns; the water rises; the fen takes back ground every year. There is no watch and no counting-house here — the Mourners' elder speaks for the village because nobody else will. It is poor, grieving, and stubborn, and it is the last place to buy a guide before the deep fen.

### Districts and Notable Areas
- **The Crooked Longhall** — the only common building. Meetings, meals, and mourning all happen here.
- **The Creeping Ground** — the burial-rise, climbing away from the rising water year by year.
- **The Fen Edge** — duckboards, fowlers' blinds, and a guide's stilt-hut just outside the holm.

### Leadership
An **elder of the Mourners' Circle**, by consent rather than office. Disputes go to her; serious crimes are punished by exile into the fen, which the village regards as sentence enough.

### Shops and Services
No inn — travellers shelter in the longhall by the elder's leave, for a copper or a kindness. One peat-and-fowl trader. No smith, no temple, no magical healing; folk herb-craft only. The village's genuinely valuable export is **fen-guiding**: local guides who know the safe ground, the blinds, and which channels move.

### Laws and Customs
No formal law. Hospitality is real but silent. The dead are buried higher each year and nobody remarks on it. Outsiders are watched warily — but the village badly needs help and will take an offered hand.

### Local Colour
A widow who has moved the same husband's grave-marker twice as the ground shifted; children who can name every safe stepping-log; a fowler who will not go out past the second blind and will not say why.
