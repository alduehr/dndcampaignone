# Wrackmouth

**Town · ~1,300 people · the coast's port and capital**

### First Impression
The Pale Road climbs a last headland and the sea opens out, grey and endless, and below in a deep cleft of cliff sits Wrackmouth: a steep town of slate roofs and salt-white timber tumbling down to a stone harbour packed with fishing boats and heavy salt-hulks. Gulls and the reek of fish and brine. Nets, salt-sheds, and the constant boom of surf on the harbour wall. Every house has salt-grass and sea-glass at the door and a chalked tide-table by the lintel — and everyone, at some point in the conversation, glances once at the broken lighthouse on the far headland.

### Public Overview
Wrackmouth is the coast's port and effective capital, where fish, salt, and frontier trade all pass. The Cinder Ledger runs the salt-works, the weigh-house, and the harbour credit, and the price it sets at that scale is felt hundreds of miles inland. The town governs itself through a **harbour-moot** under a harbour-reeve, with the Reachward Compact a thin presence. A strong chapter of the Mourners' Circle maintains the salt-and-tide rites, which matter here more than anywhere: the sea returns its dead constantly, and someone must see to them.

### Districts and Notable Areas
- **The Harbour and Salt-Hulks** — the deep-water cove, the fishing fleet, and the heavy salt ships; departures down the coast and up-river to the city.
- **The Weigh-House** — the salt-scale and credit-office; the town's real power.
- **The Moot-Stair** — the cliff-stair hall where the harbour-moot sits.
- **The Tide-Bell** — the great salt-and-tide shrine and its drowned-bell, rung for the newly drowned.
- **The Wrack** — the rough lower harbour: wreckers, smugglers, fences, and cheap beds. Barely policed.
- **The Gull & Anchor** — the big harbour-inn; trade talk, sea-gossip, and the place to hire a skipper.

### Leadership
A **harbour-reeve** chairs the harbour-moot and speaks for the coast; the post traditionally goes to a retired skipper. About a dozen harbour-watch keep the quays and generally leave the Wrack and the outer coves to themselves. In practice the Cinder Ledger's coastal factor sets policy through the salt price, and the tide-faith commands moral authority on anything touching the drowned.

### Shops and Services
The Gull & Anchor (about 5 sp a night, excellent fish, skippers and guides for hire). The Weigh-House: banking, loans, the salt market, ship's stores, and **passage to the city by sea and river** — the coast's distinctive route. A full harbour market: chandler, sailmaker, smith, a healer's house with folk medicine and a tide-priest capable of minor healing. Boats and crews for hire. The Tide-Bell performs the salt-and-tide rites.

### Laws and Customs
Harbour-moot law on the quays; very little law anywhere else. Salt-grass and sea-glass at the door; read the tide-table before doing business with anyone; never put to sea while the drowned-bell is tolling; leave the sea its due. Wrecking and unlicensed salvage-smuggling are crimes on paper and commonplace in fact, tolerated so long as the customary cut reaches the right hands.

### Local Colour
A weigh-house queue of furious fisherfolk comparing this season's salt price with last season's; the whole town falling silent, mid-errand, when the drowned-bell rings; a skipper who will name a fair price to the city and then argue about the weather for an hour.
