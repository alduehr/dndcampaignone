## One-Sentence Identity

A proud, prosperous, self-satisfied inland city-state that survived the Quietfall by going quiet and getting rich, and now runs the northwest's money, scholarship, and law from a comfortable distance.

## First Impression

After days of fen, forest, and broken road, Caradril is a shock of stone and noise: a tiered city climbing the banks of a slow green river to a still inland lake crowded with masts. Bells mark the hours — trade-bells, not funeral bells. The streets are paved. The lamps are lit at dusk by a guild that takes itself extremely seriously. The people are not afraid of the dead; here death is tidy, expensive, and handled by professionals. Coin moves everywhere. Above the counting-houses fly the banners of the chartered merchant families; above the river-college burn the green lamps of the scholars. After the frontier it feels almost untouched.

## Public Overview

Caradril is an independent city-state on the **Verdance**, where the river widens into the harbour-lake locals call the **Stillwater**. It was a modest Concord river-town before the Quietfall and, alone among the great settlements of the northwest, came through the collapse largely intact. The century since — which Caradrese call **the Stilling** — made it rich as a clearing-house and a refuge.

It is governed by the **Tidewater Council**, a body of about nine magisters drawn from the chartered merchant families (the **Charter Houses**) and chaired by a **First Magister**. Its law is kept by the **Tide-Watch**. It is home to the headquarters of the Cinder Ledger and to the Concord Remnant, whose scholars produce the continent's only fluent readers of Concord Script. Most of its people are merchants, clerks, artisans, dockhands, scholars, and the poor of the riverside undercity called **the Sill**. It honours the Three Thresholds with grand civic funerals on the temple hill of **Highmourn**, and it thinks of itself, sincerely, as the civilised future the rest of Orrun ought to imitate.

## Scale and Layout

- **Population:** roughly 18,000–22,000 — by a wide margin the largest settlement in this part of the continent.
- **Shape:** tiered on the north and south banks of the Verdance where it meets the Stillwater harbour-lake. The river runs west-to-east through the city; the lake is at the east end.
- **Upper city (north bank, higher ground):** the Magisterium, Highmourn, and the Lantern Reach.
- **River and harbour (the working heart):** the Counting-Quays, the Ashmarket, and the Crucible.
- **Low city (south bank, riverside):** the Sill.
- **Beneath everything:** the **Sunken Wards**, a partly flooded older Concord level reached by a few sealed and forgotten stairs.

## Government and Law

- **The Tidewater Council** holds all real power. It grants **charters** — trade licences — sets tolls and taxes, judges high disputes, and commands the Tide-Watch. Seats are effectively hereditary among the Charter Houses, but a charter can be bought, granted, or revoked, which makes the council as much a marketplace as a government.
- **The Wardmoot** is a commoners' assembly of district representatives. It may petition; it may not compel. It functions as a pressure valve and as a nursery for reformers.
- **The Tide-Watch** keeps order: competent, bribable at the edges, deferential to charter-holders, and considerably harder on the Sill than on the Quays. Crossing a Charter House is more dangerous than crossing the law.
- Caradril is **not** part of the Reachward Compact. It is an independent city-state, and it regards the frontier as a poor, useful, faintly alarming hinterland — a source of salvage, salt, debt, and unsettling news.

## Laws and Customs (citywide)

- **Charter law:** trading without a charter is the city's signature crime. Relic and salvage dealing is *licensed*; unlicensed relic-dealing is a black-market offence the smuggling syndicates control and the Watch mostly ignores for a share.
- **Funeral law:** the dead are handled by the Quiet Houses on Highmourn for a fee. Private or "frontier-style" rites inside the walls are frowned upon and occasionally fined.
- **Customs:** Caradrese cover the eyes of the dead rather than the mirrors; the street-lamps are lit at dusk "so the dead can find the Door and not the living"; charter-holders wear a house-token openly; debt is not a shame here, it is business.
- **Outsiders:** frontier folk are tolerated, condescended to, and watched. A capable newcomer is treated as either a tool or a threat, and sometimes both in the same conversation.

## Services (citywide summary)

Caradril offers what no town can: real magic-item commerce (modest at the low end, genuine at the high end), spellcasting services for hire, master craftsmen, moneylending and letters of credit, scholarly research and translation, every mundane good, hirelings and guides, ships and caravans onward, and discreet criminal services for those who go looking. Raising the dead is available, licensed by the council, ruinously expensive, and — the clergy will admit if pressed — not guaranteed.

# Districts

## The Magisterium

**Government quarter · upper city, north bank**

Wide swept steps, clean stone, and a hush enforced by money and habit. Colonnaded halls, the great bronze doors of **Magisters' Hall**, and a fountain that runs even in drought because the city can afford it. Liveried doorwards, charter-tokens on every important chest, criers reading out the day's rulings. After the frontier's peat-smoke it smells of beeswax and ink, and everybody here is busy being important and faintly bored.

**Notable areas:** Magisters' Hall (council chamber, high court, charter-registry); the Wardmoot building beside it; the Tide-Watch central barracks and gaol; the charter-registry offices; notaries', advocates', and the townhouses of the senior Houses. **The Pale Star**, an excellent inn at the district's edge, is neutral ground where every faction's people drink and watch each other — the standard recommendation for a visitor who wants a good bed and good information.

**Services:** notaries and advocates (contracts, charters, wills); registry access, legitimately or otherwise; bail and fines at the barracks; fine lodging and dining at the Pale Star; message-couriers. The district's true commodity is *introductions*.

**Law and custom:** a charter is sacred, the council's word is law, and appeals are expensive. The Wardmoot petitions but cannot compel. Refusing a magister's summons is a serious matter. The upper city is heavily patrolled and unfriendly to visible frontier roughness — open armour and weapons attract the Watch unless a charter-holder waves them off.

**Local colour:** charter-hearings that turn on a single document; a reform-minded magister recruiting sympathisers over watered wine; a frontier courier waiting three days for an audience that never comes.

## The Counting-Quays

**Banking and harbour · the Stillwater lakefront**

The Stillwater opens before you, crowded with masts; stone quays run with porters, tally-clerks, and the clack of counting-frames. Warehouses stand like fortresses. Above them rises the Cinder Ledger's headquarters — not a castle but a vast, severe counting-house of grey stone, its windows lamplit at all hours. The air smells of lake-water, tar, spices, and money. Nobody is idle; everybody is owed, or owing.

**Notable areas:** the great counting-house itself (offices, counting-floors, and strongrooms); the Stillwater quays, warehouses, and customs sheds; the Moneychange, where letters of credit and currency are handled; **the Last Tally**, the clerks' and captains' tavern, where deals are struck that the Magisterium merely ratifies later.

**Services:** banking — loans, letters of credit, deposits, debt; salvage and curio buying at the best prices in the city, with strings attached; caravan and ship bookings onward; warehousing; hireling muscle; and information for sale. The single best source of money on this side of the continent, and the single easiest way to end up leveraged.

**Law and custom:** charter law is strictest here, and unlicensed trade on the quays is punished swiftly. The bank's word carries near-legal weight; being blacklisted by it can ruin a newcomer citywide. Deals are sealed with a token, and debts are tracked obsessively and never forgotten.

**Local colour:** a nervous underclerk who has clearly been awake for two days; a wharf-boss shouting a barge into place; the guild of tally-clerks having a furious argument about a decimal.

## The Lantern Reach

**Scholars' quarter · terraces above the river**

The lamps here burn green — a scholars' affectation and the district's pride — lit at dusk along terraced libraries and lecture-halls above the Verdance. Students argue in the streets; copyists' fingers are ink-stained; bells call lectures. The **Lamplighters' Hall** dominates the terrace, its doors open to anyone curious about the fallen Concord's "golden age." It is warm, learned, and faintly self-satisfied.

**Notable areas:** the Lamplighters' Hall (the public college, where the continent's only fluent readers of Concord Script teach, translate, and catalogue); the Stacks, the open libraries, free to any respectable visitor; the head archivist's private study; and inner libraries closed to all but the society's senior members, which the students speculate about constantly. **Lampgate**, the terrace gate, is the short walk toward the Pale Star.

**Services:** research and translation — the best scholarly resource within a month's travel; tutelage in Concord Script for a serious and patient student; book and map copying; identification and lore services; scribes; and lodging for visiting scholars.

**Law and custom:** the college polices its own and the Tide-Watch rarely enters. Scholarship is currency: a lector's recommendation opens doors and a feud closes them. The green lamps are lit personally by lecturers, never by servants. Texts are "lent to the world"; relics are "kept for study." The closed libraries are forbidden to outsiders, and treated far more seriously than the phrase suggests.

**Local colour:** a lecturer lighting up while explaining the Concord's lost road-network; a copyist who will trade a rubbing for a story from the frontier; two students nearly coming to blows over a translation.

## The Ashmarket

**Relic and salvage trade · between the Quays and the Crucible**

A roofed-over warren of stalls: lamplight on grey Concord stone-scraps, brokers crying lots, the smell of old metal, dust, and incense burned to "settle" uneasy goods. Appraisers squint through lenses; haulers wheel crates fresh off frontier caravans; a clergyman stands at the edge muttering against the whole trade. Licensed dealing up front — and, through a curtain, the other market.

**Notable areas:** the Open Stalls (licensed salvage trade); the Curtain Market (back rooms, unlicensed goods, fences); the smuggling syndicate's quiet counting-room, which everyone can find and nobody admits to visiting; Appraisers' Row, with the best lenses, scales, and authentication in the city.

**Services:** buying and selling salvage and curios; relic appraisal and authentication; fencing of questionable goods; mundane antiques and oddments; and, under the curtain, contraband of most kinds. If you have dug something out of a frontier ruin, this is where you find out what it is worth.

**Law and custom:** a relic-charter is required for legal dealing; unlicensed relic-trade is a crime the Watch overlooks for a share. Incense is burned over lots that feel "uneasy," a buyer never asks where a piece was dug, and the clergy's standing protest is tolerated as background noise.

**Local colour:** an appraiser's lens trembling over a piece that does not read the way it should; a hawker selling three identical "unique" finds; a temple protest the crowd cheerfully jeers at.

## Highmourn

**Temple and cemetery hill · upper city**

A green hill above the city crowned by the **Threshold Cathedral**, with its three great doors for Birth, Living, and the Last Door. Terraced cemeteries — the **Quiet Houses** — fall away below it, ordered, well-tended, and carved with the names of charter families. Bells toll the hours of mourning. Incense, cut flowers, cold stone. After the frontier's failing graveyards, Highmourn looks like the way death is supposed to work: dignified, professional, and paid for.

**Notable areas:** the Threshold Cathedral (the city's great temple, its healing services, and its civic faith); the Quiet Houses below (the civic cemetery and funeral works, where the dead are washed, named, and laid to rest for a fee scaled to one's charter); a small, chronically ignored chapter-house of the Ashen Wardens at the hill's foot; and the Mourning Terraces of charter-family tombs.

**Services:** paid healing (wound-closing, restoration, preservation of the dead; curse-lifting and raising the dead by council licence, at steep cost); funeral rites and burial; blessings and consecration; religious research. Highmourn is the city's most reliable place to rest, heal, and be treated decently while doing it.

**Law and custom:** the dead are the Quiet Houses' business, and private or frontier-style rites within the walls are discouraged. Raising the dead requires a council licence and a cathedral rite. The eyes of the dead are covered; mourning-bells are rung by station; a coin is left "for the Door" at the tomb. Embarrassing the cathedral is a political act as much as a religious one.

**Local colour:** a grand civic funeral with hired mourners and a professional bell-choir; a grave-magister who takes his ledgers very seriously; the lone Warden at the chapter-house, pathetically grateful that anyone has come to listen.

## The Crucible

**Foundries and craft guilds · across the river from the Quays**

Heat and noise: forge-glow through open doors, the ring of hammers, the roar of smelters, guild banners over fortress-like halls. The district never fully sleeps; the night-shift glows red across the water. Crafters here are proud, clannish, and genuinely skilled — the best smiths, jewellers, gearwrights, and alchemists between the frontier and the sea.

**Notable areas:** the great foundries (including the largest, which has lately taken to melting down Concord salvage for its rare metals and old glass — lucrative and controversial); the guildhalls of the smiths, gearwrights, jewellers, and alchemists; Alchemists' Row; and the slag-yards, which are off-limits to outsiders and smell wrong.

**Services:** quality weapons, armour, and gear — the best commerce of its kind in the region; commissions and repairs; jewellers and fine work; alchemy and reagents (potions, oils, antitoxins, and some scrollwork by arrangement with the river-college); metallurgy and appraisal of metals and old glass. This is where a traveller upgrades between journeys.

**Law and custom:** guild law governs craft, and non-guild work for hire is restricted — a visitor may commission, not compete. A maker's mark is sacred. The night-shift "keeps the fires honest." Relic-smelting is technically licensed and practically unregulated, which is a live argument in the Wardmoot.

**Local colour:** a guild-steward walking a customer through a commission with immense ceremony; an apprentice bolting from a smelt-hall white-faced and refusing to say why; a smith and an alchemist conducting a decades-old feud through pointed courtesy.

## The Sill

**The poor riverside undercity · low south bank**

The river's low side: tenements built on older stone, walkways over backwater channels, washing-lines, smoke, and the constant slap of water under the boards. Poorer, louder, and considerably more alive than the upper city. **The Brass Ferry** tavern leans out over the water; ferrymen call for fares; children run the planks. Newcomers from the frontier huddle in doorways — thin-faced, quiet, unwelcome. Everyone here knows things, and in the Sill, knowing is currency.

**Notable areas:** the Brass Ferry (the district's heart and rumour-mill); the shifting meeting-places of the city's information brokers, who trade secrets rather than goods; the refugee boards where frontier newcomers shelter; **the Sealgate**, a sealed stair down into the Sunken Wards; and the backwater channels, which are smuggler routes and always have been.

**Services:** cheap lodging and food; ferries and quiet passage; smuggled goods and fences; information for coin or favour, which is the district's prize export; and guides willing to take you down into the Sunken Wards. It is also the best place in the city to hire capable, desperate, deniable help cheaply.

**Law and custom:** the Watch is sparse and the Sill polices itself by reputation and leverage. A fare paid honestly buys a ferryman's silence. Refugees are sheltered by the boards even as the upper city scorns them. You do not ask a broker where they heard it. Frontier-style rites are technically illegal and quietly tolerated among the grieving poor.

**Local colour:** the Brass Ferry at night, full of ferrymen, refugees, and one person in a booth who is clearly not a ferryman; a community organiser arguing the refugees' case to anyone who will carry it uphill; a lantern-lit stair-mouth with an old delver sitting beside it, waiting for custom.

## The Sunken Wards

**The drowned lower city · beneath everything**

Down the Sealgate's worn stair the trade-noise fades into dripping dark. The Sunken Wards are the bones of an older Caradril: Concord stonework, half-flooded halls, sealed bronze doors, and channels of still black water that throw a lantern back at you. The air is cold and patient. Star-marks and lines of Concord Script run along the lintels.

The city above has largely forgotten it is standing on them. There are no permanent inhabitants: delvers, salvagers, smugglers using the dry upper chambers for drops, and the occasional scholar with a permit and a nervous expression. What draws people down is readable Concord Script in situ, old salvage, and the simple fact that nobody has properly mapped the place.

**Practical notes for visitors:** flooded passages, cold water, and slow flood cycles in some halls; genuinely unstable Concord stone; and a real risk of getting lost without a guide or a map. Retreat upward to the Sill is always available, which is what makes the Wards a reasonable expedition rather than a suicide. Hire a guide from the Sealgate; the good ones will tell you exactly how far they will go and why they stop there.

**Law and custom:** none, formally. The Watch does not descend. What order exists is imposed by the delvers themselves, who have a code about marking routes and not stripping a shaft another crew is working.
