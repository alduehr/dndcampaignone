# Orchardmere

**Town · ~1,600 people · the Vale's hub**

### First Impression
The South Road crests a rise and the Vale opens below: orchards in red-gold autumn rows, a broad bright lake, and a town of mellow stone and cider-presses under woodsmoke and the sweet rot-smell of pressing apples. Bells ring the harvest-hours. People are well-fed, well-dressed for a frontier, and unhurried; they nod to strangers and watch them a beat too long. Garlands of dried apple-blossom hang over every door, and a small fresh clay star sits pressed into each lintel.

### Public Overview
Orchardmere is the richest town a traveller from the fens is likely to have seen. Cider, nuts, wool, and grain go south to the city and the money comes back. It governs the Vale through the harvest-moot and defers, in all matters concerning the dead, to the Mourners' Circle at the Old Circle. The town is proud of its orchards, prouder of its rites, and extremely conscious of its own reputation.

### Districts and Notable Areas
- **The Moot-Hall** — timber-and-stone hall on the green where the harvest-moot sits; the Vale's seat of power.
- **The Pressyards** — cider- and nut-presses and their deep cellars along the lake; the town's wealth and most of its labour.
- **The Old Circle** — the ancient grave-garden and shrine, the oldest in this part of Orrun and the town's spiritual heart.
- **The Cider Star** — the fine inn, named for the lintel-stars; where pilgrims, traders, and travelling scholars meet.
- **The Counting-Yard** — the Cinder Ledger's Vale office, credit-counter, and buying-desk.
- **The Lakeside** — quays, fishing boats, and the pilgrim-ferries that carry the devout toward the shrine track.

### Leadership
A **moot-reeve** chairs the harvest-moot and speaks for the Vale — an administrative post, elected by orchard-masters, concerned above all with trade and reputation. Roughly half a dozen **moot-wardens** keep order. Real justice here is communal and reputational: being run out of a shrine-town is a heavier sentence than a fine.

### Shops and Services
The Cider Star (about 6 sp a night, good food, better cider). Full market: smith, cooper, fletcher, a fine chandler, a healer's house with folk medicine and a hedge-priest capable of minor blessings and healing. Horses and carts for the South Road. The counting-yard offers banking, loans, credit south to the city, and buys curios and old salvage over the counter. The Old Circle performs funeral rites and grief-counsel.

### Laws and Customs
Harvest-moot law. A fresh clay star at every lintel, renewed at the new moon. Grave-meddling and relic-theft are punished ferociously by custom — the gibbet at the crossroads is not decorative. Speaking loudly of failed rites is considered ill-bred and unlucky, and earns cold stares even from people who agree with you.

### Local Colour
A press-master who can tell you which orchard a cup came from; moot sessions that run for hours over a boundary hedge; pilgrims buying blessed cider by the flask before the climb to the shrine.
