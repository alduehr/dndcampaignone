# Cobble Strand

**Village · ~150 people · a shingle-cove fishing village**

### First Impression
The cliff-road drops to a grey shingle cove and a huddle of tarred cottages with upturned-boat roofs, fish-racks, and a little salt-and-tide shrine at the high-tide line. Above, on the headland, the broken stump of an old Concord lighthouse stands black against the sky. The folk are lean, salt-cured, and grave, and they keep an empty place at every hearth "for the one the sea sends back." At the strand's edge, a row of small cairns marks the drowned, and someone has left fresh salt on each of them.

### Public Overview
Cobble Strand fishes the cove and works the near salt-pans, scraping a hard living under a salt price set somewhere else by someone richer. Its faith is the coastal Mourners' tradition in its purest form — the salt-and-tide rites kept by the village's salt-mother. The village has always lived close to the dead, because the sea is always giving them back. It reveres and slightly fears the ruined lighthouse above it, which the old songs call the light that "called the drowned home."

### Districts and Notable Areas
- **The Strand and Cairns** — the shingle beach, the boats, and the drowned-cairns along the tide-line.
- **The Tide-Shrine** — the salt-mother's shrine at the high-water mark; the village's heart.
- **The Boat-Hall** — the common hall, built from an upturned hulk, where news and grief are shared.
- **The Lamp Stair** — the cliff-path up to the ruined lighthouse on the headland.
- **The Cove Caves** — sea-caves under the headland, tide-flooded and dangerous, and used by people who prefer not to be seen.

### Leadership
The **salt-mother**, keeper of the tide-shrine, leads in everything that matters: the rites, the drowned, the old songs. The **headman**, usually the eldest fisher, speaks for the village's worldly affairs and to the port town's moot, and defers to her on the dead.

### Shops and Services
No inn — a respected guest is given hearth-room and fish-stew, and the hospitality is genuine and earned. Fish, salt, and tide-lore to trade; net-mending and boat repair; a folk-healer, plus the salt-mother's rite-aid, which is the best care on the coast outside the port. Boats and a guide can be hired for the coves and the near sea, and are essential for either.

### Laws and Customs
Custom is law; the salt-mother and the headman settle disputes; there is no militia and the sea is the great authority. A cairn and fresh salt for every drowned. An empty hearth-place kept for the returned. Nothing is taken from a wreck without the salt-mother's rite first — a rule wreckers break, to the village's lasting grief. The village shelters its own and distrusts outsiders who make light of the tide.

### Local Colour
A net-wife who is blunt to the point of rudeness and unfailingly generous; a fisher-lad who has stopped going out since his father drowned; the salt-mother deciding, over a long silence, whether a stranger has earned an old song.
