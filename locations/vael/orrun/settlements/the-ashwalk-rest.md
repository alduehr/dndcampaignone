# The Ashwalk Rest

**Waystation · ~20 residents plus travellers · the great crossroads**

### First Impression
Where four Concord causeways meet on a low rise stands the Rest: a squat, ash-grey hall of old Concord stone, re-roofed and walled with a turf rampart, a star carved deep over the gate and a thin, constant smoke of ritual ash. Travellers shelter in the yard. Inside it is warm, spare, and quiet, smelling of woodsmoke and bitter herbs. After the fens it feels like the first place in days you can put your back to a wall.

### Public Overview
The Ashwalk Rest is the wandering Ashen Wardens' main waystation in the region — not a town but a crossroads sanctuary the order has maintained for centuries. Its brethren base out of it between calls; travellers shelter under its protection; a Mourner shares the hall and tends the road-dead. It charges nothing and accepts labour, food, or donation. It is the safest roof between any two towns in the Reach.

### Notable Areas
- **The Grey Hall** — common hall, hearth, and the order's long table.
- **The Yard and Rampart** — walled ground where carts, travellers, and refugees shelter.
- **The Rite-Room** — where the dead brought in off the roads are laid and tended.
- **The Cellar** — the order's regional records. Not open to visitors.

### Leadership
A **hospitaller** who runs the hall, the stores, and the constant traffic of exhausted brethren. Senior members of the order pass through and take precedence on matters of rite, but the hospitaller runs the house.

### Shops and Services
Sanctuary — safe lodging in hall or yard, free or by donation; the most reliable safe rest in the open country. Rite-aid, lent salt, and a blessing for the road. Folk medicine plus genuine minor healing magic. Basic resupply: salt, herbs, trail food, lamp-oil. No weapons or armour. A warning, and sometimes a guide, before a bad stretch of road.

### Laws and Customs
The house rule is simple: no violence inside the rampart, no trafficking in relics, and the dead come first. Leave the hall as you found it and burn a pinch of ash for the road-dead before you go. All are sheltered regardless of who they are — though the unwelcome sleep in the yard, unarmed and watched.

### Local Colour
Grey-cloaked brethren carried in half-dead from a bad call; families from the fen villages camped by the wall; a long quiet evening at the hearth that is, for most travellers, the best night's sleep in the region.
