## Regional Character

The Sundering Reach is peat, standing water, grey weather, and old stone. Concord-era causeways cross the fens on raised stonework that predates every living institution; the roads still work, which is more than can be said for most of what the Concord built. The land supports peat-cutting, fowling, reed and thatch, salt trade, mining in the northern heights, and salvage from the ruins the water keeps giving up.

Reach folk are hospitable in a wary, practical way. They put salt at the door-sill and carve or press a small star above the lintel — the star is a Threshold sign for the door the dead go through, and folk here are unfussy about whether it works so long as it is kept up. Funeral custom is taken seriously: the dead are named once aloud, then released. Everyone knows that rites sometimes fail; nobody local claims to know why, and it is considered ill-bred to speculate loudly in someone else's village.

Common institutions across the region. (These are the publicly known bodies of the frontier — their existence, membership, and stated purpose are ordinary common knowledge. Whatever any of them privately wants is a matter for a specific story to decide, and nothing here assumes an answer.)

- **The Reachward Compact** ("the Compact") — a loose regional government of reeves, bailiffs, haywards, and a thin town watch, funded largely by bridge and road tolls. Its reach thins fast the farther you get from the roads.
- **The Mourners' Circle** ("the Mourners' Circle") — a folk death-faith of elders, grave-singers, and shrine-tenders. No central church; each chapter is its own. Widely trusted; often the only authority in a small place.
- **The Ashen Wardens** ("the Ashen Wardens") — a wandering order in ash-grey cloaks who answer calls when a funeral goes wrong. Few in number, always tired, respected everywhere, resident almost nowhere.
- **The Cinder Ledger** ("the Ledger," "the Cinder Ledger") — a large chartered counting-house out of Caradril, which finances the frontier, farms tolls, buys salvage, and sets the price of salt. Prosperous and widely resented.
- **The Gravecallers** — an outlawed, widely feared group rumoured to speak with the dead, blamed for far more than it plausibly does. Mentioned in whispers everywhere; confirmed almost nowhere.
