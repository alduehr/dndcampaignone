## Summary

Faith in Orrun centers on the **Three Thresholds** rather than a crowded pantheon: three divine principles governing Birth, Living, and Death. There is no grand unified church anymore — the old Custodian Concord was the closest thing, and it fell. Today faith is fragmented between folk death-keepers, threshold shrines, and scattered clergy. Clerics, paladins, and divine casters function exactly as expected; the gods are understood as real but distant.

## The Three Thresholds

| Threshold | Domain | Symbol | Common Title |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Opening** | Birth, beginnings, the unborn, hope | An open ring | "the First Threshold" |
| **The Hearth** | Living, kinship, oaths, the everyday world | A flame within a square | "the Threshold We Stand In" |
| **The Last Door** | Death, memory, passage, the dead | A closed door with a single star | "the Last Threshold" |

- Most people honor all three at different life moments; few are exclusive devotees of just one.
- Divine casters (clerics, paladins, and the like) draw from whichever Threshold suits their calling — domains like Life, Light, Grave, War, or Peace all fit comfortably under one Threshold or another.
- In regions where death-rites have become unreliable, the **Last Door** tends to be the most actively and anxiously worshipped of the three.

## Clergy and Practice

- There is no unified church. Clergy are local: a village rite-speaker, a shrine-keeper, a wandering preacher.
- Organized folk-faiths built around tending the dead are common wherever death-rite trouble is felt — grief-keepers rather than a temple hierarchy. In the northwest the largest is the **Mourners' Circle**.
- Some regions have quasi-religious orders of wandering rite-keepers who lay restless dead to rest; many treat them as holy even though they are technically an order, not a church. The northwest's are the **Ashen Wardens**.
- Divine magic works normally everywhere. A failed rite is understood as the world failing somehow, not a failure of the priest's faith — which is part of what makes the phenomenon so unsettling to clergy who cannot explain it.

## Folk Beliefs and Protections

- "Salt at the sill, a star at the door" — a common funeral protection against a lingering Remembrance.
- The dead should be **named once and released**, never called back. Speaking a dead name three times is said to "open the door wrong."
- Mirrors are covered in a house of the recently dead, so the dead "don't see a way to stay."

## Pantheon Overview

| Name | Domain | Church | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Opening (First Threshold) | Birth, hope | Folk shrines | Distant, real |
| The Hearth (Threshold We Stand In) | Living, oaths | Folk shrines, village rites | Distant, real |
| The Last Door (Last Threshold) | Death, memory | Folk death-keepers (the Mourners' Circle), wandering rite-keepers (the Ashen Wardens) | Distant, real; the most actively worshipped where death-rites are troubled |
