## AI Use

Load for the closed volcanic highland in Orrun's southeast. Entry is controlled; treat access itself as the region's first obstacle. Environmental hazards here are among the harshest on the continent.

## One-Sentence Identity

A closed volcanic fire-theocracy of pyre-rites, ash roads, and obsidian, ruling a smoking highland that outsiders enter only by permission and one road.

## Geography

The **Emberfells**: an active volcanic highland spurring off the continent's central cordillera. Lava-vent country, ash-plains, cinder cones, hot springs, and black glass.

Defining features:

- **The caldera** — the active heart of the highland, patrolled and restricted; the faith's holiest ground.
- **The Ashfast terrace** — the broad shelf below the caldera on which the theocracy's fortress-temple-city stands.
- **The Ash Roads** — the string of warden-posts forming the pilgrim-way and the only sanctioned approach from the west.
- **The Cindern Waste** — an ash-and-stone badland in the highland's eastern rain-shadow; forbidden by the wardens and lethal without water and protection.
- **The Glass Coast** — the obsidian shore to the south, where black volcanic glass beaches meet the warm sea.

**Borders:** the Saltmere basin and Ghostmark country (west), the Cindern Waste and eastern interior (east), the warm southern sea and Glass Coast (south), the Greatspine's southeastern reaches (north).

## Climate

Hot, dry, and dusty, with ash-fall as a routine weather condition. Air quality varies from unpleasant to dangerous depending on the mountain's mood. The pilgrim-road's cold springs are the only reliable water on approach; inside the region, water is controlled, distributed, and never casual.

## Culture And Livelihood

The most alien polity on the continent, and the most self-sufficient. The fire-faith is old, pre-Concord, and total: it orders the calendar, the law, the professions, and above all the treatment of the dead, who are given to the mountain's fire in public pyre-rites of great solemnity and considerable spectacle. Devotion here is not private.

Common trades: obsidian and volcanic glasswork, ore and metalwork, sulfur and ash-products, terraced dryland farming, and the pilgrim traffic on the Ash Roads.

Outsiders are tolerated within strict forms: enter by the sanctioned road, submit at each warden-post, keep to permitted ground, observe the rites' proprieties. The theocracy is orderly rather than cruel — it signals its displeasure long before it enforces it, and its wardens are doctrinaire rather than corrupt.

**Who holds it:** an **Ash-Hierophant** rules absolutely from the fortress-temple-city, policed by **pyre-wardens**. Mining interests at the ore-town are the closest thing to a secular power. Foreign scholars ask, politely and repeatedly, for access to the region's oldest sites, and are politely and repeatedly refused.

## Settlements

- **Ashfast** — the fortress-temple-city on the caldera terrace; the theocracy's capital and the region's only true city.
- **Cinderhold** — a volcanic mining town on the Cinder Road; obsidian, ore, and a rougher, more secular temper.
- **The Ash Roads** — the warden-post string of the pilgrim approach; a route rather than a town, but travelers spend days on it.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The caldera rim** — patrolled, restricted, and physically unforgiving; safe-conduct required and one wrong step is genuinely one wrong step.
- **The Cindern Waste** — forbidden ash badland; no water, no shade, no help, and old works entombed somewhere beneath the ash.
- **The Glass Coast margin** — obsidian beaches, hot pools, and razor-edged ground underfoot.

## Travel Routes

- **The Ash Roads** — the sanctioned western approach, warden-post to warden-post, to the city's pilgrim gate. Days of climbing.
- **The Cinder Road** east to the mining town; two to three days.
- **The Glass Coast Road** south toward the sea.
- **The Cindern Waste Track** — forbidden, unmaintained, and used only by smugglers and fools.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Ashlands**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Orderly for those who keep the forms, extremely dangerous environmentally. Within the law, the theocracy is one of the safer places on the continent to walk at night. Outside it — off the sanctioned roads, into the Waste, up the caldera without leave — the region becomes as harsh as anywhere in Orrun.

Standing hazards: ash-fall and bad air, heat, water scarcity, unstable volcanic ground, and eruption warnings that the priesthood reads as theology and everyone else reads as a reason to move.
