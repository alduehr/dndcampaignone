## AI Use

Load when travel goes up-river from the northwestern frontier into settled inland Orrun. Public geography, river conditions, and corridor politics only.

## One-Sentence Identity

The river-road corridor up the Verdance — locks, wharves, terraced farms, and barge-villages — where the cold frontier gives way to older, richer, settled inland Orrun.

## Geography

A settled river-bottom corridor: wharves, willow-banks, tilled bottomland, and terraced farmland climbing the valley sides. Drier wooded bluffs stand above the northeast bank; reedy sink-fen spreads to the southwest where the corridor brushes the boglands. Long stretches of half-sunken Concord causeway and lock-works line the water — locals call these "the drowned reaches" and route around them.

The corridor's spine is **the Verdance** itself, fully navigable, flowing northwest down toward the city-state of Caradril and the sea beyond. Its great chokepoint is the **Nine Locks**, a staircase of lock-chambers carrying boats up the corridor's biggest fall.

**Borders:** Caradril's hinterland (northwest, down-river), the Glassmere League (southeast, up-corridor), the Marrowdowns (south), boglands and sink-fen (southwest).

## Climate

Temperate and river-damp: real summers, wet springs, workable winters. Spring melt and autumn rain both raise the river; barge-crews plan their year around the water level, and a bad flood shuts the corridor for weeks.

## Culture And Livelihood

Everything moves by water and everything pays. Barge-folk are a caste unto themselves, with their own slang, their own courtesies, and an informal brotherhood that settles most disputes before any lord hears of them. Lock-town society revolves around toll-books, cargo manifests, and the perpetual argument over who owes what.

Common trades: grain and wine from the terraces, timber, river freight, lock-keeping, toll-farming, chandlery, and moneylending.

Up-river folk keep river-lore the way coastal folk keep sea-lore: there are stretches of water that boat-crews will not tie up in overnight, and everyone has an opinion about which.

**Who holds it:** nobody entirely. A **petty river-lord** taxes the Nine Locks from a hall-town on the bluffs; the **Cinder Ledger's** inland directorate works the debt side from Marrowfen Stair; independent barge-captains thread between them; and various lesser river-lords toll what they can hold.

## Settlements

- **Marrowfen Stair** — the corridor's hub: a lock-town of wharves, warehouses, and toll-houses.
- **Lord's Wend** — a petty lord's hall-town on the bluffs above the river; the corridor's river-gate.
- **Cresswater** — a barge-village on the fen margin, known for a free ferry that the toll-lords would dearly like to tax.

## Wilderness Zones

Little of the region is truly wild — the danger here is water, works, and men.

- **The drowned reaches** — flooded Concord lock-works and causeway stretches; still, dark water and unstable stone.
- **The Marrowfen edge** — sink-fen running southwest toward the boglands; boggy, trackless, and best skirted.
- **The Wend uplands** — hunting-wood and vineyard slopes above the northeast bank.

## Travel Routes

The **Verdance Road** runs the corridor as a towpath-and-river combination: Caradril to the Glassmere League takes roughly twelve to sixteen days by road, or about two weeks by barge "if the locks behave and the lord's paid."

An overland bluff road parallels the river — slower, but toll-free. A branch track runs to Cresswater and its ferry. Southeast the corridor delivers travelers to the Glassmere League; the Marrowdowns lie south.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Echo Realm**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Settled, busy, and mostly safe — the corridor is the gentle introduction to inland Orrun. The realistic risks are river-pirates (who prefer a toll to a killing), floods and currents, lock accidents, and being caught on the wrong side of somebody's debt.

Off the water, the drowned reaches are the one genuinely uninviting terrain: cold, still, structurally unsound, and avoided by every barge-crew who can afford the extra hour.
