## One-Sentence Identity

A mellow southern breadbasket of cider-orchards, hedged fields, and devout shrine-towns — the gentlest and best-fed country on the northwestern frontier.

## Geography

South of the fens the ground rises, firms, and warms into rolling farmland and low green hills. Hedgerows, drystone walls, water-meadows, cider- and nut-orchards, and villages with real bell-towers. Squat **Concord field-shrines** stand in the middle of ploughed land, worked around for a century because nobody will move them.

Defining features:

- **The Ammet** — the Vale's broad, slow river, rising toward the forest country east and running down through the farmland.
- **Orchardmere lake** — the lake the Vale's chief town sits on; fished, boated, and pretty.
- **Saint Veddow's Rest** — a hill crowned by the Vale's famous pilgrimage shrine and its tomb, drawing pilgrims from as far as the city.
- **The South Road** — a well-kept Concord causeway running the length of the Vale and on toward the southern downs.
- **Orchard-buried ruins** — Concord stonework that trees and crops have swallowed; common enough that farmers use it as gateposts.

**Borders:** Sundering Reach (north), Tollwood (east, across the Ammet headwaters), the city-state of Caradril and its hinterland (southeast), open downs and unbuilt country (west and southwest).

## Climate

Temperate and kind by frontier standards: true summers, mild winters, and a long golden autumn. Harvest is the year's great event, followed by cider-pressing and then the funeral season the Vale calls the dead-time — the weeks when the year's dead are properly seen off with a great deal of ceremony and rather more drinking.

## Culture And Livelihood

The Vale came through the Quietfall with its farms and its faith more or less intact, and it has never entirely stopped being pleased about that. Folk here are devout, proud, prosperous, tidy, and a little smug. They keep the death-rites the old way, they keep them well, and they will tell you so.

Common trades: grain, orchards and cider, nuts, wool and cheese, milling, and the pilgrim trade — inns, hospices, relic-sellers, and shrine-offerings around Saint Veddow's Rest, which is the Vale's second-largest industry after food.

A real social fault line runs through the Vale: the shrine-towns grow rich on pilgrim coin while the working farm-villages carry the debt. This surfaces at every moot as arguments about tithes, road upkeep, and whose turn it is to pay for what.

**Who holds it:** nominally the Reachward Compact to the north; in practice the Vale governs itself through **harvest-moots** — seasonal assemblies of village heads — and through the considerable authority of its shrine-keepers.

## Settlements

- **Orchardmere** — the Vale's hub: an orchard-and-cider town of ~1,400 on its lake; seat of the harvest-moot.
- **Saint Veddow's Rest** — a small, intensely devout pilgrimage town wrapped around the saint's hill-tomb; inns, hospices, and relic-stalls.
- **Tilbrook** — a working farm-and-mill village on the Ammet; plainer, poorer, and resentful of shrine-town wealth.
- **Nettlecombe** — an orchard hamlet grown up around a field-shrine.
- **Marrow Cross** — a crossroads hamlet with a gibbet; the Vale's least pleasant address.
- **Pellow Grange** — a large mortgaged farm-estate, more works than village.

## Wilderness Zones

The Vale barely has wilderness — the danger here is social, not predatory.

- **Saint Veddow's hill and the pilgrim road** — a climbing track, thick with foot traffic in season.
- **The orchard ruins** — Concord stonework buried under a century of trees and crops; picturesque and structurally unsound.
- **The Ammet water-meadows** — seasonal flood-grazing, mill-races, and weirs; drowning ground when the river is up.
- **The southern downs** — open sheep-country rolling away toward the city, dotted with old barrows the shepherds leave strictly alone.

## Travel Routes

The **South Road** (Concord causeway) runs from the fen edge at Candlewick through Tilbrook to Orchardmere — about three to four days — and on southeast toward Caradril, another five or six. A pilgrim-track climbs from Orchardmere to Saint Veddow's Rest in half a day.

These are the safest roads on the frontier: maintained, patrolled by moot-appointed wardens, and busy with carts, drovers, and pilgrims.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Waning**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Pastoral, warm, and outwardly untroubled. This is the region a traveler goes to in order to sleep somewhere with a roof and eat something that isn't peat-smoked.

Real risks are mundane: mill-race and weir drownings, foul air in a cider-cellar, an orchard ruin collapsing under someone's weight, a crush in a packed pilgrim crowd. The greater dangers are social — a shrine-town can close its doors to you, and the harvest-moot's disapproval carries further than any writ. Marrow Cross, where the Vale does its hanging, is the one spot with an actively bad reputation after dark.
