## One-Sentence Identity

A dying inland salt sea ringed by clan holds and bone-white flats, where the receding water uncovers whole drowned towns older than any road the Concord ever built.

## Geography

The **Saltmere** is a vast brackish inland sea that has been going down for generations. Its shrinking deep water lies northwest; around it runs a widening belt of exposed, crusted, salt-white ground where the shoreline used to be.

Defining features:

- **The retreat-zone** — the newly uncovered shore, where ruins, wharves, and whole street-plans stand up out of the brine as the water withdraws.
- **The Drowned Towns** — the largest of those emerging ruins: a cluster of stone towns, salt-glazed and structurally treacherous, that predate everything the clans remember building.
- **The Bonepan Flats** — cracked white salt-pan badland east and south; glare, mirage, thirst, and no shade whatsoever.
- **The Ghostmark foothills** — low eroded mountains to the north, holding crumbling Concord-era waystations and the passes toward the central ruins.
- **Barge-lanes** — the clans' navigable channels across the remaining deep water, which shift as the sea falls.

**Borders:** the Ghostmark Range and the central interior (north), the Marrowdowns (west), the Cindern country and volcanic highlands (east), the southern warm plains (south).

## Climate

Hot, dry, and brutally bright. The salt reflects the sun; unprotected eyes suffer within hours. Rain is scarce and mostly evaporates before it matters. Brine-squalls come off the remaining water without much warning and coat everything in stinging salt.

## Culture And Livelihood

The clans are a proud, elegiac people watching their world literally recede. Their culture is oriented around endurance and remembrance: songs, genealogies, and the formal marking of what the water has taken this year.

Common trades: salt harvesting from the pans, brine-fishing, barge-freight along the shrinking lanes, hide and wool from hardy stock, caravan work across the Flats, and salvage from the emerging ruins — which is lucrative, contested, and considered disrespectful by a good many people.

The **going-down song** is the region's signature custom: a long sung account of the sea's retreat, added to each year, performed at gatherings. Clan singers hold that its oldest verses record a much older catastrophe than anyone can now date.

**Who holds it:** a loose **salt-clan confederacy**, presided over by a matriarch at the largest surviving hold, with authority that frays a little more with every hold that dies. Outside money — chiefly southern merchant-banking — circles the salt trade with offers to "save" an economy it would then own.

## Settlements

- **Brackhold** — the largest surviving salt-clan hold; harbor-mole, pans, and the confederacy's seat.
- **Saltcairn** — a clan-town on the Ghostmark edge; terminus of the caravan track across the Flats.
- **The Drowned Towns** — not a settlement: a pre-Concord ruin-cluster, uninhabited, and increasingly accessible as the water falls.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The Bonepan Flats** — the region's environmental killer. Glare, mirage, and thirst; crossings require water discipline and, sensibly, a caravan master who has done it before.
- **The receding shore** — unstable salt crust, emerging ruins, and footing that gives way.
- **The deep Saltmere** — brine-squalls and shifting barge-lanes; a pilot is required.
- **The Ghostmark foothills** — rockfall, crumbling waystations, and cold nights.

## Travel Routes

- **The Saltmere Shore Road** — from the chief hold past the Drowned Towns to the Ghostmark-edge town; three to five days.
- **The Ghostmark Pass** north toward the central ruin country; weeks, and poorly kept.
- **The Bonepan Track** southeast toward the southern ports; four to six days, dry-season only, water carried in.
- **The Salt Road** arriving from the chalk downs to the west.
- **Clan barge-lanes** across the remaining water; pilot required, and the pilots' knowledge expires as the sea falls.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Pall**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Elegiac and hard. Ashore among the holds a traveler is safe enough — hospitality is real and clan feuds are not usually a visitor's problem. Out on the Flats or among the emerging ruins the region becomes genuinely lethal, and it announces this clearly: the clans refuse to go certain places, caravan masters enforce water rules, and the salt-white shapes standing at the edge of the ruins are visible from a long way off.

Standing hazards: heat and thirst, glare-blindness, mirage, brine-squalls, unstable salt crust, and whatever the receding water has been keeping.
