## AI Use

Load when travel reaches the great river-cities of central Orrun. This is a city-world: geography here is mostly urban and riverine, and the culture section matters more than the terrain.

## One-Sentence Identity

A confederacy of free river-cities grown rich on the Glasswater — banks, guilds, exchanges, and old money — and the closest thing Orrun has to a genuine urban heartland.

## Geography

The **Glasswater valley**, the continent's main commercial artery, running southeast toward the far coast. Prosperous terraced country up-river, rich orchard-plain down-river, and a spreading web of Concord-built canals feeding the exchange-wharves. The land is thoroughly worked: locks, cuts, embankments, mills, and towpaths everywhere.

The city of Glassmere straddles the river at a broad reach spanned by three great bridges — the feature that gives its banking quarter its name.

**Borders:** the Verdance Reaches (northwest, up-corridor from Caradril), the Greatspine cordillera and the Concord Heartlands (northeast), the Marrowdowns and Saltmere country (south and southwest), the Glasswater running southeast toward the Hollow Gulf.

## Climate

Mild and generous: warm summers, damp winters, a long growing season. The river fogs in autumn and floods in spring, and the league's engineers have spent a century arguing about both.

## Culture And Livelihood

This is the wealthiest, most literate, most self-satisfied society on the continent. League citizens regard the frontier the way city people everywhere regard the countryside — as a source of raw materials and rustic anecdotes. News from the north takes weeks to arrive and is generally discounted on the grounds that the north always complains.

Common trades: banking and credit, insurance and bottomry, wholesale trade, shipping, guild manufacture (glass, cloth, paper, metalwork), law, scholarship, and information.

Culture is guild-shaped. Standing depends on your house, your charter, and your credit; a merchant's word is a legal instrument and everyone behaves accordingly. Information itself is a commodity here, openly bought and sold by brokers who advertise the fact.

**Who holds it:** the league — a confederacy of free cities that governs by negotiation between city councils, great banking houses, and guild charters. A **League-Factor** speaks for its collective trade; the great merchant-banks are as powerful as any council; and the whole arrangement runs on the assumption that nobody wants a war badly enough to pay for one.

## Settlements

- **Glassmere** — the league capital and the greatest river-city on the continent. Its notable quarters include **the Floor** (the merchant-exchange), **the Three Bridges** (banking), **the Reliquary** (a great scholarly chapter-house and relic collection), and **Lowwater** (the poor riverside district).
- **Sennfort** — a smaller league-city up-river.
- **Cairnwater** — a down-river league-city; head of the great southbound river run.

## Wilderness Zones

Effectively none. This is canal-country and settled terrace. What passes for dangerous ground here is institutional: vaults, archives, private wards, closed guildhalls, and the river itself in flood.

## Travel Routes

- **The Verdance Road** in from Caradril and the northwest — roughly twelve to sixteen days.
- **The Glasswater Run** south to the Hollow Gulf ports — three to four weeks by river; the continent's main commercial artery, tolled at every opportunity.
- **The Crown Road** northeast into the Greatspine passes and the Concord Heartlands — well-traveled at this end, and contested past it.
- **The Salt Road** brushing south toward the downs and the inland salt sea.

See [`../TRAVEL_AND_ROUTES.md`](../travel/travel-and-routes.md).

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Echo Realm**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Physically safe, socially treacherous. Violence here is a failure of process; the league prefers lawsuits, embargoes, credit freezes, and the quiet ruin of a rival house. A visitor is far more likely to be sued, tailed, or priced out than attacked.

The real risks are getting caught somewhere you have no charter to be, ending up in debt to the wrong house, or discovering that something you said in confidence has already been sold twice.
