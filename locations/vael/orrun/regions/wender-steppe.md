## AI Use

Load for the roadless northern grasslands. This region has no towns, no roads, and no permanent geography beyond water and horizon — travel and hospitality customs are the whole of it.

## One-Sentence Identity

A cold, open grass-sea ridden by free horse-clans the Concord never governed, where there are no roads, no crowns, and no shelter but the one another traveler offers.

## Geography

An enormous rolling grassland between the northern mountain wall and the central cordillera. No forests worth the name, no roads, and few permanent landmarks. Navigation is by horizon-features, watercourses, and the position of the stars.

Defining features:

- **The long grass** — the trackless open steppe that makes up most of the region.
- **The Cold Springs winter-basin** — sheltered low ground with reliable water, where the clans gather for the cold season.
- **The Sky-Stones** — an ancient upland shrine-circle in the northwest; the steppe's own holy site, raised by no one anybody can name and entirely independent of the Concord.
- **The Spine-Foot margin** — where the grass meets the foot of the central mountains in the south; the neutral ground where steppe and settled worlds trade.
- **The far steppe** — the deep grass east and north, thinly ridden and effectively lawless.

No major river crosses it. Water comes from seasonal melt, springs, and dowsed water-holes whose locations are clan knowledge.

**Borders:** the Highmark Spine and its passes (west), the Karran Teeth and the northeastern mining country (east), the central cordillera (south), the far north (beyond).

## Climate

Cold and continental: brutal winters with wind that kills, short violent springs, hot dry summers, and almost no shelter anywhere. Wind is constant. Blizzards in winter and grass-fires in late summer are both routine and both lethal.

## Culture And Livelihood

The clans migrate with the grass, wintering together and dispersing in summer. Wealth is horses; status is kin. There is no writing to speak of and no fixed property, and clan society is organized around two absolute customs:

- **Guest-law** — a guest under a clan's roof-felt is inviolable, must be fed, and must be seen safely on their way. Breaking guest-law, in either direction, is the worst thing a person can do here.
- **Kin-law** — a formal system of obligation, feud, marriage, and compensation between clans that regulates almost everything else.

Common trades: horses above all, plus herds, hides, felt, dairy, dried meat, horn and sinew work, and trading at the Spine-Foot meet for metal, grain, salt, and cloth.

Clan seers keep the vigil at the Sky-Stones and read the sky as a matter of course. Steppe death-ways are sky-rites — the dead are given to the open — and have nothing to do with the Concord's practices, of which the clans have a low opinion and less experience.

**Who holds it:** a **clan confederacy** under an elected Speaker, in effect for as long as the clans agree. Kin-mothers hold parallel authority over kin-law. A raid-lord in the far grass rides against the confederacy's restraint and takes what guest-law would otherwise have given freely. No southern power holds ground here at all.

## Settlements

- **The Winter-Camp at Cold Springs** — the great cold-season gathering; the closest thing to a capital, for about three months a year.
- **The Spine-Foot trade-meet** — the seasonal neutral ground where the steppe and the settled south do business.
- **The Summer-Riding** — the clan-host itself, on the move: a settlement with hooves.

## Wilderness Zones

Effectively everything.

- **The long grass** — the region's chief hazard; without a clan guide, distance and water will do the work of any monster.
- **The far steppe** — raider country.
- **The Sky-Stones rise** — holy ground; seer-permission is expected before approaching.

## Travel Routes

- **Steppe tracks** — migration and trade lines, seasonal, unmarked, and ungoverned. They exist in the way footpaths exist: as knowledge, not construction.
- **The Summer-Riding's circuit** — the clan-host's annual round; travelers often move with it because it is safer than not.
- **The Spine-Foot trade-road** south into the settled lands.

The Highmark Passes lie west; the Karran Marches east.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Waning**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Low danger under guest-law; high without it. The steppe telegraphs everything: break no law, refuse no host, and the land's own dangers announce themselves from the horizon hours before they arrive.

Standing hazards: cold and wind, distance, water, blizzard and grass-fire, mounted raiders, and the simple fact that there is nowhere to shelter and nobody to help. Almost nothing here is a dungeon; nearly all of it is survival and hospitality.
