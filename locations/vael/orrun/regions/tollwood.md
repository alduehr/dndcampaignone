## One-Sentence Identity

A vast, ancient forest of oak, ash, and black pine, threaded by crumbling Concord toll-roads, where everyone lives by the road and everyone pays a toll to somebody.

## Geography

East of the fens the ground dries and rises into old-growth forest so deep that the Concord's great toll-causeways vanish under leaf-mould and moss for miles at a stretch. The Tollwood is the northwestern finger of the far larger **Hethewood** to the east, and shares its character: huge trees, bramble-choked ravines, cold streams, and very little sky.

Defining features:

- **The East Road** — the great Concord toll-causeway running east through the forest toward Caradril; the region's spine and lifeline.
- **The ruined toll-stations** — squat Concord structures set at intervals along the road, most collapsed, some occupied.
- **The Old Mast** — the deepest, oldest stand at the forest's heart. Locals do not go there and do not discuss it at length.
- **Toll-shrines** — offering-stones at forest crossings, far older than the Concord's roads. Travelers leave something small; woodfolk consider this non-optional.
- **Charcoal-clearings and coppice** — the human footprint: burn-mounds, cut-over patches, and hog-pannage clearings.

**Borders:** Sundering Reach (west, across the Mirewend at Kettle Bridge), Ashgarden Vale (south, across the Ammet headwaters), Caradril's hinterland (southeast), unmapped deeper forest and hills (north and east).

## Climate

Cool, damp, and deep-shaded. Very little wind reaches the forest floor; mist collects in the hollows and stays. The canopy swallows weather and sound alike, which makes the wood disorienting even in fair conditions. Autumn is leaf-fall and charcoal season, and the most confusing time of year to be off the road.

## Culture And Livelihood

People live in clearings: woodcutters, charcoal-burners, pannage-swineherds, and a few road-towns living off the through-trade. Woodfolk are insular, practical, and extremely firm about local custom.

Common trades: timber, charcoal, hogs, forest game, resin and bark, road-carting, and the toll-trade itself.

The **forest-rules** are the region's defining culture and are taught to children before letters: keep to the road, don't travel off it after dark, leave an offering at the toll-shrines, and never cut a marked tree. Outsiders who laugh at this are, in local opinion, the reason so many outsiders don't come back. Nobody in the Tollwood explains the rules; they simply expect them kept.

**Who holds it:** nobody, really. The Reachward Compact claims the region and fields a thin road-watch. Southern trading interests run the logging and charcoal concerns. In practice the wood governs itself road-town by road-town — and the bandits who levy their own toll at Tollstone Cross are as much a fixture of the road as the weather.

## Settlements

- **Hartfell** — the region's hub: a stockaded road-town of ~900 on the East Road, living off the through-trade, the toll, and timber. The only place in the wood that feels like a town.
- **Coldhearth** — a charcoal-burners' clearing-village deeper in; half-feral, devout to forest custom, and the best place to hire a guide.
- **Tollstone Cross** — a road-hamlet at a ruined Concord toll-station, where the East Road's bandits collect. Contested, unpleasant, and unavoidable.
- **Woodcutter camps** — seasonal, mobile, and scattered along the cutting lines.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The East Road and its toll-stations** — the safe thread and everything hanging off it.
- **The Coppice Shrines** — offering-stones at the forest crossings; the woodfolk's ritual geography.
- **The Green Mile** — a long stretch of causeway that has sunk into standing water; passable, slowly, and unpleasantly.
- **The Hanging Oaks** — an old grove with a grim local reputation and a lot of very old rope.
- **The Old Mast** — the deep wood. Marked at its edge by the woodfolk and left alone; the one place in the region even the loggers won't work.

## Travel Routes

The **East Road** runs Kettle Bridge → Tollstone Cross → Hartfell, about four days, and continues another five or six to Caradril. It is the only sane travel in the region.

Off-road forest travel doubles or triples the time, courts getting badly lost, and brings a traveler into country the locals avoid. Guides can be hired at Coldhearth and Hartfell and are worth the money.

Standing road hazards: the bandit-toll at Tollstone Cross (usually negotiable), the drowned stretch of the Green Mile, and washouts after rain.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Pall**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../timelines/the-last-telling.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Dark, close, and watchful — the most dangerous of the northwestern frontier's neighboring regions, and the one that most rewards respect. The wood itself is the main antagonist: getting lost, canopy-dark, silence that eats direction.

Real threats include road-bandits, genuine apex predators the fen country lacks (dire wolves, bear, boar), forest-cold at night, bramble ravines, and the drowned road. The deep wood past the marked edge is treated by every local as simply off-limits, and travelers who ask why get short answers.
