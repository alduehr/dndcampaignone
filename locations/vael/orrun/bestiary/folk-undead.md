## AI Use

Everywhere in Orrun, ordinary people know one thing about death: **sometimes the dead do not finish leaving.** Not often. Not most of them. But enough that every village has a custom about it, every road has a story, and no one is truly astonished to meet one.

This file gives the **generic lingering-dead encounter** for use anywhere in the world — a lonely, sorrowful thing far more often than a monster, usually resolvable by talking to it or by performing the local rite. It is written entirely from **public folk belief**. It is a roleplay encounter with mechanics attached, not a mystery to be solved.

> ### What This File Deliberately Does Not Explain
>
> **Why some dead linger is not answered here, and no NPC in this file knows.** Priests, grave-keepers, hedge-witches, and scholars all have theories; they contradict each other; none is confirmed, and folk generally shrug and get on with the rite that works. If a specific adventure wants a cause, that adventure supplies it. Run these encounters as **grief with a shape**, never as a breadcrumb pointing somewhere.

## Vocabulary

Common folk word: **a Remembrance** — "there's a Remembrance on the old ford." Regional variants: *a lingerer*, *one who's still walking*, *a stayer*, *a slow-goer*, *the wet ones* (coast), *the still-standing* (downs). All mean the same thing and all are ordinary, unremarkable speech.

## What Folk Believe (d6 — none authoritative, all commonly held)

| d6 | The explanation you'll hear |
|---|---|
| 1 | They had something unfinished, and unfinished things hold. |
| 2 | The burial was done wrong, or done in a hurry, or done by strangers. |
| 3 | They died far from home and simply do not know the way. |
| 4 | Nobody said their name at the end, so nothing called them onward. |
| 5 | Someone loved them too loudly and would not let go. |
| 6 | The door was busy that night. Some wait in the queue. |

Different tellers will give a different answer with total confidence, and each will say the others are ignorant. **The AI DM should never adjudicate which is right.**

## Signs (always telegraph before an encounter)

- Cold that has nothing to do with the weather, in one particular spot.
- Lamps, lanterns, and candles guttering low and not going out.
- An animal that will not go forward — the dog stops, the horse balks, the birds are wrong.
- Something repeated: a footstep with no second footstep, a door, a bell, a name.
- Ordinary people's behavior: offerings on a stone, a bowl left out, a route everyone takes the long way around, a bridge nobody crosses after dark.
- A local telling you plainly, without drama, that "there's one at the ford — just say good evening and keep walking."

## 1. A Quiet One (the standard encounter)

*Found: on roads, at fords, in graveyards, at doorways, at the tide-line, on the ground where they died.* The overwhelming majority of lingering dead. A person who stopped. **Not hostile. Not a threat unless made into one.**

- **Type/Size:** Undead, Medium (usually faint, sometimes solid enough to mistake for a living traveler at dusk). **Role:** roleplay encounter / atmosphere. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/4–1 (rarely fought).
- **Baseline:** use a **Specter (2024 MM)** profile, modified: **hostility removed by default**, and it has speech.
- **AC** 12 · **HP** 17–22 · **Speed** hover 30 ft.; passes through what it remembers as open and is stopped by what it remembers as shut.
- **Saves/Skills:** Wis-leaning; nothing notable.
- **Resist/Immune:** resistant to nonmagical physical damage; immune to poison and exhaustion; the usual incorporeal-undead condition immunities.
- **Senses:** sees the living clearly; often does not see the *place* as it currently is. **Languages:** whatever they spoke alive; speech is slow, repetitive, and stuck on a small number of subjects.
- **Actions:** *Chill Touch* (1d6+2 necrotic) — **only if attacked, robbed, or if its grave or body is disturbed.** Many never attack at all, even then.
- **Traits:** *Fixed In Place* (it will not leave the ground it is bound to — a stretch of road, a house, a shore; walking away always ends the encounter); *Repeats* (it says or does the same handful of things; the variation is the clue to what it wants); *Fades* (cannot be permanently destroyed by violence in any way that satisfies anyone — beaten down, it is simply gone until the next night, which locals will confirm with weary patience).
- **Tactics:** none. It is doing what it does. It reacts to interference, not to presence.
- **Morale:** n/a. It does not fight for a goal it can articulate.
- **Non-combat outs (the intended play):**
  - **Just pass by.** Courtesy costs nothing; most travelers greet it and walk on.
  - **Talk to it** (Insight or Persuasion **DC 12**) — learn what it repeats and why.
  - **Perform the local rite** (Religion **DC 13**, or **DC 10** if a local taught you the words this week) — see the rites below.
  - **Do the small thing it wants** (see the want table) — always the most satisfying resolution and worth a milestone's worth of credit.
- **Solo danger:** **low**, and it should be described so that this is obvious. The danger of a Quiet One is emotional, or it is the danger of what a frightened traveler does about it.
- **Scaling:** levels 1–5 as written. At higher levels the profile does not change — laying it becomes easy, and the encounter becomes purely a scene. Never inflate a Quiet One into a boss.

### What This One Wants (d8)

| d8 | The unfinished thing |
|---|---|
| 1 | To be told they are dead. Nobody has said it plainly and they will believe it from a stranger. |
| 2 | A name — theirs, said aloud, correctly, by someone who is not guessing. |
| 3 | To get home. Home is a half-day away and someone must simply walk with them. |
| 4 | To give something back: a borrowed tool, a coin, a debt, an object in a pocket. |
| 5 | To warn about the thing that killed them — a bad ford, a rotten beam, a person. |
| 6 | To know whether someone survived. The answer is findable in the nearest village. |
| 7 | To be buried, or reburied, or found at all — the body is nearby and nobody looked. |
| 8 | Nothing you can give. They are waiting for someone who is not coming. Only the rite, or company. |

## 2. One That Has Turned (uncommon)

*Found: at robbed graves, at sites of a killing, where a rite was refused or interrupted, where the dead have been disturbed for profit.* Locals talk about these in a different tone. A Remembrance that has gone bad **is understood to have been made that way** — usually by the living, usually recently, and usually by someone who did something greedy or cruel.

- **Type/Size:** Undead, Medium. **Role:** genuine threat / consequence of somebody's bad act. **CR-equivalent:** ~3–5 (scales).
- **Baseline:** use a **Specter (2024 MM)** upgraded toward a **Ghost (2024 MM, CR 4)**, or a **Wight (2024 MM, CR 3)** where the dead is corporeal and grave-bound. A wailing one at a great grief-site can be run as a **Banshee (2024 MM, CR 4)**.
- **AC** 13 · **HP** 36–52 · **Speed** hover 40 ft.
- **Saves/Skills:** Wis-leaning; Stealth.
- **Resist/Immune:** as the chosen baseline — resistant to nonmagical physical; immune to poison, exhaustion, and the usual conditions.
- **Senses:** darkvision. **Languages:** understands; rarely answers, and does not converse the way a Quiet One will.
- **Actions:** *Withering Touch* (~2d6 necrotic); one signature effect from the baseline — a fear aura, a wail, or a life-drain. Use **one**, not three.
- **Traits:** *Still Bound* (it cannot leave its ground; **retreat is always an out**, and everyone local knows exactly where the boundary is); *Telegraphed Hard* (cold, dead lights, animals refusing, and at least one villager warning you); *Made, Not Born* (there is always a discoverable reason — a robbed grave, a hidden body, a broken promise, a rite denied — and fixing it is the real solution).
- **Tactics:** drives intruders off its ground. It pursues to the edge of what it holds and no further. It attacks the disturbance first — the grave-robber, the digger, the one holding the stolen thing.
- **Morale:** does not flee, but **stops** the moment its ground is left or its grievance is addressed.
- **Non-combat outs:** put right what was done (return the taken thing, rebury the body, confess, pay the debt — Investigation/Insight **DC 14** to find out what it was) · a full rite by someone who knows it (Religion **DC 15**, or **DC 13** with a village elder, grave-keeper, or priest assisting) · give the ground back to it and go around.
- **Solo danger:** **moderate to high** for a lone traveler at levels 3–6 — but it is bound, telegraphed, and has two non-violent solutions. Never place one without a warning and a clear boundary line.
- **Scaling:** L3–4: Specter-tier, ~36 HP, no signature effect. L5–6: Ghost/Wight-tier as written. L7–8: Banshee-tier with one area effect, at a genuinely notorious site. Beyond that, use the region's own material.

## 3. The Folk Rites (how ordinary people handle this)

Every region has its own version. All of them work; none of them is theologically agreed. A traveler can learn one in an evening from any grave-keeper, elder, priest, or old woman who is willing (**Persuasion DC 10**, usually free with a drink and some respect).

| Rite | Where it's used | How it's done | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The naming** | Everywhere | Say their name aloud, three times, and then say who you are | Religion DC 13 (DC 10 if you know the name) |
| **Walking them home** | Roads, inland | Walk the route they were taking, at their pace, without turning back | Wisdom or Religion DC 12, plus the hours it takes |
| **The returned thing** | Everywhere | Put back what was taken, or carry it to whoever it belonged to | No check — an errand, and the best resolution |
| **Salt and tide** | Coast, islands | Salt, and words, at the water's edge on a falling tide | Religion DC 13 (DC 15 at the wrong tide) |
| **The lit lamp** | Fens, crossroads | A light left burning where they stand, until it goes out on its own | Religion DC 12; costs a lamp and a night |
| **The stone laid** | Downs, uplands | Add a stone to their cairn and say where you're going | Religion DC 10 — the easiest and least reliable |
| **Sitting with them** | Everywhere | Do nothing. Stay until morning. Say ordinary things. | No check; costs a long rest's benefit; **works surprisingly often** |

**A failed rite is not a disaster.** It costs the materials, an hour, and the dignity of the attempt; the dead is unchanged, and it can be tried again, or a better-informed local can be fetched. **Never make a failed Religion check turn a Quiet One hostile** — only the living do that.

## What Turns A Quiet One Dangerous

Say these aloud at the table so the choice is visible:

1. Robbing the body, the grave, or the offerings.
2. Attacking it.
3. Taking the thing it is waiting for and walking off with it.
4. Interrupting a rite that someone else is performing.
5. Lying to it about the thing it asked about, when it can tell.

Everything else — talking, refusing, ignoring, walking past, failing the rite, being frightened — is safe.

## Aftermath And Rewards

- **A laid Remembrance leaves something behind**, almost always small and almost always meaningful: what it carried, what it was bringing home, a coin, a ring, a letter, a name that means something in the next village.
- **Local standing is the real reward.** Laying the thing on the ford is worth more in a village than gold: free lodging, a guide, a favor, the truth about a neighbor.
- **A word of caution is worth as much.** Travelers who can say "there's one at the third milestone, greet it and keep walking" are welcome everywhere.
- **A turned one, put right, usually exposes whoever made it** — the grave-robber, the killer, the mill-owner who hid a body. That is a hook, and it belongs to whatever adventure is running.

## Solo-Play Notes

- The default lingering-dead encounter is **not a fight**, and the description should make that unmistakable within the first two sentences.
- A lone traveler should always be able to **walk away**. These things are bound to their ground, without exception.
- Never chain multiple hauntings against one PC in a single night. One is a scene; three is a slog.
- If the players want it to be a fight, let it be one — but a beaten Quiet One is simply gone until tomorrow, and someone local will say so, tiredly, when asked.
