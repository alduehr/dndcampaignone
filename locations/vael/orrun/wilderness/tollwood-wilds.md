## AI Use

Load when anyone travels the Tollwood's roads, camps, or deep stands. This file covers the forest as a **physical place**: the terrain, the named sites along and off the East Road, its hazards with DCs, and what lives in it. The Tollwood is survival-first country — the road is a thin safe thread and everything gets worse the further you get from it. Nothing here depends on a particular plot.

## One-Sentence Identity

A vast old-growth forest where the Concord's dead-straight roads run through trees that never agreed to them, and the danger rises with every mile from the causeway.

## What A Traveler Notices First

How *old* it is: oaks like towers, no undergrowth at all in the deep stands, just brown shade going back forever in every direction. How the Concord road — so confident out on the fens — here runs cracked and moss-covered and *bends*, more than once, around stretches of wood it plainly chose not to cross. The offering-marks cut into the trees at every crossing. And, the moment you step off the road, how quickly you lose track of which way you came.

## Terrain

Dry, rising ground under closed canopy. Oak, ash, and black pine, some of it never cut; in the oldest stands the canopy shuts out enough light that the forest floor is bare leaf-mould with no scrub at all, and sightlines run further than expected while telling you nothing useful.

Between the great stands: bramble-choked ravines, cold fast streams, coppice and charcoal-clearings, hog-pannage ground, and blowdown tangles where an old giant has come over and taken half an acre with it.

The land is not the problem — the *disorientation* is. Very little wind reaches the floor, mist collects in the hollows and stays, sound does not carry properly, and there are almost no landmarks that do not look exactly like a thousand others. Travelers do not fall in the Tollwood. They walk in circles until something else happens to them.

**Getting through it:** the East Road causeway, and nothing else. Off-road travel doubles or triples the time and courts getting badly lost. Guides can be hired at the charcoal-burners' village and at the road-town, and are worth whatever they ask. The **forest-rules** are the local safety code and are taught before letters: keep to the road, do not travel off it after dark, leave an offering at the toll-shrines, never cut a marked tree. Woodfolk do not explain the rules; they simply expect them kept, and treat outsiders who laugh as an explanation for why so many outsiders do not come back.

## Named Features

- **The East Road and the toll-stations** — the great Concord toll-causeway and its squat ruined stations at old intervals. The road is the safe thread; the stations are landmarks and shelter, and several have sunk into standing water.
- **The Coppice Shrines** — offering-stones at forest crossings, far older than the roads, kept up by the woodfolk. Leaving something small is customary and non-optional in local opinion. Defiling one is the fastest way to make an entire region unfriendly.
- **The Woodcutter Camps** — seasonal **Cinder Ledger** logging camps that push a little further east each year. Rough shelter, hot food, hard men, and the front line of a long argument between the timber trade and everyone who lives here.
- **The Green Mile** — a long stretch of the East Road causeway sunk and drowned where a forest stream broke through. Bog-flanked, slow, and a place where carts mire and travelers walk.
- **The Withy Bog** — black alder-bog flanking the Green Mile, where the broken causeway runs straight into standing water. Drifting lights after dark, and at least one abandoned cart axle-deep just off the firm line.
- **The Hanging Oaks** — an ancient grove where, by long local account, offerings were hung from the branches — and worse things than offerings. Grim, cold, and full of very old rope. People do meet there; none of them advertise it.
- **The Charcoal Burns** — a smouldering belt of charcoal-clamps and slash where the cutting meets the deep wood. Reek of smoke and sap, blue haze in the trees, stacked cordwood, and crews who go quiet when the wood creaks past the cut.
- **The Mast-Beasts' Range** — the deep reaches where the forest's apex predators den. Kill-sign, scrape-trees, den-stink, and tracks a good deal larger than a wolf's.
- **The Sentinel Oaks** — a double row of vast marked oaks lining a lost track into the deep wood, branches heavy with generations of bound tokens, bones, and ribbons gone grey. The silence between them is absolute. Woodfolk bring respectful travelers here and no further.
- **The Edge of the Old Mast** — the marked boundary of the deepest stand, where the road bends hardest away and the offering-marks grow thick and old. Every local treats what lies past it as simply not a place people go, and gives short answers if pressed.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Getting lost off-road** | DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) per leg; DC 16 in the deep stands | Failure: hours lost and a real chance of being turned completely around. A hired guide, or following a marked shrine-line, removes the check entirely. This is the Tollwood's main killer. |
| **Canopy-dark** | — | Deep stands are night-dark by day: disadvantage on sight-based Perception without light. Carrying a light in the deep wood makes you the most visible thing for a quarter-mile. |
| **The Green Mile / Withy Bog** | DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to hold the firm line; DC 13 Athletics to pull free | Failure: mired, soaked, and possibly missing gear. Carts sometimes cannot be recovered at all. |
| **Drifting lights (Withy Bog)** | DC 13 Wisdom save to disbelieve | Failure draws the traveler off the firm ground into open bog. They behave as though they want to be followed. |
| **Bramble-ravines and blowdown** | DC 13 Strength (Athletics) to cross | Failure: 1d6 and a slow, bloody scramble; blowdown tangles can cost an hour for fifty yards. |
| **Forest-cold night** | DC 12 Constitution save; DC 13 in the deep wood | Failure: 1 level of exhaustion. There is no safe fire deep in — smoke and light both travel. |
| **Charcoal clamps and falling timber** | DC 12 Constitution save in the haze; DC 13 Dexterity save near working timber | Failure: 1d6 burn near a breached clamp, or 1d6 bludgeoning from rolling cordwood. Working sites; tired crews. |
| **Breaking the forest-rules** | DC 13 Wisdom save on defiling a shrine or cutting a marked tree | Failure: shaken and watched. Practically: disadvantage on Stealth and Survival in the wood until amends are made at a shrine, and the woodfolk will know, and will not help you. |
| **Deep-wood dread** (Sentinel Oaks, the marked edge) | DC 14 Wisdom save, cumulative the further in you go | Failure: shaken, then increasingly certain you are not welcome. It is a warning system, and it is correct. |

## Fauna And Encounters

**Mundane wildlife (the common case).** The Tollwood has the apex predators the fen country lacks, and they are the honest danger here.

- **Red and roe deer, boar, and hogs** at pannage — the wood's staple game and the reason half the clearings exist. **Boar-like**, CR 1/4, dangerous when cornered; a **Giant Boar-like** CR 2 old tusker is a genuine fight.
- **Dire wolves** in packs of two to four — **Dire Wolf-like**, CR 1 each. Always telegraphed by tracks, kills, and a guide going still. A pack can overwhelm a lone traveler on open ground; treeing yourself (DC 13 Athletics) is a legitimate and locally-recommended answer.
- **Forest bear** — **Brown Bear-like**, CR 2. Territorial rather than predatory; scrape-trees and den-stink give ample warning.
- **Lynx, pine marten, and wildcat** — no threat to an adult, ruinous to snares and supplies.
- **Boar-hounds and camp dogs** at the cutting camps; the best early-warning system in the region.
- **Ticks, biting flies, and stinging nettles** in summer — **Swarm of Insects-like** at worst, but the tick-fevers are what actually put woodcutters in bed for a week.
- **Ravens and jays** — the jays especially, which shriek at anything moving through the understory and give away every attempt at stealth.

**The lingering dead.** Fewer than in the fens but stranger and older, gathering at the sunk road-stations, in the Withy Bog shallows, and thickly around the Hanging Oaks. Run per [`../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../bestiary/folk-undead.md) — **Specter-like** to **Ghost-like**, AC 12–13, ~22–36 HP, **not hostile by default**, but harder to settle than lowland dead (DC 15 Religion) and some will not be settled at all. Woodfolk deal with them by offering and withdrawal rather than by rite, and consider outsiders who try to lay the deep-wood dead to be meddling.

**Something else, at the deep edge.** Travelers who push past the Sentinel Oaks describe being turned back — not attacked, turned back — by things that come out of the standing timber and do not behave like animals. Statted as **Dryad-like** or **Awakened Tree-like** (AC 13–14, ~36–60 HP), they warn, block, and escort intruders out, and only fight someone determined to make them. **They are handled by respect and retreat, not combat**, and a solo traveler who insists on a fight will lose it. No local will explain further, and none of them go in.

**People.** **Tollmen** — the road-bandits who collect an unofficial toll where the East Road passes a ruined station (**Bandit-like**, **Scout-like**, and a **Veteran-like** leader, AC 12–15); usually negotiable, occasionally not. **Cinder Ledger** loggers and camp enforcers (**Thug-like** / **Scout-like**, AC 12–13). Woodfolk, charcoal-burners, and swineherds, insular but not hostile. **Mourners' Circle** members keeping the crossing-shrines. A thin **Reachward Compact** road-watch that is genuinely trying. Rare **Ashen Wardens**, who find the deep dead difficult and say so. See [`../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../bestiary/human-hazards.md).

## Level Range And Danger

**Level 2–7. Moderate near the road, high in the deep wood, and effectively off-limits past the marked edge.** The Tollwood is the most dangerous of the northwestern regions and the one that most rewards respect. Predators and getting lost account for nearly everything that happens to travelers; the bandits are a tax, not a threat.

**Telegraph it constantly.** The offering-marks, the road's deliberate bends, the rising dread, and the flat refusal of any local guide to go past the marked edge are all built-in warnings, and they should be given early and repeatedly.

**Retreat:** get back to the road and follow it. If the road cannot be found, follow water downhill — every stream here eventually reaches the causeway or the river at the region's western border.

**Resting:** safe in the settlements, at the cutting camps if you are welcome, and at honored crossing-shrines. Not safe in the deep wood, where a fire is a beacon and going without one costs exhaustion.

## Resources

- **Timber, charcoal, and resin** — the region's whole economy, and always hiring.
- **Game, hides, and pannage hogs** — DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) to hunt; the wood is generous if you know it.
- **Deep-wood herbs and fungi** — DC 13 Wisdom (Survival); genuinely potent healer's-kit and antitoxin components, and the reason herbalists risk the off-road stands.
- **Salvage** at the ruined and sunk toll-stations — worked stone, iron fittings, and the occasional Concord relic.
- **Guides** — hireable at the charcoal village and the road-town; the single best purchase a stranger can make here.
- **Local knowledge** — which trees are marked, which crossings want an offering, which stretch of road the Tollmen are working this month. Freely given to anyone polite; withheld from anyone who mocks the forest-rules.
