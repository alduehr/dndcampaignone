## AI Use

Load when anyone travels the Vale's orchard country, pilgrim road, water-meadows, or southern downs. This file covers the Vale's countryside as a **physical place**: the terrain, the named sites between the villages, its hazards with DCs, and what lives there. The Vale is settled, gentle country — the danger here is water, bad footing, and other people. Nothing here depends on a particular plot.

## One-Sentence Identity

Kind, hedged farm-country of cider-orchards and water-meadows, dotted with Concord ruins the ploughs have politely worked around for a century, where almost every real danger involves either a river or a crowd.

## What A Traveler Notices First

Order and plenty, after the fens: red-gold orchard rows, drystone walls, sheep on the downs, a slow bright river with mills on it. Then the odd notes — a carved standing stone that an orchard has grown up around, a field-shrine roofed over and used as a sheep-byre, a gatepost that is clearly a piece of somebody's temple. And at the crossroads, a gibbet, kept in working order.

## Terrain

Rolling, cultivated lowland: hedged fields, orchards, coppice, and drystone-walled pasture on gentle hills, warming and firming as you go south out of the fen country. The Ammet runs broad and slow through the middle of it, braided with mill-leats, weirs, and seasonal flood-meadows.

Footing is good almost everywhere. The exceptions are specific and well known: the water-meadows when the river is up, the mill-races and weirs at any time, and the collapsed cellars and voids under the orchard ruins, which are unmarked and everywhere.

South, the farmland gives out into open chalk downs — big sky, thin turf, sheep, and long views, with old barrows standing on the skyline that the shepherds strictly leave alone.

**Getting through it:** the South Road is a maintained Concord causeway, patrolled by moot-appointed wardens and busy with carts, drovers, and pilgrims; a pilgrim-track climbs from the lake town to the shrine-hill in half a day. Cross-country travel is easy and legal-ish, provided you close gates behind you. Nobody needs a guide in the Vale. Several people will offer to be one anyway, for a price, at the pilgrim town.

## Named Features

- **Nettlecombe** — a tiny orchard hamlet grown up around a squat Concord field-shrine the locals call the Sheep Stone and use as a byre. Cider, apples, and about forty people.
- **The Buried Cloister** — a collapsed Concord wayside cloister sunk under a hillside orchard south of the lake town, half-swallowed by roots. Partly enterable, structurally awful, and being carefully dug by **Concord Remnant** scholars who pay for copies of inscriptions and dislike interruption.
- **The Ammet Water-Meadows** — lush flood-grazing between the mill village and the lake town, threaded with weirs, leats, mill-pools, and pilgrim ferries. Beautiful, productive, and the Vale's most reliable place to drown.
- **Marrow Cross** — a bleak crossroads hamlet where the Vale does its hanging, gibbet and all. Crows, chains, a permanent cold in the air, and a reputation nobody in the Vale disputes.
- **Pellow Grange** — a large mortgaged farm-estate, more works than village, sitting on top of an old field-shrine. Foreclosed and now run as a quarry-and-salvage operation on **Cinder Ledger** money, worked mostly after dark, with hard men on the gate.
- **The Southern Downs** — open sheep-country rolling away toward the city, crossed by the South Road, dotted with pre-Concord barrows and drove-tracks.
- **The Cider-Wake Orchard** — an old cider-orchard on the lake town's outskirts where the Vale holds its autumn wake for the year's dead: lantern-strung rows, trestle tables, and an enormous quantity of drink. There is one row the older folk will not walk down, and they will not say why.
- **The Drowned Lane** — a low stretch of the South Road causeway that floods each autumn at an old ferry crossing. Broken ferry-posts, votive ribbons on a leaning waystone, and carters arguing about whether to risk it or go the long way round.
- **The Beacon Tump** — a turf-covered pre-Concord hill-fort on the downs, ringed with banks and ditches and crowned with weathered stones. Older than anything else in the Vale. The shepherds fold sheep in it and pull out any survey-stake anyone drives into it.
- **Saint Veddow's Spring** — the famed healing spring in a walled court below the shrine-hill at Saint Veddow's Rest, hung with crutches and votive tokens, packed with pilgrims in season, with a relic-stall trade around it. The water has turned cold and metallic these last seasons and the cures have stopped; the shrine-keepers would prefer you did not say so loudly.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Weir and mill-race** | DC 13 Strength (Athletics) to cross or to get clear of the pull | Failure: swept, 1d6, and dragged under — DC 13 Athletics to surface, or a rope or a bystander ends it at once. Telegraphed by the roar. The single most common fatal accident in the Vale. |
| **Flood-ford (Drowned Lane, Ammet crossings)** | DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to judge, DC 13 Athletics to cross | Failure: swept 1d4 × 30 ft. and soaked; carts mire and may be lost. The dry way round always exists and always costs half a day. |
| **Collapsing orchard ruin** | DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) to spot rotten timber or undercut floor; DC 13 Dexterity save to avoid | Failure: a fall of 1d6 into a cellar or void, climbing out at DC 10 Athletics. The ruins are everywhere and none of them are shored. |
| **Cider-cellar and press-pit foul air** | DC 12 Constitution save | Failure: dizziness and disadvantage on checks until fresh air. Telegraphed by a guttering candle — every cellar-hand carries one. |
| **Pilgrim crush** (holy well, shrine-hill, feast days) | DC 12 Strength (Athletics) to hold your footing or push clear | Failure: knocked down, trampled for 1d4, and separated from anyone you came with. Cutpurses work the crush deliberately. |
| **Gibbet-cold** (Marrow Cross) | DC 13 Wisdom save for anyone who mocks the place or disturbs the gibbet | Failure: shaken; disadvantage on the next saving throw against fear. Cold, even in sun. Locals are not superstitious about this so much as resigned. |
| **The spring-water** | DC 13 Constitution save after drinking deep at Saint Veddow's Spring | Failure: a lingering chill and unease for a day or so — reversible, and lifted by a proper rite or a night's rest at a tended shrine. The metallic taste is the warning. |

## Fauna And Encounters

**Mundane wildlife (the common case).** This is farmland; the wildlife is farmland wildlife, and the domestic animals are more dangerous than any of it.

- **Sheep, cattle, and hogs** everywhere. A bull in a field, or a sow with a litter, is a real hazard — **Boar-like** or better, CR 1/4, and entirely capable of putting a traveler in a ditch.
- **Sheepdogs and farm dogs** — protective, fast, and loud. **Mastiff-like**, CR 1/8. Rarely fought; frequently the reason a shortcut becomes a conversation with a farmer.
- **Deer, hare, and pheasant** in the coppices and orchard margins — poached constantly, defended vigorously.
- **Rats and rat swarms** in granaries, mills, and press-houses — **Swarm of Rats-like**, CR 1/4.
- **Bees** — the Vale keeps a great many hives; a disturbed skep is a **Swarm of Insects-like** problem and an expensive one to have caused.
- **Feral dogs and a territorial ram** on the downs — CR 1/4 and up, mostly avoidable, occasionally not.
- **Crows and rooks** in enormous numbers, especially at Marrow Cross, where they are a permanent fixture and part of the atmosphere.

**The lingering dead.** Rarer here than in the fen country, and gentler in character — a sorrowful figure at dusk in the Nettlecombe orchard, a drowned pilgrim at the Drowned Lane ford, someone standing in a mill-pool. Run per [`../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../bestiary/folk-undead.md) — **Specter-like**, AC 12–13, ~17–30 HP, **not hostile by default** and frequently settled by talking (DC 12 Insight) or by a folk rite (DC 13 Religion). The exception is **Marrow Cross**, where the hanged were denied rites and something considerably angrier walks: **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~36 HP, telegraphed by cold and by the crows going silent, and better answered by giving the denied dead a proper rite (DC 15 Religion) than by fighting it. That is the one place in the Vale that can kill a careless traveler.

**People.** The Vale's real danger. **Cinder Ledger** toughs at the Grange and at foreclosures (**Thug-like**, AC 11, ~32 HP, two to four, who prefer intimidation and break on morale); **Concord Remnant** survey-scholars at the Buried Cloister and the Beacon Tump, who will argue rather than fight but will absolutely have a stake-puller ejected; drovers, pilgrims, relic-sellers, and cutpurses working the shrine crowds; and moot-appointed road-wardens who take an interest in armed strangers. Social danger is worse than physical: a shrine-town can close its doors to you, and the harvest-moot's disapproval travels faster than any writ. See [`../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../bestiary/human-hazards.md).

## Level Range And Danger

**Level 1–5. Low across almost the whole Vale**, rising to **moderate** at Marrow Cross after dark and inside the grange and cloister ruins. This is the region a traveler comes to in order to sleep under a roof and eat something that has not been smoked over peat.

**Telegraph it.** The dangerous spots are named and the locals name them: the weirs, the flood lane, the gibbet crossroads. Anyone who asks gets told.

**Retreat:** always easy. This is open, settled, roaded country — a farm, a village, or a road is rarely more than an hour away in any direction.

**Resting:** safe nearly everywhere. Inns, hospices, barns, and hayricks are all available to a civil traveler with a little coin. The exceptions are Marrow Cross and inside the ruins.

## Resources

- **Food and drink of real quality** — grain, cheese, cider, orchard fruit, and nuts, cheap and abundant by frontier standards.
- **Wool and hides** off the downs; the drove-roads run south to the city.
- **Orchard and hedgerow herbs** — DC 12 Wisdom (Survival); good healer's-kit components and the best gathering in the northwest.
- **Salvage and relics** from the field-shrines and orchard ruins, quietly bought by **Cinder Ledger** factors and quietly resented by everyone else.
- **Scholar's pay** — the **Concord Remnant** pays coin for accurate copies of inscriptions from the Buried Cloister.
- **The pilgrim trade** — inns, hospices, guiding, portering, and relic-stalls around the shrine-hill; the Vale's second industry after food, and an easy way for a stranger to earn a bed.
