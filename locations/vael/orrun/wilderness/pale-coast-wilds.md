## AI Use

Load when anyone travels the Coast's cliff-road, coves, headlands, salt-pans, or the water offshore. This file covers the coast as a **physical place**: the cliffs and tides, the named headlands and reefs, its hazards with DCs, and what lives there. The sea is the antagonist here and needs no help. Nothing here depends on a particular plot.

## One-Sentence Identity

A cold, storm-cut coast of grey cliffs, shingle coves, salt-pans, and ruined Concord lights, where the tide sets the schedule and the danger climbs steadily from the cliff-road out to the killing water beyond the surf.

## What A Traveler Notices First

The sea — grey, vast, and never quiet — and how completely everything bends to it: wind-stunted cliff-grass, upturned-boat roofs, tide-tables chalked on every harbor wall and half the cottage doors. The ruined lighthouses and small stone sea-shrines on the headlands, weathered nearly featureless and older than anything the fisher-folk built. The long shingle of cairns above the tide-line where the Coast buries its drowned. And, in fog, how the broken great lighthouse below its headland sometimes seems to *gleam*, though nobody has tended it in a hundred years.

## Terrain

Hard grey cliff and shingle, cut by coves and headlands, with sea-caves at the cliff-foot and stacks and reefs offshore. Behind the cliff line: thin windburnt turf, gorse, and gull-colonies; inland toward the marsh, flat tidal salt-pans and their working camps.

The defining fact is **tide**. It runs far here and it runs fast: coves that are dry beach at low water are cliff-foot surf at high, several sea-caves flood to the roof, and the causeway bars out to the near stacks are walkable for a couple of hours a day and lethal either side of that. Every local reads a tide-table the way inlanders read weather.

The second defining fact is **weather**. Storms come in off the open water with little warning, sea-fog rolls in thick enough to hide the cliff edge, and autumn is gale season — every coastal village expects to lose boats and plans for it.

**Getting through it:** the Pale Road runs the cliff-tops and is the safe thread. Anything below the cliff line or on the water needs the tide-table and, ideally, a local. Skippers who know the water charge what they charge and are worth it; a fisher-child can tell you when the cove floods, and will, unprompted, because that is how children are raised here.

## Named Features

- **The Cliff-Road and the Headland Sea-Shrines** — the Pale Road along the cliff-tops, dotted with small ruined Concord sea-shrines on the headlands where, custom says, the drowned were seen off. Leaving an offering at one is the Coast's safe-passage rite, kept by everyone including people who claim not to.
- **The Coves and Sea-Caves** — shingle coves and tidal caves below the cliffs. Fishing, smuggling, and shelter, provided you know which ones flood.
- **The Drowned Lamp Headland** — the great ruined Concord lighthouse at the foot of its headland above the shingle village. The Coast's chief landmark, tide-locked, storm-cut, and long since claimed by the water. Cold, even by coast standards.
- **The Weeping Light** — a small roofless Concord sea-light on a lonely headland, offering-shells heaped at its foot. At dusk in fog there is a faint cold gleam from the dark lamp-room, and water beads down its carved walls when no rain has fallen. It is the one everyone points at when explaining what the Drowned Lamp is like.
- **The Salt-Pan Flats and Workers' Camp** — mirror-flat brine pans striped to the horizon, raking gangs working to quota, and a mean huddle of shacks with a **Cinder Ledger** tally-clerk's hut at the middle of it. Bleak, exposed, and where the whole frontier's salt comes from.
- **The Drowned-Cairns** — mile on mile of stone cairns above the tide-line, salt-crusted and weed-hung, fresh ones bright among grey. The Coast's burial shore. This season, some of the stones have been rolled aside, and there are drag-marks going down toward the water.
- **The Wreckers' Strand** — an informal, unlovely settlement on the wild coast where salvage is landed and few questions are asked.
- **The Lantern Stacks** — a near-shore cluster of black sea-stacks off the strand, white with gull-mess and spray, a wreck's ribs jammed in a gut between them. On a foul night a steady "harbor" light sometimes burns out there, where no harbor could be.
- **The Skerries** — the offshore stacks and reefs. Wrecks, seals, seabirds, and the worst water on this coast; boat-only, tide- and storm-locked, and the one place everybody — including the criminals — agrees is a bad idea.
- **The Verdance Mouth** — south along the coast, where the great river reaches the sea and the inland water-route begins. The Coast's gateway south and its great commercial advantage.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Storm and high sea (in a boat)** | DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) or seamanship; DC 16 in a gale | Failure: swamped or capsized. Cold-water immersion follows. Always preceded by a falling glass, a wrong-colored sky, and a skipper saying no. |
| **Tide-trap** (sea-caves, causeway bars, the stacks) | DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) to read the tide before committing | Failure: cut off. A cave floods (DC 13 Athletics to swim clear), or you are stranded on a stack for six to twelve hours in the wind. Telegraphed by chalked tide-tables everywhere and by every local you meet. |
| **Cold-water immersion** | DC 13 Constitution save per few minutes in the water | Failure: 1 level of exhaustion, compounding. This is what actually kills people here, not drowning. |
| **Cliff-climb / cliff-fall** | DC 14 Strength (Athletics) | Failure: a fall scaled to the cliff, which is generally a long way. Wet rock and gull-slick ledges are described before the roll. |
| **Sea-fog** | DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) to hold a course ashore or afloat | Failure: lost, looped, or set down the coast from where you meant to be. Fog also hides the cliff edge, which is the real danger of it. |
| **False lights** (Lantern Stacks, the wild coast) | DC 13 Wisdom save to see through a shown light while sailing | Failure: onto the rocks. Not a natural hazard — someone is holding the lantern. |
| **Brine-burn and exposure** (salt-pans) | DC 12 Constitution save after a shift on the flats without protection | Failure: cracked skin, streaming eyes, 1 level of exhaustion. Sucking pan-mud is a separate DC 12 Athletics nuisance. |
| **Cairn-fall / loose shingle** | DC 12 Dexterity save on the burial shore or a steep beach | Failure: 1d4 and a turned ankle. Minor, common, and inconvenient a long way from help. |
| **The old sea-lights** | DC 13 Constitution save for anyone who lingers against the warm-cold stone of a ruined light or handles its offerings | Failure: a lingering chill and unease for a day; reversible, and lifted by a proper rite or a night at a tended shrine. The sweating stone is the warning. |

## Fauna And Encounters

**Mundane wildlife (the common case).** A rich coast, and most of what a traveler meets is simply making a living from it.

- **Seabirds in enormous colonies** — gulls, gannets, guillemots. Harmless, deafening, and aggressive near nests; a colony going up all at once is worth looking around for.
- **Seals** on the rocks and skerries — **Seal-like**, CR 0. Harmless in the water, startling in fog, and hunted for oil in the north.
- **Fish, crab, and lobster** everywhere; the cove economy runs on them.
- **Giant crabs** in the deeper gullies and among the stacks — **Giant Crab-like**, CR 1/8. Nuisance-dangerous to a wading forager; the big ones can take a finger.
- **Reef-sharks and hunting seals** offshore — **Reef Shark-like**, CR 2, in packs of two or three. Genuinely dangerous to anyone in the water past the surf, and a reason nobody swims for pleasure here.
- **Giant octopus** in the deep gullies off the stacks — **Giant Octopus-like**, CR 1. Rare, telegraphed by fishermen's stories that turn out to be true.
- **Rats** in every harbor, wharf, and salvage-shed — **Swarm of Rats-like**, CR 1/4.

**The lingering dead.** Coast folk call them *the wet ones*, and there are a great many, because the sea keeps giving people back. They rise with the tide along the burial shore and stand at the sea-shrines. Run per [`../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../bestiary/folk-undead.md) — **Specter-like**, AC 12–13, ~22–36 HP, **not hostile by default**, and settled near shore by the local **salt-and-tide rite** (DC 13 Religion), which the **Mourners' Circle** salt-mothers perform and will teach a respectful outsider. The dead around the Drowned Lamp and out on the Skerries are older and worse and resist easy settling (**Wraith-like**, AC 13–14, ~45–60 HP); those are not a shore problem and should never be met by accident.

**People.** **Wreckers and smugglers** are a real criminal current on the wild coast — **Bandit-like**, **Thug-like**, and **Spy-like** (AC 12–14), with a **Veteran-like** wreck-master (AC 15, ~45 HP); morale-driven, frequently turnable, and much more interested in cargo than in murder. **Cinder Ledger** factors and salt-toughs run the pans and buy the salvage. **Mourners' Circle** salt-mothers keep the shrines and the burial shore and outrank any magistrate in local opinion. **Concord Remnant** survey-scholars turn up to measure the old lights. A thin **Reachward Compact** harbor-watch covers the ports and nothing beyond them. Honest skippers, fisher-families, and salt-gangs make up everyone else. See [`../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../bestiary/human-hazards.md).

## Level Range And Danger

**Level 2–7. Low-to-moderate on the cliff-road and around the inhabited coves; high in open water and at the Drowned Lamp; effectively lethal out at the Skerries.** Almost every death on this coast is tide, cold, or weather. The wreckers are the exception, and they are a human problem with human solutions.

**Telegraph it constantly.** Tide-tables are chalked on walls, the burial-shore bell rings, skippers refuse fares, and locals volunteer warnings to strangers without being asked. Anyone who ignores all of that has been warned four times.

**Retreat:** get above the tide line and back onto the cliff-road. On the water, run for the nearest cove rather than the nearest village.

**Resting:** safe in the harbor-towns and cove villages, and at the tended sea-shrines, where a traveler who leaves an offering is generally left alone. Never at sea, never in a tide-cave, never on a stack.

## Resources

- **Salt** — the frontier's supply, raked on the pans, and the region's most important export by a distance.
- **Fish, shellfish, and kelp** — the cove economy; DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) to forage the shore productively.
- **Salvage** off the wrecks and the ruined lights: timber, fittings, cargo, and Concord relics, all of which have a ready buyer and none of which are entirely clean.
- **Shore-herbs and seaweeds** — DC 12 Wisdom (Survival); good antitoxin and healer's-kit components found nowhere inland.
- **Passage** — coastal shipping and the water-route up the Verdance, often faster and safer than any road, and the Coast's great advantage over the fen country.
- **Tide-lore and pilotage** — the most valuable thing on this coast, held by skippers, fisher-families, and salt-mothers, and given freely to anyone who shows the sea proper respect.
