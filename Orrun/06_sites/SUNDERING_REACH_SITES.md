## AI Use

Load when anyone in the Sundering Reach goes into a ruin. Eight authored sites — flooded Concord works, a mine delving, two barrows and an archive-cairn, a fen, and a merchant's strongroom — described as **physical places a traveler walks into**: entrances, footing, water, air, what lives there, what can be carried out, and how to leave. Nothing here depends on a particular plot; drop any of them into any game set in the Reach.

## The Reach's Ruins In General

The Reach is built on the Custodian Concord's bones (see [`../03_culture/WORLD_HISTORY_PUBLIC.md`](../03_culture/WORLD_HISTORY_PUBLIC.md)). Concord stonework is instantly recognizable: vast fitted blocks with no visible mortar, star-marks cut at doorways and lintels, and long runs of the old script that nobody in the Reach can read. Salvage from it is the region's second economy, and everyone does it while claiming not to.

Three things are true of almost every Concord ruin here:

- **It is lower than it was.** The peat is still swallowing them. Assume water, and assume the lowest level is flooded.
- **It has dead in it.** Not always, not always hostile — run them per [`../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md), which is deliberately silent about *why*.
- **It has raw relics in it.** The grey glass, stone, and bone that relic-hunters call **old glass**. Valuable, portable, and unpleasant to carry for long (see the relic-exposure hazard below).

**Relic-exposure ("thin-touch").** A recurring hazard at every site with raw relics in quantity. Prolonged handling or prying loose in-situ relics: **DC 14 Constitution save** or take *thin-touch* — **disadvantage on death saving throws** until a proper folk rite is performed over the sufferer (**DC 13 Religion**, any competent grave-keeper) or the sufferer takes a long rest at a working shrine. Reversible, cumulative in fiction but not in mechanics, and always telegraphed: locals will point out somebody who has it and say "he went thin."

### The Peat Chapel

**Type** small sunken Concord chapel · **Region** Greyfens edge, a few hours from Hollowmere · **Level** 1–2 · **Danger** low (a good first ruin)

**What It Is.** A Concord wayside chapel — one of the small houses the old order kept along its roads, where travelers who died between towns were given their rites before the road went on. The peat has taken half of it. The tilted star-spire is visible for a mile across the fen and is the landmark everyone navigates by.

**Approach and Entrance.** A firm finger of ground reaches it from the Hollowmere side; no guide is strictly needed, though any local can point out the line. The door is the original one, jammed half-open by the tilt of the building. Nothing about the approach is hidden or dangerous.

**Layout.**
1. **The Tilted Nave** — the main chapel, floor canted maybe fifteen degrees into the peat, pews rotted to fibre, a hole where the floor gave way at the low end. Dry enough to rest in.
2. **The Sunk Vestry** — a side room, knee-deep in cold water, silted. Something usually dens here.
3. **The Altar Recess** — the star-altar, intact, still holding a smooth grey relic-stone. The one thing here worth real money.
4. **The Crypt-Space** — through the floor-hole, a crawl-height void with a traveler's bones and a second, cracked relic.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Rotten floor | DC 12 Perception to spot the soft boards | DC 11 Dex save or fall into the crypt-space; 1d6, climb out DC 10 Athletics |
| Cold damp (vestry) | DC 10 Con after a long stay | Minor fatigue; trivial |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con if the altar-stone is carried bare | Thin-touch (above) |

**Inhabitants.** One fen predator in the vestry — **Giant Frog-like** or **Swarm of Rats-like**, CR 1/8–1/4, telegraphed by tracks and disturbed water, and entirely skippable by not entering the vestry. Lifting the altar-stone stirs a faint lingering dead: a **Specter-like** profile, AC 12, ~13 HP, **not hostile**, sorrowful, asking wordlessly to be remembered or let go. Insight or Persuasion **DC 11** to understand it; folk rite **DC 12** to settle it.

**Salvage.** The altar relic (a genuine Remembrance relic; a fair market for one is 40 gp on the frontier and considerably more in a city). Loose Concord stonework and fittings, ~15 gp of it. The cracked crypt relic, worth little but readable.

**Getting Out.** Walk out. The path back is short, firm, and unguarded.

### The Whispering Cairn

**Type** Concord archive-cairn · **Region** on the south road between Candlewick and Hollowmere · **Level** 1–3 · **Danger** low (a reading site, not a fight)

**What It Is.** One of the archive-cairns: a squat, turf-grown stone mound built to hold Concord records. It sits on firm road-ground and so has never flooded, which is why it is unusually intact. Scholars come from as far as Caradril to make rubbings of the walls, and the Candlewick folk have long since stopped finding this strange.

**Approach and Entrance.** A star-carved lintel half-buried in turf, open and obvious. Any Candlewick villager will walk a stranger to it. There is no lock on the outer door and never was.

**Layout.**
1. **The Lintel Hall** — the entry passage. Walls dense with inscription, floor to ceiling. Dry, level, safe.
2. **The Tally Chamber** — the main vault. One entire wall is carved edge to edge in columns — names, by the look of them, with numbers beside each. Stone record-coffers line the others, most already emptied.
3. **The Collapsed Annex** — a side room half fallen in. Loose salvage, and animals get in.
4. **The Sealed Niche** — a star-marked recess closed by a Concord lock-glyph. It holds one intact record-relic. The glyph is a genuine puzzle, not a trap.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable annex vault | DC 13 Perception; DC 13 Dex save | 1d6 falling stone, blocks the annex — never the exit |
| The lock-glyph | Read the star-sequence in Concord Script, or DC 14 Investigation from a rubbing | Solved: opens clean. Forced (DC 15 Thieves' Tools or Strength): the niche's warding dead manifests once |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con if the niche relic is carried bare | Thin-touch |

**Inhabitants.** Nothing by default. The Collapsed Annex may hold 1–2 scavengers (**Bandit-like** or **Scout-like**, CR 1/8; see [`../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md)) or a fen predator, telegraphed by spoor. Forcing the niche wakes a single warding lingering dead — **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~17 HP — settled by folk rite **DC 13**, and avoidable entirely by solving the glyph.

The real pressure at this site is **other people**: Concord Remnant scholars and Cinder Ledger salvagers both want the cairn, and either may arrive mid-visit with carts and a claim. Handle it socially, hide the find, or leave early with a partial rubbing.

**Salvage.** Concord metal and fittings from the coffers and annex, ~30 gp. The niche record-relic. **The rubbings themselves** are the actual prize — portable, and scholars pay well for a clean one.

**Getting Out.** Open road-side exit throughout. Rests are safe inside if no rivals are inbound; the cairn is dry and easily defended.

### The Sunken Tollhouse

**Type** flooded Concord toll-house · **Region** under Kettle Bridge's broken fourth arch · **Level** 2–3 · **Danger** low–moderate (cold water, current, drowning)

**What It Is.** A Concord toll-house and wayside shrine that stood where the road met the river. When the fourth arch came down, generations ago, it went into the Mirewend with it. The building is intact but sunk; boatmen have avoided the broken arch for as long as anyone can remember, and lately have been avoiding it harder.

**Approach and Entrance.** By boat from Kettle Bridge's Wet Ward, or by diving at low water from the arch footing. Any bargeman will describe the entry stair for a coin, and a few will guide you to it for more. The cold coming off the water is the warning, and it is a good one.

**Layout.**
1. **The Flooded Stair** — descends from the arch footing into chest-deep water. Current runs across it.
2. **The Toll-Hall** — a half-drowned chamber, inscription on the walls above the waterline, submerged toll-coffers underfoot. Someone has already been through the easy ones.
3. **The Inner Chamber** — a small vaulted room holding a cracked block of Concord stonework, plainly the point of the whole building, and plainly not a toll-desk. Cold, and unpleasant to stand near.
4. **The Silt Vault** — a collapsed side room. A mislabeled salvage crate, and the body of a drowned bargeman nobody recovered.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Current in deep rooms | DC 13 Athletics per crossing | Failure sweeps you back toward the stair — lost progress, no damage |
| Cold water | DC 12 Con on a long stay | 1 level of exhaustion |
| Rising water | The tide/flood rises a stage per ~10 min inside | After 3 stages the lower rooms fill. A clock, not a trap — the stair stays open |
| Cracked floor (Silt Vault) | DC 13 Perception; DC 12 Dex save | Drop into a silt-pit; DC 13 Athletics to climb out, non-lethal |

**Inhabitants.** Possibly one eel or water-snake disturbed on the stair (**Constrictor Snake-like**, CR 1/8), telegraphed by movement in the water. In the Inner Chamber: one drowned lingering dead, a boatman the river took — **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~22 HP, moves freely through water, weeping. **Not hostile.** It attacks only if attacked, or if someone tries to lever the cracked stonework out of the wall. Speak with it (Insight/Persuasion **DC 13**) or settle it by rite (**DC 14 Religion**); either ends the encounter, and locals downstream will notice the difference.

**Salvage.** Water-blackened Concord coin from the coffers, ~25 gp of it (finding much more than that implicates whoever has been fishing the site already). One minor Remembrance relic from the Silt Vault crate. The drowned bargeman's signet, which has a family in Kettle Bridge.

**Getting Out.** The stair stays open the whole time. Swim up, or your boat is waiting. The rising water pressures but never seals.

### The Counting-House Vault (Hollowmere)

**Type** urban strongroom · **Region** Hollowmere, on the Rim · **Level** 2–4 · **Danger** low combat, high consequence

**What It Is.** The Cinder Ledger's Hollowmere counting-house: a solid stone building with a public counting-room at the front, a records office behind it, and a guarded corridor to a proper vault at the back. The house keeps coin, salvage stock, and — everyone in town half-knows this — one or two relics it has chosen not to put on the market.

Included here because it is the best example in the Reach of a "dungeon" made entirely of locks, patrols, and people. **Combat is the failure state.**

**Approach and Entrance.** The front door is open by day. That is the intended way in. The others are the roof of the adjoining warehouse, the strong-room corridor's service door, and the honest one: persuading somebody who works there.

**Layout.**
1. **The Counting-Room** — public, staffed by a factor and one or two clerks. Day-ledgers on the desks.
2. **The Records Office** — lightly secured. Correspondence and the house's private paperwork.
3. **The Strong-Room Corridor** — a guarded passage. One enforcer walks it on an irregular loop.
4. **The Vault** — the objective. Locked, alarmed.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Vault lock | DC 17 Thieves' Tools (DC 13 with a staff key) | Hard failure trips a bell-alarm |
| Bell-alarm | — | Enforcer and any guards arrive in 1 round. A chase scene, not a killing |
| Records office lock | DC 13 Thieves' Tools | — |
| Corridor patrol | DC 14 Stealth, or a distraction | Failure is detection — a social problem, not damage |

**Inhabitants.** Clerks and a factor (**Commoner-like** and **Noble-like**, noncombatants). One house enforcer on the corridor — **Thug/Spy-like**, AC 13, ~40 HP, built to grapple, threaten, and march intruders to the reeve; he does not want blood on the counting-house floor and neither does his employer. Possibly one **Guard-like** (AC 16, ~11 HP) on the door at night.

**Non-combat outs.** The cleanest route through this site is a turned clerk. Ledger staff on the frontier are underpaid, overworked, and often unhappy with what passes through their hands: **DC 14 Persuasion or Insight**, easier if the character has done them a favor. A turned clerk supplies the key, the patrol timing, and an explanation of what is in the vault.

**Salvage.** The vault relic (whichever one the house is sitting on). A coin reserve of roughly 200 gp — taking it makes the thief a hunted one, which is the point. The records-office paperwork, which in most games is worth more than the coin.

**Getting Out.** It is a building in a friendly town; walk out. The cost of a botched job is a powerful commercial enemy, a debt, or a blacklist — never a corpse.

### The Deep Adit

**Type** deep Concord delving beneath a mine · **Region** Sunder Heights, below Harrowgast · **Level** 3–5 · **Danger** moderate (bad air, collapse, relic-exposure, the dead)

**What It Is.** The deepest and oldest working under Harrowgast: a Concord tunnel driven far below where the ore is, for reasons no mining company has ever established. Miners call it bad rock and stop at a line they will not cross. Below that line the tunnel becomes something built rather than dug — dressed stone, star-marks, and seams of grey **old glass** running through the walls, which is the reason anybody goes down at all.

**Approach and Entrance.** Through the worked upper adits from Harrowgast, down to the barred **Deep Mouth**. The fear-line is marked by an offering-coin on a ledge and a cold draught flowing *out* of the rock, which is backwards for a mine and which every miner will point out to you.

Concord Remnant scholars currently hold a forward camp at the fear-line and are digging toward the deep chamber. Getting past them is a social or stealth problem before the site proper begins.

**Layout.**
1. **The Fear-Line** — where the miners stop. Cold; the rock carries sound strangely. The scholars' camp.
2. **The Worked Drift** — the last dug tunnel. Tool-marks, abandoned gear, stress-cracks in the roof, and a body nobody brought up.
3. **The Glass Seam** — where the old glass is embedded in living rock. Prying it loose is lucrative and stupid in roughly equal measure.
4. **The Deep Chamber** — the Concord structure the whole delving evidently exists to reach: a vaulted room of fitted stone, inscription on every surface, and a cold that gets into the teeth.
5. **The Drowned Sump** — a flooded shaft going further down. Nobody has bottomed it. Treat as scenery, not a route.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| The talking rock | DC 13 Wis save on entry and at the Deep Chamber | Unnerved: disadvantage on the next save against fear |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con at the Glass Seam or Deep Chamber, or when prying relics | Thin-touch |
| Cave-in (Worked Drift) | DC 14 Perception to spot stress-cracks; DC 13 Dex save | 3d6 bludgeoning and a blocked passage — an alternate drift always exists |
| Dark and cold | — | Standard deep-mine conditions; light is mandatory |

**Inhabitants.** At the fear-line, a scholars' camp: a lead researcher (**Scout/Mage-lite**, AC 13, ~30 HP) and two hired bravos (**Thug/Scout-like**, AC 13–14, ~16–30 HP). They would far rather hire, bribe, or warn off a newcomer than fight one.

In the Worked Drift, possibly a single cold miner's shade (**Specter/Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~30 HP). In the Deep Chamber, one or two older and angrier dead keep the room — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~36 HP each, life-drain touch (~2d6 necrotic, recharge), able to move through stone but **bound to the chamber**; the player can always back out of range. Non-combat outs: a full folk rite (**DC 15 Religion**) settles them, and reading the inscription aloud correctly (**Concord Script, DC 15**) makes them stand down and quiets the room.

**Salvage.** Old glass, in quantity, at real risk — the Cinder Ledger and the Concord Remnant both pay heavily and neither asks how you got it. Concord tools and metal. A clean **rubbing** of the deep chamber's inscription, which is the only thing here you can carry out without gloves.

**Getting Out.** Open tunnel back to the worked adits throughout, barring a cave-in (which has an alternate route). **No safe rest below the fear-line.** Short rest in the Worked Drift; long rest only back in Harrowgast, which has no healer worth the name — supply matters here.

### The Barrow of Nine Doors

**Type** pre-Concord barrow · **Region** the heart of the Greyfens · **Level** 3–5 · **Danger** moderate–high (a real delve; hire a fen-guide)

**What It Is.** A grass-grown mound rising out of the deep fen, far older than the Concord — old enough that the Concord evidently built around it rather than through it. Its name comes from the nine carved thresholds inside; eight are sealed. Fen-folk shun it, and describe it, without elaborating, as "where the old dead go and can't get through."

**Approach and Entrance.** Deep in the Greyfens, past everything marked (see [`../01_geography/wilderness/GREYFENS.md`](../01_geography/wilderness/GREYFENS.md) — mire rules apply on the approach, and a hired guide negates them). The mound's single open door breathes cold air you can see. The drift of lingering dead converging on the mound is visible from a long way off, which is both the landmark and the warning.

**Layout.**
1. **The Open Door** — the antechamber, crowded. Lingering dead press here, jammed against the inner thresholds, going nowhere.
2. **The Ring of Nine** — a circular chamber with nine carved door-thresholds around it: eight sealed, one leading down. Two layers of carving, one much older than the other. The puzzle hub.
3. **The Listening Hollow** — a side chamber with unusual acoustics, where the dead's voices carry. People come here to be spoken to, and leave offerings. Sometimes there is somebody in it.
4. **The True Threshold** — the ninth door: a genuine pre-Concord burial cell, and what is in it.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cold dread (whole barrow) | DC 13 Wis save on entry | Shaken: disadvantage on the first fear save |
| Forcing a sealed door | DC 16 Strength | Releases one angry dead and "wrongs" the room: hostile dead have advantage for 1 minute |
| Pushing through the jammed dead | DC 14 Wis save | 1 level of fear-strain (recovers on a short rest) if you shove rather than settle or calm them |
| The approach | Mire rules, [`GREYFENS.md`](../01_geography/wilderness/GREYFENS.md) | A guide negates it |

**The Ring of Nine (puzzle).** The nine thresholds must be named in the old order to open the true door cleanly. **Three valid paths, and any clever fourth:** read the layered carvings (Concord Script plus **DC 14 Religion or History**); use a Mourners' Circle elder's knowledge of the old star-order, which they will teach a respectful visitor; or simply ask, if the thing behind the ninth door is inclined to be helpful.

**Inhabitants.** At the Open Door, 3–5 weak lingering dead (**Specter-like**, AC 12, ~17 HP each) — **not hostile**, mournful, and jammed. A mass folk rite (**DC 14 Religion**) settles the lot of them and visibly eases the fen for a while. Provoking them turns a sad scene into a dangerous swarm; it is telegraphed.

The Listening Hollow may hold Gravecallers — 2–3 of them, or a speaker (**Cult Fanatic-like**). They came to listen, not to ambush; this is a conversation unless someone makes it otherwise.

Behind the True Threshold: **the barrow-lord**, an ancient dead of the pre-Concord downs. **Wraith/Ghost-like boss**, AC 14, ~60 HP, life-drain and a fear aura (DC 14 Wis), incorporeal, calls 1d4 of the jammed dead as reinforcements if fought. It is not evil, it is not in a hurry, and it will talk. Parley (**DC 15 Persuasion or Religion**) or offer it true release and it goes peacefully — which settles a great deal of the surrounding fen with it.

**Salvage.** Pre-Concord grave-goods in the burial cell: roughly 120 gp of old gold, and one uncursed minor magic item of a funerary character (a cloak, an amulet, a ring). One clean relic. A rubbing of the layered carvings, which scholars will fight over.

**Getting Out.** The Open Door is always open, and the barrow-lord cannot leave the mound. Withdraw to the antechamber and out. **No safe long rest inside**; short rest in the Listening Hollow if it is empty. Greywater Holm is the recovery point, a few hours out with a guide.

### The Signal-Cairn

**Type** buried Concord station · **Region** high in the Sunder Heights above Harrowgast · **Level** 7–10 · **Danger** high (telegraphed)

**What It Is.** A sealed Concord vault high in the cold Heights, past the worked adits and the Roofless Hall. Whoever named it thought it looked like a signal-station; scholars have argued about that for eighty years and settled nothing. What is not in dispute: it is large, it is deep, its walls carry the densest run of Concord inscription anyone in the north has found, and the **Ashen Wardens** put their own seal on the door at some point after the Quietfall and have kept it shut since.

**Approach and Entrance.** Up through Harrowgast's workings and out along the high ground. The adit ends at a bronze-and-stone door graven with star-marks, with the Wardens' later seal laid over the Concord work. The cold coming through it is the first warning; the second is that the Wardens will tell you plainly not to.

Concord Remnant scholars have been trying to open it for two seasons and may hold the approach when you arrive.

**Layout.**
1. **The Sealed Approach** — the adit's end and the door. The scholars' forward camp.
2. **The Upper Hall** — beyond the door: a vaulted chamber, inscription floor to ceiling, one or two drifting dead who have been here a long time.
3. **The Humming Chamber** — the station's centre: a close-set ring of standing Concord stones, faintly warm to the hand and faintly audible. A guardian stands in it.
4. **The Inner Vault** — a sealed chamber behind a harder seal. Its walls are covered edge to edge in a single continuous inscription. Scholars come from across the continent for a rubbing of it.
5. **The Deep Shaft** — a sealed shaft descending. It is stated plainly here so the DM can state it plainly at the table: **it goes down past anything survivable, and it stays shut.** A gate, not a puzzle.

**Hazards.**

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| The Warden seal (outer door) | Concord Script, or DC 14 Investigation with a rubbing | Sealed. A Warden's leave opens it cleanly. Forcing it (DC 17–18) wakes the guardian hostile |
| The inner vault seal | Concord Script DC 16, or DC 15 Investigation with a rubbing | Harder version of the same |
| Relic-exposure | DC 14 Con (upper hall), DC 15 (humming chamber, inner vault) | Thin-touch |
| Collapsing Concord stone | DC 14 Perception / Dex save | 3d6 and a blocked path; reroute |
| Heights cold | DC 13 Con | Exhaustion; shelter and warm gear negate |
| The deep shaft | — | Do not. Telegraph it as a gate |

**Inhabitants.** One or two lingering dead in the Upper Hall — **Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~36–45 HP, parley- or rite-able (**DC 15**), bound to the hall.

**The guardian** stands in the Humming Chamber: a Concord construct of stone and cold light, roughly humanoid, that turns its head when you enter and speaks. Use a **Stone Golem-like** profile scaled down — AC 17, ~95–120 HP at levels 8–10, resistant to nonmagical damage, a slam, a ranged force/necrotic burst on a recharge, and a relic-exposure aura within 10 ft. It is **a posted sentry, not a berserker**: it issues a challenge and waits for the correct response.

- **Answer it.** Read the challenge and speak the response (**Concord Script DC 15**). It stands aside and you may read the whole vault at leisure. This is the intended solution.
- **A Warden's leave.** A senior Ashen Warden can stand it down (**DC 14**, faction standing).
- **Fight it.** Hard but winnable at tier 3. It is **bound to the chamber** and does not pursue; retreat up the adit always works. Its morale condition is simple — it stops when you leave or when you say the right thing.

The Concord Remnant crew is the other obstacle, and the more negotiable one.

**Salvage.** A rubbing of the inner vault (the prize, and portable). Concord salvage and one high-grade relic — handle with care. A Concord script primer, which makes every future ruin easier to read. Whichever faction you did *not* sell the rubbing to will remember it.

**Getting Out.** The adit out is always open and everything here is bound. **No safe rest inside.** Harrowgast is the recovery point.

### The Greyfens Deep

**Type** environmental site (the fen as a destination) · **Region** the deep Greyfens · **Level** 3–7 · **Danger** moderate, scaling — terrain, not teeth

**What It Is.** The interior of the Greyfens treated as a place you go *into* rather than through. The fen itself is the site: no walls, no doors, no floor plan — reed-walls taller than a rider, black water, fog that eats sound, and firm ground that is discontinuous and lies about itself.

**For the fen's full terrain, named features, hazards, fauna, and salvage, use [`../01_geography/wilderness/GREYFENS.md`](../01_geography/wilderness/GREYFENS.md).** This entry only adds what running it as a delve requires.

**Layout (as a route, not a map).** A trip into the deep fen strings the named features together, typically in this order: the **Standing Dead** shallows → the **Lantern Maze** → the **Bone Weir** → the **Drowned Blind** and **Still Pool** → the deep mire → the **Barrow of Nine Doors** at the far end, which is where a fen expedition usually climaxes.

**Hazards.** Sinking ground (DC 13–14 Survival / DC 15 Athletics, staged), getting lost (DC 14–15 Survival, cumulative), false lights (DC 13 Wis), deep water and cold (DC 13 Athletics / DC 12 Con), foul air at the Weir (DC 12 Con). All per `GREYFENS.md`. **A hired fen-guide negates most of it, and that is the intended solution** — the fen is a preparation problem, not a combat one.

**Inhabitants.** Gathered lingering dead in unusual numbers, especially at the Standing Dead — **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~17–22 HP, **not hostile**, settled en masse by a folk rite (**DC 14**). A provoked gathering becomes a genuine swarm and is telegraphed before it does. Wrathful ones (**Wraith-like**, AC 13, ~36 HP) only if the fen is treated badly.

People: a **Gravecallers** cell shelters somewhere in the deep fen and moves its camp. They want witnesses more than they want a fight; treat first contact as a conversation. Smugglers work the channels. A guide or two live out here on purpose. Fen predators per `GREYFENS.md`.

**Salvage.** Drowned grave-goods and gear at the Bone Weir (60–100 gp of it, and grim). One minor relic. A smuggler's cache if you find it. Fen herbs (**DC 13 Survival**).

**Getting Out.** Follow the marked stakes, or follow running water downstream — it reaches the river or a village eventually. **No safe long rest in the open fen.** Greywater Holm and the guides' huts are the recovery points.
