## AI Use

Start here to find a ruin. **34 authored sites** across the continent, each described as a physical place a traveler can walk into — approach, layout, footing, hazards with DCs, inhabitants with reference-style stat profiles, salvage, and retreat. Pick by region, by level, or by what kind of trouble the table is in the mood for. None of them requires any particular storyline.

## What Kind Of Sites These Are

Roughly four kinds, and it is worth knowing which you are picking:

| Kind | What the challenge actually is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Concord ruin** | Dark, water, collapse, relic-exposure, dense inscription, and occasionally a posted stone sentry that wants a password | Deep Adit, Signal-Cairn, Saint Veddow's Tomb, Old Iron Forts, Frozen Works |
| **Older-than-Concord site** | Dread, misdirection, dead who resist easy laying, and giving offense | Barrow of Nine Doors, Hanging Oaks, Old Mast, Barrow-Fields, Old Holds, Sky-Stones |
| **Environmental site** | Tide, brine, cold, ash, heat, current, fog. Preparation and turning around at the right moment | Greyfens Deep, Drowned Lamp, Skerry Shrine, Drowned Steps, Wreck-Reef, Ashfast Deep, Frozen Works |
| **People site (heist)** | Locks, patrols, cover, and turned staff. **Combat is the failure state** | Counting-House Vault, Pellow Grange, Wreckers' Caves, Ashmarket Undercroft, Counting-Deep, Reliquary Vaults |

## Two Rules That Apply Everywhere

**Bound, not hunting.** Every undead and every construct in these files is tied to its site or its water and does not pursue past it. Leaving always works, at every site, at every level. Say so at the table when players start looking nervous.

**The dead are not explained.** Run every lingering dead per [`../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md): usually non-hostile, usually resolvable by a folk rite (**DC 13–16 Religion**) or by simply talking to it, and *deliberately unexplained*. People died there and did not finish leaving. Nobody in the world agrees on why, and no site in this index answers the question.

**Relic-exposure ("thin-touch")** is the recurring hazard: **DC 14–15 Constitution** on prolonged handling of raw relics or long exposure in a relic-dense chamber. Effect: **disadvantage on death saving throws** until a folk rite (**DC 13 Religion**) or a long rest at a working shrine. Reversible, and telegraphed by locals pointing at somebody who "went thin."

## Pick By Problem

- **A first dungeon for a level-1 party** — the Peat Chapel. Small, telegraphed, escapable, one relic, one sad ghost.
- **A reading-and-escorting session with no fight in it** — the Whispering Cairn.
- **A heist where drawing a weapon means you lost** — the Counting-House Vault (small), the Pellow Grange (rural), the Ashmarket Undercroft or the Three Bridges Counting-Deep (urban, high tier).
- **Water as the antagonist** — the Sunken Tollhouse, the Greenward Toll-Station, the Wreckers' Caves, the Drowned Lamp, the Skerry Shrine, the Nine Locks, the Drowned Steps, the Drowned Towns.
- **A puzzle-door with three honest answers** — the Barrow of Nine Doors, the Barrow-Fields.
- **A place that turns you around instead of killing you** — the Old Mast, the Old Holds.
- **Somewhere to breathe, with a moral problem instead of a monster** — the Great Grove at Sunhollow, the Sky-Stones.
- **A genuinely lethal capstone that respects preparation** — the Frozen Works, the Ashfast Deep, the Cindern Waste Buried Works.
- **A reminder that most of the world is fine** — the Wreck-Reef.
