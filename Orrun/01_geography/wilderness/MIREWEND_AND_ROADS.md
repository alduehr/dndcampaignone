## One-Sentence Identity

The raised stone causeways and the slow black river that hold the Reach together — the safest way to move through fen country, and slowly falling apart.

## What A Traveler Notices First

The Concord roads are unmistakable: broad stone causeways lifted above the bog on ancient piers, dead straight for miles, edges crumbling into the water, marked at regular intervals by squat **shrine-stones** carved with the old star. The Mirewend runs peat-dark and slow beside and beneath them, wide and unhurried and much deeper than it looks. Travel here feels safe compared to the open fen — right up until the causeway dips under standing water, or the fog comes up off the river, or someone is standing on the road ahead who should not be.

## Terrain

Two intersecting systems, both engineered and both a century past their last maintenance.

**The causeways** are raised stone roads on piers, running dead straight across bog, fen, and open water. Where they hold, they are the best surface in the Reach — dry, firm, wide enough for two carts. Where they have failed, the road simply *ends*, either in a collapsed span or in a stretch that has settled below the waterline. The edges are the danger: a century of frost has undercut the parapets, and a laden cart too near the verge goes into the bog.

**The Mirewend** is slow, peat-black, and effectively opaque — you cannot see a foot into it. It is navigable by punt and shallow barge along its whole lower length, and it floods hard in autumn, at which point it swallows fords, closes low road-stretches, and reroutes itself across the flats. The banks are undercut and unreliable; the bottom is soft mud, not gravel.

**Getting through it:** stay on the causeway. Fen travel off the roads doubles the journey and needs a guide. In flood season, ask locally before committing to a route — half the Reach's road closures are seasonal and everybody at the nearest inn knows which.

## Named Features

- **The Star-Stones** — the carved marker stones set at old, regular intervals along every Concord road, each cut with the same eight-pointed star. Intact ones are simply landmarks, useful for measuring a day's progress. More of them crack each season, and a cracked one is a cold, unpleasant thing to stand beside; carters make a point of not stopping at one.
- **The Long Causeway** — the Reach's spine, running Hollowmere–Reedford–Kettle Bridge. The best-maintained road in the region and the default route for everything.
- **The Pale Road** — the western causeway toward Saltmargin and the coast. Salt-carts, thin traffic, and a stretch where the causeway has half-collapsed.
- **The Broken Span** — that collapse: two piers gone, leaving a gap crossed by a decaying timber patch the carters have rigged and re-rigged for decades. A climb or a long detour.
- **The South Road** — the causeway toward Candlewick and the farm country beyond. Firm ground, gentle grades, and the safest road in the Reach by a wide margin.
- **The Heights Road** — the northern causeway toward Harrowgast; it climbs out of the fens and degrades into a rough track. Cold, exposed, and thinly watched.
- **The Drowned Mile** — a stretch of the Long Causeway that has settled below the autumn flood line and must be waded, poled, or boated for part of the year. Cold, slow, and a known gathering place for the lingering dead.
- **The Mirewend Landings** — timber boat-landings at Hollowmere, Kettle Bridge, and Reedford. River travel is faster than the road and cheaper than a cart, and the bargemen know every hazard on the water.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Crumbling causeway** (Broken Span, undercut verges) | DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) or Strength (Athletics) to cross a bad section | Failure: a fall into the bog beside the road — mired, soaked, and possibly missing a pack. Carts and animals fare much worse; use a group check and lose time. |
| **Autumn flood / fording** (Drowned Mile, Reedford ford) | DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to judge a safe crossing; DC 13 Athletics to make it | Failure: swept downstream 1d4 × 30 ft., cold-water immersion, dropped gear. Recoverable with a rope or a second pair of hands. The detour always exists and always costs a day. |
| **Road fog** | DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) to hold pace and direction | Failure adds hours to the leg and raises the chance of blundering into something. Fog comes off the river at dawn and dusk almost daily. |
| **Cracked Star-Stone chill** | DC 12 Wisdom save if lingering within a few feet of a cracked stone | Failure: shaken and cold — disadvantage on the next saving throw against fear, until a short rest. Not an attack; just an extremely unwelcome place to sit down. |
| **Night on the open causeway** | DC 12 Constitution save without shelter or fire | Failure: 1 level of exhaustion. There is no cover on a causeway — that is the whole point of a causeway. |
| **Bank collapse** (river landings, undercut banks) | DC 12 Dexterity save when standing on an undercut bank | Failure: into the Mirewend, which is deeper and colder than it looks. |

## Fauna And Encounters

**Mundane wildlife (the common case).** The roads and river are the busiest habitat in the Reach, for animals as much as people.

- **Waterfowl, herons, and river birds** in constant traffic along the Mirewend — noisy, harmless, and a decent early warning when they all go up at once.
- **Otters and water-voles** at the banks. Harmless. Ruinous to unattended fish traps.
- **Pike and eels** in the river; the eels are a genuine trade good and the pike are large enough to alarm swimmers.
- **Rats and rat swarms** around the landings, granaries, and toll-houses — **Swarm of Rats-like**, CR 1/4. A nuisance to stores and a real hazard in a confined boat or cellar.
- **Fen predators straying onto the road at night** — a boar off the drier ground (**Boar-like**, CR 1/4), or feral dogs (**Jackal-like**, CR 0, in packs). Telegraphed by the horses balking well before anything is visible.
- **Insect swarms** over the water in warm weather — **Swarm of Insects-like**; misery rather than danger, but they will drive a camp off the road at exactly the wrong time.

**The lingering dead.** They turn up on the roads more than anywhere else in settled country, and travelers are used to it — a figure standing on the causeway at dusk is a thing carters describe with irritation rather than terror. They cluster at the Drowned Mile and at cracked Star-Stones. Run per [`../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md) — **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~17–22 HP, **not hostile by default**, and usually simply walked around. A folk rite (DC 13 Religion) settles one, and the next dozen carters through will be quietly grateful.

**People.** The roads carry everyone. **Cinder Ledger** carts and salt-wagons with hired guards; **Reachward Compact** wardmen on loose, infrequent patrol; **Ashen Wardens** walking the road on their own business; **Mourners' Circle** members escorting a body home; pilgrims, drovers, and refugees. Against them: road-robbers and toll-bandits on the thinly-watched Pale and Heights roads (**Bandit-like** / **Scout-like**, CR 1/8–1/2, who prefer threats and a share of the cargo to killing anyone), and smugglers moving goods off the books, who want to be neither seen nor fought. See [`../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md).

## Level Range And Danger

**Level 1–4. Low-to-moderate.** This is the intended safe travel layer of the Reach, and it mostly earns that. Danger rises on the Pale and Heights roads, where the watch is thin and the banditry real, and at the flood-stretches in autumn.

**Telegraph it.** Locals warn about specific stretches by name — the Broken Span, the Drowned Mile, the Reedford ford in spate. Those warnings are accurate and should be given freely.

**Retreat:** a causeway always leads somewhere. Following it in either direction reaches a settlement, a landing, or the fortified Warden waystation at the central crossroads within a day.

**Resting:** safe at any settlement, landing, or the waystation. Risky in the open on the causeway (no cover, no fuel, cold off the water) and genuinely bad at the Drowned Mile.

## Resources

- **The roads themselves** — the Reach's single most valuable asset. Everything the region trades moves on them.
- **River carriage** — barge and punt hire at the landings, faster and cheaper than carting, and the bargemen's knowledge comes with it.
- **Eels, fish, and fowl** taken along the river; DC 12 Wisdom (Survival).
- **Salvage** at the Broken Span and at collapsed stretches — dressed Concord stone, iron fittings, and whatever went into the bog with the last cart that misjudged the verge.
- **Withy and reed** cut along the banks for baskets, thatch, and the stakes that mark fen paths.
- **News** — the roads are where the Reach's information travels, and an inn on a causeway is the best place in the region to learn anything.
