## AI Use

Load when anyone walks the shore of the Hollowmere basin, punts out among its reed islands, or looks at the broken stonework standing out of the water. This file covers the shore and the holms as a **physical place**: the water, the islands, the ruins that break the surface, and the hazards of all three. The deep water is a different matter and is treated here as what it is — dangerous, cold, and not to be swum. Nothing here depends on a particular plot.

## One-Sentence Identity

The eerie, half-shunned shore of a great drowned hollow, ringed with reed islands and fisher-cottages, where broken grey stonework stands out of the water and keeps going down further than anyone wants to think about.

## What A Traveler Notices First

A wide bowl of dark, still water with fog lying on it even in sunshine. The town of Hollowmere rings the near shore, its back to the water in a way you notice before you can say why. Out in the basin, broken grey shapes break the surface — and on a clear, calm day you can see them continue *down*, far down, and understand that they are much larger than any building on the shore. The waterline is marked with salt, offerings, and small stone cairns. Nobody fishes the deep water. Nobody swims at all.

## Terrain

A flooded hollow — the lowest ground for many days' travel in any direction — filled with cold, dark, still water. It is not a natural lake bed: the bottom drops away steeply not far from shore, and the depth in the center has never been credibly measured.

The shore is mixed: shingle and salt-crusted mud on the town side, reed-bed and soft bank elsewhere, with low mudflats exposed at the seasonal low water. Firm ground exists as **holms** — small islands rising out of the shallows, some of them inhabited, reachable by punt or, in low water, by wading a known line.

The water is unnaturally calm. Wind that whitens the fens will leave the basin surface only rippling, which locals find much worse than a rough lake.

**Getting through it:** the shore path is easy walking and safe in daylight. Anything on the water requires a punt and someone who knows the shallows — the reed-holm families ferry people for coin and will refuse, flatly and without explanation, to take a passenger out past the last island.

## Named Features

- **The Basin Shore** — the shunned waterline below Hollowmere. Offerings, salt-marks, cairns, and the best view of the standing stonework. Walked in daylight by everyone and after dark by almost nobody.
- **The Surfacing Ruin** — a section of great grey masonry that breaks the surface a short way out and is reachable by boat, or on foot across a bar at low water. The water level has been low these last several years and more of it shows each season. It is deeply carved, cold to the touch, and a place local boatmen do not tie up at.
- **The Offering Holm** — a small firm island where the **Mourners' Circle** has tended a shore-shrine for generations. Tokens of the dead are left there. A safe foothold, and the one place on the water that feels tended rather than avoided.
- **The Reed Holms** — a scatter of inhabited islands in the shallows, a few cottages each, with fishing families, punts, drying racks, and eel-traps. Poor, close-knit, and hospitable to anyone who arrives politely.
- **The Lantern Shallows** — a stretch of water off the eastern shore that glows faintly on dark nights, where the lingering dead drift just under the surface, visible and unreachable. Everyone in the region knows the phrase *don't swim out at the Shallows*. It is the clearest natural warning-line in the Reach.
- **The Drainage Cuts** — channel works, spoil-banks, and half-built sluices where an attempt is being made to lower the basin's level and win farmland from the margin. Backed by the **Reachward Compact** and financed by the **Cinder Ledger**; loudly opposed by the **Mourners' Circle** and the holm families. Active, muddy, and full of laborers, tools, and argument.

## Hazards

| Hazard | Check | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold deep water** | DC 14 Constitution save per few minutes of immersion; DC 14 Athletics to swim any distance in it | Failure: 1 level of exhaustion and rapidly failing strength. The basin is deep, frigid, and lightless below a few feet. **Treat swimming out as potentially lethal and telegraph it constantly** — every local says so, unprompted. |
| **Low-water mudflats** | DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to read a safe line across exposed flats | Failure: mired to the knee or worse — DC 13 Strength to pull free, half speed until then. Rising water turns this from an annoyance into an emergency. |
| **The Lantern Shallows** | DC 13 Wisdom save if lingering at the waterline there after dark | Failure: shaken and drawn toward the water — an uneasy compulsion to wade out, shakeable but unpleasant. Telegraphed by the glow and by every warning the player has already been given. |
| **Punt capsize** | DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) in a shifting punt; DC 14 Wisdom (Survival) to handle one in fog | Failure: overboard, into cold deep water, some distance from shore. Punts are flat-bottomed and unforgiving of passengers who stand up. |
| **Basin fog** | DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) to hold a course on the water | Failure: lost among the holms until the fog lifts, which may be hours. Sound carries strangely over the basin and shouting for the shore does not reliably work. |
| **Unstable cut-banks** (Drainage Cuts) | DC 12 Dexterity save near a fresh cut or spoil-bank | Failure: a slide into a muddy channel, 1d6 and a very bad afternoon. Working sites, working machinery, tired laborers. |

## Fauna And Encounters

**Mundane wildlife (the common case).** The shallows are productive; the deep water is not.

- **Eels** — the basin's staple catch and the reed-holms' living, taken in traps along the margins.
- **Waterfowl in great numbers** on the shallows and reed-beds; the fowlers work the basin edge as hard as they work the fen.
- **Pike and coarse fish** in the shallows. The deep water is fished by nobody, and the holm families will tell you the nets come up empty, which they say without elaborating.
- **Otters and water-voles** in the reeds — harmless, and hard on eel-traps.
- **Insect swarms** over the reed-beds in warm weather — **Swarm of Insects-like**; a nightly misery on the holms in summer.
- **Herons, cormorants, and gulls** working the shallows; a good indicator of where the fish are and where the water is safe to wade.

**The lingering dead.** They are present at the shore in numbers, and out at the Lantern Shallows they drift *below* the surface, pale and slow and clearly visible on a still night. This is the sight travelers repeat afterwards for years. Run per [`../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md`](../../04_bestiary/FOLK_UNDEAD.md) — **Specter-like**, AC 12, ~22 HP, **not hostile by default**, though there are more of them here than anywhere on dry land in the Reach and provoking a shoreline full of them is a way to die badly. A folk rite (DC 13 Religion) settles one at a time. Nobody has ever settled the Shallows.

**People.** The shore is busy in daylight. Reed-holm fisher-families punting between islands; **Mourners' Circle** keepers tending the Offering Holm and leaving tokens at the waterline; laborers, overseers, and **Cinder Ledger** clerks at the Drainage Cuts, with hired guards (**Guard-like** / **Thug-like**, AC 11–16, ~11–32 HP) who are there to protect tools and payroll and would much rather handle a stranger by argument than by force. Feeling about the drainage scheme runs extremely hot on both sides, and a traveler who wanders in with opinions will be asked to pick one. See [`../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md`](../../04_bestiary/HUMAN_HAZARDS.md).

## Level Range And Danger

**Level 1–5 at the shore and the holms. Low-to-moderate — in daylight, on land, it is one of the safer places in the Reach**, minutes from a town, with plenty of witnesses and easy retreat.

**The deep basin is a different category entirely and should be treated as off-limits.** Cold, depth, dark, and the Shallows make swimming out a genuine way to die. Telegraph it relentlessly: the salt-marks, the warnings, the fact that no one on the shore will get in the water for money.

**Retreat:** trivial on the shore — Hollowmere is right there. On the water, make for the nearest holm rather than the far shore.

**Resting:** safe in town or on an inhabited reed holm, where a stranger who asks properly will usually be given floor space and eel. Not on the open shore after dark, and never on the Lantern Shallows side.

## Resources

- **Eels, fowl, and reed** — the basin's steady livelihood, and the holm families' whole economy.
- **Punt hire and ferrying** from the reed holms; cheap, reliable, and the only way onto the water.
- **Salvage** from the Surfacing Ruin and the shallow stonework — dressed stone, fittings, and Concord relics the **Cinder Ledger** will buy quietly and the **Mourners' Circle** will hate you for selling.
- **Withy and thatch** cut from the reed beds; the basin supplies most of the region's roofing.
- **Work** at the Drainage Cuts — labor, hauling, and guarding, paid in coin, at the cost of everyone on the water knowing whose side you took.
- **Local knowledge** — the holm families know the safe wading lines, the fog patterns, and exactly how much lower the water sits than it did in their grandparents' time.
