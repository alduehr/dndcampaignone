## AI Use

Load for travel and conditions in the great southern delta. The environment is the region's defining feature; treat water, heat, and fever as first-class terrain.

## One-Sentence Identity

A fever-ridden delta of rice, reed, and smuggling at the Mardenflow's mouth, governed for a crown that lives across the sea and visits rarely.

## Geography

The **Mardenflow** splays into a vast maze of channels, reed-beds, mudflats, and shifting islands before reaching the southern sea. There is almost no dry ground; what exists has been drained, diked, or built up by hand.

Defining features:

- **The Rice Sallows** — flooded paddy-country in the southwest; dykes, sluices, and an enormous amount of human labor.
- **The garrison-shelf** — the drained high ground in the north where the region's capital sits; the only reliably dry land of any size.
- **The Fever Channels** — deep, shifting backwaters in the southeast. Local pilots know them; nobody else does, and channels move between seasons.
- **The tidal flats** — where the delta meets the sea; salt, mud, and enormous flocks of birds.
- **The Drowned Steps** — offshore, a submerged ancient causeway-ruin marching out to sea. Older than the Concord, thoroughly avoided, and a fixture of local warning-tales.

**Borders:** the Marrowdowns (north, up the Mardenflow), the Hollow Gulf ports (east along the coast), the warm southern forests (west), the sea (south).

## Climate

Hot, humid, and wet, with a long rainy season and a dry season that is merely less wet. Fever runs with the water: the wet months are the sick months, and everyone in the delta plans their year around them. Mist sits on the channels most mornings.

## Culture And Livelihood

The marsh-people are patient, watchful, and extremely good at outlasting things. They have been governed by outsiders before and expect to be again. Compliance is polite, taxation is theoretical, and the real decisions get made at village level and on the water.

Common trades: rice above all, plus reed and thatch, fish and shellfish, boat-building, dyke-work, herbcraft and physicking, and smuggling — which is less a criminal underworld here than a parallel economy with its own reputations and rules.

Delta faith centers on **water-shrines** where the drowned are sung down. Given how the delta lives and dies, this is a busy office, and the fen-witches who keep it are the most trusted authority most villagers have.

**Who holds it:** a **Protector-Legate** governs from the garrison-capital for a crown across the sea. His garrison is chronically undermanned and chronically feverish. In practice the delta's real authorities are its healers, its village moots, and the smuggler-bosses who move everything the crown cannot tax.

## Settlements

- **Fenward** — the garrison-capital on the delta's driest ground; causeway, customs-house, and barracks.
- **Reedmouth** — the smuggling village; hidden channel-ferries and the region's true clearing-house.
- **The Rice Sallows** — the paddy-country villages and their dyke-works, which run a collective assembly of their own.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The Fever Channels** — deep shifting backwaters. Crown boats cannot follow; unpiloted strangers do not come back out. Local warnings about them are frank and worth taking.
- **The deep-delta drowned grounds** — sunken ruin-country far out in the reeds, avoided by every villager who knows where it is.
- **The tidal flats** — vast, featureless, and lethal to anyone caught out by the incoming water.

## Travel Routes

- **The Mardenflow river-road** — north to the chalk downs, south to the sea; the region's spine.
- **Delta tracks and ferries** — slow, flood-cut, and frequently rerouted.
- **The Fever Channels** — passable only with a local pilot; the fastest way through the delta and the fastest way to die in it.
- **The delta road east** into the Hollow Gulf's port-country.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Pall**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Humid, slow, and quietly dangerous. Fever is the region's true monster and it kills more people than everything else combined. The garrison is slow and sick; the smugglers prefer avoidance to violence; the delta itself does most of the harm.

Standing hazards: marsh-fever, flood and tide windows, shifting channels, mud that will not let go, marsh predators, and the deep-delta ruins that the fen-witches warn against in terms nobody bothers to elaborate.
