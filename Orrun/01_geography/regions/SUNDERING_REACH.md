## One-Sentence Identity

Orrun's cold northwestern frontier: peat fens, moorland, slow black rivers, and the bones of the fallen Concord underfoot everywhere you dig.

## Geography

A wide, low basin between mountains and sea. Peat fens and heather moor cover most of it, cut by slow, peat-dark water. Villages sit on **holms** — islands of firm ground rising out of the wet. Raised **Concord causeways**, a century unmaintained, still carry most of the traffic; where they have sunk, the road simply ends in water.

Defining features:

- **The Mirewend River** — the Reach's main artery: slow, peat-black, and prone to autumn flood.
- **The Hollowmere basin** — a wide flooded hollow at the region's center, the lowest ground for many days in any direction. Dark, cold, and deep; local lore holds that a great Concord ruin lies drowned beneath it. The town of Hollowmere rings its shore.
- **The Greyfens** — the Reach's most dangerous wetland: fog-bound, trackless, and easy to die in.
- **The Sunder Heights** — broken highlands and old mountains to the north, honeycombed with Concord mine-workings. The region takes its name from these sundered ridges.
- **Shrine-stones and cairns** — squat Concord markers scattered across the moors, ploughed around, built into walls, or simply left where they stand.

**Borders:** Sunder Heights (north), Pale Coast (west), Ashgarden Vale (south), Tollwood (east).

## Climate

Cold, wet, and foggy for most of the year, with brutal winters and short grudging summers. Fog is the region's signature weather — it can close in within an hour and last days. Autumn brings floods that make the Mirewend impassable and turn off-road travel into drowning ground.

## Culture And Livelihood

Settlers came after the Quietfall to farm the firm ground, cut peat, fish the river, and pick over the ruins. Life here is hard, neighborly, and superstitious. Households share tools and grudges in equal measure; a stranger is watched but rarely turned away from a fire.

Common trades: peat-cutting, reed-cutting, fowling, sheep on the moor edges, small-boat river work, salt trade coming in from the west, and salvage — the picking-over of Concord ruins, which everyone does and everyone claims not to.

Local customs run heavily to burial and doorway practice: salt at the graveside, a star scratched at the lintel, a grave-song sung by whoever in the village knows it best. Reach folk take these seriously in a way that visitors from richer country find excessive.

**Who holds it:** the **Reachward Compact**, a loose council of town reeves and district representatives seated at Hollowmere. Its authority is real in the towns, thin on the roads, and largely notional in the fens. The **Ashen Wardens** (a wandering order who lay restless dead) and the **Mourners' Circle** (the folk death-faith) hold as much day-to-day standing as any magistrate.

## Settlements

- **Hollowmere** — the region's de facto capital; a town on the rim of the flooded basin. Seat of the Compact.
- **Kettle Bridge** — the second town: a toll-crossing where the East Road meets the Mirewend.
- **Saltmargin** — the western salt-trade gateway town, on the marsh edge toward the coast.
- **Candlewick** — a prim candle-and-farm village on firm southern ground; the on-ramp south.
- **Greywater Holm** — a shrinking fen-village on the Greyfens' edge; poor and dwindling.
- **Harrowgast** — a half-ruined Concord mining town up in the Sunder Heights; rough, cold, and lucrative.
- **Reedford** — a tiny reed-cutting hamlet at a ford on the central road.
- **The Ashwalk Rest** — a fortified Ashen Warden waystation at the central crossroads; the open Reach's one reliable sanctuary.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The Greyfens** — fog marsh with no reliable paths. Guides exist; hiring one is the difference between a crossing and a disappearance.
- **The Sunder Heights** — cold highland ruins, abandoned mine adits, scree slopes, and the wind. Old Concord stonework everywhere.
- **The Mirewend River and Concord roads** — the travel layer: causeways, river landings, relay-marker stones set at old intervals along the roads, and long drowned stretches.
- **The basin shore and outer holms** — the reed islands, offering-stones, and shallows around the flooded center. Locals fish it and do not swim in it.

## Travel Routes

The Concord causeways are the region's skeleton and the only sane way to move: half a day to three days between named places. Off-road fen travel doubles the time, adds real hazard, and needs a guide.

From Hollowmere: Kettle Bridge east (~1 day), Reedford and the central road (~half a day), Candlewick south (~1–2 days), the Greyfens edge (~1 day), the Sunder Heights (~2–3 days), Saltmargin west (~5 days), the Vale edge (~6 days), the Tollwood edge (~4 days), and Caradril far to the southeast (~10–12 days).

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Pall**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Eerie, cold, and quietly frightening. The Reach is not lawless so much as thinly governed — the danger is more often weather, water, and getting lost than robbery. Fog kills more travelers than anything with teeth.

Standing hazards: sinking ground, killing fog, autumn flood, cold exposure, rockfall and bad air in the Heights, and lights on the fen at night that locals will tell you flatly not to follow. Bandits and smugglers work the roads and the marsh, but sparsely. The Greyfens and the deep basin are the two places locals genuinely refuse to go.
