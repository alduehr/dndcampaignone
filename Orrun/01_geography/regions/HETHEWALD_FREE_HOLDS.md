## AI Use

Load for the great eastern forest and the confederacy that lives in it. Access, welcome, and local custom are the region's primary gate; the forest itself is the primary hazard.

## One-Sentence Identity

A great dark forest of free-holds, river-tollers, and outlaws, answering to no crown — only to its moot, its old bargains with the wood, and the prohibitions it enforces without explaining.

## Geography

The **Hethewood** is the continent's largest forest: old-growth, barely mapped inside, and functionally trackless off the known paths. The **Hethe** river runs south through it, the region's only fast route and its most contested asset.

Defining features:

- **The Hethemoot clearing** — the confederacy's one great open ground, where the holds gather.
- **Greenward** — the sacred grove-village of the forest-faith, in the southwest.
- **The Hethe river-cut** — the northeastern river corridor, where the water-road runs and the tollers sit on it.
- **The Old Holds** — a cluster of Concord ruins deep in the southeastern interior, placed under the free-holds' own prohibition a generation ago. Nobody goes; the reason given is that the dead there do not stay down.
- **Forest tarns** — deep still pools used as navigation marks; several have unpleasant local reputations.
- **The western thinning edge**, where the wood gives way to road-country.

**Borders:** the northwestern frontier's Tollwood (far northwest, the same forest's finger), the Karran Marches (north), open eastern coast-country (east, down the Hethe), settled road-country (west).

## Climate

Cool, damp, and deep-shaded under the canopy; humid in summer, raw in winter. Mist and rain persist for days at a time. The forest swallows weather and sound alike, which is a large part of why it is so easy to become lost in.

## Culture And Livelihood

The free-holds are proud, independent, feud-prone, and bound together by a moot that exists to keep their feuds from becoming wars. There is no crown, no lord, and no standing law beyond what the moot agrees and the holds enforce.

Common trades: timber and charcoal, forest game and hides, honey and resin, river freight, and — on the water — tolling, which shades into outright robbery depending on who is collecting.

The **forest-faith** is the region's spiritual spine: grove-keepers make and renew bargains with the wood on the holds' behalf, an arrangement the holds treat as literally binding. Welcome is a formal thing here: a stranger is greeted, assessed, and either taken in under the wood's law or told to leave, and the wood's law is what keeps travelers alive.

**Who holds it:** a **confederacy of free-holds**, coordinated by a Hold-Speaker at the moot; the **grove-keepers** hold parallel religious authority; and an outlaw river-toller controls the Hethe's chokepoint from a camp nobody has managed to dislodge. Outside logging money presses at the western edge.

## Settlements

- **Hethemoot** — the great gathering free-hold; the confederacy's neutral ground and the region's service hub.
- **Greenward** — the sacred grove-village; the forest-faith's seat. Entry requires the grove's welcome.
- **Tollreach** — an outlaw river-toll camp on the Hethe. Not a service stop: no inn, no resupply, no safe rest. Treat it as a hazard on the route.

## Wilderness Zones

- **The Hethewood heart** — guided travel only. Trackless, disorienting, and hostile to the unaccompanied.
- **The Old Holds** — forbidden Concord ruins deep in the interior; the prohibition is enforced by the holds themselves and is not negotiable.
- **The forest tarns** — navigation marks and, at several, places the locals will route around rather than camp.
- **The western thinning edge** — the region's main land gateway; contested by tollers.

## Travel Routes

- **The Hold-Road** in from the west — two to four days to the moot; toll-contested at the forest edge.
- **The River-Road** down the Hethe — fast north-to-south, and blocked by the toll-camp. Travelers pay, fight, or take a deep-wood bypass ford that requires real woodcraft.
- **The Greenward Path** — guided, and gated by the grove's welcome.
- **The Old Holds track** — forbidden.

## Local Name For The Last Telling

Folk here call the darkest of the alternate Tellings **the Waning**. It is the same place other regions call by other names; "the Last Telling" is the scholarly term almost nobody uses aloud. See [`../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md`](../../05_timelines/THE_LAST_TELLING.md).

## General Danger And Tone

Moderate under the wood's law; high off it. The free-holds are honest about what is dangerous — their prohibitions are the region's safety system, and travelers who take them seriously usually fare well.

Standing hazards: getting lost, forest predators and fey-touched strangeness in the deep groves, outlaw tollers on the river, the deep interior without a guide, and the Old Holds at any time. The region's culture is welcoming to the respectful and unforgiving to the presumptuous, and it makes that clear immediately.
