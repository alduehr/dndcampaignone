## AI Use

Load this file when a player asks about alternate timelines, "what if" history, planar cosmology beyond the standard Three Thresholds, or when running a one-shot / side excursion into a neighboring Telling. This is general world cosmology, not tied to any single campaign's plot — safe to use in any game set in Vael.

## Summary

History is not singular. Every threshold-moment — every point where birth, life, or death could have gone another way — casts off a **Telling**: a complete, internally consistent world that almost happened. The home world is **the Kept Telling**. The others, collectively, are **the Unmade**. Most Tellings are dull, nearly identical echoes. A very small number are catastrophic. The worst one anyone has confirmed and returned from is **the Last Telling** — see `THE_LAST_TELLING.md`.

Crossing into another Telling is possible, from anywhere, through a specific and costly rite (`THE_RITE_OF_THE_BROKEN_THRESHOLD.md`). It is not something anyone stumbles into by accident, and it is not something a party can do casually or often.

## Public Knowledge (Player-Safe)

This is not secret lore — it is obscure, scholarly, and unglamorous, the kind of thing a hedge-wizard, retired sage, or well-read priest of the Last Door might actually know. Most common folk have never heard of it. Nobody who understands it recommends using it.

- Loremasters teach that the Three Thresholds — the Opening (birth), the Hearth (life), and the Last Door (death) — are not the only doorways in principle, only the only *sanctioned* ones. Every threshold moment a life passes through could, in theory, have gone otherwise. Somewhere, in some sense scholars argue about endlessly, it did.
- A fourth, unsanctioned threshold is called **the Broken Door**. It does not appear on any shrine. It has to be forced open, and forcing it costs something real: a piece of the traveler's own Remembrance (see [`../03_culture/COSMOLOGY_PUBLIC.md`](../03_culture/COSMOLOGY_PUBLIC.md) and [`../03_culture/MAGIC_AND_REMEMBRANCE.md`](../03_culture/MAGIC_AND_REMEMBRANCE.md) — Remembrance is the in-world term for the lingering identity of the dead, the same thing at stake in a failed resurrection).
- Scholars who study this call the practice **threshold-breaking**. It has a bad reputation: expensive, dangerous, and (correctly) associated with grief-mad nobles trying to visit a dead spouse in a Telling where they lived, or desperate people chasing a world where they made a different choice. Most who try it once do not try it twice.
- The **Last Telling** is spoken of the way sailors speak of a specific reef: everyone agrees it is real, everyone agrees people have gone there and come back, and everyone agrees you should not go looking for it.

## What This Is Not

- This is **not** standard planar travel. *Plane Shift*, *Teleportation Circle*, and similar effects move a creature across the planes of a single Telling (the Astral Sea, elemental planes, etc., all still exist normally per standard 5e cosmology). None of them cross between Tellings. A caster who tries to *Plane Shift* into "the world where I married her" simply fails — there is no sigil sequence for that, because the destination isn't a plane, it's a different history entirely.
- This is **not** time travel. You cannot use the Broken Door to go to your own past or future. Every Telling runs its own present, moving forward at its own pace (see `THE_RITE_OF_THE_BROKEN_THRESHOLD.md` for how time differentials work in play).
- This is **not** a repeatable convenience. Every element of the rite is designed to make "I threshold-break to the market in the next Telling over to buy something cheaper" a bad joke, not a strategy. See the cost structure in `THE_RITE_OF_THE_BROKEN_THRESHOLD.md`.

## The Structure of the Unmade

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **The Kept Telling** | The home world — the one this game, and every settlement/region file in `/Orrun` and `/ai_solo_campaign`, describes. |
| **A Telling** | Any one complete, self-consistent alternate history. Countless exist; only a handful are ever visited by anyone who lives to describe them. |
| **The Unmade** | The Tellings collectively, and the cosmological "space" a traveler passes through between them. Not a place with geography of its own — closer to a pressure, a between-ness, than a plane. |
| **Near Telling** | A Telling that diverged from the Kept Telling recently and locally — a war won instead of lost, a person who lived instead of died, a door opened instead of closed. Mechanically the cheapest and safest to reach. |
| **Far Telling** | A Telling that diverged earlier or more fundamentally — different ruling powers, different outcomes to events centuries gone, sometimes unrecognizable geography. Costlier and more dangerous. |
| **The Last Telling** | The single worst confirmed Telling: the one where the Last Door itself never reopened. See `THE_LAST_TELLING.md`. Costliest and most dangerous of all by design. This is the scholarly name only — almost everyone who actually talks about it uses a regional folk-name instead (the Pall, the Waning, the Ashlands, the Echo Realm, and others — see "What It's Called" in `THE_LAST_TELLING.md`). Don't confuse these regional names with **the Unmade** (the row above), which names the whole cosmology of Tellings, not this one specifically. |

## Why Crossing Costs Remembrance

A Telling is, at root, a possible answer to "who did this person become, what did this place become, what was kept and what was lost." Remembrance — the substance of identity established in the Kept Telling's cosmology — is exactly the thing that answers that question for a single soul. Forcing a crossing means spending a fragment of your own answer to buy passage into someone else's. This is why the cost of the Rite is always paid in identity (memory, exhaustion, sometimes worse), never only in coin. It is also why the return trip is cheaper: you already spent the hard part getting out there. What is left of you still knows which way is home.
