## AI Use

Use these when a party wants to learn about, prepare for, or source materials for the Rite of the Broken Threshold. These are generic, reusable archetypes plus one named example each — drop them into any settlement that plausibly has scholarly, black-market, or fringe-occult contacts.

---

## Archetype: The Fray-Walker

Anyone who has crossed into another Telling and come back — especially more than once — tends to get called a **Fray-Walker**, whether or not they claim the title themselves. They are rare, usually a little strange, and often the only reliable source of a Between-Hour's timing or a fair price on Unmade glass.

**Tier:** Secondary
**Role:** Guide, broker, or cautionary tale (DM's choice per individual)
**Motivation:** Varies — some genuinely want to help other travelers do this more safely than they did; some are running a business; a few are looking for a specific Near Telling and will trade services for information about it.
**Secret or complication:** Most have paid a real cost (a lost memory, a severed anchor-thread story, a Telling they can't stop thinking about). A good Fray-Walker NPC has a specific "what it cost them" the party can learn.
**What they know:** Between-Hour timing (within a few days), where to source Unmade glass, the practical version of everything in `THE_RITE_OF_THE_BROKEN_THRESHOLD.md`, and usually one personal story about a Telling they've seen.
**How they enter play:** As a quest-giver (they want something retrieved from a Telling), a vendor (Unmade glass, at a markup), a teacher (for a party with no proficient caster), or a warning (someone who tried for the Last Telling and came back wrong).
**Combat Relevance:** Use a **Sage-like** or **Spy-like** noncombat profile; most are not fighters. A hardened one who's survived the Last Telling more than once may warrant a **Veteran-like** fallback.

### Named Example: Osset Vane, "the Fray-Walker of the Counting-Quays"

A retired Caradril relic-appraiser who crossed into a Near Telling twenty years ago looking for a sibling who died young at home — and found them alive there, grown old, a stranger. Now sells Unmade glass out of a back room off the Counting-Quays and keeps a running (accurate, hand-updated) almanac of Between-Hours. Speaks about the Rite with total honesty about its cost, to anyone who'll actually listen before asking the price. **Combat Relevance:** none; Sage-like profile, unarmed and uninterested in danger.

*(This NPC is original to `/Orrun` and does not appear in `ai_solo_campaign/00_control/NAMING_REGISTRY.md`'s campaign NPC rosters — if used in "The Long Remembering," check the registry first per project rules before treating them as established campaign canon.)*

## Archetype: The One Who Went and Didn't Come Back Right

A cautionary NPC: someone from the party's home settlement who attempted the Rite — usually for a Far Telling, sometimes for the Last Telling — and returned changed. Useful for foreshadowing the Rite's real cost before the party attempts it themselves.

**Tier:** Minor to Secondary (DM's choice based on how much the scene needs)
**Role:** Walking warning sign
**What changed:** Pick one — missing a memory that clearly mattered (they don't know what they lost, only that something's gone); still carrying a level of unshakeable exhaustion; convinced part of them is still there; occasionally speaks a name that means nothing to anyone else.
**How they enter play:** Local color in any settlement large enough to have heard of the practice; a resource if treated kindly (they know what it's actually like); a liability if the party asks the wrong question at the wrong time.
**Combat Relevance:** none by default (commoner-like).

## Using "Meeting Yourself"

The Unmade makes it possible, in principle, for a party to encounter a version of a PC (or a known NPC) from another Telling. This is a powerful trope — use it rarely and deliberately:

- **Do it for stakes, not spectacle.** A Telling-self should reveal something the player can use (a choice they didn't make, a cost they didn't pay, a warning) — not just be a mirror-match combat encounter.
- **Never require it.** Plenty of Unmade excursions should have nothing to do with the party's own alternate selves.
- **Keep agency with the player.** If a PC meets a Telling-self, let the player decide how their character reacts — don't script the emotional beat for them.
- **It's cosmologically fine, not confusing:** a Telling-self is simply what that PC's Remembrance became under a different history. It doesn't imply anything about souls, clones, or duplication that needs special new rules — it's the same character concept, differently lived.
