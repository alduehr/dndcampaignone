## AI Use

The ordinary animals of Orrun: what lives where, how dangerous it actually is to one traveler, and how it behaves when it meets people. Use this with `WANDERING_ENCOUNTERS_BY_TERRAIN.md` whenever a [Beast] row comes up.

**Two kinds of entry appear here:**

- **Referenced creatures** — standard published D&D 5e beasts, named with a source and given only the play-relevant notes (habitat, role, tactics, solo danger, adjustment). Per `RULESET_ASSUMPTIONS.md`, stat blocks are **not** transcribed; pull the numbers from the book.
- **Local variants** — a handful of Orrun-flavored reskins, each written as an abbreviated profile that states the **published chassis it is built on**. No creature here runs on invented mechanics.

Source shorthand: **2024 MM** = the current Monster Manual; **MM** = the older core Monster Manual where a creature exists only there.

## The Wildlife Rules Of This World

1. **Animals are not monsters.** They want food, territory, or their young left alone. Almost every beast encounter has a non-violent solution: back away, give up the kill, make noise, make yourself large, throw food, or take the long way.
2. **Nothing hunts an armed adult by preference.** Predators take the sick, the small, the lone, and the sleeping. A healthy party is a bad meal; a wounded traveler alone at dusk is a different proposition — and the fiction should say so before initiative.
3. **Hunger changes everything.** Late winter, drought, after a fire, or with a disturbed range, ordinary animals do extraordinary things. Say so.
4. **Morale always applies.** A hurt animal disengages. A pack that loses one member reconsiders. A mother defending young does not — but she also stops the moment you leave.

## Quick Picks By Terrain

| Terrain | Everyday | Real threat | Rare / apex |
|---|---|---|---|

## Forest And Woodland

**Wolf** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/4) — the standard woodland and highland pack hunter; fits every forest and upland in Orrun. **Role:** pack skirmisher. **Solo danger:** a lone wolf is a warning sign, not a threat; 3–4 wolves is a genuine level 1–2 fight and a real decision at level 3. **Tactics:** shadow the traveler for a mile first (telegraph — sign, then glimpses, then presence), cut off a straggler, try to drop one target and harry the rest. **Morale:** breaks when half the pack drops, when fire is used decisively, or when the prey reaches a defensible spot. **Solo adjustment:** run 2 wolves at level 1, 3–4 at level 2–3; above level 4 use them as color or as a *reason to hurry*, not a fight.

**Boar** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/4) and **Giant Boar** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 2) — woodland and field edges, everywhere farms meet trees. **Role:** brute, charge-and-gore. **Solo danger:** a boar is a nasty surprise at level 1 (it does not stop when hurt — that is its whole trick); a giant boar is a serious level 3–4 fight for one PC. **Tactics:** holds ground, warns with sound and posture, charges once, and either commits or leaves. **Morale:** stubborn — relentless enough to finish a charge, but it will leave a fight it is losing badly if given room. **Non-combat out:** boars want the rooting-ground, not you. Yield the trail and the encounter ends.

**Black Bear** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/2) and **Brown Bear** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1) — deep woods and highland forest. **Role:** brute. **Solo danger:** low–moderate at level 2+, real at level 1. **Tactics:** most bear encounters are a *bluff contest*: it wants your food or its kill defended. **Non-combat out:** noise, size, and slow retreat resolve most of these — the food or carcass is what it wants. **Solo adjustment:** never spring a bear at melee range; always give the smell, the sound, or the sign first.

**Giant Spider** (2024 MM, Monstrosity, CR 1) — old-growth hollows, hollow trees, ruined structures, cave mouths. **Role:** ambusher/controller. **Solo danger:** moderate for a lone PC at levels 1–3 — webbing a solo traveler is genuinely dangerous, so telegraph heavily (silence, webbing, husked prey) and keep it to one or two. **Morale:** retreats to its web-nest when hurt rather than pursuing.

**Swarm of Insects / Swarm of Rats / Swarm of Bats** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/4–1/2) — see also *Fly-Cloud* below. **Role:** attrition hazard. **Solo danger:** low, but they punish a wounded lone traveler and ruin rests. **Non-combat out:** smoke, water, fire, or simply leaving the area.

**Deer, Hares, Songbirds, Foxes** (Beast, CR 0) — not threats; use them as the world's **alarm system**. When the small animals stop, something is there.

## Wetland, Fen, And Marsh

**Giant Frog** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/4) and **Giant Toad** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1) — reed-beds, peat pools, flooded ground. **Role:** ambusher; the toad's swallow is genuinely alarming to one PC. **Solo danger:** low (frog) / moderate (toad) at levels 1–3. **Telegraph:** a call that stops, a wake in the reeds, bones on the bank. **Morale:** breaks quickly when hurt; both prefer easier prey.

**Constrictor Snake** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/4) and **Giant Constrictor Snake** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 2) — deep fen and slow water. **Role:** grappler/lurker. **Solo danger:** the giant version against a lone PC in water is one of the most dangerous mundane animals in the setting (grapple plus drowning). Telegraph it, and give bank, shallow water, or a companion as an out. **Non-combat out:** it is ambush-dependent; once seen, it usually withdraws.

**Poisonous Snake / Giant Poisonous Snake** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/8–1/4) — everywhere warm and wet. **Role:** hazard more than adversary. **Solo danger:** low damage, real consequence — a snakebite far from a healer is a travel problem (see `ENVIRONMENTAL_HAZARDS.md` on poison and infection).

**Crocodile** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/2) and **Giant Crocodile** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 5) — the deltas, the warm south, and any large slow river. **Role:** ambusher/drowner. **Solo danger:** moderate (crocodile) at levels 2–4; the giant is a **level 7+ threat and should be treated as a reason not to swim**, telegraphed by local warnings, livestock losses, and drag-marks. **Non-combat out:** cross elsewhere, cross in daylight, cross with a pole and noise, hire a punt.

**Swarm of Insects (biting flies/mosquitoes)** — see *Fly-Cloud* below; the fen's signature nuisance.

## Coast And Tideline

**Reef Shark** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/2) and **Hunter Shark** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 2) — shallows and open water respectively. **Role:** water skirmisher / big-water threat. **Solo danger:** low if you are in a boat; high the moment you are not. **Tactics:** circles, bumps, tests, and only then commits — always give a round of warning. **Morale:** leaves for easier prey when hurt or when the water clears of blood.

**Giant Crab** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/8) and **Giant Octopus** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1) — rock pools, wrecks, and sea-caves. **Role:** grappler/lurker. **Solo danger:** the octopus grappling a swimmer is the real danger; on land or in a boat it is minor and prefers to flee behind ink.

**Swarm of Quippers** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1) — river mouths and shoals, especially where something has been thrown in the water. **Role:** attrition hazard for anyone in the water. **Non-combat out:** get out of the water. That is the whole encounter.

**Gulls, Crows, Ravens, Seals** (Beast, CR 0–1/8) — not threats; the coast's alarm system and salvage-finders. Birds working a spot on the rocks means *something is there*.

**Blood Hawk** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/8) — cliffs and headlands; aggressive in numbers near nests. **Solo danger:** low; a nuisance that can drive a climber off a bad ledge, which is where the real danger is.

## Highland And Mountain

**Wolf** — see Forest. Highland packs are bolder and hungrier in winter.

**Giant Eagle** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1) and **Eagle/Hawk** (Beast, CR 0–1/8) — crags and thermals. **Role:** diving skirmisher. **Solo danger:** low, except to a climber or someone on a ledge (the *fall* is the threat, not the bird). **Note:** giant eagles in this world are intelligent enough to be talked past, bribed with meat, or simply respected — a fight is a failure state.

**Mountain Goat / Wild Sheep / Elk** (Beast, CR 0–1/4) and **Giant Elk** (MM, Beast, CR 2) — not predators, but a rutting bull or a defended flock will hurt someone. **Non-combat out:** trivial; go around. **Use them** to show a climbable route or a hidden water source.

**Big Cat (Panther/Lion-kin)** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/4–1) — cliff country, upland scrub, and the warm south. **Role:** stalker/ambusher. **Solo danger:** moderate to a lone traveler; the ambush is the danger, so telegraph with kills cached in trees or on ledges, prints, and the silence of other animals. **Morale:** a cat that loses surprise usually leaves.

**Giant Vulture** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1) — high country and waste edges. **Role:** scavenger, occasional opportunist. **Solo danger:** low; but circling vultures are a **map marker** — something died, and something may have killed it.

## Plains, Downs, Road, And Farmland

**Feral Dogs / Mastiff-kin** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/8) — near every settlement, ruin, and abandoned steading. **Role:** pack skirmisher, but *used to people* — which makes them bolder and more negotiable than wolves. **Solo danger:** low; 4+ is a real problem for level 1. **Non-combat out:** food, dominance, a thrown stone, or a stern voice; these were somebody's dogs once.

**Jackals / Hyena-kin** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 0–1/2; **Giant Hyena** CR 1) — dry plains and the south. **Role:** scavenger-pack; follows travelers waiting for weakness. **Morale:** cowardly in the open, dangerous around a camp at night.

**Giant Rat / Swarm of Rats** (2024 MM, Beast, CR 1/8–1/4) — barns, granaries, cellars, ruins, and roadside middens. **Role:** vermin/attrition. **Non-combat out:** fire, light, and doors.

**Cattle, Draft Horses, Oxen, Guard Dogs** (Beast, CR 0–1/4) — the most common "combat" in settled country is somebody's bull, somebody's dog, or a spooked team. Treat as a skill problem (Animal Handling DC 12–15) rather than a fight.

## Local Variants (Orrun flavor, published chassis)

These are reskins with stated anchors, in the project's abbreviated profile style. Use them when the ordinary animal wants a name.

### 1. Fly-Cloud (fen and delta biting swarm)

*Found in: fens, deltas, flooded paddy, still water in warm months.* The reason nobody sane crosses fen country in high summer without smoke.

- **Type/Size:** Beast (swarm of Tiny beasts), Medium. **Role:** attrition hazard. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/4–1/2.
- **Baseline:** use a **Swarm of Insects (2024 MM)** profile, modified: no meaningful damage spike, but it *follows*.
- **AC** 12 · **HP** 12–22 · **Speed** 20 ft., fly 20 ft.
- **Saves/Skills:** none of note. **Resist/Immune:** the usual swarm resistances (bludgeoning/piercing/slashing resistance; immune to being grappled, restrained, prone).
- **Senses:** blindsight 10 ft. **Languages:** none.
- **Actions:** *Bites* (1d4 piercing in its space, half at reduced strength); its real weapon is *persistence*.
- **Traits:** *Won't Leave* — it re-forms and re-follows each round unless smoke, running water, hard wind, or open flame is used; *Rest-Breaker* — a night in a fly-cloud costs a long rest's benefit unless smoked out (Survival DC 12) or netted (a cost of 5 sp of gear).
- **Tactics:** no tactics; it is weather with a hunger.
- **Morale:** n/a — driven off, not defeated.
- **Non-combat outs:** smoke (Survival DC 12), fen-grease or herb salve bought in any fen village (2–5 sp), a net-tent, moving to open wind or moving water.
- **Solo danger:** low damage, moderate *cost*. **Scaling:** at levels 6+, treat purely as a rest-and-resource tax, never a fight.

### 2. Mire-Lurker (fen ambush predator)

*Found in: deep fen, reed-mat, slow drowned channels.* Locals just call it "the long one in the reeds" and will tell you which pools to avoid — for a drink, or for free.

- **Type/Size:** Beast, Large. **Role:** ambusher/grappler. **CR-equivalent:** ~2.
- **Baseline:** use a **Giant Constrictor Snake (2024 MM)** profile, modified: shorter and heavier, better camouflaged, less inclined to pursue onto dry land.
- **AC** 12 · **HP** 50–60 · **Speed** 25 ft., swim 30 ft.
- **Saves/Skills:** Dex-leaning; Stealth (strong in water and reed).
- **Resist/Immune:** none. **Senses:** blindsight 10 ft. **Languages:** none.
- **Actions:** *Bite* (~2d6 piercing); *Constrict* (grapple, then squeeze; a grappled swimmer must also manage the water).
- **Traits:** *Reed-Hide* (advantage on Stealth in fen cover; Perception DC 15 to spot before it strikes, DC 12 if the traveler is deliberately reading the water); *Shallow-Bound* (will not follow onto firm ground for more than a few yards).
- **Tactics:** one strike from cover, grapple, and drag toward deep water. If it cannot get the grapple in two rounds, it disengages.
- **Morale:** disengages at half HP; will not fight for a meal it has lost.
- **Non-combat outs:** avoid the pool entirely (a local guide, a Survival DC 12 read of the water, or a warning at any fen village); throw it a carcass; make the shallows loud and unpleasant.
- **Solo danger:** **high in water, low on land** — this is the central fact to telegraph. **Scaling:** levels 2–4 as written; levels 6+, reduce to a hazard the traveler routes around.

### 3. Crag-Stooper (highland raptor)

*Found in: cliffs, passes, and sea-stacks.* Big enough to take a lamb, bold enough to test a climber.

- **Type/Size:** Beast, Medium. **Role:** diving skirmisher / climbing hazard. **CR-equivalent:** ~1.
- **Baseline:** use a **Giant Eagle (2024 MM)** profile, modified: non-speaking, purely animal, territorial in nesting season only.
- **AC** 13 · **HP** 26–30 · **Speed** 10 ft., fly 80 ft.
- **Saves/Skills:** Perception (excellent). **Senses:** keen sight. **Languages:** none.
- **Actions:** *Talons* (~1d6+2 slashing); *Stoop* (a diving attack that ends out of reach).
- **Traits:** *Nest-Bound* (aggressive only within ~300 ft of a nest, and only in spring); *Knock-Off* — against a climbing or ledge-bound target, it attempts to force a Dexterity save (DC 13) rather than deal damage. **The fall is the encounter.**
- **Tactics:** dives, breaks off, dives again; never lands to brawl.
- **Morale:** leaves after two failed passes or any real injury.
- **Non-combat outs:** route below the nest line (Nature/Survival DC 12 to spot it in time), throw it meat, raise a pack or a cloak overhead to look too large.
- **Solo danger:** low in the open, **moderate–high on a cliff**. **Scaling:** levels 1–4 as written; higher levels, keep it purely as a climbing complication.

### 4. Strand-Crab (coastal scavenger)

*Found in: shingle, rock-pools, wreck-fields, and sea-caves.* Comes for the dead and is not fussy about how recently.

- **Type/Size:** Beast, Medium–Large. **Role:** slow brute / scavenger. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/4–1.
- **Baseline:** use a **Giant Crab (2024 MM)** profile, scaled up one step for the biggest wreck-field specimens.
- **AC** 15 (shell) · **HP** 13–30 · **Speed** 25 ft., swim 25 ft.
- **Resist/Immune:** none, but the shell is the point — high AC, low output.
- **Actions:** *Claw* (~1d6 bludgeoning plus grapple).
- **Traits:** *Armored And Slow* (hard to hurt, easy to outwalk); *Comes For The Fallen* (drawn to blood and carrion — dangerous mainly to the unconscious).
- **Tactics:** advances, pinches, holds. Does not chase.
- **Morale:** withdraws into rock or water when hurt.
- **Non-combat outs:** walk away; give it the carcass; flip it (Athletics DC 13).
- **Solo danger:** low — unless someone is down. **Scaling:** groups of 2–3 at levels 3+; never more against one PC.

### 5. The Named Beast (template)

*Found in: anywhere the locals have lost enough livestock to start using a name.* Use this template when a district needs a **problem with a face** — a bounty, a rite of passage, or a reason the west road is closed.

- **Type/Size:** as the base animal. **Role:** local boss-beast. **CR-equivalent:** base CR +1 to +2.
- **Baseline:** take any beast above and modify: **+25–50% HP**, **+1 to hit and damage**, one scar-derived trait, and a **habit** the locals can describe.
- **Signature habit (pick one):** takes only livestock, never people · only hunts in fog or at dusk · has learned that fire means food · avoids one specific crossing where it was once hurt · does not fear dogs.
- **Traits:** *Wary* (has survived hunters — it will not walk into an obvious ambush; Survival DC 15 to out-think it); *Known* (any local can describe its habits for the price of a drink — this is the intended prep step).
- **Tactics:** fights only on ground it chose. If the ground is taken from it, it leaves.
- **Morale:** breaks at half HP and *remembers* — a Named Beast driven off returns later, warier, which is the point of naming it.
- **Non-combat outs:** trap or pit (Survival DC 13 to build, Nature DC 13 to site it), poisoned bait, driving it out of the district, fencing/penning the livestock it actually wants, or paying a hunter.
- **Solo danger:** moderate — but the intended play is **preparation**, not a stand-up fight. Always let scouting, local knowledge, and terrain matter more than hit points.
- **Scaling:** levels 1–3: an oversized boar, wolf, or bear. Levels 4–7: a giant-tier version with two habits. Levels 8+: use the region's own material instead; a mundane animal stops being a story at that point.

## Handling Animals Without Fighting

| Approach | Check | Typical DC | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read the sign; know what's out there | Survival / Nature | 10–13 | Learn the species, number, and how recent |
| Avoid a known range | Survival | 12 | Detour costs a few hours, no encounter |
| Back down a bluffing predator | Intimidation / Animal Handling | 13 | It withdraws; no fight |
| Calm a spooked domestic animal | Animal Handling | 12–15 | Prevents a stampede, bolt, or bite |
| Distract with food or a carcass | Animal Handling / Survival | 12 | It takes the easier meal |
| Site a camp animals avoid | Survival | 13 | No night-camp beast roll |
| Track a beast to its den | Survival | 15 | Sets up an ambush on *your* terms |
| Drive a beast out of a district | Survival + a plan | 15–18 | Solves the problem without killing it |

## Solo-Play Reminders

- Always **sign, then sight, then threat** — three beats before an animal fight starts.
- A wounded lone traveler should meet *scavengers*, not apex predators, unless the fiction has already earned it.
- Beast encounters end when the beast's goal is met or denied — they should almost never be fights to the death.
- Never open with a swarm, a grapple, or a grapple-and-drown against a lone PC without a prior warning and an escape line.
