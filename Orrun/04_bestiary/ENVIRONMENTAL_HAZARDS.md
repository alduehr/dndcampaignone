## AI Use

The ground, the water, and the weather of Orrun, with D&D 5e/2024-compatible DCs and effects. Use these when a [Hazard] row comes up, when travel needs stakes without a fight, or when a player asks whether something is risky. In a world where most of the continent is fen, coast, upland, and bad road, **terrain is the most common adversary in play**.

## DC Bands (project standard)

| DC | Band | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | Easy | Anyone competent; a check only because there is a cost to failing |
| 13 | Moderate–low | The ordinary bad step, the ordinary bad weather |
| 15 | Moderate | Real risk, correctly signposted |
| 18 | Hard | Bad conditions, bad ground, or bad timing |
| 20 | Hard+ | Doing it at night, in a storm, wounded, or carrying someone |
| 25 | Very hard | Should almost never be the only way through |

**Never gate progress behind a single roll.** Every hazard below has a slow-and-safe option, a costly bypass, or a way to try again.

## The Four Hazard Rules

1. **Telegraph.** The traveler always gets a describable warning: the reeds change color, the rock is wet, the sky greens, the tide-mark is above your head, the bridge timbers are soft.
2. **Failure costs, then escalates.** A failed check costs time, gear, a level of exhaustion, or a modest amount of damage. A *second* failure, after the situation was clearly worse, is when real harm lands.
3. **No instant death.** Falls have ledges, water has current and bank, mires have stages, cold has hours. A lone traveler should always get at least one save, one grab, or one round to act.
4. **Preparation matters more than the roll.** Rope, poles, a guide, local advice, the right hour, and the right season should each be worth advantage or a flat +2 to +5 on the DC, or should skip the check entirely.

## 1. Forest And Woodland

| Hazard | Check | DC | Effect on failure | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Blowdown / deadfall tangle** | Athletics or Survival | 12 | Half speed for the hour, or 1d4 bludgeoning and a torn pack going over | Go around (2 hours), axe work |
| **Losing the trail under canopy** | Survival | 13 (15 in fog/night) | Add 1d4 hours; risk of arriving at dusk | Blazes, a guide, following water |
| **Rotten limb / widow-maker in wind** | Perception to notice | 14 | 2d6 bludgeoning, Dex DC 13 for half | Do not camp under old trees in wind |
| **Snare or deadfall (poacher-set)** | Perception | 13 | 1d6 damage and restrained until freed (Athletics DC 12) | Watch the game-trails |
| **Forest fire (dry season)** | Survival to read the wind | 15 | Smoke: Con DC 13 or 1 exhaustion; being caught is a chase, not a save | Move crosswind, reach water or bare ground |
| **Bad water (still pools, stock ponds)** | Nature/Medicine to judge | 12 | Con DC 13 or lose a day to illness (disadvantage on Con checks, half speed) | Running water, boiling, a waterskin filled at a village |

**Woodland fires** should always be run as a **chase toward safety**, not a damage roll — the decisions are direction, pace, and what you abandon.

## 2. Wetland, Fen, And Marsh

The signature terrain of much of Orrun and the most reliably dangerous ordinary ground in the setting.

| Hazard | Check | DC | Effect on failure | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Soft ground / mire (stage 1)** | Survival to read, Athletics to cross | 13 | In to the knees: speed halved, drop something, lose 30 minutes | A pole, testing each step, following cut-reed markers |
| **Sinking ground (stage 2)** | Athletics | 15 | Waist-deep and sinking; each further failure worsens a stage | Rope, a companion, lying flat and rolling out |
| **Sinking ground (stage 3)** | Athletics, with help or a rope | 18 (12 with rope) | Chest-deep: Con checks for exhaustion, then real drowning risk **after at least two clear warnings** | Rope thrown from firm ground ends it |
| **Fen-fire / marsh lights** | Wisdom or Survival | 13 | You follow it; you are now in stage-2 ground, off the path | Never chase a light off a marked way; local rule of thumb |
| **Fog on the fen** | Survival to hold direction | 14 | Lost: 1d4+1 hours, a random encounter roll, possibly a night out | Wait it out, mark the way, follow water |
| **Fever water / bad ground (warm season)** | Con save after exposure | 13 | Sick for 1d3 days: disadvantage on Con and Str checks, half travel speed | Boiled water, local herb (2–5 sp), staying dry |
| **Rot-foot (days in wet boots)** | Con save per 3 days wet | 12 | 1 level of exhaustion until a dry day and dry boots | Dry socks, a fire, a stilt-hut night |
| **Peat cut / hidden channel** | Perception | 14 | Fall of 5–10 ft into cold water: 1d6, gear soaked, spell components ruined | Probe ahead, walk the ridge lines |

**Fen safety valve:** anywhere near settlement, a local guide (2–5 sp a day) removes almost all of this. That is the intended answer to fen travel, and villages will say so.

## 3. Coast, Tideline, And Water

| Hazard | Check | DC | Effect on failure | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tide-timing on a strand route** | Survival / seamanship | 13 (15 unfamiliar) | Cut off — wade a rising channel (Athletics DC 15) or wait 8 hours on a rock | Ask anyone local; watch the weed-line |
| **Tide-locked cave** | Survival to time the entry | 15 | Trapped for a tide cycle; cold and rising water; **never lethal on the first cycle** | A marked stick at the entrance, going at low water |
| **Cliff-path washout** | Athletics or Acrobatics | 14 | Slip: Dex DC 13 or fall 20–40 ft (2d6–4d6), ledges available | Rope, going one at a time, the inland detour (3 hours) |
| **Sea-fog on a cliff road** | Survival | 14 | Lost or dangerously close to the edge; next hazard at +2 DC | Stop and wait; follow the wall side |
| **Surf and undertow** | Athletics (swim) | 15 | Dragged out; Athletics DC 13 each round, aided by any thrown line | Do not swim in surf; boats, ropes, and waiting |
| **Cold-water immersion** | Con save | 13, rising +1 per 10 min | 1 level of exhaustion per failure; hypothermia over an hour | Get out, get dry, get a fire; a warmed blanket ends it |
| **Squall at sea (small boat)** | Seamanship (Survival/Vehicles) | 16 | Swamped or driven off course; gear lost overboard | Reef the sail, run for a lee shore, stay in port |
| **False lights on a reef** | Wisdom or Perception | 14 | Onto the rocks — a wreck scene, not a death | Know the real light's pattern; ask before sailing |

**Rule:** at sea, weather and tide should push the unprepared **off the water alive** — stranded, soaked, robbed of cargo — long before they kill anyone.

## 6. Weather (applies over any terrain)

| Condition | Mechanical effect |
|---|---|
| **Heavy rain / snowfall** | Disadvantage on Perception (sight and hearing); ranged attacks at disadvantage beyond normal range; navigation DC +2 |
| **Fog** | Vision under 30 ft; navigation DC +2 to +4; ambush and *evasion* both easier |
| **Strong wind** | Ranged attacks at disadvantage; open flames out; flying and climbing DC +2 |
| **Extreme cold** | Con save DC 10 each hour without cold-weather gear or fire; failure = 1 exhaustion |
| **Extreme heat** | Con save DC 10 each hour with insufficient water (DC rises with armor and exertion); failure = 1 exhaustion |
| **Storm at night** | Combine: no rest benefit without real shelter; every other DC in this file +2 |

**Weather should change the decision** — camp, reroute, pay for shelter, hire a guide, or push on and pay for it — rather than simply subtracting hit points.

## 7. Rest, Recovery, And Exhaustion In The Field

- **A long rest requires shelter, dryness, and reasonable warmth.** Wet, cold, fly-plagued, or hunted travelers get the time but not the benefit — this is the main mechanical teeth of overland travel in this setting.
- **Exhaustion is the standard currency of environmental failure.** Prefer it to damage: it is recoverable, it is dramatic, and it makes shelter valuable.
- **A village, a wayhouse, a bothy, a stilt-hut, or a shepherd's shelter is always within reach in settled country.** Frontier and deep wilderness is where that stops being true, and travelers should be told so before they enter it.
- **Anyone at 3+ levels of exhaustion in bad country should be offered a rescue vector** — a herder, a hunter, a road, a light. Attrition should hand out consequences, not corpses.

## Scaling Notes

| Level band | Handling |
|---|---|
| **1–4** | Hazards as written. They are the primary threat of travel at this tier; a fen crossing is a real adventure. |
| **5–10** | Raise DCs by 2 for the same hazards, or move to the harder variants (a squall instead of rain, a whiteout instead of cloud). Magic starts to solve categories of hazard — let it; reward the preparation. |
| **11–16** | Ordinary terrain is no longer a threat. Use hazards for **texture, resource cost, and NPC endangerment** — the flood that threatens a village the PC must save, not the PC. |
| **17–20** | Use hazards only at world scale (a storm that closes a coast, a season that starves a region). Personal terrain risk is gone, and pretending otherwise wastes the table's time. |
