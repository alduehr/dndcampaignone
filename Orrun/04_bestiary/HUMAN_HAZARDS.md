## AI Use

Generic human and humanoid threats for travel and frontier play in Orrun. These are **threat types, not organizations** — no named band, no faction, no livery. Give any group here a leader with a name, a grievance, and a price, and it will carry a session.

Profiles are abbreviated and reference-style per `RULESET_ASSUMPTIONS.md`: "use a [baseline]-like profile, modified as follows." Baselines referenced are the standard published humanoid adversaries (Bandit, Thug, Scout, Guard, Veteran, Spy, Bandit Captain, Commoner, Acolyte, Berserker).

## The Rule That Governs All Of Them

**Almost nobody on this list wants to kill you.** They want cargo, coin, a boat, a share, a body for a crew, silence, or to not be arrested. Killing is expensive: it brings bailiffs, feuds, and the rope. Every group below has a **price** and a **breaking point**, and both should be discoverable before initiative.

Consequently, every entry has:
- What they actually want (the negotiable thing).
- What makes them fight anyway (the line you should not cross).
- What they do with someone they beat (which is usually **not** murder — rob, strip, ransom, press, maroon, or turn over).

## Common Threat Profiles At A Glance

| Group | Terrain | CR-equivalent | Wants | Solo danger | First out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Toll-bandits | road, ford, hedge | 1/8–1/2 each | a fee | low | pay, argue the price down |
| Wreckers | coast, reef | 1/8–3 | salvage and silence | mod–high | be an unprofitable witness |
| Poachers | forest, moor | 1/8–1/2 | to not be reported | low | not care about the lord's deer |

## 1. Toll-Bandits (the road's ordinary tax)

*Found at: fords, hedge-gaps, bridge-heads, forest narrows, and anywhere the going is slow.* Three to six people with a rope across the road and an agreed fee. Often locals; often known to the nearest village; often more afraid of the traveler than the traveler is of them.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** shakedown / skirmisher. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/8–1/2 each; a leader at ~1–2.
- **Baseline:** use **Bandit-like** profiles, with a **Thug-like** or **Bandit Captain-like** leader.
- **AC** 12–14 · **HP** 11–16 (leader 30–40) · **Speed** 30.
- **Saves/Skills:** Dex-leaning; Intimidation, Stealth, Survival.
- **Senses:** normal. **Languages:** the local common tongue.
- **Actions:** spear, hand-axe, or short bow (~1d6+1); the leader hits harder and shouts orders.
- **Traits:** *A Price, Not A Grudge* (they name a fee, usually 1–5 gp or a share of goods, and will haggle); *Ambush Position* (they choose ground where running is awkward — but they never fully block the way back).
- **Tactics:** show numbers, name the price, let one person do the talking, keep an archer visible. If a fight starts they aim to *disable and take*, not kill.
- **Morale:** **fragile.** They break when the leader falls, when two of them drop, when armed help appears, or when the traveler is obviously more dangerous than the take is worth.
- **Non-combat outs:** pay · haggle (Persuasion DC 12; DC 10 if you know the going rate) · intimidate (DC 13, but this makes an enemy who remembers) · point out that the nearest bailiff is a day away and you are going that way anyway (Deception/Persuasion DC 13) · buy them off with food or drink, which is often what they actually need.
- **Solo danger:** low. Four bandits against a level 1 PC is deadly on paper — which is exactly why they should be **negotiable**, why the leader should talk first, and why they break early.
- **Scaling:** L1–2: three, with a nervous leader, and the fee is small. L3–5: six, with a Thug-like leader and a lookout. L6+: they get out of your way and remember your face — or they have become the *deserter band* below.
- **Loot:** the pooled tolls (2d10 gp), stolen goods someone in the next village will recognize, and a rough knowledge of who else uses this road.
- **If ignored:** the road's reputation worsens, carters reroute, and prices in the nearest village rise. Within a season, either the local lord clears them out violently or they grow.

## 2. Wreckers (the coast's worst habit)

*Found at: headlands, reefs, and any shore where a false light can put a hull on rocks.* Coastal communities in hard years show a light where no light should be, then take everything that comes ashore. The dangerous part is not the taking — it is that **survivors are witnesses**.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** ambushers / salvage crew. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/8–1 each; a wreck-master at ~2–3.
- **Baseline:** **Bandit-like** and **Commoner-like** (most are fishers), with a **Thug-like** or **Veteran-like** wreck-master.
- **AC** 11–14 · **HP** 9–22 (wreck-master 45–60) · **Speed** 30, swim 20 (they know the water and you do not).
- **Saves/Skills:** Athletics, Survival, Perception; excellent knowledge of tide and rock.
- **Actions:** gaff, boat-hook, knife, or a sling (~1d6+1); on the water, they use *the sea* rather than weapons.
- **Traits:** *Tide-Wise* (they can time a strand, a cave, and a channel perfectly — the terrain fights for them); *Everyone's In It* (a whole hamlet may be complicit, so there is no obvious authority to appeal to); *The Witness Problem* (their willingness to kill scales with how identifiable you are).
- **Tactics:** never a stand-up fight. They wait for the wreck, strip fast, and only confront people who catch them at it. Given a choice they strand, maroon, or scare off rather than kill in the open.
- **Morale:** breaks fast if the community is split (there is always someone who hates it), or once an outside authority is clearly already informed.
- **Non-combat outs:** be visibly unprofitable · make it clear others know where you are (Deception/Persuasion DC 13) · find the dissenter (Insight DC 13 to spot who is sick of this; usually a woman with a drowned relative) · buy the salvage instead of accusing anyone (Persuasion DC 12) · report it to a harbor authority a day's ride away and let them do it.
- **Solo danger:** moderate on shore, **high on their water or in a tide-locked cave** — the environment is the threat (`ENVIRONMENTAL_HAZARDS.md`).
- **Scaling:** L2–4: four wreckers stripping a small boat. L5–7: a redoubt of a dozen with a wreck-master and lookouts, taken by stealth, split, or law — not by frontal assault. L8+: a coast-wide problem to be solved politically.
- **Loot:** salvage (cargo worth 50–500 gp but *identifiable*), ship's papers, a manifest naming an owner who will pay to know.
- **If ignored:** the next wreck is deliberate, and the next after that has survivors who are not allowed to be survivors.

## 3. Poachers (the least dangerous people on this list)

*Found in: any lord's wood, warren, or fishery.* Two or three locals with snares, nets, and a great deal to lose. Their danger is entirely about **whether you will report them**.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** social encounter with a knife in it. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/8–1/2.
- **Baseline:** **Commoner-like** with a **Scout-like** best hunter among them.
- **AC** 11–13 · **HP** 9–16 · **Speed** 30.
- **Skills:** Stealth, Survival, Nature (they are genuinely excellent in their own wood).
- **Actions:** bow or knife (~1d6+2); snares and deadfalls already placed.
- **Traits:** *Home Ground* (advantage on Stealth and Survival here; they can lose anyone in this wood); *Everything To Lose* (the penalty for poaching in most of Orrun is maiming, transportation, or hanging — that is why they might do something stupid).
- **Tactics:** hide first. Talk second. Threaten third. Fight only if they believe you are going straight to the reeve.
- **Morale:** breaks instantly at any real resistance — they scatter into cover and are gone.
- **Non-combat outs:** promise silence (Persuasion DC 10, or free if the PC obviously doesn't care about the lord's deer) · buy meat cheap · trade the promise for guiding, local knowledge, or a shortcut — **poachers are the best guides in any wood** and this is their intended play function.
- **Solo danger:** low. **Scaling:** stays low forever; at higher levels they become a contact, not a threat.
- **Loot:** venison, hides, snare-wire, and a map of the wood no one else has.
- **If ignored:** nothing bad. Report them and a family is destroyed — a real moral choice with no monster in it.

## 4. Deserters And Broken Soldiers

*Found in: the hinterland of any war, border dispute, disbanded levy, or failed expedition.* Trained, armed, hungry, and without a future. The most genuinely dangerous *ordinary* people in the setting — because they have discipline and no longer have anything to lose.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** disciplined skirmishers. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/2–3 each; a sergeant at ~3–5.
- **Baseline:** **Guard-like** (the young ones), **Veteran-like** (the sergeant), occasionally **Berserker-like** (the one who broke).
- **AC** 14–17 (they kept their kit) · **HP** 22–60 · **Speed** 30.
- **Saves/Skills:** Athletics, Perception, Intimidation; **they use formations, cover, and flanks correctly.**
- **Actions:** spear/sword and shield (~1d8+3, often multiattack at veteran tier); crossbows on the flank.
- **Traits:** *Still Soldiers* (they post sentries, hold a line, and do not scatter when one falls — the tactical opposite of bandits); *Nothing To Go Back To* (they cannot surrender to authority, so surrender must be offered by *someone else*); *Hungry* (food is the real currency here).
- **Tactics:** occupy something defensible — a bridge, a steading, a mill. They demand supplies, not treasure. They fight competently and stop the moment the objective is achieved.
- **Morale:** **the hardest morale on this list.** They break only when the sergeant is down or when offered something better than this — a pardon, a patron, work, or passage out of the region.
- **Non-combat outs:** feed them (this genuinely works, Persuasion DC 12 with food in hand) · offer employment or a route to amnesty (DC 15) · reach the sergeant's grievance (Insight DC 14 — there is always one: unpaid wages, a betrayal, a massacre) · bring news that their war is over.
- **Solo danger:** **moderate to high — the one group on this list a lone traveler should probably not fight.** Telegraph with signs: a stripped steading, a picket, a well-kept campfire, boot-prints in step.
- **Scaling:** L3–4: three, one with real training. L5–7: six with a Veteran-like sergeant in a defensible position. L8–10: a company holding a valley, which is a *political* problem, not a fight.
- **Loot:** good military gear, a unit standard worth something to somebody, campaign pay-chits, and testimony about what actually happened out there.
- **If ignored:** they take a village. From there it is a warlord's beginning, and that is how frontier holds are born in this world.

## 5. Raiders (the frontier's periodic weather)

*Found in: any frontier, exposed coast, or steppe-edge march.* Fast, mounted or boat-borne, after portable wealth, livestock, and people. They do not besiege and do not stay.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** fast strike force. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/2–2 each; a raid-leader at ~3–5.
- **Baseline:** **Bandit-like** and **Scout-like** riders/rowers, with a **Berserker-like** or **Veteran-like** leader.
- **AC** 13–16 · **HP** 16–45 (leader 60+) · **Speed** 30 (or mounted 60 / boat).
- **Skills:** Athletics, Survival, Animal Handling or seamanship, Perception.
- **Actions:** axe, spear, bow (~1d8+2), often from horseback or a beached boat; fire as a tool.
- **Traits:** *In And Out* (a raid lasts minutes, not hours — they are gone before help comes, which is the entire mechanical problem); *Take, Don't Hold* (they will not chase a fleeing traveler far — there is nothing in it); *Hostages Are Cargo* (captives are worth more alive, which is a genuine and usable safety valve).
- **Tactics:** hit the edge of a settlement, take what is portable, burn only for cover, withdraw. Against travelers, they take goods and mounts and leave.
- **Morale:** withdraw the moment the raid stops being profitable — organized resistance, a bell, a beacon, or armed riders on the horizon ends it.
- **Non-combat outs:** **do not be in the raid's path** (their approach is always signposted: birds, smoke, riders on a ridge, a boat standing offshore) · warn the settlement, which is the real adventure · buy back captives (this is normal and expected; 10–50 gp a head) · guest-right, hospitality, or a claimed truce where such customs hold (Persuasion/History DC 14).
- **Solo danger:** **high in the raid; low outside it.** A lone traveler's role here is warning, evacuation, rescue, or pursuit — not a stand-up fight against a raiding party.
- **Scaling:** L3–5: a scouting handful. L6–8: a full raid with a leader — run it as a *timed rescue and evacuation*, not a battle. L9+: a raiding season, i.e. a regional problem.
- **Loot:** other people's property (returnable — the best reward here is goodwill), horses or a boat, and the knowledge of where they beach or camp.
- **If ignored:** the settlement is stripped; survivors become refugees; the next season's raid comes further inland because it worked.

## 6. Claim-Jumpers And Scavengers

*Found in: highlands, diggings, ruin-fields, wreck-shores, and anywhere salvage is worth money.* They want the find, and they are willing to be unpleasant about it. They are usually not willing to hang for it.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** rivals, occasionally muscle. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/8–1.
- **Baseline:** **Bandit-like**, **Scout-like**, and **Thug-like**; the smart one is **Spy-like**.
- **AC** 12–14 · **HP** 11–30 · **Speed** 30.
- **Skills:** Athletics, Investigation, Survival, Sleight of Hand.
- **Actions:** picks, hammers, knives (~1d6+2); sabotage, theft, and rumor are their real weapons.
- **Traits:** *Rob, Don't Murder* (their default is to steal the find, sabotage a rope, or move the markers); *Knows The Buyer* (they can name who purchases salvage in the nearest town — a genuinely useful thing to trade for).
- **Tactics:** follow, watch, wait for the party to do the dangerous work, then take it. If confronted, they bluster, then bargain.
- **Morale:** breaks at the first real injury.
- **Non-combat outs:** cut them in (Persuasion DC 12) · hire them (they know the ground) · out-scout them · report them to whoever holds the claim.
- **Solo danger:** low–moderate; the threat is **losing the prize**, not dying.
- **Scaling:** L1–4 as written; higher levels, they become brokers and informants rather than rivals.
- **Loot:** whatever they already stole, plus contacts.
- **If ignored:** they get the find and sell it, and it turns up later in the hands of someone who matters.

## 7. Press-Gangs And Crimps (port hazard)

*Found in: any port, on any night, wherever a ship is short-handed.* They are not bandits; some of them are legal. That is the problem.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** grapplers / abductors. **CR-equivalent:** ~1/2–2.
- **Baseline:** **Thug-like** with a **Spy-like** crimp who does the smiling.
- **AC** 12–14 · **HP** 22–45 · **Speed** 30.
- **Skills:** Athletics, Deception, Insight (they read who will be missed and who won't).
- **Actions:** clubs and nets — **nonlethal by design** (~1d6 bludgeoning, aiming to knock down and carry).
- **Traits:** *Nobody Wanted A Corpse* (they take, they do not kill — a defeated PC wakes at sea, which is an adventure, not an ending); *Reads The Room* (they pick the drunk, the friendless, and the newly arrived).
- **Tactics:** drink first, alley second, net third. Four to six of them, and they know the streets.
- **Morale:** they abandon a target who is more trouble than the bounty — usually two rounds of real resistance.
- **Non-combat outs:** be visibly connected (a name, a badge, a patron — Persuasion/Deception DC 13) · outdrink the crimp and learn the ship instead · report to the harbor authority · take the berth deliberately and get a free passage out of it.
- **Solo danger:** moderate for a lone traveler in a strange port at night — **and the failure state is a voyage, not a death.**
- **Scaling:** constant across levels; a high-level PC simply is not worth the risk, and the crimp knows it at a glance.
- **Loot:** the crimp's list of which captains pay, which is worth more than his purse.

## 8. Road-Cons And Fleecers

*Found in: fairs, shrines, ferry-queues, and the first mile outside any town gate.* False guides, salted relics, rigged games, "tolls" for bridges nobody owns, weeping women with a story, children who bump into you.

- **Type/Size:** Humanoid, Medium. **Role:** social hazard. **CR-equivalent:** ~0–1/8 (a fight here is a failure of the encounter).
- **Baseline:** **Commoner-like**; the good ones are **Spy-like** in social skills only.
- **Skills:** Deception, Sleight of Hand, Performance, Insight.
- **Traits:** *No Violence* (they run at any hint of a fight — that is the trade's first rule); *Plausible* (their story is checkable and half-true).
- **Tactics:** the mark walks into it. Loss is money, time, or a wrong direction — never blood.
- **Non-combat outs:** Insight DC 13 to smell it · Investigation DC 13 to check the story · a local will confirm or deny for free · simply refusing.
- **Solo danger:** none mechanically. The bite is **lost coin, lost time, and a wrong road** — which is a fine complication and an excellent way to seed a real hook.
- **Scaling:** constant; at high levels they target the PC's *retinue* or reputation instead.

## Running Human Encounters Solo

- **Talk before initiative.** Every group above opens with a demand, a question, or a warning. That beat is not optional.
- **Name the price out loud.** A traveler who knows the toll is 2 gp can decide. A traveler who is simply attacked can only fight.
- **Numbers are the danger.** Four ordinary people can kill a level 1–3 character. Because of that, human groups here are written with fragile morale, negotiable goals, and non-lethal defaults — do **not** also add more bodies for challenge (raise one leader's tier instead).
- **Losing should not mean dying.** Robbed, stripped, ransomed, pressed, marooned, tied to a tree, or dumped a day's walk off-route are all better outcomes and all generate play.
- **They remember.** A band beaten, paid, or humiliated is a recurring feature of that road. Note the leader's name.

## Reward And Consequence Notes

| Outcome | Typical reward | Typical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Paid the toll | passage, and often useful road-news | you are known as payable; the fee will be there next time |
| Broke the band | their pooled take, local gratitude, maybe a small bounty (10–50 gp) | survivors, kin, and whoever they were paying |
| Turned the band | guides, informants, a safe road | you now have obligations to unpleasant people |
| Reported them to authority | lawful reward, standing with the local power | a family or a village punished harder than you intended |
| Let them go | information, goodwill, a favor owed | they do it again to someone with less luck |
