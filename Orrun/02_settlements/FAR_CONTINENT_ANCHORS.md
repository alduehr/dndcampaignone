## AI Use

Load when play moves beyond the northwestern frontier and its city, or when a traveller, merchant, or scholar refers to somewhere far away. These are **light anchors**, not deep-built settlements: enough for a scene, a route, a market, a night's lodging, or a plausible answer to "what is it like there?" Deliberately lighter than the northwestern gazetteers. Public-facing only; offices are named, officeholders are not.

## How to Use These

Orrun is large — corner to corner is two to three months' travel by road. The northwest is a cold, poor, ruin-strewn frontier; the settled centre and south are warmer, richer, and older. Everything below is at least weeks from the frontier, and most of it is a season away. Distances should feel real: reaching any of these places is a journey with a plot of its own.

Institutions recur across the continent in local forms: the folk death-faith the northwest calls the **Mourners' Circle** (present everywhere, under many local titles — salt-mothers, fen-witches, moot-mothers, sun-singers); the **Three Thresholds** temples (formal, urban, respectable); the big chartered **merchant-banks** and trade leagues, of which the northwest's **Cinder Ledger** is only one arm; and the scholarly societies that study the fallen Concord, the largest of which is the **Concord Remnant**. Concord ruins are found on every part of the continent and are uniformly old, broken, and unexplained.

## The Verdance Reaches — the river corridor inland

The long river-and-lock corridor that carries trade between the northwestern city and the central river-cities. Prosperous, watched, and heavily regulated: permits, checkpoints, tolls, and barge-traffic.

- **Marrowfen Stair** — the corridor's chief working lock-town (~1,000 people), built around a lock-staircase that raises and lowers barges twelve metres. Lower lock-gate crowded with waiting barge-masters, a merchant directorate's hall on the high side, river wharves and cargo warehouses below, an inspection post where permits are checked, and the **Stair Inn** (about 2 sp), where river news is traded at the common table. A small scholars' reading-room keeps maps and records for anyone with a civil manner. Passage through the locks requires a permit, and carrying undeclared salvage through the checkpoint is a reliable way to lose it.
- **Lord's Wend** — a petty lord's hall-town further up the corridor. Formal, old-fashioned, and conscious of precedence; good horses, indifferent beds.
- **Cresswater** — a barge-village and river landing on the towpath. A meal, a mooring, and gossip.
- **The Nine Locks** — the great lock-staircase itself, a landmark and a route node; an impressive piece of engineering with older stonework beneath it.

## The Glassmere League — the river-cities of the centre

A confederacy of mercantile river-cities on the Glasswater, and the wealthiest region on the continent. Cosmopolitan, banking-mad, and confident to the point of complacency.

- **Glassmere** — the continent's greatest inland city (45,000–65,000), straddling both banks of the Glasswater and joined by three great bridges. Districts: **the Floor** (the merchant exchange, and the largest trading hall anywhere in Orrun), **the Three Bridges** (the banking quarter), a civic quarter of league council halls, a river-port and warehouse district on both banks, a scholars' quarter around a great chapter-house called **the Reliquary**, and **Lowwater**, the poor riverside district — poorer than the equivalent quarter in the northwestern city, and considerably larger. Services are extensive: inns at every price, a grand Three Thresholds temple with proper healing, specialist importers dealing in overseas goods, professional information brokers, and banking on a scale the frontier cannot imagine. The city watch is professional, underfunded, and bribable at the lower ranks.
- **Sennfort** and **Cairnwater** — smaller league-cities up-river and down-river respectively; each proud, each convinced it is underrepresented in league councils.

## The Concord Heartlands — the ruin'd crown

The broken heart of the old Concord: a vast, unexplained field of imperial ruin, dangerous, picked over, and never fully mapped. There is no government here, only what the living hold.

- **Crownmouth** — a fortified scavenger-town (~700 people) at the ruin's edge, the only functioning settlement for a very long way. Walls face the supply road; the ruin-edge gate faces the other way and is controlled. Inside: a war-captain's hall that is the real government, a large pilgrim-quarter of rough lodging for relic-seekers, a salvage market selling rope, tools, provisions and buying whatever comes out of the ruin, and a healer's row that is better than the town's size deserves. Entry requires stating your business. Going through the ruin-edge gate without a guide is permitted and strongly discouraged.
- **The Pilgrim Camps** — shanty-camps of refugees, relic-seekers, and preachers on the ruin's southern approaches. Chaotic, devout, and dangerous in a hundred small ways.

## The Marrowdowns — the chalk country

Rolling chalk downs, sheep, wool, and old barrows. The most peaceful-looking region on the continent, governed by shire-moots and long custom.

- **Marrowmoot** — the shire-town where the moots gather (~600, swelling past 1,500 on moot-days). An open chalk-marked moot-ground at the centre, a Mourner's hall that is the moral centre of the downs, a substantial wool-market, a shire-reeve's circuit office handling law and land-disputes, and **the Drover's Inn** (about 2 sp) — warm, open, and generous with news.
- **Penmark Hold** — the shire-reeve's manor, half a day off; formal hospitality, good stables.
- **Wether** — a wool-trade village and road-stop.
- **The Barrow-Fields** — ancient burial-downs northeast of the shire-town, far older than the Concord. Barrow-robbers camp at the edge and hire out as guides. Local opinion on whether robbing them is a crime or a trade depends entirely on who is asking.

## The Sallowmarch Protectorate — the fever delta

A hot, flooded river-delta claimed by a distant crown and governed, in practice, by whoever keeps people alive. Boats matter more than roads.

- **Fenward** — the garrison-capital (~800), sprawling over raised ground in a wetland basin: a military garrison on the high side, a formal protectorate hall that is rarely used for actual governance, working delta wharves that are the real trade and news centre, and a plague-doctor's compound that is, unofficially, where the region is run from. Lodging is cheap or free for crown-affiliated travellers. Medicine here is genuinely excellent and hard-won.
- **Reedmouth** — a delta smuggling-village downstream; flat-bottomed barges, no questions, and a startling amount of commerce.
- **The Rice Sallows** — flooded paddy-country, worked by village communes with their own moots and their own opinion of the garrison.
- **The Fever Channels** — the deep delta backwaters. Locals will guide you in. Locals will also tell you not to go.

## The Hollow Gulf Ports — the southern sea-gate

Rival port city-states on the great southern bay, and the continent's door to the sea and to lands overseas.

- **Calderport** — the greatest Gulf port (30,000–50,000), built around a crescent harbour: a working docks district, an overseas traders' **Foreign Quarter**, free-port markets, privateer and naval wharves, and a walled upper city behind a customs gate where the port-mistress sits. Warm, crowded, polyglot, and rich. Services: sailors' inns at every grade, ship's stores, overseas goods and exotic materials, a sea-temple of the Three Thresholds with paid healing, coastal Mourners who tend the drowned, and a harbour-master who sells shipping intelligence. Rough at the docks, orderly above the customs wall.
- **Saltgate** — a rival port further east (~10,000), walled, climbing a rocky headland above a deep-water harbour, with a port-lord's citadel, a busy salt market, a separate wharf where Calderport ships moor apart from everyone else, and a customs gate through which every cargo passes. **The Anchor-House** (about 3 sp) is neutral ground and the best place on that coast for maritime news.
- **The Mardenmouth** — the delta-mouth wharves just west of Calderport, where the river trade meets the sea trade.

## The Wender Steppe — the grass sea

Cold open grassland north of the settled centre, held by a confederacy of horse-clans. There are no cities; there are gatherings.

- **The Winter-Camp at Cold Springs** — the largest cold-season gathering (400–800 during the season, a fraction of that otherwise), a great felt-tent camp around a cluster of permanent springs. The clan-speaker's tent is the political centre; the horse-lines are the heart of steppe life; a seasonal trade-ring serves as the steppe's market; and a permanent stone circle serves the clans' seers. Guest-right is absolute and taken with total seriousness — a guest is safe, fed, and not charged, and reciprocity is expected rather than demanded.
- **The Spine-Foot trade-meet** — the meeting-ground where steppe and settled traders do business; the route to the wider world.
- **The Sky-Stones** — an ancient shrine-circle, older than the Concord and unconnected to it; the clans' holiest place and none of an outsider's business.

## The Karran Marches — the iron frontier

Cold mountain country in the northeast: ore, mercenaries, petty warlords, and no government worth the name.

- **Brask's Hold** — the strongest warlord's fortress-town (~600), compact on a mountain shoulder: an iron-reinforced gate where every entrant is inspected and tolled, the warlord's hall, barracks holding fifty or more hired fighters at all times, ore-yards where mine output is staged and auctioned, and a market row selling weapons, tools, cold-weather gear, and rations — no luxuries, no magic.
- **Karran-Gate** — the Marches' largest trade-hub (1,200–2,000), sprawling and comparatively open, with an ore exchange, a whole street of competing mercenary companies and their hiring-boards, a way-station keeping records and permits for anyone who wants them, and **the Rough House**, the big tavern-inn where disputes are settled by the crowd.
- **The Deep Cuts** — the great ore-mines; a mining-camp complex, days out along a hard road.
- **The Old Iron forts** — scattered Concord ruins high in the mountains. Salvage camps cling to their edges. The weather kills more people out there than anything else does.

## The Emberfell Theocracy — the fire-country

A closed, self-sufficient theocracy on the flanks of a volcanic highland. Foreign visitors are unusual, watched, and require permission.

- **Ashfast** — the fortress-temple-city and seat of the faith (15,000–25,000). A wall rings the temple-city; the pilgrim gate admits the ash roads and the cinder gate faces the mining country. Inside: the great fortress-temple on the volcanic rise, its archives beside it, ritual pyre-grounds, warden posts around the perimeter, and a **permitted market** that is the only sanctioned commerce — obsidian goods, ash-metals, local produce, and nothing foreign without approval. Pilgrims are housed free in a monitored state waystation; recognised envoys get guest-cells in the temple complex. Healing is available through the fire-rites, to the faithful and to those holding safe-conduct.
- **Cinderhold** — the volcanic mining town east of the seat; hotter, dirtier, blunter, and where anything unofficial happens.
- **The Ash Roads** — the pilgrim-ways up through the highland, lined with fire-shrines and statues, and the only sanctioned approach.

## The Saltmere Reaches — the shrinking inland sea

Clan country around a great inland salt sea that has been slowly retreating for generations, uncovering things.

- **Brackhold** — the largest surviving salt-clan hold (~1,000), facing the water: a fortified quay-gate where a clan toll is collected, the matriarch's hall where confederacy politics happen, extensive salt-works of drying pans and scale-houses, a Mourner's hall offering old rites and practical healing, a harbour-mole for clan barges, and a delver-quarter of salvage guides and relic-buyers. A visiting non-kin pays a customary toll and observes clan protocol — greet at the gate, do not interrupt a rite.
- **Saltcairn** — a clan-town on the far shore, on the salt caravan road.
- **The Drowned Towns** — settlements the lake covered long ago and is now, year by year, giving back. Delvers work them from barges. Locals will tell you plainly that the ruins are not safe and are not always empty.

## The Hethewald Free Holds — the forest confederacy

A confederacy of forest free-holds in the great eastern wood, governed by moot and by a wood's-law that is stricter than it first appears.

- **Hethemoot** — the great gathering free-hold (300–500 permanent, up to 2,000 in full moot). Not a dense town but a large forest clearing: the speaker's permanent moot-hall at the centre, his longhouse beside it, an open moot-circle for full gatherings, a small permanent trade-ground selling forest goods, timber, furs and hunting tools, and forest gates at the edges where outsiders are scrutinised on arrival. Lodging at the trade-ground is symbolic in price and conditional on guest protocol.
- **Greenward** — a grove-village deeper in the wood, two or three days off and guided-access only.
- **Tollreach** — an outlaw river-toll camp on the eastern river; not respectable, entirely functional.
- **The Old Holds** — deep-wood Concord ruins the free-holds treat as forbidden. Asking to go there is a serious request, not a casual one.

## The Sunmark — the sacred groves

Warm southern grove country, held by tribes whose faith is a living one rather than a memorial one. The most hopeful-feeling region on the continent, and the least interested in outside politics.

- **The Great Grove at Sunhollow** — the faith's holy heart and gathering-place (150–250 grove-keepers in residence; a thousand or more at major rites). An open grove, not a walled town: a great old-growth heart-tree at the centre, a gathering-ground where travellers first meet the keepers, a sacred spring-fed pool where healing rites are performed, the grove-keeper's hut serving as the faith's administrative hub, a storytellers' grove where the seers keep their fire, and a welcoming-gate of stones where the pilgrim paths arrive. Shelter and healing are freely given to respectful visitors; food and herbs are traded; no weapons or armour are sold, made, or particularly welcome.
- **The Grove-Camps** — the tribes' dispersed settlements, hours out along the grove paths.
- **The Green Roads** — pilgrim-and-trade paths through the groves, running north toward the chalk downs and east toward the southern coast.
