# Widdershin Cave

**A standalone one-shot for one level 1 character. Non-canon — see [`../README.md`](../README.md).**

> Row *widdershins* around the point at dead-low tide, or the sea won't let you back out.

---

## THE BONES

**Premise:** A generation ago, smuggler captain Perrin Locke and his crew
stole a carved idol from something that lived beneath the waves, and the sea
took the whole crew as payment. Villagers say their ghosts still haunt the
sea-cave under Widdershin Point, and that the captain's treasure is still
down there for anyone brave — or foolish — enough to time the tide right.
The truth: Locke was trying to *return* the idol when the storm hit. His
crew still serve him, not out of malice, but because none of them have
figured out how to stop.

**Where:** A headland cove on Orrun's Pale Coast. Written to sit a few
miles down the coast from Cobble Strand, but it needs nothing from that
settlement's own canon — drop it near any small fishing village on any
coastline you're running.

**Level:** 1 (one character). Scaling notes for a small party or higher
level are at the end.

**Playtime:** ~3 hours.

**The shape of it:**

1. **The village (10–15 min)** — pick up the legend and the practical
   problem (the tide) from two or three fresh, disposable NPCs.
2. **Getting in (15–20 min)** — a real logistics puzzle: time the tide, or
   find the harder alternate route down the cliff.
3. **The cave, zones 2–5 (90–110 min)** — tide-pool hazard, a wreck full of
   atmosphere and a friendly ghost, a skeleton-crew fight in a choke point,
   and a three-part tide-dial puzzle with three independent clue paths.
4. **The boss, zone 6 (30–40 min)** — Captain Locke, playable as a straight
   fight, a puzzle-first narrative resolution, or a hybrid of both.
5. **Getting out / aftermath (10–15 min)** — the tide is rising again, and
   the ending changes based on what the player did with the idol.

**The two ways the climax can go**, at a glance:
- **Fight it out.** Locke is dangerous but fair — resistant to harm while
  the idol still glows on his belt, a normal fight once it's gone.
- **Solve it.** Return the idol to the tide-altar in his lair (found via the
  dial-vault clues) and the fight ends without a single die rolled, if the
  player chooses to try talking first.
- Most tables will land on a hybrid: fight until the idol's gone, then
  finish him off or let him go.

## THE MEAT

### The Village

Two or three short scenes are enough — don't overbuild this part, it's a
hook, not the adventure.

- **Old Ebb Tarn**, a retired fisherman with a bad hip and a good memory. He
  actually watched the *Widdershin* go down as a boy. He's not superstitious
  about the cave so much as *respectful* of it — "the sea keeps what it's
  owed, and it was owed Locke's crew, and it took 'em fair." He'll draw a
  rough tide-timing chart if asked (see **Getting In**, below) and mention,
  almost as an aside, "there's an old climbing path down past the
  lighthouse, if you don't fancy swimming for it." He does **not** know
  about the idol or the true history — just the legend.
- **Young Sennow**, a fisherman's kid who dared a friend to swim into the
  cave mouth last summer. The friend came back with a fever and swore they
  heard someone crying. Sennow will absolutely try to tag along if not
  stopped, and is a decent source of the "ghosts, not monsters" framing —
  useful for softening the player's expectations before the fight.
- **Optional: Widow Mera Cask**, who runs the one inn/tavern in town, offers
  the job in the first place — a merchant paid her (in advance, oddly
  generously) to find someone willing to retrieve "anything valuable" from
  the wreck, no questions asked. This gives a concrete reason to go beyond
  curiosity, and a mildly suspicious hook (who's really asking, and why?)
  that a GM can leave unanswered or pay off however they like — it's not
  load-bearing for the plot and needs no resolution.

None of these three need stat blocks. If violence somehow reaches the
village, treat them as ordinary commoners (AC 10, HP 4, no meaningful
attack).

### Getting In — The Tide Puzzle

This is the adventure's first real puzzle, and it's a logistics one, not a
riddle: **the cave mouth is a half-submerged sea-arch that's only passable
on foot at dead-low tide** (roughly a 1-hour window). Get the timing wrong
and the direct entrance is chest-deep or fully submerged.

- **Old Ebb's tide chart** (if the player asked) gives the next low-tide
  window directly — the "easy" path, costing an hour or two of waiting or
  timing their arrival.
- **A DC 12 Survival or Nature check**, reading the water and the exposed
  rockline, works out the timing without Ebb's help.
- **The cliff route** bypasses the tide puzzle entirely: an old,
  overgrown fisherman's path down the headland near the lighthouse, ending
  in a rope-and-chimney climb into the cave from above (**DC 13 Athletics**,
  or automatic with a rope and an anchor point, which is obviously
  available to anyone who thinks to bring one). This route drops the player
  into **Zone 3** directly, skipping Zone 2's crabs — a reward for players
  who scout ahead or ask around, not a trap.
- **Getting the timing wrong** isn't a dead end — it just costs time (an
  uncomfortable, cold wade with disadvantage on the first check inside) or
  forces the cliff route. Never block entry outright.

### Zone 1 — Tidewrack Landing

The sea-arch entrance: barnacled stone, the tideline a sharp dark stain
overhead, littered with old wreckage — a snapped mast, a bleached ship's
wheel half-buried in sand. Cold, salt air, and, faintly, something that
might be singing, or might just be the wind through the arch.

No encounter here. It's the establishing beat — let the player poke around;
a **DC 10 Investigation** check turns up a bent brass **belt-buckle
engraved with a stylized eye** (foreshadowing the Fathom Eye idol, no
mechanical effect).

### Zone 2 — The Tide Pools

A wide, uneven cavern floor cut through with a dozen tide pools, some
ankle-deep, some chest-deep, glowing faintly green-blue with luminous algae.
Skippable via the cliff route (Zone 1 → Zone 3 directly).

**Encounter — reef crabs (2):** *Giant-crab-like* profile. AC 15 (natural
armor), HP 6 each, Speed 20 ft (swim 20 ft), claw +3 to hit, 1d4+1 piercing,
advantage on grapple checks against a target they've already hit. **CR
~1/8 each — low threat, fully telegraphed** by clicking claws and rippling
water before they attack, and fully avoidable with a **DC 11 Stealth or
Acrobatics** check to cross via the drier stone spine running through the
room. Fun, not scary — this is the "warm-up" fight.

**Treasure:** a corroded iron strongbox half-buried in the largest pool
(**DC 10 Strength to pry open**) holds 22 gp in mixed coin and a
water-stained scrap of paper — **Log Fragment #1** (see **The Rune-Dial
Vault**, below).

### Zone 3 — The Wreck Gallery

The tide pools give way to a wide, high-ceilinged chamber where the actual
wreck of the *Widdershin* rests, canted against the cave wall, half-buried
in sand and glowing fungus that casts the whole room in soft blue-green
light. Genuinely beautiful, and the first place the adventure asks the
player to feel something other than caution.

**NPC — Tam, the drowned cabin-boy:** a translucent, barefoot boy of about
twelve, sitting on the wreck's broken rail, swinging his legs like he's
still waiting for someone. He's shy, a little embarrassed to be seen crying,
and immediately, disarmingly friendly if the player doesn't attack on sight
(he can't hurt anyone and won't try). **Tam knows the true story** — that
Locke was trying to give the idol back, that the storm caught them
mid-ritual, that the crew stayed loyal to the captain even in death because
none of them blame him. He'll share all of this if asked kindly, and will
flatly **give away the tide-dial combination** (see below) if the player is
warm to him — there is no roll gate on kindness working. He's frightened of
the captain's grief, not the captain himself, and begs the player not to
hurt him "if there's any other way."

If Tam is treated well, he'll show up in **Zone 6** and use his one ability
— **a single round of distraction** (see boss tactics) — to help.

**Treasure:** **Log Fragment #2**, tucked in the wreck's shattered captain's
locker — see below.

### Zone 4 — The Smuggler's Cache

A narrow, low tunnel opens into a cramped storeroom stacked with rotted
crates and barrels — the actual contraband cache, mostly worthless now
except as color. This is the fight room, and the tunnel leading in is
**deliberately a choke point**: only one creature can reach the player at a
time.

**Encounter — drowned skeleton crew (3):** *Skeleton-like, salt-crusted*
profile. AC 13, HP 13 each, Speed 30 ft, scimitar +3 to hit (1d6+1
slashing) or shortbow +3 to hit at range (1d6 piercing), vulnerable to
bludgeoning, immune to poison damage and the poisoned/exhausted conditions,
resistant to cold damage. **CR 1/4 each.** Mindless and relentless (no
morale — they fight until destroyed), but **slow and clumsy climbing**,
making retreat back into the tunnel or Zone 3 always available and always
effective. Fighting them one at a time in the tunnel mouth is the obviously
correct tactic and the room is built to reward noticing that.

**Treasure:** 35 gp in a strongbox, a decent (mundane, sellable ~15 gp)
silver hip-flask, and, pinned under a fallen crate, a carved stone tide-chart
disc — see **The Rune-Dial Vault** below.

### Zone 5 — The Rune-Dial Vault

A round chamber, obviously artificial rather than natural — smoothed walls,
a single heavy stone door on the far side carved with a wide, staring eye,
and set into the door: **three stone dials**, each ringed with eight
symbols the player will recognize as tide/moon/wind markers once they've
seen the chart from Zone 4.

**The puzzle:** the dials must be set to **full moon / dead-low tide /
wind from the west** — "the drowning tide," the exact conditions of the
night the *Widdershin* went down — to open the door.

Three independent ways to get there, so no single roll or lucky find gates
progress:

1. **The tide-chart disc (Zone 4):** shows which moon/tide/wind
   combinations are dangerous, with the drowning-tide combination scratched
   and circled hard enough to gouge the stone.
2. **Log Fragments #1 + #2 (Zones 2 and 3), read together:** *"...on the
   full moon, dead slack tide, we went in against the west wind and never
   came..."* — the sentence is split across the two fragments, so finding
   either alone gives half the answer, both together gives all of it.
3. **Tam (Zone 3):** will just say the combination out loud if asked kindly
   — the free, no-puzzle bypass for a table that wants less friction.

**Setting the dials wrong** triggers a harmless flooding jet from the door
itself (**DC 12 Dexterity save** or take 1d4 cold damage and get thoroughly
soaked) and resets all three dials to their start position — a cost, not a
dead end. There is no limit on attempts.

Once solved, the eye carved into the door "blinks" open into Zone 6.

### Zone 6 — The Widdershin Deep

The cave opens into a vast sea-cavern, its far wall open to the ocean
itself — waves roll in slowly through a low natural arch, and moonlight (or
daylight, refracted oddly) ripples across black water. A wrecked
ship's-chair, lashed with kelp and barnacles, sits on a raised stone
shelf above the water — Locke's "throne." Beside it, on a low stone altar
worn smooth by centuries of tide, sits the source of everything: **the
Fathom Eye**, a fist-sized carved idol of black coral and mother-of-pearl,
shaped like a wide, unblinking eye.

This is both the treasure room and the boss lair — the fight and the loot
are in the same breath, which is the point.

**Boss — Captain Perrin Locke, the Widdershin Warden.** See full writeup
below.

**Treasure (regardless of the fight's outcome):**
- A locked sea-chest behind the throne (**DC 13 to pick, or the key is on
  Locke's belt**): 140 gp in old coin and jewelry, a fine (but mundane)
  boarding cutlass worth 25 gp to a collector, and **the Widdershin Coin** —
  a genuine minor magic item (see below).
- **The Fathom Eye idol itself** — see **Resolution**, below, for what
  happens to it and why it matters.

## The Boss — Captain Perrin Locke

*A tragic figure, not a villain. Play him grieving and tired, not cruel —
he stopped the storm from taking anyone else the only way he knew how, and
he's been paying for it for thirty years.*

**Type:** Undead (unique). **Size:** Medium. **AC:** 13. **HP:** 35 while
the Fathom Eye is intact and in the room (see *Phase 2*, below); effectively
harder than that against ordinary weapons — see **Idol Resistance**.
**Speed:** 30 ft, hover 20 ft (can move freely over the water in Zone 6).

**Ability emphasis:** Strength and Charisma both above-average for his
role; he was a leader in life and still commands respect from what's left
of his crew. **Saves:** proficient in Wisdom and Charisma saves.
**Skills:** proficient in Perception and Intimidation.

**Idol Resistance (Phase 1):** while the Fathom Eye idol is intact and
anywhere in Zone 6, Locke is **resistant to bludgeoning, piercing, and
slashing damage from nonmagical weapons.** The moment the idol is destroyed,
thrown into the sea-arch, or physically carried out of the room, this
resistance ends immediately (**Phase 2**) — he visibly becomes more solid,
more tired-looking, and easier to hit.

**Actions:**
- **Cutlass of Drowning.** Melee attack, +4 to hit, reach 5 ft: 1d8+2
  slashing plus 1d6 cold damage.
- **Tide Call** (uses his action; recharges on a 5 or 6 at the start of his
  turn, **capped at twice per fight** regardless of recharge rolls): the
  water in the room surges — the whole chamber becomes difficult terrain
  for 1 round, and any creature standing in water when it triggers must
  succeed on a **DC 13 Strength save** or be knocked prone. Telegraph this
  clearly (the water visibly begins to churn on the round before) so it
  never reads as a cheap hit.
- **Wailing Command** (once per fight): if any of the drowned skeleton crew
  from Zone 4 are still standing, he can call them to his side as a bonus
  action (2-round travel time from Zone 4, giving fair warning). If they've
  all been destroyed already, this does nothing but a mournful, echoing
  shout — flavor only.

**Tactics:** Locke fights from the water when he can (mobility, Tide Call
setup) and doesn't chase a retreating player out of Zone 6 — he's bound to
this cave and knows it. He does not target Tam. If reduced below 1/3 of his
current HP, or at any point after the idol's resistance ends, he'll pause
and can be reasoned with (**DC 14 Persuasion, DC 12 if Tam is present and
vouching**) into lowering his weapon and talking — this is always available,
not a one-time window.

**Morale:** he does not fight to the death by default. A player who talks
first, fights smart (dealing with the idol early), or simply asks him to
stop after drawing blood has a real shot at ending this without a killing
blow. A player who never engages with any of that will get a straight,
fair, winnable fight instead.

**Solo danger rating:** Moderate for a single level 1 character — genuinely
threatening while Idol Resistance is active, comfortably manageable once
it's gone. The choke-point fight in Zone 4 and the puzzle in Zone 5 both
exist partly to make sure the player arrives here with resources and
information intact.

**Retreat:** always available — Zone 6 connects back through the vault
door, and Locke won't follow past it.

### Resolution: what happens to the Fathom Eye

- **Returned to the tide-altar with respect** (an action, no roll needed —
  just the choice): Locke's crew finally goes still and peaceful; he
  thanks the player, sits back in his chair, and fades. The village later
  reports the tide "feels calmer" past Widdershin Point — no mechanical
  effect, pure flavor payoff.
- **Kept:** it's a striking curio worth ~100 gp to the right collector, and
  nothing bad happens to the player for keeping it — but the GM can seed a
  single follow-up rumor hook (an old sailor gets nervous around it, a
  storm seems to follow the ship that carries it) if they ever want to spin
  this into a sequel. Not required, not load-bearing.
- **Destroyed:** ends Locke's curse the hard way — same peaceful fade, but
  the room's water surges once, hard (everyone present takes 1d6 cold
  damage, no save) as whatever bound the idol lets go all at once.

### The Widdershin Coin (magic item, minor)

A tarnished silver coin, stamped with the same staring eye as the idol.
**Once per day**, when the holder fails a Strength or Dexterity saving
throw against being knocked prone, moved, or swept off their feet, they can
flip the coin as a reaction to reroll the save. A small, thematic, level-1-
appropriate reward — not remotely game-breaking, but a nice thing to hold
onto.

## Scaling Notes

Full party-size (1–6) × level (1–4) scaling — foe counts, stat lines for
Reef Crabs/Skeletons/the Bosun's Bones elite/Locke at every level, boss
HP and Tide Call pacing by size tier, the Zone 4 chokepoint-width call, and
treasure scaling — now lives in its own file:
**[`PARTY_AND_LEVEL_SCALING.md`](PARTY_AND_LEVEL_SCALING.md)**. It covers
every combination from a solo level 1 character to six level 4s, with
worked examples for 4 level-1s, 3 level-3s, and 5 level-4s. The puzzle,
tide-timing logistics, DCs, NPCs, morale, and retreat options are identical
at every scale — only foe count and toughness ever change.

**Running it for a full table:** treat it as a single "spotlight dungeon" —
split the village scene and the tide-puzzle logistics across characters
(Survival/Nature/Investigation each get a moment) rather than having one
player carry the whole hook.

## Running Notes

- **If the player stalls on the tide-dial puzzle:** any one of the three
  clue paths resolves it. If a table is visibly stuck despite having a
  clue in hand, let Tam show up early (he can visit Zone 4 or 5 if the
  player was kind to him) and just say the answer — never let a puzzle
  eat more than 15–20 real minutes.
- **If the player skips Zone 2 or 4 entirely** (cliff route, or clever
  avoidance): that's fine. Nothing there is required to reach or beat the
  boss — losing access to Log Fragment #1 just means the player leans
  harder on the tide-chart disc or Tam instead. The three-clue design
  means no single skip creates a dead end.
- **If the player goes straight for a killing blow on Locke without ever
  engaging the idol:** let the fight be exactly as hard as the stats say —
  resistant and dangerous. That's the honest cost of skipping the puzzle
  path, not a punishment, just the harder route made real.
- **Tone:** melancholy-adventurous, not horror. The skeletons are a fair
  fight, not a jump-scare; Tam and Locke are sad, not sinister. Save any
  real dread for the boss's Tide Call going off unexpectedly.
