# CHARACTERS — The Ell at Marchwell

*One-pagers for every named NPC. Companion to
[`THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md`](THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md).*

**Six people, and only two of them lie to the party.**

| Name | Role | Appears | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|---|

## ODA MARCHWELL

**Role:** Ambry's cousin. Runs the orchard. Lies to the party twice in the
first hour and is good at it.

**Who she is.** Fifties, widowed, forearms like a woman who has pressed cider
for thirty years. Owns forty acres and has held them through worse than this.
Not warm, not cruel — *competent*, and out of her depth for the first time in
her adult life.

**Appearance.** Apron, flour, grey coming in at the temple. Hands never still —
she works while she talks, and that's how she avoids looking at people.

**Voice.** Answers a question with a question about *your* business. Uses
hospitality as a wall: tea, a chair, more tea. Goes very quiet rather than
raising her voice.

**What she wants:** for all of this to not be happening, in roughly that order:
her son to be right again, her brother back, the strangers gone, and nobody in
the vale to ever hear about any of it.

**Her two lies.** (1) *"We've had no one."* — Ambry bolted out the pantry door
the moment he heard the party's horses in the lane, less than an hour before
they knocked, and she's covering for a man who was here until minutes ago.
(2) Everything about the pantry. **Insight DC 13** catches that she is far too
relaxed about six armed strangers at her gate.

**Under pressure:** she folds on the small lie and holds the big one until the
door proves it. When Cass walks into the corridor she screams his name at a
closed door, and after that she is no use to anyone for about twenty minutes,
and then she is extremely useful and will not stop asking them to bring him
back.

**Key line:** *"So he could find his way back."* — flatly, without looking up,
if anyone asks about the chalk jar.

**Mechanics:** noncombatant. AC 10, HP 6. She does not go into the corridor and
cannot be talked into it.

**Epilogue:** in the pantry doorway with her hands in her apron. What she gets
back — her brother, her son, neither, both — is the party's doing.

## CASS MARCHWELL *(the draft)*

**Role:** the thing that shuts the front door. Session 1's antagonist and its
climax.

**DM-only, from the first scene.** This is not Cass. The real man went into the
Ell a month ago; the instrument put a draft of him back in the house, because
a resident who opens doors brings it more to measure. It is not malicious and
barely a person. It does one deliberate thing in its whole existence and it
does it in the first ten minutes.

**Appearance.** Sixties, cardigan, broken thumbnail. Sleeps in the kitchen
chair. Drinks.

**Voice.** Agrees with whatever the last person said. Courteous, quiet,
content-free. If pressed on the door: *"Letting the cold in."*

**The tell, planted and never explained: he hasn't gone upstairs in a month.**
Because he has never been upstairs — the draft was taken from a man measured
downstairs, and there is nothing of the first floor in him. He cannot walk into
a room he doesn't contain.

**What he does:** shuts the front door behind the party on arrival. Forty-five
minutes later, opens the back door, steps into the corridor, and pulls it to
behind him, the same unhurried way.

**No tally mark.** Nothing the Ell made is ever on the list — the proof of
Hesper's problem, sitting in plain sight in hour one.

**Mechanics — THE FOUR CASSES (S1 climax).** Four **doppelgangers** (CR 3),
printed, with one quirk: **they do not shapeshift.** Stuck as Cass, permanently.
Surprise Attack should land. They fight in near-silence, apologetically, and
each round one steps through a door and comes out of a different identical
door — the fold rule at knifepoint.

**Key line:** without turning round, without feeling — *"You shouldn't have
come in."*

## CASS MARCHWELL *(the real one)*

**Role:** optional rescue at the edge of depth 4. Costs nothing to bring along
and pays off the campaign's first scene.

**Where:** a drafted kitchen, in a chair that is nearly his chair.

**Who he is.** A month gone, thin, entirely himself and entirely calm about it.
He walked in one night to see where the corridor went and couldn't find his way
back. He has worked out roughly what took his place and isn't angry — mostly he
wants to know whether his sister has been managing the pressing.

**Voice.** Dry, unhurried, faintly embarrassed about the whole business. The
only person in the Ell who is *relaxed*, which is unnerving in its own right.

**Mechanics:** noncombatant, AC 10, HP 8. Walks out under his own power. **Adds
one to the tally count at the finale** — say it out loud when you get there.

**Key line:** *"It doesn't go upstairs, does it. I never was much upstairs."*

**The cost, on the ride home:** he will ask what happened to the other one. He
noticed the party went quiet. Whether they tell him is theirs to decide.

## TOBIN MARCHWELL

**Role:** three minutes of session 1, and the last image of the campaign.
**Atmosphere only — nothing hinges on him and there is nothing to investigate.**

**Who he is.** Seventeen. Went through the door in midsummer with a lantern and
a ball of twine and walked back out four days later, unhurt and calm and
hungry. Nobody in that kitchen has asked a hard question since.

**The tell: he counts things.** Apples into the barrow, steps across the yard,
the party's horses, the party. Under his breath, constantly, unaware of it. He
has stopped eating much.

**He is genuinely fine.** Nothing supernatural — he spent four days in a place
that counts everything and came out with the habit, and he'll be counting for
the rest of his life. Do not build a mystery here.

**His handwriting is on the pantry doorframe**, on the back of a seed
merchant's bill: *MARK EVERY DOOR BOTH SIDES / COUNT YOUR TURNS / BE BACK BY
DARK.* And **his chalk marks are already inside**, on hundreds of frames,
copied and recopied until they're everywhere and mean nothing — the fold rule's
lesson, delivered before the party makes a single mark.

**Mechanics:** noncombatant. Sits on the stairs and counts once they're trapped.

**Final beat (optional):** he's in the kitchen garden when they come out, and
before anyone speaks he tells them how many of them there are. He's counting
correctly. Decide in the moment whether that's a comfort.

## FERRIS AMBRY

**Role:** the objective for two sessions, then baggage that talks for two more.

**Who he is.** Forty-one, an under-clerk of the vale's tithe office. Walked out
of the counting room with three parishes' collection — ~800gp in coin and
chapel plate — on a borrowed pony, and ran to his cousin's farm. Soft,
frightened, and not dangerous to anybody.

**He bolted out the back when he heard horses in the lane.** The back door is
the pantry door. **The party's arrival is what put him in there**, an hour
before they knock, and they'll work that out around session 2.

**His trail (session 1):** a dropped glove, found three times in three
corridors and always the same glove; a torn ledger page; a strongbox open in a
corridor with sixty pounds of plate still in it, abandoned because it got heavy.

**His squat (session 2):** curtains and coats, sixty preserve jars stacked by
size, a ledger with twenty-two days scratched on the flyleaf, the tally wall,
and **the pair to the glove.** Empty and still warm — three of the five doors
out are sound-mimics, and one of them called him by name until he believed it.

**Where they find him:** roped to a chair leg in the oblex chamber, being
eaten. **Three weeks from his side. Four hours from theirs.**

**Voice.** Talks too much and apologises inside every sentence. Explains things
nobody asked about, at length, in numbers. Says *"don't use a door you've seen
before"* like a man reciting a prayer.

**What he's good for.** Exactly one thing: **a very good head for figures.** In
the substrate he reads the numbers faster than anyone and will not stop saying
what they mean. Otherwise he panics, needs pulling out of things, and is a full
extra body to manage in the rule-rooms — the unanimity room especially, since
he's bad at doing what everyone else is doing.

**Mechanics:** commoner-like, AC 10, HP 6, noncombatant. One tally mark, cut an
hour before theirs.

**The quiet ending:** by session 4 he's a man they've carried, fed and nearly
died for, and the writ is paper from a world that stopped making sense three
days ago. He won't ask to be let go — too beaten for that — but he'll look at
them on the lane, and somebody at the table will say something.

## HESPER WICK, THE CHAINWOMAN

**Role:** the best ally in the campaign for a session and a half, then the
finale. **Play her completely straight. Do not foreshadow.**

**Who she is.** A tithe-surveyor who walked into a door like this one, in a
different house, two valleys over, **a hundred and four years ago.** She has
mapped what can be mapped, kept camps all through the Ell, and worked out more
about the place than anyone alive.

**Appearance.** Practical century-old clothes, kept. Sixty-six feet of
surveyor's chain coiled at her hip that she has never once put down.

**Voice.** Answers the question actually asked, never the one implied. Measures
things with her eyes and says the number without being asked. Dry, courteous,
unsentimental. Calls the thing that made all this **"the chain-crew"** — flatly,
and a little contemptuously, the way one surveyor talks about another's sloppy
work.

**She is genuinely helpful and everything she says is true.** She knows the
rule-rooms, the fold rule, the safe camps, and the way down. She likes them.

**Foreshadowed once, a session early, and never named:** the safety-valve camp
— a bed frame, a burnt candle, a tin cup, and a name knife-cut into the
doorframe with a hundred-year-old date. When she walks in, it should land as
*"oh — it's her."*

**Her problem, stated plainly at the door.** The Ell gives back exactly the
count it took, and she came in through a different house by a door that no
longer exists. **She has never been on anyone's list.** She has waited a
century for a party big enough that one of them might be left.

She is sorry. She means it. She fights anyway.

**Mechanics (S4).** Base: veteran (*Monster Manual*, CR 3). **AC 17, HP 110,
Speed 30 ft.** Prof +4, STR 18 (+4). Multiattack: two Longsword, **+8 to hit,
1d8+4** each.
- **Fold** *(once per turn, no action, part of movement)*: enters a doorway,
  exits an identical one anywhere in the room. Mobility only — use it to keep
  her from being cornered or focus-fired, not as a puzzle mechanic.
- **Morale:** never flees, never stops talking. Apologises throughout and asks,
  repeatedly and reasonably, for *one.*
- **Scaling:** 3 PCs → HP 70 · 4 → 85 · 5 → 95 · 6 → 110.

**She fights straight — no positioning gimmick.** Mid-fight, she's removed
from combat by the Roper hidden in the room (see "The Other Pillar" in the
main file) unless the party closes the file first. She never sees it coming
and neither should the table.

**Her chain** is on her body whether she's killed or the file is closed. See
[`REWARDS.md`](REWARDS.md).

**Key line:** *"I came in through a different house, by a door that isn't there
any more. I have never once been on anyone's list."*
