# THE ELL AT MARCHWELL — Premise & Design Notes

### *The pantry door is on an outside wall. It opens onto a corridor.*

**Six characters, level 5 → 6. Four sessions. Non-canon — see
[`../README.md`](../README.md).**

> **This file is the *why*. [`THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md`](THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md)
> is the *what*, and it is the one you run from.** Where they disagree, the
> adventure file wins. Rev. 5 — reconciled with the adventure after the
> session-1 rewrite, draft-Cass, the chalk trigger, the tally, and the rest
> rule.

---

## The pitch

The party has a writ for **Ferris Ambry**, a soft, frightened tithe-clerk who
walked off with three parishes' collection — about 800gp in coin and chapel
plate. He's run to his cousin's orchard farm in the cider country of the
Ashgarden Vale. It is a boring afternoon's work.

They ride up the lane, they come in the front door, and **Cass Marchwell shuts
it behind them.** Oda says *"No—"*, one syllable, too late. Nothing happens for
forty-five minutes.

Then somebody tries to leave, and every exterior door in the house opens onto
a hundred and forty feet of corridor with a rag rug in it.

**Objective one: catch the man. Objective two, from the midpoint on: get out.**
Neither requires the party to solve, decode, or reconstruct anything.

---

## Where, and why there

**Ashgarden Vale.** House-of-Leaves horror needs a *boring, beloved* house —
jam in the pantry, a name carved in a doorframe — and the vale is the one
prosperous, whitewashed-farmhouse region on the continent. Its culture is
denial and quiet cover-up, which is exactly the family dynamic this needs. And
land measurement is a real local profession there, so the adventure's whole
vocabulary is already the local vocabulary.

Marchwell House stands alone half a day west of Tilbrook, nearest neighbours
three farms and a press-house at **Coldapple**. House, hamlet, family, clerk
and surveyor are all invented here and appear in no campaign file. **No
registered campaign NPC, faction or artifact is used** — it lifts out and drops
into any farm country in any setting.

## The truth (DM-only)

**Nobody built the Ell. Nothing in it was designed.**

Something came through this world before there were people in it and **took
its measure** — the way a chain-crew measures a field: exhaustively, and with
no interest whatsoever in what the field is *for*. It was a **surveyor, not an
architect.** It recorded everything and left. It is not coming back, it is not
in the adventure, and there is nothing to fight. Hesper calls it *the
chain-crew*, flatly and a little contemptuously.

**The Ell is the notebook, and it is also the instrument.** There is no
machine — the measured space itself is the equipment, left running because the
survey was never closed. A theodolite standing on a hill for ten thousand
years, still taking readings.

It works by making **drafts**: approximations built out of measured space, so
it can check its numbers. It has drafted the vale, the orchards, the weather,
and the Marchwells' house. Its drafts fail in one consistent direction: **it
observed interiors exhaustively and was never once inside one.**

**And the survey has been stalled a century**, because it can't close the file
on a *house* — a house isn't a volume, it's a thing that happens inside one,
and there is no unit for that. Its question is cut into the substrate in every
language it has ever copied: **HOW BIG IS A HOME?**

---

## The three design pillars

**1. Most rooms mean nothing.** The wrong objects — the anchor in the bedroom,
the gallows in the nursery — are never clues. This is the hardest thing to
hold at the table and the most important. If everything means something, the
Ell becomes a puzzle box and stops being alien.

**2. The fold rule is learnable, then exploitable.** *Identical things are the
same thing.* It's taught by eight push-hints that happen *to* the party rather
than waiting on clever ideas, and it's triggered by a jar of chalk and a dead
boy's instructions on the pantry shelf. Once they have it, it becomes their
tool: mark a room unique, fold two rooms together, strand a pursuer, and
safely rest.

**3. Every stat block is printed and run as written.** The wrongness is
cosmetic and behavioural and never touches the numbers. A player who knows
what a displacer beast does is still right — they're simply never going to
sleep well again.

## Structure

| | Depths | Climax |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — In** | 0–1: the house, then the repeating house | **The Four Casses** (4 doppelgangers) |
| **S2 — Down** | 2: tallow light, wrong objects, the Coat Closet | **The oblex, with Ambry in the room**, then the way home is a lie. **Level 6** |
| **S3 — Deeper** | 3–4: wrong scale, the rule-rooms, the drafted people | **Hesper Wick**, and the last comfortable hour of the campaign |
| **S4 — Out** | 5: the substrate, deep time, the instrument seen working | **Hesper at the door**, plus one copy of the party per long rest taken |

**The tally** threads all four: scratches cut on the doorframe as each person
crosses, struck through when they come back out. The party counts them in hour
one with no explanation. In session 4 it is Hesper's entire argument — she came
in by a different house and has never once been on anyone's list.

**The rest rule is the difficulty dial.** Long rests only work in rooms the
Ell can tell apart, and every one taken puts another copy of the party in the
final room. The players set the finale's difficulty themselves, in advance,
without being told they're choosing.

## What was cut, and why

- **The two-Tobins plot.** A novel's worth of coordination hanging on players
  making a chain of correct calls. Tobin survives as three minutes of session-1
  atmosphere: he went in, he came back, he counts things now.
- **The gelatinous cube and the rescue timer.** Replaced by finding Ambry in
  the oblex chamber, which explains why the party has been hearing his voice
  all session.
- **Quantity-as-absurdity** (four hundred chairs, sixty coats). Straight out of
  the Backrooms. Replaced by *the wrong object* — one impossible thing per
  otherwise-ordinary room.
- **Rooms-that-copy-rooms as depth 4.** Also too Backrooms. Replaced by the
  rule-rooms, which deliver the twisted physics the brief actually asked for.
- **The party choosing to go in.** They're trapped instead — the windows show a
  perfect autumn afternoon until you open one, and then the wind stops and the
  birds are gone.
