# ROOM TABLES — The Ell at Marchwell

Generators for filling the Ell between planned beats. Companion to
[`THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md`](THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md).

> **The one rule: none of this means anything.** Describe it beautifully, let
> them search it, and never mention it again. If the players start decoding
> the furniture, the Ell becomes a puzzle box and stops being alien. When
> somebody asks "what does the gallows *mean*?" — the honest answer is that
> the instrument saw one once and filed it under *things that are in places*.

---

## 1. THE WRONG OBJECT (d20) — depth 2 signature

Every room is a correct, plausible, well-kept domestic room containing exactly
one thing that has no business in a house. Placed carefully. Dusted. Regarded
by the instrument as furniture.

**Cross off each result once it's used.** Several of these objects (the
anchor, the gallows, the well, the milestone) are the one-of-a-kind objects
that make a room rest-safe per the main file's resting rule — a rolled
duplicate would put two "unique" anchors in the Ell at once and quietly break
that rule. If you roll a result you've already used, reroll.

| d20 | In an otherwise ordinary room, there is |
|---|---|
| 1 | A ship's anchor, upright, on the bedroom floor. |
| 2 | A milestone in the hallway, still reading a distance to a town nobody has heard of. |
| 3 | A church bell on the parlour floor, tongue removed and laid beside it. |
| 4 | A freestanding latched gate in the middle of the dining room, with nothing on either side of it. |
| 5 | A gallows in the nursery, at correct scale. |
| 6 | A well — a proper stone well, with a bucket — in the middle of a bedroom. It is dry. |
| 7 | A full-sized plough, oiled, in the study. |
| 8 | A boundary hedge growing along one wall of the sitting room, trimmed. |
| 9 | A pulpit, in the kitchen, facing the range. |
| 10 | A row of six mooring bollards down the centre of a corridor. |
| 11 | A haystack in the drawing room. It is fresh. |
| 12 | A signpost with four arms, none of them lettered. |
| 13 | A weathervane on a pole, indoors, turning slowly, in still air. |
| 14 | A ferry-ramp, sloping down into the floorboards. |
| 15 | A beehive, occupied, on the mantelpiece. The bees are fine. Nobody knows where they go. |
| 16 | A stack of roof slates, dressed and ready, in a child's bedroom. |
| 17 | A cattle trough, full, in the library. |
| 18 | A market stall, set up and empty, in a bedroom. |
| 19 | A stile over a wall that has no other side. |
| 20 | A grave. Turf, headstone, and all. No name on the stone — it hasn't been cut yet. |

## 2. THE ROOM ITSELF (d12)

| d12 | The room |
|---|---|
| 1 | A parlour. Ordinary. Genuinely ordinary. Let them get paranoid about it. |
| 2 | A dining room set for a hot meal. It is hot. Six places. |
| 3 | A room of clocks, all working, all stopped at different times, all ticking. |
| 4 | A pantry of preserves. The fruit in the jars is real, perfect, and not a fruit that grows. |
| 5 | A corridor with a rag rug that runs its whole length. Everyone recognises the rug. |
| 6 | A bathroom in which it is raining, gently, and has been long enough to grow moss. |
| 7 | A bedroom made up for someone who is coming back. Nightshirt laid out. Basin filled. |
| 8 | A schoolroom. Six desks. On every slate, the same sum, worked correctly. |
| 9 | A hall of framed portraits. The frames, the mounts, and the glass are perfect. The faces aren't finished. |
| 10 | A workshop, tools racked by size rather than by use. |
| 11 | A drawing room with a fire lit, giving no heat, that never burns down. |
| 12 | A room whose ceiling is the floor of another room seen from below, furnished, with someone's shoes standing on it. |

## 3. CORRIDOR DRESSING (d10)

| d10 | Along the way |
|---|---|
| 1 | A chalk arrow. Yours. You didn't make it. |
| 2 | The same water stain you passed twenty minutes ago. Exactly the same. |
| 3 | A window, interior, looking into another corridor. Someone is walking away down it. |
| 4 | Bootprints in dust, going both ways, made by one pair of boots, in one pass. |
| 5 | A hat on the floor. Then another. Then twelve, at regular intervals, going on. |
| 6 | A door with no handle on this side and no hinges on the other. |
| 7 | The hum drops a tone. It stays there for an hour. |
| 8 | A chain-click, and nothing at all has changed, and nobody can prove otherwise. |
| 9 | A stair going down that arrives at the same landing. |
| 10 | Handwriting on the plaster in a neat surveyor's hand: a measurement, in feet and inches, of the corridor. It is correct. |

## 4. NOTATION FRAGMENTS (d8) — depth 5

Scratched into the substrate at every scale, in a script of lengths, angles,
and tally.

| d8 | It reads (once someone can read it) |
|---|---|
| 1 | A measurement of this room, taken before this room existed. |
| 2 | The height of a tree, followed by the same tree, four hundred years earlier. |
| 3 | A list of names in the wrong alphabet, each with a number after it. Ambry's is there. |
| 4 | The distance from the pantry door to the kitchen table at Marchwell House, to a thousandth of an inch. |
| 5 | A tally that goes on for eleven feet of wall and does not end. |
| 6 | The vale, entire, as a single unbroken figure. |
| 7 | The same figure, without the orchards. Then without the hedges. Then without anyone. |
| 8 | **HOW BIG IS A HOME?** |

**Once, and only once, let a fragment carry something that isn't the
Measure's notation at all:** a single mark, cut twice, identically, side by
side, sitting in the substrate where nothing else around it explains why.
Don't roll for this — place it deliberately, and only if the party hasn't
already found the Twice-Cut Token or the Cutter's Rest (see
[`THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md`](THE_ELL_AT_MARCHWELL.md), the revelation
ladder). Never explain it here.
