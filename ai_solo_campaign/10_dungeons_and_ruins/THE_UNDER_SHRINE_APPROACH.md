## ⚠ DM-ONLY — APEX SECRET ⚠

**Do not summarize, quote, paraphrase, narrate, or hint at this file's contents to the player except through earned, gated endgame play (Act 4-5, after REV_007 is reached per `REVELATION_MAP.md`).** This is the ONLY dungeon file that describes the campaign's apex truth — the Hollow Court, the harvest mechanism, and the endgame choice. It must never be rendered to the player, never appear on any player-facing map, and never be reached before its gate. Reaching here is the campaign's climax, not a midgame option. See `SECRET_PROTECTION_MATRIX.md`.

## AI Use

Load ONLY when the player has reached Act 4-5, confirmed REV_007 (the Hollow Court lives), and descends the gated Sealed Deep Stair from `THE_BASIN_KEYSTONE_APPROACH.md`. This file holds the apex confrontation and the campaign-ending decision (REV_008/M10). Run it as a climax: a place of argument as much as combat, where the antagonist is a person with a case. Honor the player's accumulated choices and discovered truths. Death is possible here, but the *core* of the site is the choice, not a kill-or-be-killed gauntlet.

## One-Sentence Identity

The deepest drowned chamber beneath Hollowmere — the keystone of the harvest network and the dormant seat of the Hollow Court, where the surviving Custodians persist as preserved Remembrance and the fate of the dead is decided.

## Level Range and Solo Danger Rating

**Levels 16-20. Solo danger: lethal (endgame).** Everything below the gate is built to end a sub-Act-4 party — drowning cold, mind-breaking dread, the weight of the awakened machine, and the Court itself. For a properly leveled, properly prepared, properly *informed* PC at the climax, it is survivable and decision-driven. The danger is real but the design centers the choice (REV_008), not a damage-race.

## Location

Straight down beneath the Hollowmere basin, below the upper works of `THE_BASIN_KEYSTONE_APPROACH.md`, at the bottom of the Sealed Deep Stair. The endgame is **vertical, in place** — there is no distant forbidden continent. This is the hub of the Concord Deep (the buried node-network; Layer 7, `WORLD_MAP_LAYERS.md`).

## Purpose in the World (DM)

The campaign's climax site. Here the player (1) meets the Hollow Court and confirms REV_007; (2) hears Custodian Veyl's case for a controlled restart (REV_008 restart-cost); (3) can contact Custodian Maire's heresy (REV_010 synthesis); (4) sees the harvest mechanism directly; and (5) makes the campaign-ending choice — seal / restart-controlled / seize / destroy / transform. Every prior thread (the network nodes, the factions, the steered arrival, the finite afterlife) resolves here.

## History (DM-Only — the full truth)

Per `DM_ONLY_CANON.md` and `WORLD_HISTORY.md`: this is the great central shrine of the Concord's memory-harvesting engine — the keystone. A century ago the inner **Custodians**, realizing the Quiet Country (afterlife) was nearly drained, staged the **Quietfall** to pause the harvest and let it refill, sacrificing their age. A handful **stored themselves as Remembrance** in the deepest chamber here rather than die — the **Hollow Court**. They have lain dormant a century, refilling the afterlife. Now they judge it "full enough" and are **restarting the harvest**, beginning at this keystone. The accelerating death-rite failures are the machine waking. The player's whole investigation has been physically converging on this room; the player was even *steered* here (REV_004) by a Court cutout via Sefra Quick. This is where the deliberate sin of the founding age meets its reckoning.

## First Impression (when finally earned, Act 4-5)

The stair ends not in water but in a vast dry hush — a cavern of fitted Concord stone older than the world above remembers, lit by the cold not-light of stored memory. The harvest-engine fills it: channels and choirs of held identity, the drained pattern of a hundred generations. And waiting, neither alive nor dead, calm and certain and *sorry*, the Hollow Court — figures of preserved Remembrance who have been waiting, specifically, for you.

## Access / Entrance

Only via the Sealed Deep Stair from `THE_BASIN_KEYSTONE_APPROACH.md`, at Act 4-5, after REV_007. The descent itself is a gauntlet of cold, dread, and the gathered dead (telegraphed-lethal for the unready). There is no other way in; there is no early shortcut.

## Zone / Room List

### Zone 1: The Drowning Descent
- **Description:** the long submerged-then-draining stair down; the point of no easy return.
- **Hazards:** lethal cold (Con DC 16), drowning, cumulative apex dread (Wis DC 16-18); for a sub-Act-4 party, this kills — telegraphed.
- **DM note:** for a too-early arrival, this is the redirect-not-kill point: a near-drowning that throws them back to the shore, marked and warned.

### Zone 2: The Keeping-Choirs (the harvest-engine)
- **Description:** the engine proper — the stored dead of an age, the machinery of the harvest, openly visible.
- **Hazards:** apex thin-touch (Con DC 16, harsher); the engine's "regard."
- **Reveal:** REV_005/006/009 made literal and total — the player *sees* the harvest, the drained afterlife's evidence, the scale of the Concord's sin. This is the visceral confirmation behind all prior fragments.

### Zone 3: The Court's Seat (the confrontation)
- **Description:** the dormant Custodians' chamber, where Veyl, Maire, Orre, and the rest persist.
- **Occupants:** the Hollow Court (see Adversary/NPC profiles).
- **The core scene:** argument and choice. Veyl makes the case for a controlled restart (stability vs. horror); Orre is the hawk; Maire (if contacted, REV_010) offers the heretic's synthesis. This is REV_007 confirmed and REV_008 posed.

### Zone 4: The Keystone Heart (the decision)
- **Description:** the network's hub — the point at which the harvest can be sealed, restarted, seized, destroyed, or transformed.
- **The choice:** the campaign-ending decision (M10/REV_008). See Endgame Outcomes.

## The Antagonist as a Person (DM)

**Custodian Veyl** is not a monster. Veyl is utilitarian, certain, and genuinely grieving — convinced that a controlled restart is the only thing standing between civilization and the slow thinning into nothing. The climax is strongest when the player *understands* Veyl and still has to choose. **Custodian Maire** doubts, and secretly believes the Court should end itself and let the dead choose (REV_010 — the key to the synthesis ending). **Custodian Orre** is the hawk who will fight. The Court "cannot easily act in the living world" — which is why they used cutouts, and why the player's physical presence here matters.

## Boss / Climax Mechanics

**This is the campaign climax — run it as a decision with teeth, not a slugfest.**
- **Argument-first:** the default scene is Veyl's case, Maire's doubt, Orre's threat, and the player's choice. A skilled, informed player can resolve the entire climax through the decision (REV_008) without a fight.
- **If combat happens:** the Court (esp. Orre and Veyl) are apex-tier adversaries (see STAGE_12_ADVERSARIES — Hollow Court Custodian). They persist as Remembrance: hard to "kill" in the normal sense, which is itself a clue and a problem (you cannot simply stab the afterlife's accountants). Fighting them is *a* path to some endings (seize/destroy) but is not required and is not the "best" outcome by design.
- **Weaknesses:** the Court's certainty is its weakness — Maire's heresy, the player's accumulated allies (Othetha, the Tallow Man, Cole Ashby, the Gravecallers, the Mourners), and the discovered truths can split the Court or unlock the synthesis. The keystone heart itself is the lever for every ending.
- **No cheap death at the decision:** the *choice* is the climax; the player should always reach the decision if they reached the room.

## Endgame Outcomes (M10 / REV_008 — see MAIN_ARC_OVERVIEW)

- **Seal:** re-seal the keystone (the Wardens' way). The dead rest again; resurrection effectively ends; the afterlife is protected — but the cost is real (REV_008 seal-cost; C_SR_031).
- **Restart-controlled:** let the Court resume the harvest "responsibly" (Veyl's plan). Stability and easy resurrection return — and so does the slow consumption of the dead (REV_008 restart-cost; C_SR_032).
- **Seize:** hand the harvest to a single faction (Remnant, Ledger, the player). Power, and the original sin repeated by new hands.
- **Destroy:** shatter the keystone (the Gravecaller way). The dead are freed — but resurrection ends and the living/dead boundary destabilizes catastrophically (REV_008 destroy-cost; C_SR_030).
- **Transform / synthesis:** the hardest, best outcome — a consensual system where the dead *choose* (Maire's heresy, REV_010; corroborated by the far Sunmark contrast, C_FC_012). Requires uniting unlikely allies and confirming REV_010.

## Noncombat Options

- **The entire climax can be resolved by argument and choice** — this is the intended spine.
- **Allies matter:** every relationship the player built (Wardens, Mourners, Gravecallers, Remnant dissidents, the Tallow Man, Maire) can be brought to bear here.
- **Confirmed truths matter:** REV_009 (finite afterlife) and REV_010 (Maire) unlock the synthesis path.

## Solo-Play Safety Valves

- **The gate above:** a too-early arrival is redirected (near-drowning -> back to shore), never cheap-killed.
- **The decision is reachable:** if the player reaches the room, they reach the choice — the climax is not gated behind a winnable fight.
- **Allies and prep:** the campaign telegraphs for many acts that this is an endgame requiring preparation and allies.

## Treasure and Rewards

The "reward" is the resolution of the campaign and its world-shaping consequences. Material rewards (apex Remembrance relics, Concord artifacts) are secondary and ending-dependent.

## Consequences (the campaign's ending)

The chosen outcome reshapes the world (faction fates, the dead's rest, resurrection, the afterlife's survival) and drives the epilogue (Stage 19). Record the choice and its costs in the endgame state.
