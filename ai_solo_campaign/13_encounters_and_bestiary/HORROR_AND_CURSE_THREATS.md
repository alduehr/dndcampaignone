## AI Use

The campaign's **original horror and curse bestiary** — the undead, hauntings, memory-echoes, and afflictions that arise from the world's hidden truth: the Concord's harvest of the dead's **Remembrance** and its slow reawakening leakage. These are the campaign's signature threats. They are **mournful, not monstrous**; most are **resolvable without combat** (a true rite, a name spoken, a grief answered). This is intentional — the horror of this world is *grief and forgetting*, not gore.

**This is original content built on the campaign's own Remembrance cosmology** — the *world* is original (Vael / Orrun is not Ravenloft, the Domains of Dread, or any proprietary setting; no darklord, domain, named NPC, or setting lore is imported). For **mechanical creature references**, this bestiary draws on published D&D 5e gothic-horror sources — **Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (MToF)**, **Volo's Guide to Monsters (VGtM)**, **Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft (VRGtR, 2021)**, and **Ravenloft: The Horrors Within (RtHW, 2026)** — exactly the way it references the Monster Manual: **Track A, name + source + role + solo notes, no stat blocks copied.** All source attributions below were **verified against the published books in the 2026-07-07 pass** (several were previously misattributed to VRGtR; one non-official creature was replaced by the Sorrowsworn). These creatures are used **only where the campaign's own themes already call for them** (memory-drain, soul-consumption, grief-swarms, hollow/thin-born dread). See `CREATURE_SOURCE_REFERENCE.md` for the source-handling rule. Original Remembrance creatures remain Track-B **anchored to a named official chassis**; reskins ride a Track-A chassis. No copied stat blocks anywhere.

**Secrecy:** the *cause* of all of this (the harvest, the Hollow Court, the keystone) is **DM-only** until its gates (REV layer). The *creatures* are player-facing as folk horror ("the dead won't rest"); the *explanation* is not. Never narrate the true mechanism before it is earned.

## The Cosmological Spine (DM-only, why these exist)

The Concord harvested **Remembrance** — the substance of a dead person's identity — and stored it. The harvest paused (the seal), but the stored Remembrance is **leaking and reawakening**. Where it leaks, the dead come back *wrong, incomplete, grieving, or hungry for what was taken from them*. This single cause produces the whole horror roster below. The deeper one goes (nodes → Concord Deep → Under-Shrine), the worse and stranger it gets. Do not reveal this spine; deliver its symptoms.

## 1. The Drowned / Drifting Remembrance — the signature dead

*(Full Track-B/R profile in `STAGE_12_ADVERSARIES.md` #1; summarized here as the horror archetype.)*
- The mournful incorporeal dead drawn to the nodes — the campaign's default haunting.
- **Tone:** grieving, slow, drawn toward the basin/keystone, not malicious unless provoked.
- **Resolution:** true rite (Religion DC 13), parley (Insight/Persuasion DC 12–13), or naming the dead. Combat optional.
- **Warning signs:** cold, weeping sounds, grey light, dimming lanterns, the dead facing one direction (toward the keystone — a clue, C-M5).
- **Solo danger:** low alone; moderate as a provoked group (use a mass-rite or retreat out — never a swarm with no answer).

## 2. Memory-Echo Haunt — a place that remembers

A **non-corporeal scar** left where Remembrance was torn out. Not a "ghost" with a will — a *recording* that replays. It cannot truly attack but can **frighten, disorient, and afflict** (see The Forgetting). It is a clue you can stand inside.
- **Type/role:** Spirit (echo), CR ~1–6 by depth. **Encounter role:** atmosphere / clue / soft hazard. **Chassis:** use **Specter** (2024 MM), modified as below.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** no HP to "kill" in the normal sense — it is dispelled by *resolving the memory* (Religion/History/Insight DC 13–16) or by a true rite; if forced, treat as a Specter-like presence (AC 12, ~22 HP) that cannot leave its scar and re-forms unless the memory is laid.
- **Effect:** within it, Wis save (DC 13–16) or be **frightened** and relive a fragment of the harvested dead's last moment (deliver a clue; never a lore-dump).
- **Warning signs:** the air "skips," sounds arrive late, a smell of cold water and old smoke, a scene half-glimpsed.
- **Noncombat outs:** witness it, record it, answer the grief, leave. **Solo danger:** low (it cannot pursue); the *hazard it stands on* may be real (deep water, collapse).
- **Linked:** M5 (the restless dead), the node trail, REV_001/002.

## 3. The Wrong-Come-Back (Revenant) — a botched return

A dead person **leaked back into a body** by a failed or stolen resurrection (Gravecaller work, relic mishandling, harvest leakage). It is *mostly* the person — and that is the horror. It remembers loved ones, has a grievance, and is **coming apart**.
- **Type/role:** Undead, Medium, CR ~3–8. **Role:** tragic antagonist / moral dilemma. **Chassis:** use **Revenant** (2024 MM, CR 5), scaled down/up by tier.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** AC 14, HP ~50–90 by tier, Speed 30; relentless (immune to fright, doesn't stop at half HP — fights *to its purpose*, not survival); a single fixated grievance drives it; resistant to nonmagical physical at higher tiers.
- **Tactics:** pursues its **one purpose** (reach a person, a place, a truth); ignores bystanders unless they block it; cannot be reasoned out of the purpose, only *helped to complete or release it*.
- **Morale/retreat:** does not flee; **only stops when its purpose is met or it is laid to rest** (true rite Religion DC 15–17, or fulfilling/redirecting the grievance).
- **Warning signs:** a dead person "seen in town," a grave disturbed from inside, a family's terror, a name repeated.
- **Noncombat outs:** discover and complete/redirect the purpose; the rite; rarely, a bargain. **Solo danger:** moderate–high (relentless), but its single-mindedness is the lever — redirect it.
- **Linked:** the opening (Wren Brevin is a *gentle* version of this), M5, Gravecallers, REV_001.

## 4. Barrow-Wight / Barrow-Shade — the old defended dead

Pre-Concord and early-Concord burial-folk whose Remembrance was *taken late or incompletely*, leaving angry, possessive dead in the barrows.
- **Type/role:** Undead, CR ~4 (reskin of a Wight-like/Wraith-like chassis). **Role:** crypt brute / drainer. **Chassis:** use **Wight** (2024 MM, CR 3) or **Wraith** (2024 MM, CR 5) by depth, modified as below.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** AC 14, HP ~50–60, Speed 30; life-drain touch (necrotic + max-HP reduction flavor); can rouse 1–2 lesser dead (skeletons/zombies); **bound to its barrow** (no pursuit past the threshold).
- **Tactics:** guards grave-goods, drains intruders, rouses the lesser dead once when pressed; **stands down if its grave is respected** (a Mourner's rite, replacing a taken grave-good).
- **Warning signs:** disturbed barrow-mounds, a cold that "watches," grave-goods laid out wrong, lesser dead deferring to one.
- **Noncombat outs:** grave-rite (Religion DC 14–15), restore what was taken, withdraw past the threshold. **Solo danger:** moderate; bound and retreat-rich.
- **Linked:** Marrowdowns barrows, the Barrow of Nine Doors, pre-Concord echoes (C_MD_001), REV_005.

## 5. Salt-Mummy / Desiccated Dead — the preserved dead of the Saltmere

In the dying salt sea, the dead **do not rot — they dry and persist**, and where old Remembrance lingers in the salt, they wake. A different flavor of the same wound: preservation instead of return.
- **Type/role:** Undead, CR ~3–6. **Role:** slow brute / curse-bearer. **Chassis:** use **Mummy** (2024 MM, CR 3), modified: the dreadful-glare fright becomes the desiccating curse below; salt-themed.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** AC 12–14, HP ~45–75; slam + a **desiccating touch** (necrotic + reduces healing/hydration flavor; a curse of thirst on a failed Con save DC 13–15); vulnerable to a *true rite* and to fresh water as ritual; immune to poison/exhaustion.
- **Tactics:** shamble, surround in the flats, curse rather than kill; **break when their salt-grave is named/blessed**.
- **Warning signs:** salt-white standing figures in the flats, cracked-earth footprints, a taste of brine, mirage-shapes.
- **Noncombat outs:** rite (Religion DC 14–16), fresh-water offering, avoid the flats at midday glare. **Solo danger:** moderate (the *environment* — heat, thirst, glare — amplifies them).
- **Linked:** Saltmere drowned towns, Bonepan Flats, pre-Concord echo (C_SR2_001).

## 6. Drowned Mariner Dead — the sea's returned

The coast's drowned, returned worse than before — the **drowned-tide** (the maritime harvest leaking). Wreckers, the salt-and-tide rite, and the Pale Coast / Hollow Gulf folk all know them.
- **Type/role:** Undead, CR ~2–5. **Role:** coastal ambusher / mournful drowned. **Chassis:** use the published **drowned-dead series — Drowned Ascetic / Drowned Blade / Drowned Assassin / Drowned Master (GoS)**, retuned mournful-not-vengeful; or a Zombie/Ghoul (2024 MM) at the low end.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** AC 12–13, HP ~30–60; amphibious, drag/grapple toward water (Athletics contest), chilling touch; can rise in 1–2s from surf or wreck; bound to tide-line/wreck (won't pursue far inland).
- **Tactics:** rise from surf, drag toward deep water, then release the drowned; **settle when the salt-and-tide rite is performed**.
- **Warning signs:** a wreck the tide won't take, drowned faces in the foam, the salt-bell tolling on its own, a "wrong" low tide.
- **Noncombat outs:** the salt-and-tide rite (Religion DC 14–15, Salt-Mother Bryd's lore), stay above the tide-line, name the drowned. **Solo danger:** moderate (drag-to-drown is the real risk — telegraphed).
- **Linked:** Pale Coast, Hollow Gulf, Drowned Steps, the drowned-tide (M5 oblique), REV_002.

## 7. Ash-Bound Dead — the fire-preserved dead

In Emberfell and the Cindern Waste, where heat and old works meet, the dead are **fused into ash and slag**, animate and burning. The volcanic mirror of the Saltmere's preserved dead.
- **Type/role:** Undead (fire-touched), CR ~4–7. **Role:** burning brute / area-denial. **Chassis:** use **Wight** (2024 MM) with a **Magma Mephit/Salamander-style** heat aura and fire immunity bolted on, or a **Mummy** (2024 MM) reflavored ash-and-slag at the high end.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** AC 13–15, HP ~55–90; immune fire, vulnerable cold; burning touch (fire + ongoing), a heat aura (Con DC 14 within 10 ft); slow but hard to put down.
- **Tactics:** advance through fire/ash the living must avoid, corner targets against heat hazards; **quelled by a rite + dousing** or by the Emberfell heretic-sage's lore.
- **Warning signs:** standing slag-figures, a corpse that won't cool, ash that holds a shape, the air shimmering wrong.
- **Noncombat outs:** rite + water/cold, fire-ward reading (Arcana/Concord Script DC 15), the heretic-sage's aid. **Solo danger:** high (environmental fire + the dead together); gear/water-gated.
- **Linked:** Emberfell, Cindern Waste Buried Works, Caldera Descent, C_FC_016.

## 8. The Drift-Line Chorus — a mass-haunt phenomenon

Not a creature: a **field of dozens-to-hundreds of drifting dead** all facing one direction (toward the keystone). Standing in it is the campaign's purest horror-clue. It is environmental and **cannot be fought** — it can only be witnessed, navigated, or resolved en masse.
- **Type/role:** Spirit (mass) / environmental. **Role:** clue / dread set-piece (C-M5 field-proof). **Chassis:** none needed (it is a hazard, not a combatant); if a portion *manifests* under provocation, use **Banshee** or **Ghost** (2024 MM) for the focal spirit and treat the rest as the environmental saves below.
- **Abbreviated mechanics:** within it, periodic Wis saves (DC 13–15) vs. **Hungering Grief** (see curses); a mass-rite (Religion DC 16+, often needing an ally or multiple casters) can lay a *portion*; otherwise navigate (Survival DC 13) and leave.
- **Warning signs:** the count rising, all faces turning the same way, the cold deepening, lanterns failing across the whole field.
- **Noncombat outs:** witness, map the direction (it points home — a clue), mass-rite, retreat. **Solo danger:** low physically, high *psychologically* and as a curse-source.
- **Linked:** Greyfens drift-line, basin shore, Saltmere drowned towns, M5, REV_001/002 (it *points at the keystone*).

## 9. Hag-Touched Grief (Green/Sea Hag opportunists)

Where the dead won't rest, **hags move in to feed on grief** — Track-A creatures (Green Hag, Sea Hag, CR 2–3) given a campaign role: they *bargain* with the bereaved, sell false comfort, and worsen hauntings for their own gain.
- **Reskin notes:** the hag is a *predator of mourning* — she offers to "bring back" or "quiet" a dead loved one, at a terrible price. A moral-dilemma antagonist, not just a fight.
- **Noncombat outs:** see through the bargain (Insight DC 15), expose her, refuse the deal, a true rite that undercuts her hold. **Solo danger:** moderate; her *bargain* is the trap, not her claws.
- **Linked:** Greyfens deep, Sallowmarch, Pale Coast caves; folk-horror flavor of Gravecaller appeal (false comfort), MYSTERY_ENCOUNTERS.

## 10. The Deep Horror (DM-ONLY — gated)

The deepest expression: **Memory-Eaters / Mind-fog Lurkers** (custom aberrations) in the Drowned Steps and the deepest Concord nodes, and ultimately the **Hollow Court Custodians** (apex, `STAGE_12_ADVERSARIES.md` #8). Here the harvest is not leaking but *present*; the dead are not mournful but *consumed*; memory itself is prey.
- **Mind-fog Lurker (custom aberration, CR ~6–10):** **Chassis:** use **Adult Oblex** (MToF, CR 5) scaled up, trading impersonation for the fog-form; or pair with the published **Mist Horror** (RtHW) at tier. AC 14, HP ~80–130; a fog that *eats recent memory* (Int/Wis save DC 15–17 or lose the last hour, a scene, a name — see The Forgetting); avoids light/fire; lurks, never charges. **Resolution:** light, anchors (write things down — a literal solo-play tip), exit the fog; combat is a poor option (it has no morale to break and re-forms). **Solo danger:** very high — it is a *time/memory* threat, not a HP threat. **DM-only depth; gate to high tier.**
- **The Custodians:** never deploy before REV_007 / the endgame. They are persons with an argument, not monsters; the climax is a **decision**. See the gated profile.

## Published Gothic-Horror Creatures (MToF / VGtM / VRGtR / RtHW) — Track A

These are **published D&D 5e creatures referenced (not reproduced)** for use where the campaign's horror themes already fit. Each is reskinned to the Remembrance cosmology in prose; the AI DM pulls the actual stat block from the named book. **Never copy stat blocks. Never import Ravenloft setting lore** (no domains, darklords, or named NPCs) — only the creature, retuned to our themes. All keep the campaign's design rules: **grief not gore, telegraphed, bound/retreat-rich, resolvable** wherever possible.

### H1. Oblex (Adult & Spawn) — *the memory-drinker; the harvest's mirror*
> **Oblex Spawn** (MToF, Ooze, CR 1/4) and **Adult Oblex** (MToF, Ooze, CR 5; reprinted in MotM) — an ooze that **absorbs and can mimic the memories** of creatures it consumes. The single best published mirror of the Harvest itself; treat any oblex as a *thing that is "remembering" the dead it ate.*
> - **Habitat fit:** Remembrance-leak ruins, Concord nodes, memory-scarred fen (Greyfens deep, Sallowmarch fever channels), Ashmarket/relic-vault sludge, deep Concord vaults.
> - **Role:** lurker/controller — drains memory on touch (Int/Wis drain), **impersonates remembered voices** to lure (a missing loved one, a dead Warden, the PC's own past).
> - **Campaign hook:** an oblex in a Concord ruin may be *replaying* the harvested — it can speak with a dead person's words, delivering an M5/M6 fragment as horror. **DM-gate:** it knows fragments, not the apex truth.
> - **Solo danger:** moderate (spawn) to high (adult) at L5–8; the *deception* is the trap. **Outs:** disbelieve the false voice (Insight/Wis), light + fire, refuse the bait, fight it as the ooze it is. Reskin note: it is **harvest-residue given hunger**, not a generic monster.

### H2. Allip — *the secret-maddened dead* (Hollow-Court echo)
> **Allip** (MToF, Undead, CR 5) — an incorporeal undead **driven mad by a terrible secret** it is compelled to spread. Tailor-made for the campaign's buried truth.
> - **Habitat fit:** deep Concord nodes, the Sealed Archive's edges, sites where someone *died holding the harvest's secret*, Heartlands noble halls.
> - **Role:** controller/haunt — maddening whispers, Wisdom drain, drifts and cannot leave its haunt.
> - **Campaign hook:** an allip's whispered "secret" is a **gated M6/M9-adjacent fragment** — deliver only the oblique piece (e.g. "they kept us; they ate the keeping"), never the full mechanism. A magnificent dread-vector that must stay *partial.*
> - **Solo danger:** moderate at L5–8 (Wis-save attrition). **Outs:** resolve/answer the secret (a rite, naming the wrong done), leave the haunt, magic that ends incorporeal undead. **DM:** keep the secret *cryptic*; an allip should unsettle, not explain.

### H3. Sorrowsworn — *grief made flesh; the mass dead's despair*
> **The Sorrowsworn** (MToF, Monstrosities: **the Lost** CR 7, **the Wretched** CR 1/4, **the Lonely** CR 9, **the Hungry** CR 11, **the Angry** CR 13) — published embodiments of **grief, loss, loneliness, and despair**. The single best official match for what this campaign's unanswered mass-death sites produce. *(Replaces the earlier "Caller in Darkness" entry — that creature has no official 5e printing; the Sorrowsworn are verified MToF and a tighter thematic fit.)*
> - **Habitat fit:** the worst drift-lines, the Saltmere drowned towns, Drowned Steps, a battlefield or plague-pit soaked in unmourned dead — places the **Drift-Line Chorus** has curdled past mourning into hunger.
> - **Role:** the Lost is the elite grief-swarm (embraces and clings to the despairing); the Wretched hunt in pitiable scavenging packs; the Lonely/Hungry/Angry scale the theme up by tier.
> - **Campaign hook:** a sorrowsworn is what an unanswered mass-haunt **becomes** — grief refused so long it takes a body of its own. A warning that the rites are failing (M5). It is the Chorus weaponized.
> - **Solo danger:** the Wretched are moderate in packs at L3–6; the Lost is high at L7–10; the greater three are tier-3/4 threats. **Outs:** a mass-rite (as with the Chorus, often needing an ally), answering the specific grief that spawned it, breaking line of sight and leaving; do not slug the Lost toe-to-toe alone. Reskin: **collected harvested grief**, facing the keystone like all the rest.

### H4. Soul Monger — *the devourer of life and memory* (high-tier Custodian mirror)
> **Soul Monger** (MToF, Undead, CR 11) — devours the **life force and memories** of its victims, hoarding stolen souls. A thematic mirror of what the Custodians *do*, made monstrous.
> - **Habitat fit:** the deepest Concord nodes, the Concord Deep's outer reaches, a far ruin where a lesser harvester *kept going* — **DM-only depth; gate to high tier (13+).**
> - **Role:** apex drainer — soul/memory theft, hoarded-soul defenses.
> - **Campaign hook:** a soul monger is a **fallen would-be Custodian or a runaway harvest-engine** — a dark foreshadow of the Hollow Court, used to make the apex truth *felt* before it is *named.* **Never** let it explain the keystone; it is a symptom, a sibling-horror, not the answer.
> - **Solo danger:** very high. **Outs:** free its hoarded souls (a rite/the right relic), light/anchors vs the memory theft, exit; this is a set-piece, not a wandering fight. **DM-gate hard.**

### H5. Bodak — *the death-gaze drained dead*
> **Bodak** (VGtM, Undead, CR 6; reprinted in MotM) — a humanoid **hollowed by exposure to annihilating death**, spreading death through its gaze.
> - **Habitat fit:** the deepest harvest-scarred sites, Drowned Steps, a place where the *taking* was total — where a person was **drained past returning** (a wrong-come-back that came back *empty*).
> - **Role:** dread lurker — death-gaze, aura of annihilation; shuns light.
> - **Campaign hook:** a bodak is the **thin-born horror taken to its end** — what's left when *all* of a person's Remembrance was stripped. A grim mid-tier omen near deep nodes.
> - **Solo danger:** high at L6–9 (the gaze). **Outs:** avoid eye contact / fight in light, terrain, a rite for the emptied dead; bound to its dark haunt — retreat works.

### H6. Deathlock — *the patron-bound dead caster*
> **Deathlock** (MToF, Undead, CR 4; **Deathlock Mastermind** CR 8; **Deathlock Wight** CR 3; reprinted in MotM) — an undead spellcaster **bound in death to a dark patron's service**.
> - **Habitat fit:** Gravecaller deep-work gone wrong, a Concord caster who *would not stop serving the order*, a Custodian-touched ruin — reskin the "patron" as **the harvest / the Concord's purpose**, not an outside power.
> - **Role:** caster-controller — ranged necrotic, undead command; the *spellcasting* dead the campaign otherwise lacks.
> - **Campaign hook:** a deathlock is a **dead Concord ritualist still tending the work** — a chilling proof that the order's purpose outlived its people (M6).
> - **Solo danger:** low (basic) to high (mastermind) by tier. **Outs:** sever the binding (a rite, destroying its anchor-relic), cover vs its spells, parley with what's left of the person.

### H7. Will-o'-Wisp (grief-flavored) & Vampiric Mist — *the luring deaths*
> **Will-o'-Wisp** (2024 MM, Undead, CR 2) and **Vampiric Mist** (MToF, Undead, CR 3; reprinted in MotM) — drifting lights/mists that **lure the grieving to a drowning or draining death**.
> - **Habitat fit:** Greyfens, basin shallows, Sallowmarch, Drowned Steps, any fen/coast where the dead and deep water meet (already seeded in the Sundering Reach Greyfens table).
> - **Role:** lure/ambush — the wisp baits toward deep water/sinkholes; the mist drains blood/life in fog.
> - **Campaign hook:** folk say the lights are *the dead calling their loved ones home* — a grief-lure that **weaponizes** the campaign's central ache (the wish to follow the dead).
> - **Solo danger:** moderate (the *environment* it lures you into is the real killer). **Outs:** disbelieve (Wis), stay to known ground, light/sunlight vs the mist.

### H8. Ghost & Specter Variants (grief/memory) — *the dead who remember their dying*
> **Ghost** (2024 MM, Undead, CR 4), **Specter** (2024 MM, CR 1; **Poltergeist** is the 2014 MM specter variant), **Banshee** (2024 MM, CR 4) — already used; gothic framing sharpens them: dead who **re-enact their death** and can possess, drain, or wail.
> - **Habitat fit:** Concord halls, Heartlands noble ruins, drowned towns, old barrows, any grief-saturated site.
> - **Campaign reskin:** these are **wrong-come-backs that stayed incorporeal** — the person's Remembrance partly leaked back but never found a body. Possession/wail/drain are framed as *the dead trying to be remembered* (M5).
> - **Solo danger:** moderate; **outs:** answer the death (a rite, completing an unfinished purpose — like the Wrong-Come-Back), consecrated ground, leaving the haunt. Always offer the *help-it* path before the fight.

### H9. Loup Garou (rare apex) & Lycanthrope-Horror — *use sparingly*
> **Loup Garou** (VRGtR, CR 13; updated in RtHW) and standard **were-creatures** (2024 MM) — pack-lord werewolf and lesser lycanthropes.
> - **Habitat fit:** **only** the deepest, most isolated wilderness where a *curse-of-the-beast* theme already fits — far Hethewald deep-wood, remote Karran/Highmark valleys. **Not** a Remembrance horror; an *independent* gothic threat for high-tier wilderness.
> - **Role:** apex predator / curse-bearer (the curse is the lasting stakes, not just the claws).
> - **Solo danger:** very high (loup garou) — a true high-tier wilderness boss; lesser lycanthropes moderate. **Outs:** silvered/magic weapons, the cure-quest for the curse, avoidance; telegraph heavily (kills, tracks, a terrified hold). **Use rarely** — this is flavor at the edges of the map, not a core campaign monster.

### H10. Gallows Speaker — *the bound dead that speaks* (RtHW)
> **Gallows Speaker** (RtHW; verified in the published bestiary — AI DM pulls type/CR from the book) — an undead bound to the place of its death, compelled to **speak the stories of the dead**.
> - **Habitat fit:** gibbet-crossroads (Marrow Cross), execution grounds, the Heartlands' fallen halls, barrow-edges — anywhere the dead were made publicly and left unmourned.
> - **Role:** dread-oracle — a *talking clue* wrapped in horror. It does not primarily fight; it **tells**.
> - **Campaign hook:** the single best published vector for gated M5/M6 fragments — the harvested dead speaking *through* it in pieces ("they kept us; we were kept"). **DM-gate hard:** it delivers fragments per `SECRET_PROTECTION_MATRIX.md` wording, never the apex mechanism.
> - **Solo danger:** low-moderate physically; the *cost of listening* (fright, grief-saves) is the price. **Outs:** listen and leave, answer a named dead's grief, a true rite to release it.

### H11. Necrichor — *the death-fluid horror* (VRGtR / RtHW)
> **Necrichor** (VRGtR, updated in RtHW) — undead essence in **liquid form**, seeping, possessing, and puppeting.
> - **Habitat fit:** harvest-leak sites, the Rendering-Deeps' residue, botched Gravecaller work, relic-vault spill — the campaign's "blood of the taken."
> - **Role:** lurker/controller — a horror that hides *in* things (a font, a flooded chamber, a wound).
> - **Campaign hook:** what leaks from a cracked node is not water. A necrichor at a Concord site is **residue of the taking given hunger** (M5 oblique; corroborates the Relic-Sludge trail).
> - **Solo danger:** moderate-high (possession/control is the trap). **Outs:** radiant/holy answers, don't touch the fluid, sever what it hides in, a rite over the spill.

### H12. The Preserved-Body Horrors — *the kept dead* (VRGtR / RtHW)
> **Jiangshi** (VRGtR/RtHW — rigid preserved dead), **Boneless** (VRGtR/RtHW — collapsed skin-and-meat dead), **Carrionette** (VRGtR, Construct CR 1 — soul-trapping puppet), **Brain in a Jar** (VRGtR/RtHW — preserved mind), **Bodytaker Plant & Podling** (RtHW — body-snatcher flora).
> - **Habitat fit:** Jiangshi → Saltmere preserved dead (kin to the Salt-Mummy); Boneless → drowned towns and plague-pits; Carrionette → Ashmarket relic-trade and Glassmere curiosity-vaults (a "relic" that moves); Brain in a Jar → deep Concord vaults **only, DM-gated** — it is the harvest's stored-identity horror made literal (M6-adjacent; fragments only); Bodytaker Plant → Hethewald deep-wood / Old Holds (the "wrong returned" as flora).
> - **Role:** preservation-horror — the campaign's ache (keeping the dead) made monstrous. Each is a dark mirror of what the Concord did *on purpose*.
> - **Solo danger:** low (Carrionette) to high (Bodytaker/Brain). **Outs:** every one is telegraphed (wrong stillness, preserved smell, a "doll" that watched); rites, fire/radiant, refusing the lure, leaving.

### H13. Mist Horror & the Deep Cosmic Terrors — *DM-GATED* (RtHW)
> **Mist Horror** (RtHW — memory-mist made predatory) and the cosmic-horror deep entries: **Nightgaunt**, **Gug**, **Shoggoth**, **Greater Star Spawn Emissary** (RtHW; Star Spawn Emissary also VRGtR).
> - **Habitat fit:** Mist Horror → the drift-line fog curdled (pairs with the custom Mind-fog Lurker; Greyfens deep, basin fog, Drowned Steps approaches). The cosmic four → **Drowned Steps depths, the Concord Deep's outer reaches, the deepest Karran cuts ONLY** — tier 3–4, heavily telegraphed.
> - **Role:** the *alien* end of the horror spectrum — where grief-horror gives way to things the Concord's diggers should never have reached.
> - **Campaign rule:** these are **atmosphere-of-the-deep threats, NOT the apex answer.** They never explain, guard, or substitute for the keystone/Court; they are what lives *past the edge* of the harvest's map. Deploy sparingly — one per campaign is plenty for most.
> - **Solo danger:** very high to extreme. **Outs:** these are set-pieces with retreat routes, not wandering encounters; scouting, light, and leaving are always viable. **DM-gate to tier 3–4; never before.**

> **Design note for all of the above:** these published creatures are the *worst-case* end of the campaign's gentle-horror spectrum. Default to the mournful, resolvable Track-B dead (sections 1–8); deploy VRGtR creatures when a site has **curdled** — when grief was refused, when the taking was total, when the secret festered. Even then: telegraph, allow retreat, and where the fiction allows, offer the rite that *answers* rather than the blade that *ends*.

## Original Curses & Afflictions

These are **conditions**, not creatures — Track-B custom mechanics on top of 5e, all *reversible* (cheap permanent curses are bad solo design).

| Curse / Affliction | Trigger | Effect (abbreviated) | Cure / Relief |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Thin-touch** (registered term) | exposure to raw Remembrance relics ("old glass"), deep nodes | Disadvantage on Death saves until relieved; cold, hollow feeling | a true rite, or a long rest at a settled shrine (`MAGIC_RULES.md`) |
| **The Forgetting** | memory-echo haunts, Mind-fog Lurkers, deep Custodian work | lose recent memory (an hour, a scene, a name) on failed Int/Wis save | leaving the source; written anchors; a restorative rite; time. **DM-gate severity by tier** |
| **Hungering Grief** | drift-lines, failed funerals, mass-haunts | compulsion to grieve/stay/join the dead (Wis save or lose actions to mourning) | answering the grief, a rite, an ally's anchor, leaving the field |
| **Remembrance-Drain Curse** | botched rites, mishandled relics, wight/wraith drain | reduced max HP / "thinned" vitality flavor until relieved | true rite (Religion DC 15), rest at a true shrine, removing the relic |
| **The Drowned-Tide mark** | the salt-and-tide rite failed, drowned-dead contact | the sea "claims" you — disadvantage near deep water, drowned dreams | complete the salt-and-tide rite; Salt-Mother Bryd's lore |

**Solo-play rule for all curses:** every curse above has a *clear, reachable relief* and **telegraphed onset**. Never apply a curse with no path out, and never let one silently end the campaign — it should drive a quest, not a tombstone.

## Horror Encounter-Design Reminders (apply to all)

- **Grief, not gore.** The fear is forgetting, returning wrong, and losing the dead twice — not monsters in the dark.
- **Most of the dead can be *helped*, not just fought.** A rite, a name, a completed purpose. Always offer it.
- **Telegraph the supernatural** before it acts (cold, light, sound, the dead's posture).
- **The dead are clues.** They face the keystone; they replay the harvest; they grieve a specific theft. Deliver the breadcrumb (M5, REV_001/002), never the whole truth.
- **Bound and retreat-rich.** Site-bound dead cannot chase; the PC can always withdraw.
- **DM-only cause.** Never explain the harvest/Court/keystone before it is earned.
