## AI Use

**MIXED SECRECY.** Player-facing description blocks are safe to surface when the player finds/identifies a relic. **DM-Only blocks are never revealed early** — several relics point toward M6–M9 and the harvest, and a leaked DM block would spoil the central mystery. Load this file when a major relic enters play, is sought by a faction, or is named in a quest.

**Golden rule:** every relic here is a **source of choices, costs, leverage, risk, or access — never a clean solution.** All are intentionally placed (no random artifact drops). All have owners, histories, and factions that want them.

## Tier Legend

- **Rare / Very Rare / Legendary / Artifact** — D&D rarity, gating roughly to the level bands in `SOLO_REWARD_BALANCE.md`.
- **Access gate** — what the player must do/reach before the relic can be acquired *or* fully used.
- **Mystery link** — which of M1–M10 the relic corroborates. Relics may *point toward* a mystery but must **never resolve the apex (M7/M9/M10) early**.

## 1. The Grief-Glass — *Rare relic (requires attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (Remembrance relic; a fist-sized sphere of cloudy "old glass").
- **Origin:** A Concord harvest-relic; the clearest single demonstration that the dead can be *kept* in an object.

**Player-Facing:** A heavy sphere of grey-green glass, warm to the touch, that seems to hold a slow drifting smoke. When you cradle it and concentrate, it shows you — felt more than seen — the **final memory of the last person whose Remembrance it held**: a face, a place, a last unfinished thought. Folk call it a saint's-tear or a "weeping stone." Both the Mourners' Circle (who would lay it to rest) and the Gravecallers (who would use it) want it badly.

**Mechanical effect (abbreviated, 5e-compatible):** Once per long rest, touch the Grief-Glass and spend 10 minutes to replay one held death-memory as a clear vision (DM delivers a clue scene). It can also store one *Speak with Dead*-style answer for later release (1 charge; recharges when a true death-rite is performed near it). Unprotected handling (no gloves/rite) for over a minute inflicts **Thin-touch** (disadvantage on Death saves until a true rite or shrine long-rest).

- **Limitation / cost:** It is a clue engine, not a weapon. Each vision is harrowing (Wis save DC 13 or shaken/1 level of stress flavor). It cannot be sold without drawing the Ledger, the Mourners, and the Gravecallers into conflict over the holder.
- **Links:** M2 (proves relics store the dead), M5 (the drift). Mourners' Circle Rank-3 reward path; Gravecaller temptation. Found in `THE_PEAT_CHAPEL` or `THE_GREYFENS_DEEP` (DM choice).
- **Solo balance:** Pure utility/clue value; no combat power. Safe to hand a low-mid PC because it *creates* danger (Thin-touch, faction heat) as much as it solves problems.
- **DM-Only:** It is genuine harvested Remembrance. A careful caster studying it (Arcana/Religion DC 15) can begin to deduce M3. Do not let it name the Hollow Court.

## 2. The Warden's Unlit Lantern — *Uncommon relic (no attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (an old Ashen Warden ceremonial lantern, its wick never burned).

**Player-Facing:** A plain iron-and-horn lantern carried in Warden rites but never lit. Given to you by a senior Warden as a mark of deep trust. It does nothing — until you come near a place where the dead "won't rest right." Then, without flame, it **glows a cold blue**, brighter the nearer you stand to the wrongness.

**Mechanical effect:** Within 1 mile of an active harvest node / sealed-but-stirring shrine, the lantern emits cold light (gradient: faint at a mile, bright within 100 ft). Functions as a **detector for the harvest network** and an early-warning device for restless-dead danger. No charges; always on near nodes.

- **Limitation:** It points to *where*, never *why*. It cannot be aimed or used as a weapon. Some Wardens consider lending it close to heresy — flaunting it costs Warden standing.
- **Links:** M1 (Wren's rite; the lantern reacts to her grave), M2/M5 (node detection). Ashen Wardens **Rank 3** trust reward (from Warden Sister Othetha or Brother Hale).
- **Solo balance:** A superb solo *scouting/avoidance* tool — exactly the kind of utility a lone PC needs to not walk into lethal sites blind. No power creep.
- **DM-Only:** Othetha knows the lantern reacts to the seal weakening; giving it to the PC is her quiet way of recruiting a deniable agent. It glows brightest at the Hollowmere basin — never explain why early.

## 3. The Ledger of Quiet Debts — *Rare relic (no attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a Cinder Ledger account-book bound in grey hide; a Remembrance-touched record).

**Player-Facing:** A merchant's ledger that writes down **every transaction made in its presence** — including the ones people paid to have forgotten. Bribes, sales of "old salvage," debts erased by a death. The Cinder Ledger wants it back so badly they will negotiate, threaten, or kill for it.

**Mechanical effect:** Once per day, ask the Ledger one question about any *transaction, debt, or sale* that occurred within 100 ft of it at any time in its history; it writes a true (if terse) answer. It is also a master key to Ledger bookkeeping (advantage on Investigation to trace Ledger money; reveals C-M3 paper trails).

- **Limitation / cost:** It records *you*, too — anything you trade near it is permanently logged, which can be subpoenaed, blackmailed, or read by the next holder. The Ledger's enforcers (Collector-Captain Mauld Tallow, Tallytooth Ren) hunt it.
- **Links:** M3 (what Remembrance is, demonstrated commercially), the Monopoly clock (#6). Cinder Ledger opposition reward; recoverable from `THE_LEDGER_VAULT` or `THE_SUNKEN_WARDS_DEEP`.
- **Solo balance:** Investigation/leverage tool. Powerful socially, zero combat power, and it actively *creates* a pursuit clock — self-balancing.
- **DM-Only:** Its earliest entries (Concord Script, DC 16 to read) record the Ledger's first knowing purchase of Remembrance relics — a hard M3/M6 proof. Mother Calla Vyre fears these pages most.

## 4. The Gravecaller's Knock — *Uncommon, CURSED (no attunement)*

> Full curse mechanics in `CURSED_ITEMS.md`. Summary here.

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a door-knocker carved from a chip of Concord keystone-stone).

**Player-Facing:** An ugly iron-and-stone door-knocker, cold as a grave. Strike it three times at a sealed shrine and *something answers*. The Gravecallers use it to call the dead they wish to speak with.

**Mechanical effect / curse:** Used at a sealed or stirring shrine, it summons a **Wrong-come-back** (flavor a specter/revenant/ghost per the danger) that *will* speak — but is hostile or anguished and does not always leave. Repeated use marks the bearer to leaked Remembrances (they find the bearer at night). Removing the curse requires a true Mourner/Warden rite.

- **Links:** M5 (the drowned-tide / drift), Gravecaller cult. Found in `THE_DROWNED_BLIND` or `THE_HANGING_OAKS`.
- **Solo balance:** A *trap reward* — tempting (it talks to the dead, a clue source) but each use raises danger. Always telegraph the cost.

## 5. The Compact Seal — *Rare (no attunement)*

> Hidden trap nature in DM-Only; cross-ref `CURSED_ITEMS.md`.

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a Reachward Compact authority-seal of wax-and-bronze).

**Player-Facing:** The bronze seal-matrix of the Reachward Compact. Pressed into wax on a writ, it grants real **legal standing** across Compact territory — open gates, commandeer aid, override a local bailiff, requisition supplies. A genuine token of frontier authority, earned through service to the council.

**Mechanical effect:** Invoking the Seal on official business grants advantage on social checks with Compact officials, lawful access to controlled areas, and the cooperation of watch/wardens (within reason). Treated as a writ of authority.

- **Limitation:** Authority has limits; abusing it (corruption, threats) burns Compact standing fast.
- **Links:** M4 (who is pushing the basin scheme). Reachward Compact **Rank 3** reward.
- **Solo balance:** A noncombat *access* reward — opens doors a lone PC couldn't force.
- **DM-Only (do not reveal):** The Compact is infiltrated (Councilor Aldous Reke, a knowing Hollow Court agent). Each **major** invocation of the Seal is logged through compromised channels — using it to invoke *high* authority quietly **notifies the Court's cutout** of the PC's movements. It is genuinely useful and genuinely watched. This is the Court's surveillance leash, never named. M7 trap; surfaces only if the player later uncovers Reke.

## 6. The Sealed Archive Key — *Very Rare (no attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a Concord Remnant key of black glass and Script-etched brass).

**Player-Facing:** A heavy key that opens the **Sealed Archive** beneath Caradril's Lantern Reach — the Remnant's true vault of Concord records. Scholars whisper that whoever holds it holds the Concord's memory. The Reclaimers (the Remnant's inner circle) will kill to keep or take it.

**Mechanical effect:** Opens the Sealed Archive and its inner cases (otherwise Script-gated and warded). Inside lies the campaign's clearest **written** proof of the harvest and the deliberate Quietfall (C-M6-1, C-M9). The key also disarms minor Concord wards (advantage vs Concord lock/ward checks).

- **Limitation:** It is the single most contested object in Caradril; carrying it openly invites Reclaimer ambush and Ledger interest. The Archive itself is act-paced (L9–12).
- **Links:** M6 (Concord's true purpose), M9 (deliberate Quietfall). Concord Remnant inner-trust reward **or** theft target; tied to Archivist Quorrin Vane / Keeper-Archivist Wessel Dree.
- **Solo balance:** Access reward that unlocks a *truth-cache*, not power. The danger is social/factional, not a stat boost.
- **DM-Only:** The Archive points hard toward M7 but the records **never name the Hollow Court** — the apex stays for convergence elsewhere. Vane wants the key to control *which* truths surface.

## 7. The Custodian's Compass — *Very Rare (requires attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (an ancient Concord navigation instrument of bone, brass, and a floating shard of old glass).

**Player-Facing:** A beautiful, unsettling compass whose needle ignores north. Instead, near certain old places, the glass shard **drifts to point toward "the nearest deep water that remembers"** — the player will slowly realize it points at Concord ruins, and at something beneath them.

**Mechanical effect:** When attuned and within ~10 miles of an active harvest node, the needle points toward the nearest one and dims/brightens with proximity. Grants advantage on navigation toward (or deliberately *away* from) Concord sites. Reveals hidden node entrances (Investigation advantage at a node).

- **Limitation:** It only works near the network — useless in clean country. It points the player *at the most dangerous places in the world*; following it blindly is its own hazard.
- **Links:** M6/M7. Found deep in `THE_CONCORD_RELAY_VAULT` or carried by a far-region Reclaimer expedition.
- **Solo balance:** The premier *exploration/mystery* tool; lets a lone PC find or avoid nodes. Endgame-adjacent but safe to hold mid-game because understanding ≠ access.
- **DM-Only:** Its needle ultimately points down, toward the Under-Shrine beneath Hollowmere — the keystone is the deepest, strongest node. The Hollow Court would suppress it; a far-off Custodian-made twin may exist (never built). It is the cleanest tool for *finding* the endgame, which is why it is gated and why the Court's agents watch for it.

## 8. The Quiet Country Vessel — *Legendary relic (requires attunement)*

> Endgame-gated. Dangerous. Cross-ref `CURSED_ITEMS.md` for misuse consequences.

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a Concord ritual urn of sealed grey glass, fitted with Script-locked stops).

**Player-Facing:** An ancient urn the size of a helm, sealed with rings of etched glass. Legend says it can **hold a person's memory — a Remembrance — outside the normal flow of death**, keeping someone "neither gone nor returned." Terrible and tempting in equal measure.

**Mechanical effect (gated):** Can store a single Remembrance (a dead person's identity) for a time, suspending them from the Quiet Country's drain. Until the player has earned the M8 revelation, **using it is blind and dangerous** (DM rules a wrong-come-back, a Thin-touch contagion, or worse on a botch). After M8 it becomes a deliberate, high-stakes tool with a real cost each use.

- **Limitation / cost:** Every use *takes from* the Quiet Country balance the campaign turns on — a moral and cosmological cost, not a free benefit. Filling it always raises a clock.
- **Links:** M8 (why the PC is here / the soul-economy), M9. Endgame relic; found in `THE_UNDER_SHRINE_APPROACH` outer works or a Concord vault.
- **Solo balance:** Not a power item — a **choice engine** for the endgame. It is one of the objects the final decision can turn on.
- **DM-Only:** It is a miniature, portable cousin of the harvest engine. The Hollow Court considers it Concord property. It can hold *a Custodian* — a late lever the player may or may not realize.

## 9. The Drowned Flame — *Rare relic (requires attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a hand-sized lamp of stormglass holding a flame that burns underwater).

**Player-Facing:** A lamp of dark sea-glass cupping a small steady flame that does not gutter even when submerged. Drawn from a drowned shrine on the Pale Coast. While you carry it lit, the "wrongness" of the dead can't sink its hooks into you.

**Mechanical effect:** While lit and attuned, the bearer is **immune to Thin-touch** and has advantage on saves against Remembrance-based fear/charm effects from the restless dead. Sheds dim warm light underwater and in fog (useful in drowned sites). 1/day, snuff-and-relight as an action to end one such effect already on you.

- **Limitation:** It does nothing against living foes or ordinary magic; it is a ward, not a weapon. Going out (dispel, deep cold) leaves the bearer exposed mid-crisis.
- **Links:** M5 (the drowned-tide; the Coast nodes). Pale Coast quest chain (Salt-Mother Bryd / the Drowned Lamp). Found in `THE_DROWNED_LAMP` or `THE_SKERRY_SHRINE`.
- **Solo balance:** A defensive *survivability* relic — exactly the protective tech a solo delver into harvest sites needs. No offense, so no power creep; just lets the player *survive* the places the plot pulls them.
- **DM-Only:** Coast Gravecallers sabotaged the shrine it came from; recovering it cleanly is itself a Coast mystery beat. It is a Concord safety device — proof the Concord *knew* the relics were dangerous (a quiet M3/M6 corroboration).

## 10. The Ash-Crown Fragment — *Very Rare relic (no attunement)*

- **Item type:** Wondrous item (a broken arc of an imperial Concord crown; blackened gold and grey glass).

**Player-Facing:** A jagged third of a circlet, found in the ruins of the Concord Heartlands far to the south. It hums faintly near the dead. Reclaimers, Hollow Court agents, and ambitious Compact magisters all want it — it is a symbol of the Concord's lost imperial majesty, and a piece of a greater whole.

**Mechanical effect:** Near restless dead or harvest sites, the fragment grants the bearer advantage on Persuasion/Intimidation against those who revere the Concord (Remnant, certain scholars) and on checks to *command or calm* a single Remembrance (DM discretion; 1/day). It is a key of legitimacy more than a weapon.

- **Limitation:** It marks its bearer as a player in the Concord-succession game; carrying it draws far-region factions (Crown-Reclaimer Magister Hollin Vane, etc.) and the Court's quiet attention.
- **Links:** M9 (the Concord's true scale and sin). Concord Heartlands far-expedition reward; `THE_OLD_CONCORD_HEARTLANDS_RUIN`.
- **Solo balance:** A *political/leverage* relic. The full crown is an intentional far-game MacGuffin (never required for the endgame, which is vertical under Hollowmere).
- **DM-Only:** The full crown is a surface-imperial treasure, **not** a harvest device — corroborates the Concord's scale (M6/M9) without ever implying the harvest-heart is in the Heartlands. The Remnant covets it to crown a restored Concord; the Court doesn't care about the crown, only that the player not gather proof.

## 11. The Harvest Engine Shard — *Legendary / Artifact (DM-ONLY in true nature)*

> **DM-ONLY true nature. Cross-ref `CURSED_ITEMS.md` (extreme).** The single most dangerous object the player can hold.

- **Item type:** Artifact fragment (a palm of black-glass-and-bone machinery that should not fit in a hand).

**Player-Facing (what the player perceives before M8):** A fragment of some old Concord *device* — gears of glass, a lattice of fine bone, a faint cold pull. It feels wrong to carry. Scholars who see it go quiet. It seems important beyond price, and nobody will explain why.

**Mechanical effect (only legible after M8 revelation):** A working fragment of the original engine that **processed Remembrance** — it can draw, store, or release the substance of identity. Before M8 the player cannot safely operate it (any attempt risks Thin-touch contagion, a wrong-come-back surge, or harming themselves). After M8 it becomes a true endgame lever in the final decision (seal / free / control / transform the harvest).

- **Limitation / cost:** Holding it advances the harvest's awareness; the **Hollow Court will dispatch agents** to recover or destroy the bearer. Using it has moral and cosmological cost every time.
- **Links:** M8 (apex). Found only in deep Concord works (`THE_UNDER_SHRINE_APPROACH`, or carried by a Reclaimer who paid dearly for it). Endgame, L16+.
- **Solo balance:** Not a stat item — a **plot detonator**. It exists to make the endgame choice concrete and weighty. Never give it as casual loot.
- **DM-Only:** This is the clearest physical proof of the entire hidden truth. **Its description, history, and effect must never be surfaced to the player before the M8 revelation gate.** The Court's pursuit of it can drive Act 4–5 tension.

## 12. The Last Voice — *Legendary sentient artifact*

> Full personality/agenda in `SENTIENT_ITEMS.md`. Summary here.

- **Item type:** Sentient wondrous item (a Concord instrument — a thin reed-and-glass pipe that plays without breath).

**Player-Facing:** A slender instrument of reed and grey glass that, when held to the lips of the recently dead — or played over a grave — sounds out **the last words that person spoke**, in their own voice. Beautiful and unbearable. It seems, faintly, to *want* things.

**Mechanical effect:** Plays back the final spoken words of any dead person whose body or strong relic is present (a unique clue/closure tool). Sentient (see `SENTIENT_ITEMS.md`): it has a quiet, grieving personality and an agenda about the dead being *heard*. 1/long-rest deep playback can deliver a full last-testament clue.

- **Limitation / cost:** It is sentient and sorrowful; overuse, or using it cruelly, turns it against the bearer (it can fall silent, or play the *wrong* last words to wound). It draws the attention of the Quiet Country.
- **Links:** M9 (the Concord's sin, heard firsthand from the dead), M10 (the endgame — it has opinions about freeing the dead). L17+; the Quiet Country sends a messenger for it.
- **Solo balance:** A clue/closure artifact and a *companion-of-sorts* — gives a lone player a voice in the quiet. Never a combat solution.
- **DM-Only:** It carries the preserved last-testament of a Concord member who *witnessed and regretted* the harvest — a built-in late witness for M9. The Quiet Country's envoy comes not to destroy it but to *claim the voices it holds*; the player's choice about it foreshadows M10.
