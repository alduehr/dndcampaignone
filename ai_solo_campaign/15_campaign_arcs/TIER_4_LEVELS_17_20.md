## AI Use

**MIXED SECRECY — DM-facing endgame spine for levels 17–20 (Act 5).** Load when the player owns REV_007 and is moving to enact a decision about the Remembrance. **This tier reaches the campaign's keystone secrets** — the harvest mechanism, the Custodians' nature, the choice and its costs (REV_008). They are written in DM-only sections here and in `../10_dungeons_and_ruins/THE_UNDER_SHRINE_APPROACH.md` (D23, DM-only). The *choice* becomes player-known in Act 5; the **costs** are learned through play. The endgame is a **choice, reachable many ways** — never a forced damage race.

This is an **open-world kit, not a track.** There is no single correct ending.

## The One-Paragraph Shape (DM)

The player descends — vertically, beneath Hollowmere — toward the keystone, gathering the means to enact a decision: **what should be done with the Remembrance?** The Hollow Court, led by Veyl, makes its case directly and *persuasively*: a controlled, "merciful" restart is the only thing that can stop the slow thinning of all souls. The player has learned that **every ending has a terrible price** (REV_008), and that a **synthesis** — a consensual system where the dead choose — is possible but hard, reachable only by uniting unlikely allies and turning Maire against Veyl (REV_010). Rival claimants race the player to the keystone; the Court's defenses, the basin's lethality, and the weight of the choice are the final obstacles. The climax is not a boss fight by default — it is the *enacting of the decision*, which the player can reach through confrontation, alliance, ritual, artifact, politics, sacrifice, exposure, or synthesis. The epilogue reflects everything the player learned, chose, and built across twenty levels.

## Main Pressures (Tier 4)

1. **The keystone is the prize.** Whoever reaches and controls it decides the harvest's fate. The player, the Court, and the rival claimants all converge here.
2. **The Court makes its case.** Veyl is the campaign's final *voice*, not its final monster. The pressure is moral and existential as much as physical.
3. **The clock is near its end.** The master clock (#1) is at Stage 3–4; if the player does nothing, the harvest fully restarts under the Court's rule — the worst default outcome.
4. **The rivals race.** The Remnant (a living tyranny), the Ledger (a market in souls), and the Gravecallers (catastrophic destruction) each want to enact *their* ending; their Tier 2–3 postures determine how hard they push and whether they help or hinder.
5. **The synthesis is fragile.** The best ending (transform) requires REV_010, Maire's turn, the right allies, and often a key artifact — it is *not guaranteed* and must be earned.

## Encounter Danger Profile (Tier 4)

Reference `../13_encounters_and_bestiary/`:

- **Apex threats:** the **Hollow Court Custodians** (DM-only, CR 16–20; Under-Shrine only) — Veyl as a Tier-4 solo-boss *with built-in weaknesses and non-combat win conditions* (sever the keystone, persuade Maire, the player's chosen artifact/ally); Orre as the most combat-prone (bypassable via Maire/Veyl); Maire as an ally-capable non-combat lever.

The under-shrine provides terrain, allies, alternatives, and the Court's own internal divide (Maire) as a non-combat path. Telegraph everything; offer non-combat resolution always. ## Failure States (Tier 4)

Summary — full logic in `FAILURE_STATES_AND_WORLD_CONSEQUENCES.md` and `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md`:

- **Do nothing / arrive too late:** the master clock completes; the harvest restarts under the Court's rule — the worst default outcome (a playable, bleak epilogue, not a "game over").
- **Choose under-informed:** without REV_010 the synthesis is unreachable; the player picks among seal / restart / seize / destroy, each with its full cost.
- **Lose the keystone race:** a rival claimant (Remnant/Ledger/Gravecallers) enacts *their* ending; the player can still contest, sabotage, or live with it — an epilogue, not a dead end.
- **The Gravecallers shatter it alone:** catastrophic liberation — the dead freed destructively, resurrection ended, the boundary destabilized.
- **Death at the keystone:** meaningful, telegraphed, and survivable by clever play, allies, retreat, or the Vessel/Last Voice levers — never cheap.

## Solo-Play Balance Notes (Tier 4)

- **Bring people and levers, not just a sword.** The endgame is designed so a solo PC arrives with allies (banked factions), artifacts (the Vessel/Shard/Last Voice), and knowledge (REV_010). The DM should ensure at least one non-combat win condition is live for the player's path.
- **Fallbacks for missing tools:** no *dispel/remove curse*? Maire, Othetha, or the Last Voice can break the Court's holds. No reliable AoE/burst? The synthesis, the seal-rite, or the Vessel resolves the climax without a damage race. No *resurrection*? That is the *point* — the choice is partly about whether resurrection should exist at all.
- **Death is allowed but earned.** Telegraph the keystone's lethality at every prior level; provide retreat from D20/D23; never spring an unwinnable wall.
- Detail: `SOLO_CAMPAIGN_BALANCE_GUIDE.md`.

## Guidance for Off-Path Players (Tier 4)

- **The combat-focused player:** can fight to the keystone and confront Orre/Veyl — but even then, weaknesses, terrain, and Maire's lever exist; a pure-damage path is *possible* but the hardest and bleakest.
- **The diplomat:** can talk the Court down, turn Maire, or broker a faction coalition — the synthesis and political endings reward this.
- **The saboteur:** can sever the keystone, poison the harvest, or arm the Gravecallers safely — the destroy/seal endings reward this.
- **The under-prepared player:** still gets an ending; the DM offers the costs honestly and lets the player choose among what they've earned (no synthesis without REV_010, but seal/destroy/restart/seize remain).
- **The player who ignored the main arc entirely:** the world delivers the endgame to their doorstep (the harvest reaches their region); they choose late, with fewer allies, but they still choose.

## Endgame Approaches (Index — Full Detail in ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md)

1. **Direct confrontation** with the Hollow Court / Custodians.
2. **Alliance-building** — a faction coalition against the Court.
3. **Ritual / counter-ritual** — using the Concord's own mechanisms.
4. **Artifact strategy** — the Vessel / Shard / Last Voice.
5. **Political / faction victory** — dismantle the Court's proxy control (Reke, the basin, the deal).
6. **Sacrifice / costly containment.**
7. **Mystery-resolution** — expose the truth publicly and let the world decide.
8. **Partial / bittersweet victory.**
9. **Failure and aftermath.**

The five canonical *outcomes* (seal / restart-controlled / seize / destroy / transform) map onto these approaches in `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md`. No outcome is labeled "correct" in play.
