## AI Use

**DM-ONLY.** How to keep levels 5–20 fair and survivable for **one player character**. This is the arc-level companion to `../13_encounters_and_bestiary/SOLO_ENCOUNTER_SCALING.md` (the per-encounter budget math) and `../01_runner_protocol/SOLO_PLAY_PRINCIPLES.md`. It addresses the higher-tier problems: action economy at scale, the solo PC's *missing tools* (no party to cover gaps), boss adjustments, and ally/hireling support. **Do not balance for four PCs. Telegraph lethal danger. Cheap death is bad design.**

---

## The Core Solo Problems (and the Standing Answers)

| Problem | Why it bites a solo PC | Standing answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Action economy** | One PC vs. many enemies loses the round-count war | small enemy numbers; telegraphed terrain; allies; stealth/parley bypasses; enemy *goals* over fights-to-the-death; morale |
| **Missing roles** | No party to cover healing, scouting, tanking, AoE, utility | faction allies, hirelings, companions, and *relics that fill gaps* (the Warden's Lantern scouts; the Drowned Flame wards; the Last Voice informs) |
| **No backup on a bad save** | One failed save can spiral | telegraph; allow retreat; never spring a save-or-die without warning; consequences over instant death |
| **Resource attrition** | No party to share the spotlight or the rests | rest opportunities tracked; time-pressure used sparingly; consumables and safe-houses provided |
| **Boss action economy** | A solo boss with legendary actions can overwhelm one PC | bosses have weaknesses, terrain, allies-for-the-PC, and non-combat win conditions |

## Per-Tier Balance Notes

### Tier 2 (5–10)
- **Threat scale:** a single Ward-Keeper Remembrance, a hag, or a faction strike-team is a *real* fight. Keep enemy numbers low; give terrain and retreat.
- **Survivability tech to hand over:** the **Warden's Unlit Lantern** (scout nodes), the **Drowned Flame** (Thin-touch immunity for node-diving). Without these, a lone PC is blind/exposed in harvest sites — provide them as faction rewards.
- **Allies on tap:** Brother Hale (rites/companion), honest officials (Coalmont, Kade), hireling guides (Brann, Wenna Roke, Old Sashe), cultivated faction contacts.

### Tier 3 (11–16)
- **Threat scale:** node climaxes (Ward/Choir-Keepers CR 5–8), Construct-Guardians (CR 7–17), far-region apexes (chimera, wyvern, giants, young dragons), and DM-gated horror (Soul Monger CR 11, Bodak) in the deepest sites. Far environmental sites (Emberfell/Highmark/Cindern) are *very high* hazard.
- **Survivability tech:** the **Custodian's Compass** (find/avoid nodes), continued Drowned Flame use, faction support that now fields *real* numbers (rite-circles, mobs, idealist cells, a turned Reclaimer).
- **Rule:** never send a solo PC into a very-high far site without a route to allies, the Drowned Flame, and a telegraphed retreat.

### Tier 4 (17–20)
- **Threat scale:** the Custodians (CR 16–20, Under-Shrine only) and the deepest descent horror. **Veyl is a Tier-4 solo-boss with built-in weaknesses and non-combat win conditions — never an out-damage wall.**
- **The player should arrive with people and levers:** banked allies (turned factions, Maire), endgame artifacts (the Vessel, the Shard, the Last Voice), and knowledge (REV_010). A solo PC is never expected to out-fight an apex alone.

## Fallbacks When the Solo PC Lacks a Key Ability

A single-class solo PC will lack tools a four-PC party covers. The campaign provides lore-correct substitutes so missing an ability never blocks the arc:

| Missing ability | Why it matters here | Fallback the campaign provides |
|---|---|---|
| **Resurrection / raise dead** | death is dangerous *by design* (the Quiet Country is thin) | the setting makes death meaningful; the Quiet Country Vessel and Last Voice are the lore-correct stakes; allies/Wardens can perform true rites; a "wrong-come-back" is the cautionary tale, not a free respawn |
| **Teleportation** | far travel and quick escapes | water-routes (the Verdance), sky-stone/grove guides, faction transport, and the road network; travel is meaningful but never a dead end |
| **Flight** | skerries, calderas, frozen passes | hireling guides, climbing gear, faction support, alternate ground routes; no site *requires* flight |
| **Dispel magic** | Concord wards, harvest effects | the Sealed Archive Key (disarms Concord wards), Remnant/Warden assistance, Script-reading to bypass, physical/stealth routes |
| **Remove curse / restoration** | Thin-touch, the Gravecaller's-Knock curse, harvest curses | a true Mourner/Warden rite, Mother Ysarra, Othetha, or a far grove-keeper clears these; the Drowned Flame *prevents* Thin-touch |
| **Reliable AoE / burst** | swarms, multiple enemies | terrain choke-points, stealth bypasses, faction muscle, enemy morale, and *avoiding* mass fights via negotiation/scouting |
| **High mobility / tanking** | a solo PC can be focused down | allies to split aggro, retreat routes, terrain, and the fact that most enemies have *goals* other than killing the PC |

> **DM rule:** if a session hinges on an ability the PC lacks, route the obstacle to a fallback above (an ally, a relic, a noncombat path) rather than letting the PC stall.

## Boss / Scenario Adjustments for One PC

Per `../13_encounters_and_bestiary/BOSS_AND_APEX_THREATS.md`:

1. **Lower HP / damage bands** for solo (use the solo column in `SOLO_ENCOUNTER_SCALING.md`); a boss tuned for four PCs will TPK one.
2. **Always a non-combat win condition** (sever the keystone, turn Maire, the right artifact, a coalition, exposure).
3. **Terrain as a third party** — interactable objectives, cover, hazards the PC can turn on enemies.
4. **Allies present** for major bosses — the campaign hands the player people before the keystone.
5. **Telegraph and phase** — bosses have foreshadowing and weaknesses the player can discover and exploit; never a surprise unwinnable wall.
6. **Morale and goals** — enemies flee, negotiate, or pursue objectives; few fight to the death.

## Companion / Ally Discipline (so they support, not solve)

Per `DND_MECHANICS_REQUIREMENTS.md` companion rules:
- Allies (Hale, hirelings, turned faction agents, Maire) **support** the solo PC; they do not solve the campaign.
- They have limitations, loyalty triggers, and real death/injury risk; they can't overshadow the player's choices.
- The endgame's decision is the *player's* — allies enable it, never make it for them.

## Rest, Time-Pressure, and Attrition

- **Track rest opportunities** (short/long) and safe-houses; a solo PC can't afford to be ground down with no recovery.
- **Use time-pressure sparingly** — the world clocks create *narrative* pressure; don't also deny rests unless the scene specifically warrants it.
- **Provide consumables** (Stage 14 `CONSUMABLES_AND_MINOR_MAGIC.md`) so a lone PC has self-sufficiency.
