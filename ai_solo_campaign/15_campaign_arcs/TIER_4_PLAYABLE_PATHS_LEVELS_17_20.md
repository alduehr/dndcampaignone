## AI Use

**MIXED SECRECY — nine concrete endgame playbooks for levels 17–20 (Act 5).** Each is a runnable spine for one of the nine endgame *approaches* (the verbs from `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md`), mapping the player's banked Tier 2–3 allies, artifacts, and knowledge onto a step-by-step climax. They are **not exclusive** — the player can combine approaches, and clever solutions are honored even if unplanned. **This tier reaches the campaign's keystone secrets** (the harvest mechanism, the Custodians' nature, the choice's costs — REV_008); they live in DM-only sections here and in `../10_dungeons_and_ruins/THE_UNDER_SHRINE_APPROACH.md` (D23, DM-only). **Never render D23's content, the Court's name-truth, or the keystone's location-truth to the player before REV_007's gate (L13+).** The endgame is a **choice, reachable many ways — never a forced damage race.**

The five canonical *outcomes* (seal / restart-controlled / seize / destroy / transform) are in `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md`; each playbook below notes which outcomes its approach can reach.

## Endgame Path 1: Direct Confrontation — "Cut Down to the Keystone"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player owns **REV_007** (the Hollow Court lives beneath Hollowmere) and chooses force; has the **Drowned Flame** (Thin-touch immunity) and ideally a combat-capable ally band.
Alternate Entry: if the player lacks REV_007 but the master clock (#1) has reached Stage 4, the surfacing shrine and the Court's manifestations force the descent — the player learns REV_007 by *meeting* the Court at the keystone (lethal-telegraphed).

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): The Vertical Descent
The player breaches the **Basin Keystone Approach** (D20) and descends the gated stair to the **Under-Shrine Approach** (D23, DM-only). The descent is drowning, cold, dread, and Concord Construct-Guardians at full power; **the Relay-Warden** and deeper guardians bar the way. **Councilor Reke** is the surface link who must be passed or turned. *(DM: telegraph lethality at every prior level; provide retreat from D20/D23.)* The player can fight, sneak, or blast through — but **Custodian Orre** (the hardliner) will meet a forced approach with violence.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The Court at Bay
At the keystone the player confronts **Custodian Veyl** (the final *voice*) and **Custodian Orre** (the fight). Even here, force is never the *only* path: Veyl has built-in weaknesses (the keystone can be **severed**; **Custodian Maire** can be turned mid-fight via REV_010), and the terrain offers the harvest mechanism itself as a lever. A pure-force player can **seal** (force the Warden seal-rite at swordpoint), **destroy** (shatter the keystone with the Harvest Engine Shard or Gravecaller tools), or **seize** (take the machine) — but the bleakest, hardest version. *(DM: even Confrontation honors a last-moment non-combat off-ramp — Maire's defection, the Last Voice's testimony.)*

### Regions Involved
Sundering Reach (the descent, Hollowmere); whichever far region supplied the player's combat allies and the Drowned Flame (Pale Coast for the Flame).

### Key Factions
- **Hollow Court:** Veyl bargains even mid-fight; Orre fights; Maire may defect.
- **Player's banked combat allies:** Warden rite-circles (Othetha), Mourner mobs (Wend), a turned Reclaimer band (Wenna Stone), or Gravecaller breachers (Cole Ashby) physically present at the keystone per Tier 2–3 alignment.
- **Rival claimants:** Remnant champions (Hollin Vane/Sabine Ferrant), Ledger enforcers (Mauld Tallow), or the Gravecaller breach-cell may converge depending on the keystone race.

### Key NPCs
Veyl (fought or yielded), Orre (fought/bypassed via Maire), Maire (last-moment lever), Reke (surface link, passed/turned), Othetha (seal-rite if sought), the Last Voice (witness).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
D20 Basin Keystone Approach → D23 Under-Shrine Approach (DM-only apex).

### Major Threats
Custodian Orre (CR ~16–18, the most combat-prone Custodian), Custodian Veyl (CR ~18–20 solo-boss with non-combat win conditions), Concord Construct-Guardians (CR 11–17), the deepest harvest horror (Soul Monger CR 11, Bodak, Callers in Darkness) — DM-only, Under-Shrine only.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
Veyl tries to *recruit even as the player fights* — his certainty is the campaign's final argument. Orre defends the keystone to destruction. Maire watches; a player who shows mercy or doubt can turn her (REV_010) even mid-combat, collapsing Orre's defense.

### Key Decisions
- Whether to pass/kill/turn **Reke** at the surface (turning him reveals the Court's plan; killing him hardens the descent).
- Whether to accept Veyl's mid-fight offer (→ Restart) or press on.
- Whether to turn **Maire** (→ opens Transform even from a force approach).
- Which outcome to enact at the keystone (seal/destroy/seize).

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Seal by Force:** the player overpowers Orre, forces the Warden seal-rite; resurrection mostly ends, the Quiet Country survives, Veyl is sealed away. (Outcome A.)
- **Destroy by Force:** the keystone is shattered (Shard or Gravecaller tools); the dead are freed, possibly catastrophically; resurrection ends entirely; Veyl is destroyed. (Outcome D.)
- **Seize by Force:** the player or a banked faction takes the machine; a new tyranny or a market in souls; Veyl displaced. (Outcome C.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: breaching D20; the descent begins.
Level 18: through the guardians to the Under-Shrine.
Level 19: confronting Veyl and Orre.
Level 20 / Endgame: enacting the chosen outcome.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
**Never a "you must out-damage me" wall.** Veyl always has a non-combat off-ramp (sever the keystone, turn Maire, deploy the Last Voice/Vessel). Bring banked allies; the under-shrine provides terrain (collapsing galleries, floodgates, the harvest mechanism as a hazard the player can turn on the Court). Retreat from D20/D23 is always available to re-prepare.

### Catastrophic Failure State
Death at the keystone is meaningful and telegraphed; if the player falls and no ally finishes the work, the master clock completes — the harvest restarts under the Court (Restart-by-default). A solo Gravecaller breach in the chaos can shatter the keystone catastrophically (uncontrolled Destroy). See Path 9.

## Endgame Path 2: Alliance-Building — "Bring the Whole Reach Down the Stair"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player owns REV_007 and has banked at least two of: rallied **Ashen Wardens** (Othetha; QW4), **Mourners' Circle** (Wend; QM4), a reformed **Reachward Compact** (Coalmont; QC4), or a reform-**Remnant** (Wenna Stone/Briss).
Alternate Entry: with only one banked faction, the player can still build a coalition late by exposing the Court's plan to the others (costlier, fewer numbers).

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): The Coalition Forms
The player unites the factions that want the dead to *rest*: **Warden Othetha** brings seal-knowledge and rite-circles; **Grandmother Wend** and the Mourners bring numbers and moral weight; a reformed **Compact** under **Marda Coalmont** (Reke exposed) holds the basin so the Court cannot use it; turned **Remnant idealists** (Wenna Stone, Briss) bring Concord lore. The coalition is assembled in Hollowmere and at the Ashwalk Rest, then marches on the basin together. *Changes:* the player arrives at the keystone with an *army of the grieving*, not alone.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The United Front at the Keystone
The coalition holds off rival claimants (Remnant inner circle, Ledger enforcers, the Gravecaller cell) while the player reaches Veyl. With the Wardens' seal-rite the coalition can **seal**; with the Mourners + a turned Maire + the Sunmark/old-songs model they can **transform**; with the basin held and Reke exposed they can **deny the Court the keystone** entirely (political seal). *(DM: the alliance approach is the strongest route to both Seal and Transform.)*

### Regions Involved
Sundering Reach (the descent); Ring 1 and the far continent (where the coalition's far members were banked — Vale/Ezrith Combe, Coast/Bryd, Sunmark/Sael Sunmark, Karran/Wenna Stone).

### Key Factions
- **The seal/rest coalition:** Wardens + Mourners + reformed Compact + reform-Remnant + far old-songs network.
- **Hollow Court:** Veyl negotiates with a coalition (it is harder to fight an army of mourners than a lone adventurer); Maire is more likely to defect when she sees the living united in grief.
- **Rival claimants:** held off by the coalition while the player acts.

### Key NPCs
Othetha (seal-rite), Wend (Mourner numbers), Marda Coalmont (holds the basin), Wenna Stone/Briss (Concord lore), Sael Sunmark (synthesis model), Maire (turned by the united grief), Veyl (negotiates).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
D20 → D23; the basin held by the Compact above.

### Major Threats
Rival claimant champions (Hollin Vane, Mauld Tallow, the Gravecaller cell) — the coalition absorbs these so the player faces fewer; the Custodians (Veyl/Orre) at the keystone.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
Veyl is shaken by the coalition — it proves the living can *choose* to honor the dead without consumption, the heart of Maire's heresy. A well-built coalition makes Maire's defection (REV_010) almost inevitable, opening Transform. Orre may stand down if Veyl yields.

### Key Decisions
- Which factions to bring (each shapes the available outcome — Wardens→Seal, Mourners+Maire+Sunmark→Transform, Gravecallers→Destroy).
- Whether to let **Maire** defect openly (risking Veyl learning before the keystone is reached).
- Whether to seal, transform, or deny.

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Seal (coalition):** the Wardens' rite, backed by the coalition; resurrection ends, the dead rest, the Wardens and Mourners are vindicated. (Outcome A.)
- **Transform (coalition):** the broad coalition + turned Maire + the Sunmark model enact a consensual system; the Court ends itself; the best, hardest ending. (Outcome E.)
- **Political Seal:** the Compact holds the basin and Reke is exposed so the Court never gets keystone access; the harvest stalls without a single blow. (Outcome A by denial.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: the coalition forms in Hollowmere.
Level 18: the march on the basin; the basin held.
Level 19: the united front at the keystone.
Level 20 / Endgame: seal, transform, or deny.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
This is the *designed* solo endgame: the player arrives with people. The coalition absorbs the combat; the player's job is the *choice* and turning Maire. No fight is mandatory; the Compact's holding of the basin is a pure-political win condition.

### Catastrophic Failure State
If the coalition fractures (the player angered a key faction in Tiers 2–3), it arrives thin and the rival claimants break through — the player may have to fall back to a lone Confrontation (Path 1) or lose the keystone race (Seize-by-rival). Still an ending, never a dead end.

## Endgame Path 3: Artifact Strategy — "The Vessel, the Shard, the Voice"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player has at least one of the gated legendary artifacts — **The Harvest Engine Shard** (M8/L16+), **The Quiet Country Vessel** (M8/REV_004/L13+), or **The Last Voice** (L17+) — earned through Tier 3 play.
Alternate Entry: with the **Custodian's Compass** alone, the player can still navigate the descent but must find an artifact or another approach at the keystone.

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): Attuning the Levers
The player learns each artifact's true endgame function (DM-only until used): the **Harvest Engine Shard** is the plot detonator — it can drive, redirect, or shatter the harvest mechanism; the **Quiet Country Vessel** is a containment/choice engine that can *hold a Custodian* or a soul safely; the **Last Voice** is a sentient Concord member who regretted the harvest and can testify for the dead. The player gathers what each requires (the Shard needs M8 understanding; the Vessel needs REV_004; the Voice needs to be *heard*). *Changes:* the player carries a decisive lever to the keystone.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The Lever at the Keystone
- **The Shard** → enact *any* outcome by driving the mechanism (seal it, redirect it to a consensual rite for Transform, or overload it to Destroy).
- **The Vessel** → contain Veyl (a late Transform/Seal option), or hold the harvest's draw safely (Seal); misused, it causes a wrong-come-back catastrophe.
- **The Last Voice** → testify at the climax; its witness can turn Maire, shame Veyl, and anchor the consensual rite (Transform/Exposure).
*(DM: artifacts are levers for the decision, not power spikes; each carries a moral/cosmological cost.)*

### Regions Involved
Sundering Reach (the keystone); the far regions where the artifacts were earned (the Shard from a far node, the Vessel from a Concord ritual site, the Last Voice from a Concord instrument-cache).

### Key Factions
- **Hollow Court:** Veyl recognizes the artifacts (especially the Last Voice — a Concord colleague) and is shaken; Orre tries to seize the Shard.
- **Rival claimants:** the Remnant and Ledger covet the artifacts and may try to take them at the keystone.

### Key NPCs
Veyl (recognizes the Last Voice), Maire (turned by the Voice's testimony), Orre (tries to seize the Shard), the Last Voice (sentient artifact; the dead's witness).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
D20 → D23; the far sites where artifacts were earned (referenced).

### Major Threats
Orre and Concord Construct-Guardians (try to seize the Shard); rival claimants; the artifacts' own costs (the Vessel's catastrophe risk; the Shard's instability).

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
The **Last Voice** is the Court's nightmare — a Concord member who *regretted* and speaks for the dead, undercutting Veyl's certainty. The **Shard** is the mechanism's master key; whoever holds it controls the harvest's fate. Veyl will bargain hard for the artifacts; Orre will fight for them.

### Key Decisions
- Which artifact to deploy and toward which outcome (the Shard reaches any; the Vessel→Seal/Transform; the Voice→Transform/Exposure).
- Whether to risk the Vessel's catastrophe to contain Veyl.
- Whether to let the Last Voice speak (turning Maire, but revealing the player's hand to Veyl).

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Seal via the Vessel/Shard:** the mechanism is shut down and the draw contained; resurrection ends gently. (Outcome A.)
- **Transform via the Last Voice + Shard:** the Voice anchors a consensual rite, Maire turns, the Shard redirects the mechanism; the Court ends itself. (Outcome E.)
- **Destroy via the Shard:** overload the mechanism; the dead are freed; resurrection ends entirely. (Outcome D.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: attuning the artifact(s).
Level 18: gathering what each requires; the descent.
Level 19: deploying the lever at the keystone.
Level 20 / Endgame: enacting the outcome.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
Artifacts *are* the solo-play guarantee here — a lone PC with the Shard or Voice has a decisive non-combat win condition. The Vessel can resolve the climax without a fight; the Voice provides an ally (its testimony) at the keystone.

### Catastrophic Failure State
A misused **Vessel** triggers a wrong-come-back catastrophe (a freed, broken Custodian or soul); a lost **Shard** (seized by Orre or a rival) hands the harvest's fate to the Court or a tyrant. Still resolvable — the player can contest, sabotage, or fall back to another approach.

## Endgame Path 4: Ritual / Counter-Ritual — "Use the Concord's Own Machine"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player has **Warden seal-knowledge** (Othetha's confession + QW4) OR the **Harvest Engine Shard** + M8 understanding of the mechanism, plus access to the keystone.
Alternate Entry: with the Sunmark grove-faith model (Sael Sunmark) the player can attempt a *new* consensual rite instead of the Concord's.

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): Learning the Rite
The player masters the Concord's own seal-rite (from **Othetha**, the last keeper of the true purpose) or the harvest mechanism's workings (from the Shard, the Custodian's Compass, and Concord Script from the Sealed Archive/Frozen Works). This is the Concord's machinery turned against itself. *Changes:* the player can operate the keystone, not just attack it.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The Counter-Ritual
At the keystone the player performs the rite: the **seal-rite** (re-seal the shrine, pause the harvest forever → Seal), the **harvest mechanism redirected** (run it consensually, the dead choosing → Transform, with Maire's heresy as the key), the **safe-break** (dismantle it without catastrophe → a controlled Destroy), or — Veyl's offer — running it "gently" (→ Restart, if the player is persuaded). *(DM: ritual is the most *surgical* approach; it reaches Seal, Restart, safe-Destroy, and Transform.)*

### Regions Involved
Sundering Reach (the keystone); Caradril/Highmark (Concord Script for the mechanism); Sunmark (the consensual-rite model).

### Key Factions
- **Ashen Wardens:** Othetha's seal-rite is the backbone of the Seal ending.
- **Hollow Court:** Veyl knows these rites intimately — he will argue the player's counter-ritual is naive or cruel; Maire knows the consensual variant.
- **Gravecallers:** Cole Ashby's safe-break knowledge enables a controlled Destroy.

### Key NPCs
Othetha (seal-rite), Maire (the consensual-rite key; REV_010), Cole Ashby (safe-break), Veyl (the rite's original master; foil), the Last Voice (anchors a consensual rite).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
D20 → D23; the harvest mechanism itself (DM-only) as the ritual focus.

### Major Threats
Orre and guardians try to stop the rite mid-cast (a "defend the ritual" climax); the mechanism's own instability; Veyl's interruptions (argument and force).

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
This is the approach Veyl most respects and most fears — the player using the Concord's own tools. Maire's heresy (REV_010) is *the* unlock for the consensual variant; without her, the player can seal or safe-destroy but not transform via ritual.

### Key Decisions
- Which rite to perform (seal / consensual-Transform / safe-Destroy / gentle-Restart).
- Whether to trust Maire's consensual variant over Othetha's seal.
- Whether to accept Veyl's "gentle restart" framing.

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Seal (the Warden rite):** the cleanest Seal; resurrection ends, the dead rest. (Outcome A.)
- **Transform (consensual counter-ritual):** Maire's variant + the Last Voice/Sunmark model; the Court ends itself. (Outcome E.)
- **Safe Destroy:** Cole Ashby's safe-break dismantles the mechanism without catastrophe; the dead freed cleanly. (Outcome D, controlled.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: learning the rite.
Level 18: gathering the ritual components/knowledge; the descent.
Level 19: performing the counter-ritual (defend it).
Level 20 / Endgame: the outcome resolves.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
The ritual is a non-combat win condition; the climax becomes "perform/defend the rite" with allies holding the line. Othetha or Maire can co-cast if the player lacks the ability. No damage race required.

### Catastrophic Failure State
A botched rite (interrupted by Orre, or attempted without Maire for the consensual variant) can backfire — a partial seal that traps souls, or an uncontrolled break. Telegraphed; the player can retry with more support or fall back to another approach.

## Endgame Path 5: Political / Faction Victory — "Dismantle the Proxy Network"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player has exposed **Reke** (REV_003) and can hold the **basin** (reformed Compact under Coalmont; QC4), and/or has split the Ledger (Holt) and Remnant (Wenna Stone) so no living claimant can seize the keystone.
Alternate Entry: with only Reke exposed, the player can still deny the Court basin-access politically, stalling the harvest without descending.

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): Cut the Court's Hands
The Hollow Court acts through proxies: **Reke** (basin access), the **Vyre–Quorrin deal** (the relic-money), and the **Reclamation race** (a living tyranny that would seize the keystone). The player dismantles each: expose Reke and empower **Marda Coalmont** so the Compact holds the basin shut; shatter or expose the Vyre–Quorrin deal in Caradril/Glassmere; split the Remnant (turn **Wenna Stone**/idealists against Quorrin and Hollin Vane). *Changes:* the Court loses its surface reach; no living faction can win the keystone race.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The Court Starved
With the basin held shut, the relic-money cut, and the rivals neutralized, the harvest **cannot restart** — the Court is sealed not by a blow but by *denial*. The player may never descend at all (the keystone stays buried and inaccessible), or may descend to confront a starved, weakened Court for a clean Seal or to turn Maire for Transform. *(DM: this is the "win without a fight" approach — Seal by denial, or Seize by the people if the player exposes the truth publicly.)*

### Regions Involved
Sundering Reach (the basin); Caradril (the deal, the Council); Glassmere (the Ledger heartland); Concord Heartlands/Karran (splitting the Remnant).

### Key Factions
- **Reachward Compact (reformed):** Coalmont holds the basin shut — the political seal.
- **Cinder Ledger / Concord Remnant:** split and neutralized so neither seizes the keystone.
- **Hollow Court:** starved of proxies; Veyl cannot act without the basin.

### Key NPCs
Marda Coalmont (holds the basin), Reke (exposed/removed), Sefa Dann/Yorell Kade (reformed Caradril), Sefwy Holt (split the Ledger), Wenna Stone/Briss (split the Remnant), Veyl (starved).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
The basin (held shut above D20); D20/D23 optional (a starved Court is easier if the player descends).

### Major Threats
Political: enforcer retaliation (Mauld Tallow), assassination attempts on Coalmont/the player, the rivals' last grabs. Combat is survivable and telegraphed; the threat is *losing the political fight*, not a boss.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
Veyl is helpless without the basin and the proxies — a chilling "the monster never gets to act" ending. But a player who only seals politically without descending leaves the Court *alive and buried*, a permanent latent threat (a bittersweet note). Maire cannot be turned without contact.

### Key Decisions
- Whether to descend at all (political seal vs. confirming the Court is dealt with).
- Whether to expose the full truth publicly (→ Seize by the people / Exposure) or keep it quiet (→ clean political Seal).
- Whether to leave the buried Court alive (bittersweet) or finish it (descend).

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Seal by Denial:** the basin held shut, the proxies cut; the harvest never restarts; the Court buried and starved. (Outcome A, no blow struck.)
- **Seize by the People (Exposure):** the player makes the truth public (the Sealed Archive + the Last Voice + the Ash-Crown legitimacy); the world demands a "responsible" living owner of the machine. (Outcome C.)
- **Bittersweet political seal:** the Court is starved but alive and buried — safe for now, a shadow for later. (Outcome A, degraded.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: exposing Reke; reforming the Compact.
Level 18: cutting the relic-money; splitting the rivals.
Level 19: the basin held; the Court starved.
Level 20 / Endgame: seal by denial, expose, or descend to finish.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
The most non-combat endgame — built for a diplomat/saboteur. The player's banked political allies do the heavy lifting; no descent or boss is required. A pure-social PC can win here.

### Catastrophic Failure State
If a proxy slips loose (Reke escapes, the deal closes, the basin is breached by a rival), the Court regains access and the player must fall back to a confrontation or alliance approach. The political seal is fragile if not finished.

## Endgame Path 6: Mystery-Resolution / Exposure — "Let the World Decide"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player has assembled the full proof — **REV_005/006** (the Sealed Archive, the Frozen Works, the Ruin'd Crown records), **the Last Voice** (a Concord witness), and ideally **the Ash-Crown Fragment** (legitimacy) — and chooses to make the truth public rather than act unilaterally.
Alternate Entry: with partial proof, the player can still expose a damaging fragment, forcing the Court and the rivals to react.

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): The Case Assembled
The player gathers the continent's proof of the harvest into an undeniable case: the Sealed Archive's signed Quietfall (REV_006), the Frozen Works' cold-preserved records (REV_005/006), the Heartlands' imperial confirmation, and — decisively — the **Last Voice**, a Concord member who can *testify* that the harvest was real and the architects survive. The player brings this to the powers that can act: the reformed Tidewater Council (Dann/Kade), the Glassmere League, the Mourner old-songs network, the free-holds. *Changes:* the truth becomes public; the Court loses its concealment, its deepest weapon.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The Court Unmasked
Once the world knows the Hollow Court is real and restarting the harvest, the Court's century of secrecy collapses. The living powers must decide together what to do with the keystone — and the player's accumulated allies and legitimacy (the Ash-Crown) steer that decision. This can force **Seize** (the world demands a "responsible" living owner), **Transform** (the Sunmark model + public consent + the Last Voice anchor a consensual system), or a public **Seal** (the world agrees to let the dead rest). *(DM: Exposure removes the Court's leverage and concealment; it cannot reach Restart, because secrecy was the Court's only path to a "gentle" restart.)*

### Regions Involved
Caradril (the Council; the Archive); Glassmere (the League); the Reach (the basin); the far regions whose records and allies built the case.

### Key Factions
- **The living powers:** reformed Caradril, Glassmere League, the Mourner network, the free-holds — convened by the player's exposure.
- **Hollow Court:** unmasked, stripped of secrecy; Veyl can no longer act in the dark.
- **Cinder Ledger / Concord Remnant:** scramble to control the narrative (and the machine).

### Key NPCs
The Last Voice (the decisive witness), Sefa Dann/Yorell Kade (reformed Caradril), Oren Glass/Sefwy Holt (the League), Wend and the old-songs network (moral authority), Veyl (unmasked), Maire (vindicated by exposure).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
The Sealed Archive (Caradril); the Frozen Works/Ruin'd Crown records (far); D20/D23 (the unmasked keystone).

### Major Threats
Assassination and suppression attempts (the Court's surface agents, the Ledger, the Remnant trying to bury the truth); the climax is an *information war*, not a boss fight.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
Secrecy was the Court's only weapon for a "gentle" restart — exposure makes Restart impossible. Veyl is undone not by force but by being *seen*. Maire is vindicated; her heresy (REV_010) becomes the public's hope. The danger is that a living power (Remnant/Ledger) seizes the machine "responsibly" — repeating the sin in daylight.

### Key Decisions
- Whom to expose the truth to (which power steers the outcome).
- Whether to include the Ash-Crown legitimacy (→ Seize) or the Sunmark model + Last Voice (→ Transform).
- Whether to let a living power seize the machine, or push for public Seal/Transform.

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Transform (public consent):** the world, the Last Voice, the Sunmark model, and a turned Maire build a consensual system openly; the Court ends itself. (Outcome E.)
- **Seize by the People:** the world demands a "responsible" living owner; the player is kingmaker (or new tyrant). (Outcome C.)
- **Public Seal:** the world agrees to let the dead rest; resurrection ends by consensus. (Outcome A.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: assembling the case.
Level 18: bringing it to the living powers.
Level 19: the Court unmasked; the world reacts.
Level 20 / Endgame: the public decision enacted.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
A non-combat, social/investigative endgame. The player's allies and proof do the work; the Last Voice is the win condition. No descent or boss required, though a confrontation can punctuate it.

### Catastrophic Failure State
If the Court or a rival suppresses the truth (kills the Last Voice, buries the Archive), exposure fails and the player must fall back to direct action. A botched exposure that names the Court without proof can be dismissed as a ghost story — and warns the Court.

---

## Endgame Path 7: Sacrifice / Costly Containment — "Pay the Price Yourself"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player has the **Quiet Country Vessel** and accepts a personal/cosmic cost, OR is willing to spend their own Remembrance (especially if the PC is thin-born — REV_004/009).
Alternate Entry: a faction can pay the price instead (the Wardens bind themselves to the seal forever; a Custodian is contained).

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): The Weight Understood
The player learns that containing the harvest cleanly may require a sacrifice the machine itself demands — a soul to anchor the seal, a Custodian to be contained in the Vessel, or the player's own Remembrance offered to balance the drained Quiet Country (REV_009). The Last Voice and Maire explain the cost honestly. *Changes:* the player faces a personal stake no artifact or army can pay for them.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): The Price Paid
At the keystone the player enacts the sacrifice: **contain Veyl in the Vessel** (he is removed, the harvest stalls — a Seal/Transform hybrid), **bind themselves or an ally to the seal** (a forever-watch, like the original Wardens), or **offer their own Remembrance** to anchor a consensual system (a Transform that costs the player part of themselves). *(DM: Sacrifice reaches Seal and partial Transform; it is the most personally costly approach and the most emotionally resonant for a solo PC.)*

### Regions Involved
Sundering Reach (the keystone); the Vessel's origin site (far Concord ritual site).

### Key Factions
- **Ashen Wardens:** the model of the forever-watch (the original Custodians' sacrifice mirrored).
- **Hollow Court:** Veyl can be *contained* rather than destroyed (a mercy and a horror); Maire may offer herself for the synthesis.

### Key NPCs
The player (the sacrifice), Maire (may share the cost; REV_010), Veyl (contained in the Vessel), Othetha (the forever-watch model), the Last Voice (witness to the cost).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
D20 → D23; the Vessel deployed at the keystone.

### Major Threats
The cost itself (the player risks their Remembrance, their life, or their freedom); Orre's defense; the Vessel's catastrophe risk if the containment fails.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
Containing Veyl rather than killing him is the path that most honors the Court's tragedy — Veyl is not a monster but a grieving idealist, and the Vessel "keeps" him as he kept the dead. Maire may volunteer to be contained or to share the synthesis-cost, redeeming the Court from within.

### Key Decisions
- Who pays the price (the player, an ally, a Custodian).
- Whether to contain Veyl (mercy) or destroy him.
- Whether the sacrifice buys a Seal or anchors a Transform.

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Containment Seal:** Veyl contained in the Vessel; the harvest stalls; the player or an ally keeps a forever-watch. (Outcome A.)
- **Sacrificial Transform:** the player/Maire offers their Remembrance to anchor a consensual system; the Court ends itself; a costly, beautiful ending. (Outcome E, partial.)
- **Bittersweet self-sacrifice:** the player binds themselves to the seal — the world saved, the PC's story closed. (Outcome A, personal.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: understanding the cost.
Level 18: gathering the Vessel/preparing the sacrifice.
Level 19: the descent; the price faced.
Level 20 / Endgame: the price paid; the outcome enacted.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
The most personal solo ending — the player's own stakes (life, Remembrance, freedom) are the win condition. The Vessel provides the mechanism; no army or fight is required, though the player may want allies to keep the forever-watch.

### Catastrophic Failure State
A failed containment (the Vessel misused) frees a broken Custodian (wrong-come-back catastrophe); a sacrifice given in vain (the system not anchored) means the price was paid for nothing. Telegraphed; the Last Voice/Maire warn of the cost before it is paid.

## Endgame Path 8: Partial / Bittersweet Victory — "Save What You Can"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player arrives under-informed or under-supported (lacking REV_010, key allies, or a decisive artifact) but still acts. **This is the default for a player who engaged the arc but didn't complete the deepest preparation.**
Alternate Entry: any approach above, attempted with incomplete means, degrades into this.

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18): What the Player Has
The player descends or acts with whatever they banked — perhaps the Wardens but not Maire, or the Shard but not the Last Voice, or political leverage but no descent. The DM honestly assesses which outcomes remain reachable (per the Approach × Outcome map in `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md`): **without REV_010, Transform is locked**, but Seal, Destroy, Seize, and Restart remain. *Changes:* the player chooses among what they earned, knowing each cost.

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20): A Flawed Resolution
The player enacts a degraded version of an outcome: a **partial Seal** (the harvest paused but the seal fragile, needing future tending); a **messy Destroy** (the dead freed but some not cleanly); a **compromised Seize** (a living faction takes the machine with the player unable to fully control the terms); or a **delayed Restart** (the Court paused but not stopped). *(DM: every outcome has a bittersweet degraded form; the campaign always ends in a real, consequential resolution.)*

### Regions Involved
Wherever the player's incomplete preparation reaches; the keystone.

### Key Factions
Whichever the player banked; the rivals exploit the player's gaps.

### Key NPCs
The player's banked allies (fewer than ideal); Veyl (exploits the player's missing pieces); Maire (unreachable without REV_010 — the locked Transform).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
D20 → D23 (if the player descends); whatever the player's means allow.

### Major Threats
The player's *gaps* — missing allies, missing knowledge, a fragile seal, a rival who exploits the weakness.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
Veyl exploits exactly what the player lacks. Without Maire (REV_010), the player cannot end the Court from within — the best they can do is seal, destroy, or seize, each leaving a thread (a buried Court, a destabilized boundary, a new tyranny).

### Key Decisions
- Which degraded outcome to accept (the DM offers the costs honestly).
- Whether to risk a fragile Seal or a messy Destroy.
- Whether to live with a thread left dangling (sequel hook).

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **Fragile Seal:** the harvest paused, the seal needing future tending; a Warden forever-watch under-resourced. (Outcome A, degraded.)
- **Messy Destroy:** the dead freed but not all cleanly; the boundary destabilized; resurrection gone. (Outcome D, degraded.)
- **Compromised Seize:** a living faction takes the machine on its own terms; the player a reluctant kingmaker. (Outcome C, degraded.)

### Milestone Guidance
Level 17: assessing what the player has.
Level 18: choosing the reachable outcome.
Level 19: the flawed climax.
Level 20 / Endgame: a bittersweet resolution.

### Solo-Play Adjustments
This path *guarantees* a solo player always gets an ending, even under-prepared. The DM offers the honest costs and lets the player choose; no synthesis, but a real, consequential close.

### Catastrophic Failure State
If even the degraded action fails (the player loses the keystone race entirely), it becomes Path 9. But Path 8 itself is the safety net: a flawed victory, not a defeat.

## Endgame Path 9: Catastrophic Failure & Aftermath — "The World After the Player Fails"

Levels: 17–20
Entry Condition: the player does nothing, arrives far too late, loses the keystone race, or is defeated — and no banked ally finishes the work.
This is **not a "game over" screen** — it is a playable, consequential aftermath.

### Phase 1 (Levels 17–18 equivalent): The Default Completes
If the player never engages, the master clock (#1) reaches its final stage: **the harvest fully restarts under the Hollow Court's rule.** **Veyl** and the Court emerge as immortal stewards; the Quiet Country is "managed" by consumption; the thinning is addressed by feeding on the dead. *(DM: the worst default — a slow horror beneath a calm surface.)*

### Phase 2 (Levels 19–20 equivalent): The Aftermath World
Alternative catastrophes by *how* the player failed:
- **Restart (Court rules):** an age of order under the Court; the dead consumed forever; a calm, monstrous peace. **Veyl** rules; **Maire** is silenced or sidelined; the Wardens are obsolete.
- **Rival Seize (lost the race):** the **Concord Remnant** (Hollin Vane/Quorrin) seizes the keystone for a living tyranny, OR the **Cinder Ledger** (Vyre) makes a market in souls. The original sin repeated by living hands.
- **Uncontrolled Destroy (Gravecaller breach):** **Cole Ashby**'s cell shatters the keystone alone; the dead are freed catastrophically; resurrection ends; the living/dead boundary destabilizes; the Quiet Country's fate uncertain — an honest but broken world.

### Regions Involved
The whole continent — the aftermath reshapes every region (see `REGIONAL_ESCALATION_PATHS.md` fall-states).

### Key Factions
Whoever won — the Court, the Remnant, the Ledger, or the chaos of the Gravecaller breach.

### Key NPCs
Veyl (rules, in the Restart aftermath); Hollin Vane/Quorrin or Vyre (the new tyrant/monopolist, in the Seize aftermath); Cole Ashby (triumphant/horrified, in the Destroy aftermath); the player's surviving allies (mourning, resisting, or fled).

### Key Dungeons/Sites
The keystone (now controlled by the victor); the basin (the Court's open door, in the Restart aftermath).

### Major Threats
The victor's consolidation; the player (if alive) becomes a hunted resistance figure or a survivor in a changed world.

### The Hollow Court in This Path (DM-ONLY)
In the Restart aftermath, Veyl achieves his life's purpose — and it is a horror precisely because he is *right* that it stops the thinning. Maire's heresy dies with her silencing. The campaign's tragedy is complete: the grieving idealist won.

### Key Decisions
- Whether the (surviving) player resists, flees, or lives within the aftermath.
- Whether a late, desperate action can still salvage something (a sequel hook).

### Possible Endings (2–3)
- **The Court's Age (Restart):** immortal stewards; the dead consumed; calm horror.
- **The New Tyranny / Market (Seize):** the Remnant or Ledger rules the dead; sin in daylight.
- **The Broken World (uncontrolled Destroy):** the dead freed catastrophically; resurrection gone; an honest, destabilized world.

### Milestone Guidance
(This path is reached by failure/inaction; leveling is moot — the campaign resolves into its aftermath epilogue.)

### Solo-Play Adjustments
Even total failure produces a *playable bleak epilogue*, not a dead screen. The player can roleplay the aftermath, attempt a late salvage, or close the campaign on the tragedy. The DM frames it as a real consequence of choices, never an arbitrary punishment.

### Catastrophic Failure State
This *is* the catastrophic failure state — and it is authored, consequential, and survivable as story. The world is changed; the player's choices (or absence of them) made it so.

## Cross-Playbook Notes (DM)

- **The nine approaches map to the five outcomes** per `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md` Part 3. Only **Transform (E)** is locked behind REV_010 + Maire + allies/artifact; every other outcome is reachable by multiple approaches.
- **At least one non-combat win condition is always live** for the player's path (Solo-Play Guarantees, `ENDGAME_STRUCTURE.md` Part 6).
- **Mix freely.** A player can build a coalition (Path 2), bring an artifact (Path 3), turn Maire (REV_010), and enact a ritual Transform (Path 4) — the playbooks are facets of one open endgame.
- **Keep DM-only apex content (the harvest mechanism, the Custodians' nature, the keystone's location) in D23 and DM-only sections.** Never narrate it before REV_007's gate.
