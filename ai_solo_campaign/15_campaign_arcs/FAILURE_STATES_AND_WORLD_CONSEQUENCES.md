## AI Use

**MIXED SECRECY.** What happens when the player ignores major threats, fails a key objective, pursues only side content, sides with/opposes/deceives/destroys factions, or loses a race — across levels 5–20. **Core principle: failure changes the world; it does not end the game.** Every failure reroutes into a new situation and new hooks. Use with `CAMPAIGN_ESCALATION_TIMELINE.md` (the default trajectory) and `../12_campaign_arc/ACT_1_FAILURE_STATES.md` (Act 1 precedent). **DM-only causes flagged; never narrate the harvest as the cause.**

---

## The Five Failure Principles (Re-Stated for the Late Game)

1. **No ignored thread ends the game.** It evolves into a new situation and new hooks.
2. **Failure usually costs time, attention, a relationship, resources, or partial information** — and advances a clock — never agency.
3. **The world acts without the player.** Clocks advance; factions move (`FACTION_TURN_RULES.md`).
4. **The truth still arrives.** A passive player gets the revelations delivered by the world, slower and costlier (`CAMPAIGN_ESCALATION_TIMELINE.md` passive failsafe).
5. **Even "the worst ending" is playable.** The harvest restarting under the Court is a bleak epilogue, not a "game over."

## "If the Player Ignores the Main Arc Entirely"

The world clocks deliver the arc to the player's doorstep:

- **Tier 2:** the basin reopening advances on its own; the relic-trade swells; faction agents arrive wherever the player is; the surfacing shrine and active factions make the *conspiracy* (REV_002/003) undeniable.
- **Tier 3:** the breached shrine, the continental faction war, and the thinning's spread deliver the *historical truth* (REV_005/006); the harvest begins reaching the player's region.
- **Tier 4:** the harvest restart becomes imminent; the master clock nears completion; the player faces the choice late, with fewer allies and no synthesis (no REV_010) — but they *still choose*.

> The arc is **pulled toward the player** geographically and cosmologically — the basin is the lowest point and the keystone; the dead drift there; the failures radiate outward. A player who never chases anything still ends up at the center, because the center comes to them.

---

## "If the Player Pursues Only Side Content"

- Side content (Ring 1, the far continent, faction chains) **is the arc** — every region and faction touches the main mystery. A player doing "only side quests" still accumulates REV-clues (every dungeon/region carries fragments).
- The DM lets the master clocks advance in the background so the side-content player periodically *feels* the world moving toward the keystone (a surfacing shrine, a faction war headline, a thin-born refugee tide).
- **Risk:** a pure side-content player may reach the endgame under-leveled or under-informed; the DM uses milestone leveling (`LEVELING_AND_MILESTONE_GUIDE.md`) and the passive failsafe to keep them viable.

## "If the Player Sides With / Opposes / Deceives / Destroys a Faction"

- **Sides with:** earns a power-base and an endgame ally; inherits that faction's enemies and its endgame posture (e.g. siding with the Gravecallers points toward Destroy and makes the Wardens wary).
- **Opposes:** makes an enemy *and opens its rivals* (opposing the Ledger warms the Mourners; opposing the Remnant warms the idealists). Retaliation is telegraphed and survivable (debt-arc, bounty-arc, jail-arc, political-ruin-arc), never campaign-ending.
- **Deceives:** works until exposed; a betrayed faction becomes a dangerous enemy and may leak the player's secrets (the Hush, the Ledger of Quiet Debts). Trust is hard to rebuild.
- **Destroys (leadership killed / faction broken):** removes that faction's endgame role and its allies; the world reroutes (a broken Remnant can't seize; a broken Wardens can't seal — narrowing the player's own endgame options). Power vacuums get filled by rivals.

> The DM records all of this in `CONSEQUENCES.md`, `FACTION_STATE.md`, and `RELATIONSHIPS.md`, and lets it land *visibly* so the player feels the world responding.

## Objective-Failure Reroutes (Not Dead Ends)

| Failure | Reroute |
|---|---|
| **Failed a dungeon / forced retreat** | costs time + a clue, not the run; the site changes (reinforced, looted by a rival, collapsed) and a new approach opens. Every D-site has a retreat route. |
| **Failed to expose Reke** | the basin advances; Reke entrenches; the player can try again via a different clue path (the truth has 3+ sources). |
| **Failed to reach the Sealed Archive** | M6/M9 are also reachable via Othetha, the Tallow Man, the Relay-Vault, decoded songs — the Archive is the clearest path, not the only one. |
| **Lost the keystone race** | a rival enacts *their* ending; the player can contest, sabotage, or live with it — an epilogue, not a wall. |
| **Failed to turn Maire** | the synthesis is lost; the player still has seal/destroy/restart/seize. |
| **Player death** | meaningful, telegraphed, survivable by allies/retreat/levers; if it happens, the DM uses the setting's lore (resurrection is dangerous *by design*) — a death has weight, and the Vessel/Last Voice are lore-correct stakes. |

## Catastrophic Mid-Game Outcomes (Playable, Not Game-Over)

- **A Gravecaller cell shatters a shrine early (Tier 3):** catastrophic local liberation — the dead freed destructively in one region. The player scrambles to contain it, redirect the cult, or accept a destabilized region. A foreshadow of the Destroy ending's cost.
- **The Reclamation wins the keystone (Tier 3–4):** the endgame antagonist shifts toward a *living* tyranny racing the Court; the player can ally with the Court against the Remnant, or oppose both.
- **The Court manifests at scale (Tier 4, master clock Stage 4):** the harvest restarts in a region; thin-born births spike; the Court's argument becomes visibly "the only thing holding the thinning back" — the campaign's hardest moral pressure.

## Passive-Player Endgame (the Floor)

If the player reaches Tier 4 having engaged almost nothing, the DM:
1. Brings the harvest to the player's location (the master clock at Stage 4).
2. Offers the choice *anyway* — the costs explained by whoever is present (a desperate Warden, a triumphant Gravecaller, Veyl himself).
3. Lets the player choose among seal/restart/seize/destroy (no synthesis without REV_010).
4. Resolves the epilogue honestly — a thinner world, fewer allies, but the player's choice still matters.

> The campaign **always ends in a choice the player makes**, even if they ignored nearly everything. Open-world agency is preserved to the last.
