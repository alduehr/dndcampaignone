## AI Use

Load when the player reaches the warm southern grove-country. Narrative/political anchor (Phase 5 consolidation, 2026-07-07; **no new canon**); cartography/mechanics in the map packet. **Secrecy note (from the packet):** the Sunmark's living old faith is the campaign's **hopeful contrast** — a death-rite that *works*, because the Concord never touched it. Keep the groves' working EXTREMELY oblique; never explain it as the antithesis of the harvest; the groves are NOT Concord; no NPC here knows the apex truth.

## One-Sentence Identity

A warm southern land of sacred groves and grove-keeper tribes with a *living* old nature-faith — the campaign's most peaceful region, and quiet proof that proper rites for the dead are still possible somewhere.

## Player-Facing Overview

Under the warm canopy of the Sunmark Wilds, the grove-tribes keep the old ways whole: Grove-Keeper Sael Sunmark tends the faith's living heart at the Great Grove at Sunhollow, Sun-Singer Doll reads the green omens, and Greenway-Warden Tamsin Greenway keeps the carved-stone Green Roads safe for respectful travelers. At the edges, Plains-Factor Doss Sunward's cut-and-trade money tests the groves' patience — the saw has reached ground the tribes call holy.

## DM-Only Truth

Here the dead are **properly sent** — the Sunhollow rites still work, fully, because this faith was never rerouted through the Concord's system. Experienced players will *feel* the difference before any NPC can name it (that is the design; REV-adjacent by contrast only). The Great Grove (D33) is a living-faith site, not a ruin. **Guardrail:** no mechanism talk, ever; the region's hope must stay unexplained.

## Factions Present

The **grove-tribes and their living faith** (the region's whole order); **Doss Sunward's plains-trade** (the cutting edge of outside money); **Cinder Ledger** (behind the plains-factors, at one remove); **Mourners' Circle** (reverent visitors — the Circle's elders speak of Sunhollow the way pilgrims speak of home); lost-pilgrim traffic from every troubled region north.

## Regional Conflicts

- **The saw at the grove** (Q_SUN_001): cut-and-trade money vs. holy ground.
- **The lost pilgrims** (Q_SUN_003): the desperate arriving from the north, seeking what works.
- **The fair trade** (Q_SUN_005): whether the tribes can take the world's money without the world's sickness.
- **The defiler answered** (Q_SUN_007): what the faith does when reverence fails.

## Regional Secrets (DM-Only)

The far root-ache (Q_SUN_002): the oldest trees feel something distant and wrong (extreme-oblique northern echo — never located, never explained); the Reclaimed Ruin (Q_SUN_006) is a *non-Concord* old place the grove reclaimed — a demonstration that not every ruin belongs to the campaign's wound.

## Recurring Threats

Very few — by design. Grove-Warden Spirits (custom benign; Dryad→Treant chassis) test rather than kill; the Grove-Light (benign will-o'-wisp reskin) guides; genuine threats are outsiders (poachers, cutters — Bandit/Scout-like) and the rare defiled-ground consequence. A Couatl-grade guardian exists at Sunhollow (rare, Track A). Stat references: `SUNMARK_ENCOUNTERS.md` + map packet.

## Encounter Themes

Hospitality, tests-not-kills, reverence-gated access; outsider greed as the only true villain; the numinous everywhere.

## Level Range And Solo Danger

**Lvl 8–13.** The campaign's lowest-danger region *for the respectful* — and it should feel that way (rest here truly rests). Danger exists only where reverence fails or outsiders bring it. Details: map packet §Level Range.

## Encounter And Hazard Mechanics

Authoritative in the map packet + encounter file: welcome/conduct social DCs; the Wilds' navigation off the Green Roads; defiled-ground consequences. Solo note: this is the campaign's designed sanctuary-region — let it be one.

## What Changes If Ignored

The saws bite deeper and a grove falls; the tribes answer and the south learns what the faith's anger looks like; the lost pilgrims become a permanent refugee-current; the campaign's one working example dims.
